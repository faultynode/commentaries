# Hua 33 \- *Die Bernauer Manuskripte über das Zeitbewußtsein (1917/18)*

# 

# **Editors' Introduction**

## **I**

The editors (Rudolf Bernet and Dieter Lohmar) open by noting that the Bernau manuscripts on time-consciousness were first publicly mentioned by Martin Heidegger in his 1928 foreword to Husserl's *Vorlesungen zur Phänomenologie des inneren Zeitbewußtseins* (Jahrbuch IX). Heidegger remarked that further investigations on time-consciousness, connected with the problem of individuation and taken up again especially since 1917, were reserved for a later publication. This mention created great expectations for the publication of this material.

An overview of three distinct phases in Husserl's investigation of time is given. The editors caution that numerous manuscripts produced between these main phases show Husserl was continually occupied with the theme. The phases are thematically and methodologically distinct, but not isolated from one another.

The first phase spans approximately 1905–1911. It begins with the lectures for WS 1904/05, which were first edited by Edith Stein in summer 1917 and then by Husserl himself. Heidegger published this material in 1928 along with thirteen supplements from 1905–1910. The editors note that less than half of the published text actually derives from the 1904/05 lectures; portions come from 1909–1911 research manuscripts, and still others from the 1917 redaction. The critical edition in Husserliana X documented the various working phases.

The second phase of intensive engagement is the Bernau manuscripts of 1917/18. They were written during two holiday stays in Bernau, immediately following Husserl's redaction of Stein's compilation of his time-manuscripts in autumn 1917\. Husserl regarded these manuscripts so highly that in 1927 he described them to Roman Ingarden as "my main work" (*mein Hauptwerk*). From 1926, Husserl unsuccessfully sought students to prepare the material for publication. Later, Eugen Fink (Husserl's personal assistant from August 1928\) was entrusted with the task.

The third phase consists of the so-called "C-manuscripts," research manuscripts composed from approximately 1930 onward. Publication plans existed for these as well: after the first volume (the Bernau manuscripts), a second was to follow with the later time-investigations. Neither project advanced beyond the planning stage.

The external occasion for the renewed engagement with time in 1917 was Edith Stein. She had worked on the *Ideas II* manuscript from October 1916 to about April 1917\. Husserl barely engaged with the results, to Stein's dismay. After working on Husserl's revision of the Sixth Logical Investigation (1913/14), Stein turned to the "Zeitbewußtsein" (time-consciousness) dossier in summer 1917\. In July 1917 she apparently completed the compilation. In September 1917, she reports to Ingarden that Husserl is now working intensively on time in Bernau.

During the holiday stay in Bernau, Husserl first redacted the time-consciousness portion of the 1904/05 lectures, making extensive changes. Following this resumption of the problems of inner time-consciousness, Husserl composed the Bernau manuscripts proper. During two stays in Bernau (July 30–October 1, 1917 and February 1–April 27, 1918), the writings on time grew to a considerable volume.

The years 1916–1918 were a difficult time for Husserl and his family. His youngest son Wolfgang had fallen on March 8, 1916\. His elder son Gerhart was seriously wounded in Champagne in spring 1917\. Husserl's mother Julie died in early July 1917 in Vienna. The shifting fortunes of war and the heavy German losses also affected Husserl deeply. It was this partly oppressive atmosphere of war that Husserl sought to combat through intensified work.

Despite these adversities, Husserl was in excellent working condition during the Bernau stays, working about 9–10 hours daily. He writes of a "flood tide" after "years of ebb," and postpones the start of his WS 1917/18 lecture on logic to give his time-investigations a certain completion. He was clearly aware of the new direction his time-analyses had taken.

During the second Bernau stay, Husserl's excellent disposition continues, though he brings multiple projects along. He intends to review a large dossier on judgment theory that Stein had organized. His descriptions of the time-work still sound enthusiastic: he speaks of a "great work growing up here in the quiet high valley—Time and Individuation, a renewal of rational metaphysics according to principles."

By April 1918, Husserl describes his goal as a "phenomenologically grounded rational ontology." He saw a tight connection between the investigations of inner time-consciousness and the individuation problem: he was not merely working on a phenomenology of time—which "cannot be detached purely by itself"—but on the whole enormous problem of individuation, the constitution of individual (and thus "factual") being in general and according to its essential basic forms.

In a June 1918 letter, Husserl calls his project a "phenomenology of individuation" and connects it with *Ideas II*: the main effort concerns a phenomenology of individuation, i.e., the concept of a natural and spiritual philosophy, to which *Ideas II* also belongs.

One reason why these important works were never published by Husserl is his working style: an untiring forward striving toward ever new insights. He would begin with the intention to unify and present a topic, but would then be carried along by new problems. Over time, manuscripts accumulated and it became increasingly difficult to bring them into unified form. He eventually tried to hand this task to his assistants or students (Heidegger, Ingarden, Stein, Landgrebe, Fink).

Publication preparations occupied first Stein, then Landgrebe, then Fink. The collaboration was very difficult. In decades of cooperation with Landgrebe, only *Erfahrung und Urteil* was ultimately realized.

No earlier than April 1926, Husserl proposed to Heidegger that he prepare the earlier time-manuscripts for publication. It is likely that Husserl already thought of the Bernau manuscripts at this point. Heidegger declined to work with stenographic research manuscripts, citing his teaching load in Marburg. He received only Stein's handwritten 1917 compilation, which he published in 1928 with minimal editorial intervention.

In September 1927, Husserl turned to Ingarden with the idea that he should prepare the Bernau manuscripts for print. Ingarden could not comply. Husserl then entrusted the manuscripts to Fink, who was to prepare them for publication in collaboration with Husserl. When this failed, Fink handed the material to the Leuven Husserl Archive in 1969\.

The L-group, in which the Bernau manuscripts reside, comprises nearly 800 stenographically written pages. These are research manuscripts—short analyses of time-problems that are not homogeneous in theme, method, or perspective. Some are experimental, standing in explicit or implicit contradiction with others. The quality of argumentation and depth of analysis also varies.

Slightly less than half the pages are in group L I (357 pages), the rest in L II (430 pages). Fink first sorted the whole group by formal criteria. L I contains 21 dossiers of time-analyses from 1917/18. L II contains material already published in Heidegger's edition as supplements, plus material Fink assumed would go into Landgrebe's *Erfahrung und Urteil*. For similar reasons, little from L II could be used for the present edition.

Some further Bernau-era analyses touching on the time-investigations exist in other manuscript groups. These manuscripts, not selected by Husserl for Fink's treatment, could only sparingly be included. Strict criteria of relevance to the time-thematic and text quality were applied to keep the volume from growing further.

Fink reports in a spring 1929 note about Husserl's wishes for the reworking: bringing out the most essential advances over the 1905–1910 time-consciousness work, a "restitution of the Brentano-Aristotelian doctrine" in phenomenological deepening, furthermore the diagram, the individuation-problematic leading already toward "transcendental logic," including the doctrine of the temporality of ideal objects.

The attempts to bring the Bernau manuscripts into consistent, print-ready form fell during a time of intense, sometimes hectic labor on various publication projects in which Fink was always involved: the *Encyclopaedia Britannica* article, the Amsterdam and Paris lectures, *Formal and Transcendental Logic*, the "systematic basic work of phenomenology," the "second logical book" (*Erfahrung und Urteil*), and the *Crisis*. Fink contributed to all these projects to Husserl's great satisfaction, and the fruitlessness of his preparations for the Bernau manuscripts becomes understandable against this background of overflowing work.

In February 1931, Husserl reports to Ingarden that Fink has already brought the Bernau time-investigations to "the unity of a text" and that this, along with the Cartesian Meditations, should appear in the *Jahrbuch*. In subsequent years (1932–33), the project is repeatedly mentioned but under increasing reservations.

By late 1933, Husserl proposes that the "writing on time" appear under both Fink's and Husserl's names. The working title is now "Time and Temporalization" (*Zeit und Zeitigung*). Husserl also announces a planned second volume containing "time-investigations since 1930" (the C-manuscripts). From now on, the planned edition of the Bernau manuscripts is often called the "first volume." In July 1934, Husserl proposes that Fink take complete responsibility. The project continues to be mentioned through 1935–1937.

Despite Husserl's repeated expressions of hope for an imminent conclusion, the publication of the Bernau manuscripts fell increasingly into the background amid other large publication projects. Fink's own work on the time-problem was not entirely fruitless; his elaborations for the jointly published treatise on "Zeit und Zeitigung" transformed into his own manuscript on time, which he reportedly destroyed.

## **II**

The editors explain their text-selection decisions. They chose to include only texts that can be securely dated to the 1917/18 Bernau stays. The further development of the individuation problematic in the early-twenties Nachlass texts is therefore not represented. Earlier-dated texts within the L-group, which Fink had already assigned to a separate group, were reserved for a revision of Husserliana X.

Greater difficulties attended the selection of Bernau manuscripts that Husserl himself had excluded from his L-group compilation. Given the already substantial volume and thematic unity of the L-group manuscripts, a more restrictive selection principle was applied: to bring the individuation-problematic, which comes only limitedly to expression in the L-manuscripts, to adequate presentation.

Still greater problems than text selection arose with their arrangement. Since exact dates could only be determined for a few manuscripts, and all texts were produced within a very short span, a purely chronological ordering was both impossible and unsatisfying. A systematic reconstruction of the interconnections among the questions treated had to be attempted—a risky venture since Husserl's own indications are entirely lacking.

These are "research manuscripts"—texts in which Husserl tried to gain clarity by experimenting with various, possibly contradictory solutions. Expressions of doubt (including the memorable exclamation "Da steht einem also der Verstand still" — "One's understanding simply comes to a standstill") and aporetic, sometimes contradictory treatments are far more frequent than presentations of secured results. Husserl often interrupts his investigation, starts anew, or turns to a different question. Some dossiers contain several sub-dossiers; sometimes a single dossier was divided by the editors into several texts with more unified content.

The editors organized the texts into six groups, each defined by a leading question. This division can rely on a (vague) hint from Husserl in only one case (Group III). The editors acknowledge their arrangement is not the only possible one and that it inevitably highlights some connections while obscuring others. The first three groups especially, despite their different perspectives, must be understood as a unity. The group headings, most text and supplement titles, and the paragraph titles are all editorial additions, marked with angular brackets per Husserliana convention. Given the abstract and difficult content, the titles were deliberately formulated in relatively detailed fashion.

## **III**

Husserl's earlier time-texts (Husserliana X) begin with the description of the perception of temporal objects (a sustained tone) or time-objects (the duration of a tone). Through engagement with Brentano, Meinong, James, Stumpf, Stern, and others (the Bernau manuscripts, notably, contain no engagement with these thinkers), Husserl came to the conviction that while perception of a lasting object normally occurs in a lasting act of perception, the duration of perception does not yet explain how the perception of an objective duration is possible. The analysis sharpened into the question of whether and how a present act can perceive a temporal extension exceeding the actual present. Husserl's insight that a duration can only be presently grasped if the object's preceding and now past givenness is co-grasped along with its present givenness was still in line with the contemporary literature.

For this present grasping of an objective past connected with the present grasping of an objective present, Husserl first used the term "primary memory" and somewhat later his neologism "retention." He differed substantively from Brentano and Meinong in that he understood retention neither as an act of (phantasy-like) re-presentation nor as a categorical act of subsequent synthesis, but rather as a modality of perceiving itself. He wanted to maintain the distinction between the present ("ur-impressional") grasping of the present tone and the present ("retentional") grasping of the past tone, while insisting that both graspings are non-independent moments of a single act of present perceiving. This was and remained Husserl's basic intuition, whose analytically elaborated determination occupied him for years beyond the failed attempts of the 1904/05 lectures. Research into the "protention"—the present grasping of, say, a future tone—remained secondary. Only in the Bernau manuscripts does protention and especially its interweaving with retention receive due attention.

An essential role in the development of Husserl's determination of retention was played by his multi-year study of the nature of intuitive phantasy and its demarcation from acts of recollection. From this emerged the insight that retentional consciousness—in contrast to phantasy and recollection—is not an act of re-presentation (*Vergegenwärtigung*), but is nevertheless a modified consciousness of an original presence. Retentional consciousness of the past is thus neither a reproductive re-presentation like recollection nor a simple presencing of the present like original perception, but, as a modification of the latter, it is the present perception of a pastness. Husserl found it difficult to precisely determine this perception of the past within his doctrine of intentional consciousness, and the associated difficulties troubled him in the Bernau manuscripts as well.

Problems arose especially from applying the schema of intentional apprehension of a sensation-content (borrowed from the phenomenological determination of outer perception of spatial objects) to time-consciousness and thus to retentional perceptual consciousness. With respect to the latter, the question arises how a present apprehension of an equally present sensation could succeed in retentionally perceiving a past presence.

Husserl's various efforts to solve this puzzle led him repeatedly to the same point: the givenness of the sensation-content implied in retentional consciousness. It became clear that the modification characterizing present retentional consciousness of a past presence must affect not only the apprehension but necessarily also the underlying sensation-content. Otherwise it remains incomprehensible why in one case sensation occasions a perceptual apprehension of something present and in the other a retentional apprehension of something past. The "phantasma" (the modified sensation-content of a phantasy-like apprehension) offered itself as a model, but closer investigation of phantasy-consciousness revealed that this model contains its own puzzles concerning the nature and givenness of the phantasma.

The parallel investigation of retention and phantasy, continued into the Bernau manuscripts, led early on to the insight that the difficulties with the phantasma are fundamentally the same as those in determining the modified sensation-content in retentional consciousness. Calling the retentional sensation-datum a "phantasma" does not help and threatens to erase the distinction between presencing retention and re-presenting phantasy. The phenomenological analyses of retention and phantasy can only mutually enrich each other if one maintains the distinction between a phantasy-like and a retentional modification of the apprehension-content while acknowledging that both involve a modified sensory consciousness whose general nature still requires clarification. This task concerns not only retention and phantasy but already the consciousness of the unmodified sensation-content underlying a perceptual apprehension. Husserl calls the givenness of such an unmodified, originally present sensation an "Urimpression" in the earlier texts and an "Urpräsentation" (primal presentation) in the Bernau manuscripts.

The first decisive step toward clarifying the modified or unmodified givenness of the sensation-content underlying intentional apprehension is already found in Husserl's earlier time-texts, and these insights are never questioned in the Bernau manuscripts. However the intentional apprehension of this sensation-content may look—whether perceptual, retentional, or phantasy-like—it is certain that this content is itself given and that there must be a modified or unmodified consciousness of it. The difference between a present sensation and a retentionally given sensation ultimately derives from the different consciousness of these sensations.

Husserl calls this inner consciousness of sensation "absolute consciousness" in the earlier texts and "Urprozeß" (primal process) or "Urstrom" (primal stream) in the Bernau manuscripts. Sensations are called "immanent time-objects" in the earlier texts, distinguished from transcendent objects in objective time (e.g., a violin tone). The Bernau manuscripts usually call immanent time-objects simply temporal "events" (*Ereignisse*) and the inner consciousness of them "experience" (*Erlebnis*). This terminological change signals that Husserl no longer took for granted that sensations are given in the primal process as objects and that the primal process itself has the form of an objectifying intentional consciousness.

Even in the earlier texts, the introduction of "absolute" consciousness was attended by major problems that still vexed Husserl in Bernau. A first difficulty was the precise essential determination of this "absolute" consciousness or primal process in its relation to the "immanent time-objects" or "events" conscious within it. In his earlier texts, Husserl reached a double insight: first, the "absolutely" named consciousness of sensation-data must be a (sub-)species of intentional consciousness—the sensation-data cannot belong to it as "reell" components (otherwise the difference between retentional and ur-impressional consciousness would be unintelligible). Second, following the introduction of the transcendental-phenomenological reduction, the relation between "absolute" consciousness and sensation-datum was re-determined as a constitutive relation. Just as a transcendent object constitutes itself in the apprehension of a sensation-datum, so sensation itself constitutes itself in "absolute" consciousness as a (present, past, or future) immanent time-object.

These two insights were subjected to renewed thorough examination in the Bernau manuscripts, apparently without definitive result. In some Bernau texts, Husserl was again seized by doubt about whether the relation between primal process and temporal events is really one of constitution and whether they really belong to two different levels of consciousness. This doubt was grounded especially in the question whether events in the primal process actually come to prominence and grasping as (immanent) objects. This then put the first insight back in question—the characterization of the primal process as a species of intentional consciousness. These doubts and questions also pressed upon Husserl in the late C-group time-texts, confirming that the Bernau manuscripts reached no definitive determination of the primal process/events relation. Yet it is unmistakable that the late C-group texts develop a doctrine that—alongside other conceptions—is already found in the Bernau manuscripts.

This doctrine involves, briefly stated, a radical separation between egoic and pre-egoic (i.e., hyletic) time-processes. The C-manuscripts ultimately lead Husserl to deny all original constitutive achievement to the primal stream running without any ego-participation. Constitution of temporal objects is possible only when an ego-achievement is present. Only through the subsequent performance of an egoic act of reflection on the pre-intentional primal process, i.e., through an intervening modification of its nature, does the hyletic primal stream gain constitutive significance. This new doctrine is grounded not only in the deepening separation of active and passive forms of consciousness but also in the danger of infinite regress that haunts Husserl's phenomenology of time-consciousness like an uncanny shadow—in the early texts, the Bernau manuscripts, and the late C-manuscripts alike.

Already from the early Husserliana X texts, it is clear that there are actually several forms of infinite regress. A first, relatively harmless form arises because each new "ur-impression" modifies the givenness of all preceding ur-impressions by converting them into retentions, retentions of retentions, and so forth—potentially ad infinitum. This infinite modification process does not threaten the possibility of grasping the first ur-impression.

In the Bernau manuscripts, Husserl becomes increasingly uncertain whether such an infinite modification of the retentional past represents a phenomenologically confirmed finding. In the context of genetic phenomenology, he now examines how the growing distance from the present affects retentional givenness. He describes how far-removed past ur-presentations crowd together, losing their distinct differentiation and their affective pull on the present ego. The 1918 Bernau manuscripts devote detailed investigations to this phenomenon of "fading" (*Abklang*), distinguished from retention. Without active egoic attention, retentions of the remote past soon die off, and the process of their steady modification may cease.

In its most dangerous form, the infinite regress concerns the consciousness of "absolute" consciousness or the primal process. Husserl determines intentional consciousness of a transcendent time-object as an intentional apprehension of a pre-intentional sensation-datum. This sensation-datum is then also conscious in a deeper-lying consciousness—"absolute" consciousness or the primal process. The precise determination of this primal process still troubled Husserl in the Bernau manuscripts. The questions recur: is this "absolutely" named consciousness really a self-standing level of consciousness? Is it an intentional, transcendentally constituting consciousness? Does its intentionality still have the form of intentional apprehension of a pre-intentional content? If this consciousness required a further consciousness in which it becomes given, it would lose its "absolute" character, and a form of infinite regress would arise that renders the entire edifice of stacked levels of consciousness groundless.

The danger of infinite regress connected with the consciousness of "absolute" consciousness was not only clearly formulated in Husserl's early texts but eventually received an appealing solution. The core of this solution is the assumption that absolute consciousness must necessarily be simultaneously consciousness of immanent time-objects and consciousness of itself. Husserl tried to show that in the continuum of retentional modification, through the continuous change of time-perspective, a unitary immanent time-object with its time-position comes to prominence; and that, through the interlocking of each retention with all other retentions, a consciousness of the flow of the constantly self-modifying retentional consciousness itself arises. The flow of "absolute" retentional consciousness thus harbors a double intentionality: one directed at immanent time-objects ("transverse intentionality" / *Querintentionalität*) and one having the form of self-reference or self-consciousness of the flow ("longitudinal intentionality" / *Längsintentionalität*). These two intentional directions belong to one and the same process in the flow of continuous retentional modification and are thus inseparably connected.

This attempt to overcome the infinite regress was taken up anew and further developed in the Bernau manuscripts. In agreement with the earlier texts, a clear distinction is maintained between the retention of an immanent object and the retention of the primal process. Husserl made a crucial advance by now devoting much more attention to protention. This newly awakened interest in protention's contribution not only to consciousness of the primal process but also to the increasingly pressing question of consciousness of novelty is reflected in the new time-diagrams. Significant new insights arose especially from the precise investigation of the interweaving of retentional and protentional modification-continua. Husserl no longer contented himself with noting that each ur-presentation is embedded in a two-sided horizon of retentional and protentional givenness; he analyzed in often microscopic descriptions the role of protentions within the retentional continuum and the influence of retentions on the content-determination of protentions.

This rediscovery of the function of protention within retention and retention within protention arose especially from a more dynamic determination of time-consciousness connected with genetic phenomenology. The ur-presentation is now no longer the original core of time-consciousness but a mere limit-point where retentional and protentional modification-continua intersect. For the primal process, the genetic analysis shows it is not simply a mechanical process of continuous modification driven by displacement from present to past. As a life-process of consciousness, it is governed by passive tendencies and anticipations, forms of intensification and weakening of intuitive fullness, experiences of fulfillment and de-fulfillment.

Applying these results to consciousness of the primal process meant that Husserl no longer regarded the self-consciousness of this primal process exclusively as an achievement of retention (retentional "longitudinal intentionality"). According to the new Bernau doctrine, self-consciousness arises from the experience of present intuitive fulfillment—the consciousness of the continuous transition from retentional protentions to their intuitive realization. In this dynamically conceived fulfillment-process, the flow of consciousness comes to present appearance because what is presently given is simultaneously conscious as something already anticipated as future in past protentions. This present fulfillment-experience is actually a consciousness of the becoming-present of something anticipated in past protentions. Unlike in the early texts, this is no longer a merely subsequent (retentional) consciousness of the primal stream, but a self-consciousness of the primal stream essentially anchored in the flowing present.

This new doctrine did not establish itself unchallenged in the Bernau texts; alongside it, alternative and competing conceptions also gave the confrontation with infinite regress renewed prominence. Husserl was apparently not entirely satisfied. A difficulty can be seen in the fact that this doctrine straightforwardly treats the primal process as a self-standing level of intentional consciousness in which immanent time-objects (sensation-data, acts of apprehension) constitute themselves. Several Bernau texts seriously question this determination. In his detailed discussion of the difference between implicit perception and grasping (*Erfassung*) of immanent time-objects in the primal stream, Husserl also considers the possibility or rather impossibility of an "unconscious" primal process. Even if such an unconscious primal process cannot exist, the question remains whether an ungrasped primal process can properly be credited with constitutive achievement. If a subsequent egoic grasping (an act of reflection) were required, both the primal process's independence and its intentional character would become questionable, and its original self-consciousness could scarcely be maintained.

In the Bernau manuscripts, Husserl pursues at least two paths. A first leads him back near Brentano's well-known doctrine of inner consciousness, for which Husserl had already shown much sympathy despite all criticism. The primal process would then be nothing other than a concomitant phenomenon of the egoic performance of intentional acts in which transcendent time-objects constitute themselves. Rather than intentionally aiming at these acts as immanent time-objects, the primal process would simply be the non-independent, implicit performance-consciousness (*Vollzugsbewußtsein*) of these acts—intentional but not yet objectifying act-consciousness. Whether and under what circumstances the consciousness of the act and the consciousness of the consciousness of the act come apart in the intentional act of reflection on the original act-performance remains, it seems, undecided in the Bernau manuscripts.

A second path consists in returning to the schema "apprehension – apprehension-content," applying it not only to the constitution-relation between primal process and immanent time-objects but also to the primal process itself and the ur-presentations, retentions, and protentions animating its flow. This re-exposes Husserl to the danger of infinite regress, and it is characteristic of the relevant Bernau manuscripts that the treatment of the apprehension/content model and attempts to avert infinite regress typically go hand in hand.

Nevertheless, the difficulty of infinite regress cannot simply be blamed on any particular, more or less convincing doctrine of the primal process. It may be a far more fundamental problem affecting Husserl's entire systematic approach to a phenomenology of time. Perhaps there simply can be no determination of the origin of time that does not already presuppose time. Such concerns are not entirely foreign to the Bernau manuscripts, appearing at least in Husserl's treatment of novelty and the question of the possibility of a "first" ur-presentation.

## **IV**

The editors caution that their presentation of the Bernau manuscripts' systematic anchoring in the earlier texts should not suggest these are merely an appendix to Husserliana X. Between the last early time-texts and the first Bernau manuscripts (1911–1917) falls the publication of *Ideas I*, with its systematic account of the phenomenological reduction, transcendental idealism, the noetic-noematic correlation, and the introduction of the pure ego. These new findings entered the Bernau time-manuscripts.

Husserl often reflects on the phenomenological reduction and transcendental idealism in treating the constitutive function of time-consciousness, though the constitution of objective time notably recedes behind the analysis of immanent time-objects' constitution through the primal process. The Bernau manuscripts also develop for the first time a specifically noematically directed description of time-consciousness: Husserl devoted extensive, independent investigations to noematic time-modalities and their modifications. From this, in connection with the individuation of different kinds of intentional objects, emerged the already-mentioned ontological turn of his phenomenology of time. He investigated whether the modes of temporal givenness belong to an object's noematic sense or not, and whether experience-objects, phantasy-objects, and ideal objects behave the same in this regard.

Husserl also discussed the newly pressing question of the relation of time-consciousness to the pure ego. The relevant texts concern the egoic temporalization of events and the temporalization of the pure ego itself, as well as the connection between the hyletic temporality of the primal stream and the temporal experiences performed by an active or passive ego.

The decisive advance over the earlier texts is not achieved through the still "static" transcendental phenomenology of the *Ideas* but through the newly developed and consistently carried-through transition to a "genetic" phenomenology. This breakthrough occurs not, as previously assumed, in the WS 1920/21 "transcendental logic" lectures (Husserliana XI) but already in the 1917/18 Bernau manuscripts. This explains why the reader of the Bernau texts often feels reminded of the *Analyses Concerning Passive Synthesis*. It is no coincidence that Husserl's new genetic phenomenology originates in reflections on time-consciousness.

This genetic time-analysis means that Husserl no longer unreflectively treats the intentionality at work in time-consciousness as an egoic act-intentionality with an objectual correlate (as in static correlation-analysis). Such intentionality plays a role, but Husserl now focuses on its genesis from pre-egoic tendencies, strivings, and inhibitions characterizing the intentionality of a passively running primal process. As a life-process, this primal process has a goal and the tendency to approach it. This determination of the primal process of life as striving toward intuitive givenness forces a new, dynamic reformulation of the temporal fulfillment-process.

The passively experienced hyletic primal process stands at the origin of the egoic acts of attention, perception, and grasping it awakens. Not only the subject of egoic performance but already every present givenness is born from this primal stream. The consciousness of presentness arises from the interplay of the retentional and protentional intentionality of the passively experienced primal stream. This leads genetic phenomenology to question the privilege of the present as the most primordial dimension of time-consciousness. If every present has its genesis-history—is a become present—one also understands why Husserl so extensively explored whether anything like a first ur-presentation can even exist.

Husserl's investigation of the connection between passively and actively, anonymously and egoically performed intentionalities of time-consciousness also led to the discovery of a secondary form of passivity accruing to habitualized egoic acts. These habitualized achievements of time-consciousness are discussed, as expected, especially in connection with the genetic analysis of recollection—e.g., the distinction between habitually performed recollection-acts with a practical aim and active recollective performance with the theoretical aim of ideal exactness.

More than the analysis of recollection, the treatment of the individuation-problematic shows that, as Husserl wrote to Ingarden, "the phenomenology of time cannot be detached purely by itself." If this phenomenology's task is to exhibit the constitution of temporal determinations of objects in transcendental consciousness, it must also attend to the ontological determination of these objective time-modalities. Time-modes are, as Husserl puts it, modes of being or existence. Not only being-present, being-past, or being-future constitutes itself in time-consciousness but, more generally, the individuality of objectual being.

Husserl soon encounters the more complicated problem of the individuation of ideal objects, for which the relation to an individuating givenness is inessential. Does this mean only empirically real objects have an essential relation to time? What about phantasy-objects? If phantasy-objects occupy a middle position between empirically individual and ideal objects, then investigating their temporality can reveal essential features of the differing modes of individuation of empirical and ideal objects, of "facts" and "essences." Hence Husserl repeatedly returns to the phenomenological analysis of phantasy-time in the later, ontologically oriented Bernau texts. The investigation of the subjective givenness of ideal objects and their insertion into possible logical "worlds" yields results relevant not only to Husserl's project of a "rational metaphysics" but also of potential interest to today's logician.

## **V**

The editors explain the arrangement of the published texts into six groups. Within each group, texts of more general content providing an initial overview of the group's characteristic questions are placed first. Alongside this general-to-specific movement, the criterion of substantive progress in problem-treatment also played an important role.

The six groups are arranged so that:

* **Group I** presents the basic concepts of Husserl's phenomenology of time-consciousness in a general analysis, often illustrated with graphic representations.  
* **Group II** examines the connection between the primal process and the immanent time-objects constituted therein.  
* **Group III** systematically investigates the dual question of applying the apprehension/content schema and the danger of infinite regress.  
* These first three groups form a thematic unity insofar as they stay within the framework of a formal analysis of time-consciousness (ur-presentation, retention, protention in temporal flow; flowing time-modalities vs. rigid time-order).  
* **Group IV** investigates the relation between hyletic and egoic temporality/temporalization, forming a transition to the concrete questions of the last two groups.  
* **Group V** treats the problem of individuation of experience-objects, phantasy-objects, and ideal objects.  
* **Group VI** analyzes recollection within the framework of genetic phenomenology.

# **Part I: On the Basic Structure of Primal Time-Consciousness: The Flowing Connection of Ur-Presentation, Retention, and Protention**

(*I. Zur Grundstruktur des ursprünglichen Zeitbewußtseins: Der fließende Zusammenhang von Urpräsentation, Retention und Protention*)

---

## **Text Nr. 1**

### **The Interweaving of Retention and Protention in Primal Time-Consciousness. Ur-Presentation and Consciousness of Novelty**

(*Das Ineinander von Retention und Protention im ursprünglichen Zeitbewußtsein. Urpräsentation und Bewußtsein der Neuheit*)

#### **§ 1\. The Intentionality of Ur-Presentation. The Attentive Turning toward a Present-New, Past, or Future**

Husserl begins by analyzing what it means to "direct oneself toward a perceptual object." This directing involves yielding to the stimulus (*Reiz*) exerted by an object that is already in the process of being constituted as a background-object. The ego can turn to the object either "immediately, as soon as it begins" or only in the middle of its perceptual givenness—the latter occurring because attention was bound to another object by a stronger stimulus. Husserl asks what "turning immediately" involves. The turning is an attentional transformation (*attentionale Wandlung*) that is always something subsequent to the initial onset of the temporal object's constitution. The onset-point of the process (the point of ur-presentation) has already passed into retention, and a small stretch of new ur-presentations has already accrued in steady primal springing-forth (*Urquellen*). The attentional transformation thus consists in the gaze directing itself through the flow of retention toward the first stretch of the temporal expanse. As long as this stretch is "small," it can carry primary attention across its breadth, with the discrete onset-point still preferred. Once the ego has established its footing in the primal-stretch stream, the privilege of the new generally prevails: attention remains directed at the freshly retained beginning (which continually loses freshness—retentions become more mediate, apprehension-contents paler, losing differentiations), but it shifts continually. The difference between primary and secondary attention proves to be continuous. The grip remains a grip but weakens; the culmination-point of the grip's firmness is the grip on the new—the ur-presently appearing through primal springing-forth. More precisely, a very small stretch terminating in the new Now has the privilege of attention.

How does the gaze, the grasping, reach the new Now itself? Does it only come after the fact, as in the first turning? Evidently not. The Now (the ur-presentation) is a limit-point of two kinds of "re-presenting" acts: retentions and protentions. As soon as the grasping ego is already involved in perceptual consciousness, it has the open future-horizon—the horizon of possible actual expectation. Actual expectation is the entry of attention into this horizon. Being-turned-toward the perceptual object is catching the approaching with open arms; what is caught, i.e., the fulfillment of the more or less determinate future-intention in the moment of fulfillment, is grasped in a privileged way. The newly grasped is fulfillment and thus especially privileged for attention. This privilege continually weakens with the increasing mediacy and fluctuating obscurity of the ensuing retention. The grasped remains in the grip, but the grip steadily loosens. In the stream of perception, every point has its intentionality; the ur-presentation is not a mere appearing of ur-presences that would only subsequently take on intentionality, but a continual appearing of them in the mode of fulfillment of expectation-intentions. Fullness fits itself to the form of the intention, which thereby becomes intuitively grasping. Fulfillment does not consist in the coincidence (*Deckung*) of two agreeing intentional experiences. In the succession of the flow, we can say: first there is an empty expectation, then the point of ur-perception, which is itself an intentional experience. But this experience arises in the flow only through the entry of ur-presences as filling contents into the preceding empty intention, which thereby transforms into ur-presenting perception.

Other distributions of attention are obviously possible. A predominant interest, or a deliberate primary fixation of the just-past, can persist through the flow of retentions while the event proceeds in constitution without primary interest turning toward the new and normally preferred. Likewise, after an event has passed, attention can remain attached to it without turning to new events—though soon recollections will set in and bring the still-conscious to re-presentation. The recollection then coincides with the empty total-retention. There is a primal law: an empty concrete retention can find fulfillment only through a recollection that (quasi-)constitutes anew the concrete perception of the same event in the mode of "re-consciousness" (*Wiederbewußtsein*). A reversal (fulfillment in reverse running) is impossible; at most one can fulfill in leaps—directing attention through the empty total-retention to various parts of a melody, each of which comes to repeated givenness only through a re-presentative modified concrete perception run through from beginning to end, not in reverse. Only in empty consciousness and partially living intuitive retention can the attending gaze be counter-directed. But making intuitive, fulfilling, can only proceed in the direction of the time-flow, of new constitution.

Husserl notes that the discussion of ur-presentations under conditions of inattention—when no proper "noticing" takes place, when the event runs its course entirely unheeded—would need renewed treatment. This question is left open.

#### **§ 2\. The Interweaving of Retentional and Protentional Intentionality in the Course of the Primal Process**

Husserl leverages the foregoing to establish that even if we do not in every sense conceive all experiential being as consciousness-of, it remains the case that the primal process of "consciousness" is an intentional process, truly consciousness. The "primal stream" (*Urstrom*)—the stream of the "last" transcendental life-constituents—includes all events of phenomenological time that "are" but constitute themselves without any "participation of attention" or any grasping by the pure ego, as well as the stream of constituting constituents that live in the mode of attention for those events that do constitute themselves in perceptual grasping.

Husserl asks us to imagine a hyletic (i.e., purely sensory) process running off without any attentional ego-participation. In the hyletic sphere, processes that continue an already-initiated process must be distinguished from those that newly begin. He observes that in the course of a contentually unified process—one fulfilling conditions for the unity of an event—each new process-point already finds protentional horizons into which it is taken up, while for the onset-point of such a process this is not the case. The primal hyletic life must be conceived so that the modification each "new" point of the process undergoes necessarily becomes the core of a retention, and in the flow becomes a continual retention of ever-higher continual levels, or escalations of interwoven intentionalities. But the appearance of new ur-presences means not only the appearance of data but equally (by essential necessity) a forward-directed intentionality—a protention directed at what is coming—as long as the tone sounds, new ur-present data of the same kind appear, and "expectation" (without attentional ego-participation) takes them up in the mode of fulfillment and thereby intentionally shapes them. Every ur-presence is thus not merely content but "apprehended" content. Ur-presentation is fulfilled expectation.

Retention itself must accordingly also carry the moment of fulfilled expectation—in a double way. First, retentionally modified: it is the modification of an ur-presentation that was fulfilled protention. Second, the retention-process is itself a process in which the process constitutes itself as an event, and the "expectation" goes not only toward the new datum but also toward coming retentions and retentions of retentions. Every middle phase (excluding onset and end phases) thus has a triple face: it is retention with respect to the elapsed system of ur-data, retention with respect to the elapsed consciousness-apprehensions, and therefore fulfilled expectation—from which unfulfilled expectation radiates: a whole linear horizon, an intentional stretch-continuum, but empty.

The earlier diagram, which signified only retentions and hyletic ur-data with their modifications, was incomplete. Regarding retentions as well, the inner intentional structure was not fully represented. Every constituting total-phase is retention of fulfilled protention, which is the limit of a horizon of unfulfilled and itself continuously mediate protention (a stretch-continuum). Every retention must, as retention of retentions, have all this conscious in a modified way.

#### **§ 3\. The Role of "Expectations" (Protentions) in the Constitution of Phenomenological Time. Continuous Modification of the Double Intentionality in the Protentional Fulfillment-Process and the Retentional De-fulfillment Process**

Husserl gives a schematic account: if the event is Eo...Ep...En, the protention goes steadily through this series, meaning each new ur-present datum meets the steady expectation. One would say the expectation fulfills itself steadily, from phase to phase, in chain-like-continuous fashion.

But the future-directed expectation is aimed at the coming event or the flowing event-stretch. It is not the case that the expectation living at one point targets only the next point (a mere limit), with a new expectation flashing up aimed only at the "next point," and so on. The expectation targets the coming event, a flowing event-horizon, a variable stretch. Its intentionality is continuously mediately directed at everything ideally distinguishable in what is coming. This continuous mediate intentionality—going from one phase to the next, through it to the following, and so on through all phases—is described in its necessary structural features. At each inner limit-point, the intention targets some adjacent sequel-stretch but through it every further subsequent stretch.

Husserl analyzes the structure of this intentionality in detail. We have an intention that fulfills itself with the entry of the new ur-presence datum, but only in one phase of its intentionality, while a continuous stretch of unfulfilled open "horizon" remains. In the consciousness that has taken up the new ur-presence datum, no new expectation enters; the same expectation with its intentional continuity proceeds, having filled one empty point of intention in sequence. But the steady point-by-point fulfillment itself belongs to the intention as intention on the entry of the still-flowing event. The intention goes steadily through the new points and retains beyond them the character of unfulfilled expectation. Each preceding protention relates to each following one in the protentional continuum as each subsequent retention relates to the preceding one of the same series. The preceding protention intentionally harbors all later ones (implies them); the succeeding retention intentionally implies all earlier ones. The later protentions are fulfillments of the earlier; the earlier retentions are (in a different sense) fulfillments of the later—they are acts of the same sense but with stronger and richer fullness. Every later retention "de-fulfills" itself in the course. On both sides we have mediate intentionality, and to every mediate intentionality belongs the double "direction" of intentionality: toward the primary object and toward the secondary object (the "acts" and the primary objects in their mode of givenness). On neither side does this lead to infinite regresses of intentionality. However, the difficulty is not identical on both sides, since in the flow of the primal process the empty (relatively empty) protentions come first and the full ones follow, while the full (relatively full) retentions come first and the relatively empty ones follow.

Husserl asks: is everything now clarified? How do retentions and protentions interweave? How do they have the unity of primal time-consciousness in this interweaving? He begins the clarification with retention: the newly appeared remains conscious—it modifies but is seized by a retentional consciousness as apprehension-content. This retentional consciousness is likewise modified with its apprehension-content, and so on—a stream of ever-higher-level retentions, with each phase bringing a new and modified content. What characterizes it as "new"? In a certain way the apprehension-data of protentions are also new. But they appear as apprehension-data; the ur-present datum does not. Yet it comes "expected," fulfilling the protentions.

#### **§ 4\. The Interweaving of Retention and Protention in the Constitution of Phenomenological Temporal Objectivity and Time. Now-Consciousness and the Problem of the Ur-Presentation of a New Event**

The onset-point of the phenomenological event can be expected through protentions (attentive or inattentive). But it can also appear without any protention directed at its content. The event itself can constitute itself for me as a present without forewarning. This yields a special problem of ur-presentation for the onset-point and onset-stretch. For a mediate point (after a small onset-stretch has already run off), protentions may establish themselves by primal genetic necessity, and the future apprehension-continuum retroactively extends back to furnish the hitherto missing apprehensions.

Taking a mediate point Ek arising in fulfillment of preceding protention: this protention is a continuously intentional experience; in its steady transformations, each transformation-phase is fulfilled protention, but in each phase only one point of the continuous intentionality is fulfilled and a stretch of emptiness remains unfulfilled. This point in the protentional intentionality-continuum is the ur-presenting point, the ur-presenting consciousness. At each point of the further process, it has a new character, since the protention, though it is in each phase fulfillment of a protention and simultaneously itself protention, is concretely different at each point—even when ur-present sense-data are entirely alike, as in the limit-case of a completely unchanged enduring tone.

Every newly appearing content is intentionally distinctly characterized through a consciousness-form that necessarily changes (while retaining a community of essence), and this opens an understanding of ur-presentation as what constitutes an ur-presence of the event, an ur-givenness of the temporal-object-point as Now and ever-new Now. The protention can be relatively determinate (when a definite event is expected—presupposing it or one like it was already given). It is then a fore-memory (*Vorerinnerung*), a modification of recollection directed at the past. Here, however, we deal with original constitution without such complications—events that appear without being definitely foredrawn through fore-memory, as foredrawn time-events through empty intentions fulfilling themselves through a time-constituting process.

The protention running through the primal process is not the fulfillment of a fore-memory-reproduction directed at the whole process or its time-events. It has originally arisen. Husserl invokes a primal law of necessary genesis: if a stretch of primal sequence of hyletic data has elapsed, a retentional connection must form—Hume already saw this—but not only that. Consciousness remains in its course and anticipates the further: a protention "directs" itself at continuation in the same style, and this protention is modified by each differential of the retentional course. Thus the event constitutes itself with an open event-horizon that is itself a protentional horizon. Genetically: even if no concretely determinate expectation precedes the event, every primal process—once primal processes have become constitutive at all—will be constitutively apperceived. Once the play of appearing and modifying hyletic core-contents has been spun, every datum will be caught by protention. Within the process, a forward-directed protention on a continuing event must establish itself, and retroactively the elapsed "differential" must be constitutively laid into the event.

The genetic task would be to make comprehensible how a constitutive process can form at all before any constitutive process has been accomplished—the idea of the awakening ego, an ego whose life begins and must necessarily become consciousness-life. Whether this idea designates a possibility remains uncertain. As a minimum, we can assume that continuous primal sequences of hyletic data as core-data of a constitutive process necessarily carry with them continuously connecting fading-continua, which by further primal lawfulness become apprehension-cores of retentional apprehensions. By necessary law, not only is retention exercised after a "differential" has elapsed, but protention directs itself at the coming (generally determined in content—a tone begun protends a future tone, though specific intensity or quality remains indeterminate). Now every process-phase is: a stretch of retention, a point of ur-presentation as fulfilled protention, and a stretch of unfulfilled protention.

But in the middle of the process, every retention must be a retention of earlier fulfilled protention and its empty horizon. In the continuous phase-series, each succeeding one harbors in retentional modification the fulfillment of a point of the preceding protention, with coincidence of the unfulfilled, and in the course the unfulfilled proceeds, persisting in unfulfilledness stretch-wise, just as the fulfilled proceeds through stretches of retention. The Now is constituted through the form of protentional fulfillment; the past through retentional modification of this fulfillment. In the continuity of identification running through the de-fulfillment, the time-point is the same as what was conscious as Now, as just-past, and so on. The question suggests itself: is retention first a genuine retention of a time-point-object and an identical point only because protention has already created a Now and thereby something identifiable across different modes of givenness?

### **Supplement I (to § 4 of Text Nr. 1): Some Attempts to Represent the Interweaving of Retention and Protention in the Diagram**

Husserl describes the diagram's dimensions. The vertical represents momentary consciousness with its ur-present point Ek and its retentional time-stretch in past-gradation mode. But also: ur-present consciousness with its retentional continuous accompaniments as retentions of the elapsed stretch, where the stretch simultaneously indicates the continuum of ur-present and ur-retentional core-data. Each sub-stretch is retention of all earlier stretches of the same flow-stripe. From the differential onward, the vertical stretch grows in the continuum of vertical rows in the primal flow. Each stretch sinks and grows from above, with an ur-presence point as ur-differential continuously accruing. The past enriches itself as long as ur-presence springs forth.

Protentions move analogously to retentions. Every retentional series arises at its Ek in primal springing-forth; the springing-forth phase is only a point. We also have a protentional ur-differential—primal protentions springing forth at each Ek. At Eo, as soon as a differential has set in, an empty protention is already there, having the entire schema (still to be "drawn" with the process), only with very incompletely determined content for an unknown event. At each Ek, alongside the retentional schema, a protentional schema is distinguished. A combined diagram of protentions and retentions is described.

Husserl works through the formal notation and diagrammatic representation in detail: how each vertical row is fulfillment of a protention directed at it from the "immediately preceding" row, how Eo is conscious as the limit of retention in its continuous mediacy and also as the limit-zero of the fulfillment-sequence, how core-data are never given except as fulfillment of protentions (with the exception of the onset-point Eo, which is in a special sense "unconscious" and only mediately comes to "consciousness" through retention). The Now is constituted as the limit-point in which Now-consciousness constitutes itself through Eo and its apprehension. A "differential" precedes in which bare retention works and alone creates a stretch-unity, thereby motivating a protention on "continuation." Each row is fulfillment of the protention directed at it from the immediately preceding row. The retentional phases of each row are simultaneously reproduced Nows in various continuous degrees—reproduced fulfillment of intentions on core-data. The onset-phase alone is the pure impression: the Now-thing comes as new, not as impoverishment of an old, not as retention. This seems to clarify the structure of time-consciousness.

## **Text Nr. 2**

### **The Complexion of Retention and Protention. Gradualities of Fulfillment and the Consciousness of the Present. Graphic Representation of the Primal Process**

(*Die Komplexion von Retention und Protention. Gradualitäten der Erfüllung und das Bewußtsein der Gegenwart. Graphische Darstellung des Urprozesses*)

#### **§ 1\. Protention within Retention – Retention within Protention. A New Diagram**

In the primal process stream, we have: (1) the sequence of hyletic data sinking retentionally; with each new datum, retentions of step-wise mediacy connect. The momentary hyletic ur-datum and its entire retentional stretch would then not be properly conscious except as the limit of preceding retentional stretches. (2) Adding protentions: each vertical stretch comes "welcome"; each retentional momentary-continuum receives a protention on what follows and, in continuous mediacy, on what follows after that. Genetically: when new core-data continually appear and old ones sink retentionally, a protentional consciousness "arises" that meets the new ur-data and terminatingly fulfills itself with them. But not merely point by point—we have not merely a sequence of ur-data in consciousness of their sequence, but a sequence of retentional stretches, and this sequence too is "projected into the protentional."

Husserl builds a new diagram step by step. Starting from a stretch E₁–E₂ already elapsed, with the vertically downward-directed retentional stretch of consciousness of the elapsed: this retention must carry a protention (designated by a slanting stripe) indicating that E₂E₁² will steadily sink. Each point of this stretch is not only retentional consciousness (regarding the slanting lines going back to corresponding points) but also protentional consciousness (regarding slanting lines going in the downward-sinking direction). Beyond this, protentions directed at the appearing of new ur-data from E₂ onward through E₃, E₄,... are designated by upward-directed vertical half-rays. This intentionality is necessarily mediate: protentional consciousness is not momentary but steady, directed in each moment at the future entering, just as for its retentional complement. The protentions fulfill themselves in one another through continuously mediate intentionality, leading to termini (the points of the base-series).

We have a great stream of consciousness: in the forward direction it is protention, in the counter-direction (and what lies in it) retention. But how these difficult relations can be further understood requires showing that the figure really contains everything necessary for time-constitution and nothing else.

#### **§ 2\. The Interweaving of Retention and Protention as Fulfillment-Process**

Husserl replays the genesis: first bare retention, then, necessarily modified, protention. Protentions also seize the already-given retentional stretch—meaning the new series of "the past as such" not only comes but "will and must come." Consciousness takes on a modification making it not merely retentional continuity but simultaneously protentional continuity. In essence, it is steadily fulfillable such that every fulfillment is simultaneously intention for a new fulfillment, and so on. "On account of" the successive appearing, this reshaping of intentionality arises in necessary immanent "causality"—from one-sidedness comes double-sidedness, the new side a kind of mirror-image of the original. Husserl then works through how, in the transition, the modification is double: on one side, the modification by which consciousness as modification of preceding consciousness further modifies in this sense (consciousness of the just-past, which is in turn past of its just-past); on the other side, the modification by which consciousness as protention of something future modifies (the consciousness being itself a modification of the preceding toward itself as future). Thus also protention is, as such, constantly backward-directed, although it is forward-directed: something is had in consciousness characterized not as past but as future, with its mediacy and its continuous modification (in the simultaneous). The whole passage is a remarkably intricate unpacking of the temporal double-modification.

#### **§ 3\. Does the Retentional and Protentional Fulfillment-Process Imply an Infinite Regress? Levels of Time-Consciousness**

One might object: the fulfillment itself should enter retentionally into later consciousness-phases, new protentions on future fulfillments should arise, these fulfilling themselves should again become retentionally conscious, again awakening protentions on these higher fulfillments, and so in infinitum. But Husserl dismisses this as a mere verbal entanglement: the protention not yet fulfilled passes into a fulfillment (a mode of the new protention), which passes into a new fulfillment, and since the earlier protention is conscious (as a retention), its fulfillment-mode is also conscious—and so in infinitum, which yields no vicious regress.

Husserl considers what would follow if we set aside all genesis and assumed: in the primal process, every phase is simultaneously retention of a preceding fulfilled time-stretch and protention regarding the following. Why should this still contain a difficulty? We have no temporal beginning but only a beginning of consideration—we stand always in the middle of an infinite process and pick out a phase that is a double-branch of intentionalities in which the ur-datum is merely a distinguished moment. A footnote, however, qualifies this: "it's not so simple"—one must distinguish between differentiated moments and an infinity-horizon that is a mere potentiality of possible recollections. Otherwise every Ux would contain an actual infinity of moments. And an onset as the irruption of a completely unexpected event? "So that doesn't work."

One cannot simply designate the lower branch as retention and the upper as protention—rather, these words have different and essentially connected meanings. The lower branch is retention regarding the elapsed fulfilled event-time-stretch; the upper branch does the same for the not-yet-present, the coming and thereby more indeterminately conscious. Phenomenal time and temporal content with the givenness-modes of past and future can constitute themselves only in the primal process through the continuous modification of the stretch built from both branches—and in a way that the process not only runs but becomes conscious of itself as a process, constituting a second "time" with temporal content. Phenomenal time (transcendental time of the first level) is only possible through an innermost transcendental time of the second level and in a last transcendental event: the endless process that is for itself consciousness of the process. Essentially, only in a primal process can a process become conscious; a momentary consciousness is only possible as a moment of a process. This is a remarkable passage that lays bare the stratification of temporal constitution.

#### **§ 4\. The Primal Process: Consideration in Steps**

Husserl conducts a systematic, step-by-step formal analysis of the primal process. (1) It is an infinite "protentional" process: in each phase Ux, consciousness-tendency of transition to a new phase, and each entering phase is in accord with the preceding tendency. Tendency is a consciousness-mode, and what enters in accord with it is conscious as such and is in turn tendency on what is "coming." Each phase is intention and fulfillment into infinity. Every point of a process-phase (of the continuum Ux) can be chosen as zero-point, with two opposite directions and infinity in both. The process is irreversible—essentially, every Ux is characterized as earlier or later relative to every other, an order as irreversible as that of the number-series, though involving "individuals" rather than species. (2) What makes the transition determinate is a fixed lawful assignment of the points of each series to those of every other, consisting in the fact that each Ux steadily "sinks" in a determinate way into the following. (3a) The privilege of the horizontal direction arises from the special structure of the Ux phases: consciousness-phases have a varying relative fullness or core-quality (*Kernhaftigkeit*), and each Ux has one and only one phase containing a maximum of core-quality—the ur-phase. This "ur-core" is what it is only as an intentionally enclosed core. (3b) What law connects all ur-cores and modified cores? The positive (upper) branch is distinguished by the fact that in the process, the empty is led continuously to corresponding fullness and finally to a determinate maximal point. Each positive stretch has a maximal point, and each non-maximal point has an intentionality pointing toward a maximal point as terminus ad quem. The law of intentional intensification and termination in the ever-new maximum selects from the parallel ray-system a "ur-core line" (Urkernlinie) and a corresponding "horizontal direction." Similarly, the negative (lower) half: in the essence of time-constituting intentionality, every maximal consciousness-point "impoverishes" immediately—once the maximal point is reached, "sinking" begins on the negative side, the side of de-fulfillments.

#### **§ 5\. New Graphic Representation of the Connection of Progressive Fulfillment and De-fulfillment in the Primal Stream. Constitution of Experience-Time in the Connection of Retentional and Protentional Modification**

Husserl introduces a changed symbolism: the Ux is represented not as a single line with two branches but as two lines meeting at an angle, the whole parallel system forming two half-planes making a dihedral angle whose edge-line is the E-E line. The paper is imagined folded at EE and lifted. The process then runs so that in the positive half-plane (the plane of intensification), all streaming runs toward the edge and there steadily reaches the maximal point. As soon as this point is achieved, sinking begins on the negative side. The primal stream is thus a continuum built from unbounded one-dimensional continua as its phases—a twofold continuum (a doubly continuous point-manifold) built from two "half-planes" as a double stream. The distinguished edge has as its correlate phenomenological time—the time of experiences as enduring phenomena. It is the continuum of maximal cores; as form, it is thought as time; with content, it is temporal objectivity. "Now" is the form of the correlate of a consciousness-point of maximal fulfillment, i.e., an edge-point. The consciousness of time is consciousness of a sequence of points, each conscious only as actual Now or as past or future Now. Each moment of the primal process is consciousness of an actual Now and simultaneously consciousness of a retentionally conscious Now-continuum with continuous pastness-characters.

The objective phenomenological present as a point is the edge (or the correlate of edge-consciousness)—the constituted unity, time in the mode of the present, as far as intuitiveness reaches. The time-point itself is identical, presenting itself in different modes of givenness, identical in its form. Time is the form of identical objectivity necessarily constituting itself in the orientation-form of present, past, future. None of this makes difficulties. The constitution of time is accomplished by the edge-consciousness continually distinguished as fulfillment in the stream, but this is only thinkable as the edge-line of the two streams. The discussion turns to the difference between the successive coming-to-givenness of event-points and the succession in streaming through which the former becomes possible, and then to how multiple events constitute simultaneously, how one event begins while another ends, with special attention to the protentional and retentional branches at the onset-point and end-point of events.

#### **§ 6\. The Ultimately Constituting Consciousness Is in Each Phase Positive and Negative Tendency. The Bodily Present as Zero-Point of Modification**

The ultimately constituting consciousness in each phase (Ux+, Ux−) is consciousness-of: intention toward something and intention away from something; directed tendency, positive and negative. This directedness is the fundamental character of consciousness-of in its most primordial essential constitution. Each momentary consciousness is a continuum of consciousness-points, each either positively or negatively directed, with one exception: the transition-phase. A relative consciousness-concretum like Ux can be both—directedness-toward and directedness-away—only through the way unidirectionally directed points of consciousness unite in continuous transition including the neutral point. In each Ux, all degrees are formally represented, from zero of nearness to infinity and from zero of distance to infinity. The zero-point is consciousness of saturation—the null of positive tendency, "fulfilledness." In the process of self-fulfilling (progressing along the slanting direction-lines in the upper diagram), the zero-point is the consciousness-point of completed achievement: originary consciousness (*Originärbewußtsein*), consciousness of the bodily "self there" (*leibhaftige Gegenwart*), of "immediate" having, consciousness in which its object has the givenness-mode of "bodily presence"—present as really immanent, in the original. Originary consciousness is intuitive consciousness. All non-intuitive consciousness is mediate, in need of fulfillment, pointing to processes of possible fulfillment. In the primal stream, the non-intuitive is necessarily a transitional stage toward fulfillment, continuously mediately directed at "its object." Mediacy is equivalent to the degree of fulfillment-nearness; immediacy is equivalent to fulfilledness itself—the canceled intention. "Intention" thus again means mediacy of consciousness, which always functions intentionally but as a limit-case is canceled intention, immediate original consciousness. Equivalently: positive tendencies and canceled tendencies toward the object itself. Analogously for the negative intentional directions: retentions that have the object conscious in the mode of pastness-distance and in the stream of distancing into the past.

Now-being as being in the mode of bodilyness, self-presently-having, is the mode to which every other is back-referred. Past-being is not being (being simpliciter, now-being) "but" having-been. Future-being is first-of-all becoming-being. Now-being alone is "actually-being"—what is given in the consciousness of fulfillment. The past is not merely given differently in a different givenness-mode but given as "past Now"; the future is future Now. Here, however, the relative, new-sense-introducing talk of present, fulfillment, etc., already plays a role.

#### **§ 7\. Consciousness of Time-Objects and Consciousness of the Flow**

The consciousness-stream is a stream of twofold "intentions," but this characterization proceeds from the perspective of certain positive and negative intensifications, the transverse direction in the diagram, and the base-line to which everything is referred. Consciousness in all its momentary phases and points is not only directed at its objects appearing in bodily selfhood on the E-E line; it is also "inner" consciousness, consciousness of itself and its intentional process. It has alongside its primary objects its "secondary" ones. Consciousness has a primary object—absolute, prescribed by the phenomenologically (primarily) time-constituting stream—and infinitely many objects it can be said to be directed at, which it has conscious through its intentionality but does not intend in a distinguished way. Every intention goes through new and new intentions in transition, and in this process not only the last intention "fulfills" itself but every one; the null is fulfillment for every preceding one. But attention can direct itself to these mediacy-connections, to the implied, and prefer them over the primary object. The consciousness-stream as self-conscious stream has its present, past, future, its succession, its givenness-modes—but here divergent circumstances must be discussed.

Husserl distinguishes: the continuous order of positive and negative intentional graduality (within each Ux and between the two branches joined by the identical maximal/minimal point); and the order of the Ux, i.e., the order of the process-succession and the consciousness-order conscious in each Ux. The base-line emerges through the peculiarities of positivity and negativity and the associated intensification-relations. The base-line is the geometric locus of maximal/minimal points; the process-continuum is simultaneously a continuum of succession in which the process runs not only as continuous process but toward maximal points and away from them. Temporal succession constitutes itself through the peculiarities of the consciousness-stream making it consciousness of a continuous succession—a succession in which a temporality already lies. Consciousness is and is as flow, and it is consciousness-flow appearing to itself as flow. The being of the flow is a self-"perceiving" (not counting attentive grasping as part of the essence of perceiving) in which the being of the perceived is immanently enclosed. How this is possible and to be understood—"that has been the great and constant problem of this treatise."

#### **§ 8\. Present Consciousness of Reality and Unreality. Finite and Divine Consciousness**

The consciousness-stream is not a stream like a water-current possessing being in objective time. It is not in objective time; rather, it carries this time—the form of all objectivity and first of all transcendental objectivity of the first level—within itself. Yet consciousness is in itself a stream having a form of being as time, as "stream." Consciousness is present consciousness that in itself is consciousness of present consciousness, and only as present consciousness is it "actual." It was and will be, and as present consciousness it is simultaneously consciousness of a stream of past and future consciousness. As present, it is actuality; but the present changes to past, actuality passes into un-actuality, and future changes to present, un-actuality to actuality. Un-actuality is not nothing but truly-having-been and truly-going-to-be. The present is all-spanning, "all-knowing" consciousness of itself and all its intentional constituents—potentially harboring omniscience of the world—as an ideal possibility. The darkness-horizon in which past and future fade is a contingent limitation extendable in infinitum, so that as an "idea" there arises an all-knowing "divine" consciousness spanning itself in perfect clarity. Even "finite" consciousness is all-knowing in that its intentionality encompasses its entire past and future, but only partially clearly—the rest lies in a darkness that is a potentiality for clarities and recollections.

#### **§ 9\. The Self-Consciousness of the Stream in the Continuous Change of its Actualization and De-actualization**

How is "inner consciousness"—the being-self-conscious of the consciousness-stream—to be understood? Only through the structure of each consciousness-phase as a continuum of consciousness-points as ur-phases of "intentionality" and the peculiarity of this structure. Each momentary consciousness Ux has a double horizon: forward-directed and backward-turned. Flowing along, Ux changes: what in each point-phase is pre-consciousness transforms into what is actualized; actuality is actualization of anticipating consciousness. Conversely, each Ux is backward-turned—retention of the preceding, conscious as preceded. Ux changes into a new present actuality that is retention of Ux, and so continuously, so that every retention becoming actual in consciousness becomes conscious in further streaming as retention of the retention. But the main point is that these protentions and retentions belonging to every Ux involve a continuous process proceeding in two connected uniform modifications. Fulfillment in its continuous immediacy and for discrete points of mediacy is something in infinitum entirely uniform, with no prescribed limits. The same holds for the modification of retention.

**Nota (pp. 47–48).** Husserl suspects he has left a gap in the description: he has always pointed out that each momentary consciousness contains protention and retention with their structures, but has not spoken of the utterly unique consciousness of "transition" required for consciousness of the stream. The present-moment modifies itself, passes into modified Ux, and modification is characterized by its own essence as modification (consciousness-of, retention-of, etc.). But modification also means a self-transforming in which what was pre-consciousness in each point-phase transforms into actualized. Is this not fully understandable? A streaming consciousness structured in this way is necessarily consciousness of itself as streaming.

#### **§ 10\. Supplementary Questions. Discontinuities in the Flow**

Husserl notes several questions he deliberately set aside to simplify matters: pauses in a time-event built from individual temporal objects as a terminus; whether the protention (the forward-gaze) has intuitiveness; what happens when nothing comes that was pre-viewed; the attention-relations (approached before the protentional diagrams). These are flagged for treatment but not resolved here.

## **Text Nr. 3**

### **Retentional and Reproductive Re-Presentation of Ur-Presence**

(*Retentionale und reproduktive Vergegenwärtigung der Urpräsenz*)

#### **§ 1\. Modification of Clarity with Identical Objectual Apprehension in Perception, Retention, Recollection, and Phantasy**

Husserl distinguishes within phantasy: (1) clear phantasy giving a present actuality in the "as if," re-presenting it itself; (2) unclear, wavering phantasy making the object present through pale, partially dissolving, inadequate "phantasy-images," as "through a fog." The unclear is a medium of representation; through all wavering, the unity of meaning persists—I mean the same. The object does not change with these givenness-modes of unclarity. In clarity I have quasi-givenness of the object itself; in unclarity, its manifold changing givenness "through" the dissolving unclear. These givenness-modes are "appearances" relative to which clarity presents the fulfilling itself. So too for retention with its fading representations: recollection of the retained re-presents the past "itself," namely the represented Now. And the same distinction holds for recollection: clear recollection re-presents the object as quasi-presence; unclear recollection gives not a quasi-perceiving "itself" but a givenness of it through a mediation. In all this, the sharpest phenomenological attention is needed.

This difficulty is to understand how an objectual consciousness maintains itself when its constituted object becomes unclear—or equivalently, when the "representing" content undergoes a modification of obscuration. Through all this, unity of a "retention" persists. Husserl distinguishes: (1) retention of time-consciousness belonging to the sphere of constancy in clarity, where steady equally clear ur-presence is followed by a fixed style of modifications through which what was posited in each Now is maintained in apprehension; (2) deviating from this style, clear recollection becomes unclear—"everything changes toward a new direction of unclarity," the colors of ur-presence become pale, and "a weariness enters for all temporal modifications that belonged to constant clarity." The maintained objectual apprehension—despite modifying representation—is analogous to cases of perceptual illumination-change: the same object is steadily perceived without break, though unclear data no longer represent it fully originally but serve as "medium" through which the immediate gives itself.

When something gives itself originally (a sensation-datum, say) and in the modification through unclarity "represents" itself through the medium of modifications: the original giving is not a representing in the sense of "re-presenting," but the medial representing is a re-presenting—it does not count for itself but represents the original itself. This is a continuous modification of the original apprehension, connected in all phases by the unity of the apprehension of the same object. The original apprehension remains potentially preserved—its content is overlaid by the modified content and is conscious through it. The overlaying medium "stands in for" the covered and represents it, as far as resemblance reaches, at a "distance." This is significant for clarifying the structure of ordinary perceptual presentations in manifolds, where the relation to an optimal point is accomplished through "shining-through," mediately "meaning-through."

#### **§ 2\. Retention and Re-Presentation. Unlike Phantasy and Image-Consciousness, Retention Is Not a Reproduction but a Moment of Impressional Consciousness. Ur-Presence and Fading**

Retention (consciousness of post-presentation) is not, properly regarded, a memory and should not be called "primary memory." It is not a re-presentation (*Vergegenwärtigung*). A recollection makes non-present quasi-present and posits it as actual. A phantasy quasi-presents and quasi-posits. A retention holds what is no-longer-present. Does the content become a phantasma? Does reviving the retained reproduce the phantasma? No, on both counts. For there to be a continuous transformation of content into a phantasma, phantasy and perception would have to be a discrete difference, and the phantasma would be the phantasy-modification of sensation. But in the modification of presentation into retention (post-presentation), we have a continuous modification preserving the generic commonality of consciousness. Now and Past are homogeneous. The past is a past Now. The representing content belongs to the Now, is itself presently present. Reflecting, the whole momentary phenomenon is again something presently present. The ur-phase does not represent, or it represents itself; it is originally presently conscious. The other representing moments are continuously post-presentingly apprehended; the apprehending consciousness is present, the apprehended is post-present, is past.

Husserl raises and examines the question whether this is not a re-presentation after all (since in present consciousness a non-present is given) and considers the image-consciousness analogy. Is the "fading" tone an image for the earlier tone? There are analogies: I can compare the mobile retention with a recollection of the formerly present, identifying. But the analogy breaks down: I have not mere image-likeness but a relation of the "depicted" Now (mobile) to the ever-new Now—it is past and ever further past relative to this actual Now, with evidence that the "depicted" past Now is different from the actual one and from every already-past one. In sinking into pastness, the identity of what sinks is maintained. The something that is now and then is given unbroken—the identical object-point and the identical object itself as time-object. This is "perceived," and the continuous modification of presenting into post-presenting consciousness belongs to the essence of this perception. If so, then in this continuity the present datum transforms into steadily other present data and present-consciousness transforms into an image-consciousness\! But there is not the slightest break in consciousness, while perception-consciousness and image-consciousness are discretely different. Must we return to saying the retentional sensory data are modified like phantasms? But then we must accept that phantasy-consciousness can continuously transition into perception-consciousness or vice versa.

After thorough weighing, Husserl concludes: time-consciousness is indeed a distinguished re-presentational consciousness, but re-presentation in a different sense than that of genuine reproductive re-presentation. Retention is not reproduction but a component of "impression." The impression (the original consciousness) has the essential structure that it has within itself a distinction between what is originary and what is derived (but not reproduced), and that the originary consciousness, absolutely taken a "moment" (limit), continuously passes into the derived consciousness such that an identification steadily runs through the derivation-series—the original apprehension of the originally given this-as-this is maintained through the transformation of representing contents. For each new moment, a representation-character phenomenally arises in relation to the shifting Now-point: the "past."

#### **§ 3\. Reproduction and Retention. Apprehension and Apprehension-Content in the Givenness of Ur-Presence and Retentional Past**

Re-presentations must be principally separated into retentions and reproductions. A recollection re-presents: it is a reproducing, generating in the mode of "again" the entire constituting series. Recollection cannot continuously pass into the corresponding presencing, while retention continuously passes into the corresponding presentation. The former is not originally constituting for objectivity; the latter is constituting for temporal objectivity. Husserl then raises the question: is ur-presentation itself already an intentional experience? He distinguishes two views: (a) ur-presentation already has the character of an intentional experience, with the usual distinction between the experience and its intentional object, between reell apprehension-data and their "animating" apprehension-character; (b) alternatively, ur-presentation is an ur-experience-phase not yet having the character of intentional experience, but continuously transitioning into one—into a consciousness of the ur-datum—in the mode of continuously mediate intentionality. The ur-presence-moment is a mere moment, limit of a "flow," a transformation-continuum. The ur-present datum transforms itself and gains in this transformation a "animating" apprehension—the character of a "representative," an "apprehension-content"—with continuous transformation bringing continuous mediacy of intention. There is a graduality of mediacy: the closer to the zero-point of the ur-present, the lower the degree, the less past the ur-present is conscious; the higher the degree, the more remotely past. The ur-present as experience is the starting-point of this process, a mere limit; whenever attention turns to "it," it goes as after-coming through the already-spun retentional modification, through the consciousness-of that holds the ur-present intentionally fast, but in mediate intentionality. It appears pushed back, perspectivally contracted, ever poorer in intuitive differentiations. Husserl tries to describe this as a continuous kind of "analogizing," an originary kind of "depiction" (*Verbildlichung*): the matter is the ur-present datum, and it is a depiction in which the depiction-object continuously transforms and undergoes further depiction, so that the modified image is bearer of depiction for the relatively original image/image-object, and so continuously ever further.

## **Text Nr. 4**

### **On the Phenomenology of the Fading-Phenomena**

(*Zur Phänomenologie der Abklangsphänomene*)

#### **§ 1\. Introduction of the Basic Concepts: Continuous Fading of a Present Givenness, Intuitively Fulfilled and Empty Fading-Forms, Fading of a Living or Dead Duration-Unity**

Husserl notes that the foregoing took no account of the perspective and the decrease of modifications in their intuitive fullness and livingness. Originarity is the zero of modification as modification; as modification it increases. We have a continuous transformation of consciousness of the same, "contentually" entirely identically determined, which noematically bestows a continuously new mode of pastness. But this is not yet objective pastness—which must not be smuggled in here. It is best in this sphere to speak not of pastness but of "fading" (*Abklang*) of the present, or the modification of fading. Each fading of a present is itself present and thus must itself fade, and so on. The fading-modification is continuous, yielding distance-comparisons and differences of fading-stretches ("greater" and "smaller"). But there is a great limitation: fading-modifications are two-sided and in mathematical idealization go to infinity but have a limit "in the finite"—ideally a modification that undergoes no further modification. How is this possible, since it has a present and thus must itself fade? The answer: fading is a continuous impoverishment, a continuous decrease in "fullness"—a decrease of intuitiveness with the limit zero. This does not mean that when intuitiveness reaches zero, the modification ends—it continues as empty consciousness, even after we no longer have the fading tone "in the ear." We still have a "living consciousness" of the vanishing tone, not a "sunken consciousness" (what psychologists in their empirical setting reduce to reactivatable "dispositions").

How is this second impoverishment to be understood? If the consciousness streaming on as non-intuitive were identical to ongoing sense with fullness-zero, the continuous course should break off sharply. But reflection teaches that no sharp breaking-off occurs, and the stretch from zero onward has its own graduality—possibly a continuation of one belonging to the intuitive stretch as well. A kind of "consciousness-livingness" (*Bewußtseinslebendigkeit*) already decreases within the intuitive stretch; the fading is already during intuitive fading a dying-away, a vanishing, and remains so after intuitiveness is gone. The phenomena of temporal perspective (the "apparent size" of one and the same filled duration in different stretches of its fading) are also noted but set aside for a separate chapter.

The investigation is not concluded but must in fact be restarted: the concrete must come first, the punctual treated as limit of the extended. Distinctions are needed between the ur-livingness of a duration (in living ur-temporalization, where new ur-impressions continually appear and unifications constituting a closed individuality originally "form" themselves) and the dead duration (what was originally generated sinks without its own livingness). But this concept of life and livingness is different from the one just used. Here a fundamental theme is designated.

#### **§ 2\. The Problem of Intuitive Impoverishment (Darkening) and Perspectival Shrinking in the Continuum of Fadings and Retentions. Analogy between Spatial and Temporal Orientation**

The temporal orientation-field encompasses all "simultaneous" events (sharing the same orientation-level: Now, any mode of the just-past). Simultaneity is maintained during the flow—"time flows equally fast" for all events. The given time-field is surrounded by an emptily representing time-horizon—a potentiality of empty consciousness-components finding fulfillment through chains of recollection. The orientation-field has the peculiarity that with "distance" from the Now, the content weakens—a darkening down to the zero-point. Does the time-orientation-field have an outermost "distance" like the spatial orientation-field? That would mean that between different time-points of the outermost sphere, no distance-differences are any longer originally conscious—that distance-intervals continually shrink and converge toward zero. This is indeed the case. This shrinking is not identical with darkening but goes hand in hand with it. A closed event sinking back becomes not only dark but its temporal orientation-depth also becomes negligible—the sinking phenomenally ceases.

Even for clear retention, we cannot say the past's content is completely given—only given in the form of the fading representation. But this incompleteness does not give up the absolute givenness of having-been, nor the representation (which by its essence can only represent the like through the like). In recollection, consciousness of complete givenness, but also only within certain limits. The question of the outer boundary of the time-field connects with the question of whether the dark ("the below-zero of clarity") still properly participates in the process of sinking, and whether within the time-field the clear and dark must be distinguished.

#### **§ 3\. An Unsuccessful Attempt at Solution: The Fading or Perspectival Shrinking in the Time-Field Could Be a Matter of Intensity-Difference within Sensory Fusion**

Husserl explores and rejects the hypothesis that what is at issue in fading/perspectival shrinking might be an intensity-difference within sensory blending (*Verschmelzung*). The attempt fails because it cannot explain the specific modal structure of temporal orientation.

#### **§ 4\. A New Attempt at Solution: The Time-Perspective Could Be Understood as a More or Less Rapid Further-Crowding-Together of the Fading Events. On Determining the Zero-Point of Intuitiveness and Differentiation**

Husserl develops an alternative model: the time-perspective as a progressively closer crowding-together of fading events, approaching a zero-point of intuitiveness and differentiation. The investigation proceeds through formal-diagrammatic analysis, working through how ur-data and their fadings relate to retentions, how the "horizontal" series of modifications (fadings) and the "vertical" series (retentional modifications) combine, and what the resulting two-dimensional manifold looks like. The analysis is tentative, with Husserl explicitly noting its exploratory character.

### **Supplement II (to Text Nr. 4): Aporetic Models of Understanding for the Connection of Intuitive Fadings and Retentions of Primal Soundings and Fadings**

#### **a) Hypothesis: Unlike Retentions, Fadings Are Reell Presences, Just Like the "Primal Soundings"**

Husserl develops and explores the hypothesis that fadings (*Abklänge*) might be treated as sensation-data truly present, unlike retentions. This would give them the status of new impressional contents that resemble the ur-data and their modifications but are genuinely present. The analysis works through how such fadings would combine with retentions, how the "decking" (coincidence) of the supposedly non-intuitive retentions with the present fadings and their retentions might or might not yield intuitiveness. The result is inconclusive: it is "not to be seen how intuitiveness should become understandable through this."

#### **b) Attempt to Conceive the Intuitive Consciousness of the Just-Past as Image-Likeness. Also on the Phenomenology of Recollection**

Husserl considers whether the consciousness of the just-past might be understood on the model of image-consciousness (depiction): a presently appearing semblance that is not taken as actuality but as an image of a non-present actual thing through the covering-over of a recollection. He explores but ultimately rejects this model: the retention is not a mere empty modification, and the retentional consciousness is a modification of ur-presenting consciousness—a continuous modification stretching the ur-presenting consciousness into an expanse. What is "still" perceived is anschaulich (intuitive) but modified. The whole analysis attempted with the fadings retains some validity if we assume that along with the retentional memory-adumbrations, genuine after-soundings also appear bearing resemblances to the primal soundings.

### **Supplement III (to Text Nr. 4): If Fading Is Determined as a Sensation-Datum, How Do Perception, Retention, and Phantasy Still Differ?**

If the "fading" sensation-data functioning in temporal representation are themselves sensation-data, then it seems understandable that doubt could arise—"Am I still hearing the bells, or am I imagining it?" The "weak" sensory data seem to be the same on both sides, but in one case they are phantasy re-presentations, in the other case presencings of present tones. The passage marks the boundary of Part I's investigations and the transition into Part II's questions about the givenness of the primal process and the constituted temporal objectivities.

## **Text Nr. 5**

### **The Flow of Intuitive Time-Modalities and the Consciousness of Flowing**

(*Der Fluß der anschaulichen Zeitmodalitäten und das Bewußtsein vom Fließen*)

#### **§ 1\. By Way of Introduction: The Rigid Time-Order and the Flow of Time-Modalities. Solipsistic and Intersubjective Time. Constitution of Objective Time. The "Presence-Time." Time and Space**

Husserl opens Part II with a fundamental ontological claim: time is not merely given in a modality to the knowing subject (the pure ego); it is in itself (*an sich*) the necessary form of individual existence and of an individual "world." Modal determinations belong to time as form of an existence objectively and wholly inseparably. By analogy with space (for which exactly the same principle holds): time does not merely have an "orientation" for us and our world—it is in itself oriented. Therefore it can be given originarily to no cognitive subject except in the form of an orientation. For time, this means: time is what it is only in relation to "the" flowing, primally springing Now, and this actual Now is the midpoint of a continuum of infinitely flowing (and inversely infinitely elapsed) presentations. This midpoint "Now" is the mode of presentation of a determinate time-point, and in the flowing of the Now this point of time is continuously a different one. If we ideally fix the Now and pick out a phase from its flow, then to each point of time there corresponds its own mode of presentation standing in determinately graded, describable relations to what is presented in the Now as the present time-point. Every individual or every time is, relative to the respective Now, either present, past, or future. The future becomes past in the flow of the Now. Time as the order of individuals is rigid; the time-modalities flow, and exclusively in the indicated direction—pastness can never become present, present never future.

Husserl lists several desiderata: a parallel account for the necessary orientation of space; the relation of time-modalities and the ego; the Now and the actual ego. He notes the necessary relation of ego-foreign objects to the Now and to ego-life, and then introduces the critical distinction between immanent and transcendent time: how do modalities constituted for transcendent solipsistic time become objective for intersubjective time? All egos (constituted as mutually transcendent) simultaneously have the same modality of time, stand in the same Now. By contrast, different simultaneous subjects can be differently oriented to transcendent space. There is objectively only one present—what is now is for me and everyone one and the same. But there are as many spatial "Heres" and spatial-modal systems (orientations) as there are subjects.

Husserl separates the question of how time-modalities constitute themselves from the description of immediate ("sensory") representations of time and time-modalities—dark and intuitive representations, where dark ones essentially fulfill themselves in corresponding intuitive ones. He distinguishes: (1) self-fulfillment as immediate clarification in the transition from dark representation to its intuitive counterpart; (2) the unfolding of horizons, which requires the dark first becoming clear, then the horizons attached to the clearly appearing being unfolded—leading to an infinite process. He introduces the concept of momentary presence-time (*Präsenzzeit*) as the time actually intuited in one moment of originally intuitive consciousness. He considers various terminological options and constructs the idea of an infinite continuum of momentary presences and presence-times, in which time itself as infinite time would establish itself through continuous integration—but notes this idea contains an impossibility, since an actual infinity cannot be given in an intuition.

#### **§ 2\. How Is a Flowing Consciousness of the Elapsing Presence Possible? The Flow of Momentary Data and the Flow of Their Time-Modalities. Phenomenological Essential Laws**

To the flow of time-intuition corresponds the flow of presences, and time-intuition is not only a flow but consciousness of a flow. In each moment, not a whole sequence of presences is intuited (that would contradict the concept of presence), but we can perform a momentary reflection and look at the presence—the stretch of the present from the ur-present of the individual content into the originally living intuitive past. Yet we see this presence is already in flow; the reflecting "looking" is itself a perceiving of something temporal, a flowing perceiving in which something flowing gives itself. The ray of fixating attention grasps a "moment" and holds it identically fast—but only in such a way that this identical thing elapses, does not remain identical in intuition. Attention holds fast only an identity of the meaning initially established, while the intuitive content continues changing.

Husserl raises the central question: how is consciousness of flowing possible? A flowing could not become originally conscious if in each moment of actual consciousness-presence only what is intuitive in that moment were conscious. If flowing consciousness consisted of intentionally unconnected phases, each phase would make only one phase of the flow intuitive. For consciousness of flowing, each moment must involve consciousness of a continuous transition from something intuitively conscious to another, and indeed consciousness of an entire flow that is as a whole continually in transition and simultaneously a stretch in original retention. We have multiple streams unified into a manifold stream-continuum—not only the stream of momentary tone-data (of which a stretch as tone-presence is conscious in each "moment"), but also the stream of these data as modally conscious with their present or changing pastness-mode. Each such continuum is not the intuitively present time-stretch or time-object-stretch, but harbors it within, represents it in a certain way.

Husserl discusses legitimate and illegitimate ways of asking about the possibility of consciousness of succession and flow. The question is one of phenomenological clarification: what essential character must a successive consciousness have so that it can and must be consciousness of a temporal object? The answer must come through noetic and noematic analysis and description. If we say a duration cannot be intuitively conscious without more than one phase of the duration being given in each moment, this has value as an immanent essential description only if: (1) consciousness of a duration is itself necessarily a lasting consciousness; (2) each phase of this consciousness-duration harbors a phase of the conscious duration—an intuitive stretch of duration must be present in each phase (the mere intuitiveness of one point at a time does not suffice). We can trace the connections of necessity: how the momentary intuition's reaching beyond its assigned moment retentionally makes the already past stretch conscious, and how in the flow the extensions of momentary intuition continuously cover each other through retentional unities, enabling a unified consciousness of the one and same object, the duration. But the description of these essential connections (which yield insight into incompatibilities and necessities) does not yet describe the streaming itself. Continuity is not yet streaming. A continuity can be "originally" given only in a streaming course, but continuity and streaming continuity are not identical.

#### **§ 3\. The Perception of Streaming in the Presence-Field and the Living Consciousness of Flowing. Graduated Sequence of Flowing Presences**

Consciousness of streaming is (as originary consciousness of enduring) intuitive consciousness of it. We "perceive the streaming." Each phase of this consciousness is characterized as streaming. Each consciousness-moment is not only consciousness of the ur-present phase as streaming (momentary consciousness of something streaming) but reaches beyond the moment regarding the streaming—making the contents conscious through their time-positions in their duration, and the presences conscious as streaming through their stream-continuity. Streaming is not a dead character attached to the phase; it is something wholly peculiar, not to be equated with sensory data. Living in the course of flowing presences, some presence streams over into others continuously. In the field of just-elapsed presences, there is a first (the last still "intuitive" among those characterized as elapsed) and a last (the presently originarily given presence). Each has its horizon. Every presential mode of time is given as a phase of a presential mode of the continuous flow of presences. We must distinguish the time of an objectivity from the time of its presenting modes; this second time also has its modalization. These second-order presential modes form a two-dimensional continuum. The editors mark this as a crucial structural insight about the layering of temporal constitution.

How do we gain this second-order momentary presence? In the flow, we seized a moment. It immediately flows, but we fix what was given in it as the field of modality of a time-stretch of an individual thing. This field is itself a unity given in flowing modes. We can reproduce it in recollection and repeatedly gain it in statu nascendi, identifying it across recollections. Through repeated covering (*Deckung*), it gains thematic force as a discretely intuitively covering unity. But before such abstraction we have a living consciousness of flowing itself. Life itself is in flowing living consciousness of flowing. For each phase of flowing, we have a fluent horizon, that fluent double-continuum. If consciousness could flow without being by its own nature consciousness of this flowing, we would never know anything of flowing. This flowing is given as original becoming—the lighting up of a new Now, original elapsing of this new in sinking, original sinking of the already sunk.

#### **§ 4\. Various Levels of the Primal Fact of the Flow. Arising and Passing of the Present in the Flow. Various Directions of Reflection in the Description of the Flow**

An immanent individual—say, a tone—is given. It is constituted as a unity of a flow. The tone has an onset; it enters actuality, becomes actual. It endures. It continually enters the present with new (same or altered) content, but the present continuously transforms into past, and the same tone with new content becomes present. There arises an ever-new tone-present together with the transformation of the just-become present into a tone-past. We have a "flow" that is a constant arising of new Nows and constant "passing" of become Nows—their constant transformation into pasts and constant transformation of already existing pasts into further pasts.

Husserl describes various possible attitudes of reflection. One can look at the Now and follow the present, continually pursuing what is present—attention binds itself to the form of the present. In the flow, the content of this form constantly changes, but an identical something is conscious, just as through the whole fused unitary series of continuous pasts an identity runs through. Holding the gaze fixed on the identically persisting, the change of content appears as its alteration, and the remaining-the-same as non-alteration. At the identified and retained object, the form of the present flows past. We have an outflow of objective "states" into the past and, in contrast, the flow of becoming-new. Making the flow itself into an object—the original succeeding and its correlates in their covering relations—everything seems to move into time: a future point, when fixated, retreats, sinks, while everything new "surfaces." All these different possible directions of gaze, the different appearing "processes," duration, the advancing of present into present, the sinking of each present into "the" past—all these "appearance-modes" must be described.

## **Text Nr. 6**

### **Acts as Objects in "Phenomenological Time." Temporal Objectivities and the Constituting Primal Stream**

(*Akte als Gegenstände in der "phänomenologischen Zeit." Zeitgegenständlichkeiten und der konstituierende Urstrom*)

#### **§ 1\. How Does the Perception of Immanent Data Differ from the Inner Consciousness of This Perception?**

Husserl formulates a chain of interconnected problems: whether acts, like hyletic data, are constituted as duration-unities in phenomenological time; whether to distinguish "immanent acts" (acts as objects in immanent time) from the primal time-constituting consciousness (the primal stream) in which all immanent objects allegedly constitute themselves; what this means for the perception of immanent hyletic data (whose being consists in their being immanently perceived—does this lead to infinite regresses?); and how to show that perceptions of immanent data are themselves perceived and constituted as temporal unities in experiences not identical with them. Finally, the distinction between perception and the constituting act of inner consciousness.

Husserl addresses whether perception of an immanent tone can be an unchanging perception (paralleling an unchanging tone). If "perception of the tone" means the consciousness that originally constitutes the tone's duration-unity, then no—from the time-diagram, no two phases of the constituting consciousness can be fully alike. But if we understand "perception" as an act-objectivity in immanent time (something constituted in the primal time-consciousness), then perception-consciousness and tone-constituting consciousness must be separated. "Immanent" here has the *Ideas* sense: relation to the unity of adequately given objectivities in an adequately given time-form. At this point, the earlier argument against unchanging perception fails, and there is no reason to deny that the immanent perception of an unchanging tone is itself unchanging. Acts can also persist unchanged—though concrete retention is an example of an act that cannot fill its time with essence-identical phases.

#### **§ 2\. Givenness of the Primal Stream and Givenness of Acts and Act-Correlates as Immanent Time-Objects. Various Concepts of Immanent Perception**

An immanent hyletic perception (say, of a tone-datum) is a lasting object in immanent time, just as the tone it perceives. Both are correlative and inseparably constituted unities in the primal time-consciousness. Since the perception is constituted therein, it cannot be identical with this consciousness. But could it be a stratum within it? Husserl recalls the systematic structure of the consciousness constituting a hyletic duration-unity: it is a two-dimensional act-continuum containing a distinguished linear act-continuity—the continuous succession of ur-presentations, each bringing a tone-time-point to ur-presence. In each, the tone-phase is given in bodily selfhood, in pure adequation. The tone-points in the mode of bodily presence are what they are as correlates of the acts, and vice versa. These acts are a non-independent linear continuity within a broader concrete continuity. The ur-presentations have the character of truly, purely, absolutely bodily-giving acts only in this total context. The ur-presented tone-data are the tone-points of the tone-duration-stretch themselves; correlatively, their ur-presenting acts are their perceptions themselves. There is an apparent antinomy: on one hand, the immanent perception with its immanent object counts as concrete; on the other, the succession of ur-presentations is something non-independent, a non-independent limit-line in the time-constituting consciousness. The resolution: "concretion" refers to a given time-objectual sphere or the individual givennesses lying in one direction of grasping.

We can also direct our gaze at the primal stream itself—the sequence of continua—and when we do, this sequence itself becomes visible. Then we have a new time-form and a new objectual sphere, a new concretion: the most encompassing one, in which all previously independent objects with their times appear as "enclosed." Whether this constitutes time and a concrete sphere in the analogous sense needs investigation. We have a prescribed sequence of levels: a first field of concrete givennesses in the hyletic data; a reflection leading to a new field—the time-objectual modalities.

#### **§ 3\. The Course of Immanent Time-Objects and of the Process Constituting Them Is Parallel but Not Properly Simultaneous**

Instead of being directed at the becoming tone, I can be directed at its present, past, the change of modalities, and find the streaming of time-modalities. The modalities of immanent data coincide with the givenness-modes of each tone-point. A change of attitude is needed to go from the tone as thing to the time-constituting consciousness. In reflection, holding the constituted thing fast, we must call the consciousness simultaneous with the thing—their forms (duration of the thing, process-form of consciousness) cover each other. But is this an identity-covering? The way the tone constitutes itself through presentation and retention, and the way the process constitutes itself as process, are ultimately the same—each process-phase is given in an ur-presentation and then in protentions and retentions, just like the constituted material. Both are temporally and essentially alike. Yet this is different from two simultaneous things constituting in the same time. The constitution of thing and consciousness-of-thing belong to different dimensions. The process-phases form a continuous succession not constituting itself in a new process—the process itself has the wonderful property of being simultaneously consciousness of the process. The covering of thing-time and consciousness-stream-time is not a pure identity-covering like that between the temporality of different things, but rather a covering of necessarily correlated parallels.

#### **§ 4\. On the Concept of Simultaneity: Various Layers of Temporal Courses and the Unity of Temporal Order. Doctrine of Ideas I**

Husserl transfers these results to the relation of immanent time and objective time. What constitutes itself in different directions of reflection constitutes itself as objectivity in the form of its time, but only objects of the same reflective direction share identical time-form—they form a closed field of things with one all-encompassing form of time. Not even sensation-data and outer objects appear in really identical time—they are objects of two "worlds" whose time-forms have necessary parallel connection. The concept of simultaneity thus contains a phenomenological equivocity. All constitutively connected objects of direct and reflective directions have their time in their constitutive layer, but all these times are united through the parallelism of their constitution—covering point for point, forming a single ordering system whose ordering points are multiply constituted. Husserl draws an analogy with space-constitution (visual aspects and spatial objects) and reflects on his own earlier position in *Ideas I*, where he had treated noeses and noematic correlates as equally belonging to one phenomenological time alongside hyletic data, with the primal time-constituting consciousness behind them all. He provisionally confirms this was essentially correct.

#### **§ 5\. Noetic and Noematic Temporality**

An outer object endures in objective time; an immanent object (hyletic datum) endures and fills its duration with hyletic point-data given moment by moment as present. A perception, phantasy, or judgment is also a time-object, a duration-continuum whose phases come to original givenness in sequence. Husserl carefully distinguishes the total time-constituting consciousness (in which not only the enduring tone but also moment by moment the present tone, the just-past tone, the expected tone constitute themselves) from the constituted perception as an act-unity in immanent time. The act-time and the time-modalities in which the act is given must be separated just as with any immanent object. The judgment 2\<3 as an event-unity has its contributions from each present-moment, and the retentional and protentional modifications belong to the judgment-phenomenon of inner consciousness, not to the judgment-event itself. So we do have a proper act-time, and the immanent time is a form common to everything immanent. The immanent perception is not what properly constitutes the perceived but is a correlative, essentially belonging unity of immanent time. Husserl concludes: all essential characterization of acts, act-correlates, intentional actualities, quasi-actualities, etc. is carried out in immanent time. But immanent temporal objectivity is itself constituted—pointing back to inner consciousness and its primal process. Here we have a radical demarcation. This primal process does not constitute itself in the same way as the objects of immanent time. From the diagram it must be visible that not only the unity of the enduring tone constitutes itself through the interplay of ur-presentations, retentions, and protentions, but also the unity of the immanent perception of the tone constitutes itself—and the analysis shows how, through the noetic-noematic structure of the modifications.

## **Text Nr. 7**

### **On the Doctrine of the Time-Modalities**

(*Zur Lehre von den Zeitmodalitäten*)

#### **§ 1\. Original Time-Constitution in the Case of the Sensation-Object. The Connection of Consciousness of the New and Past Now of an Enduring Tone**

Picking out a "moment" from the constituting consciousness, we find a point-phase "Now" (ur-originality phase) and a continuity of "just-having-been" (retentionally fading originalities). This form holds for every moment but is contentually different for each new Now—each new Now has a different "comet's tail." For each moment, the comet's tail is a unity of fusion (*Verschmelzung*), with continuous covering of similarity that is simultaneously a degree of intentional gradation. Comparing the retentional transformation-series of one Now with that of another: these series are necessarily different—the same Now cannot be conscious in both. In the unity of a consciousness where both Nows are given, they are in different intentional experiences never connectable through identity-covering. Each new Now creates a new intentionality; this novelty is a novelty of sense that maintains itself identically through all corresponding retentions.

#### **§ 2\. What Distinguishes a New Now from Another Now of the Same Content? Streaming of Consciousness and the Consciousness of an Objectual Duration**

In the flow of the originally time-constituting process, ever new Nows appear on the correlate-side. What distinguishes the new from the new? Not the "quality" of Now (essentially indistinguishable). Not the "content" (which can be entirely the same, as in an unchanging tone). It seems we have the continuum of time-positions, the continuum of Nows, and the continuum of tone-points—but what distinguishes the time-positions, which are again specifically fully alike? This leads into oddities that must rest on a misunderstanding of what is really given. Objectively speaking, the process is in each phase intrinsically distinct; but what are the problems? Given is the phenomenological fact that something like an unchanging enduring tone can be given. The consciousness must have the character of a flow; the constitution of every experience as an immanent temporal objectivity and as given from phase to phase in other modes is a necessary fact. In each phase, an originary point of the appearance of a "tone-point" in the ur-form of the Now—and the Now means here originary and most primordial givenness of the tone-datum, a point of it itself. It is not a second content but the content itself, not as merely meant or merely intuited but as originally given. This original givenness is not something composing the content but an intentional character.

#### **§ 3\. The Time-Form of the Process and the Time-Form of the Constituted Objects. Time-Order and Time-Modalities**

We have the time-form of the process as form for its experiences and correspondingly for its experience-correlates. But clearly distinguished are: the time-form belonging to the tone's own essence (constituted through these noemata) and the form belonging to the consciousness-processes and their noematic correlates. The structures of this consciousness that allow the process itself and the continuous sequence of noemata to be distinguished, and each Now to be distinguished from every other, must be clarified. We can speak of the present Now, the past Now, the past present, the present past, the past past—all of which, rightly understood, have their good sense. The tones have a time; the tones have their time-modal givenness-modes—one-dimensional manifolds in which the tone-time "represents" itself as "objective" time. These manifolds are also temporally ordered, also have a time, which also has its givenness-modes. And the noetic experiences of the time-constituting consciousness are also temporally ordered—and so the chain returns. It must be shown why all this is correct, why no infinite regresses (and certainly no actual infinity of time-orders) result.

#### **§ 4\. The Multiple Times and the One Time. Subjective and Intersubjective Time**

Every time points back to a constitutive context, but these contexts are not separate—they all form a single context. They all cover each other in a certain way. Does this covering mean all these times are one time? We all believe that everything—ego-foreign and egoic, things and acts of subjects, consciousness-streams and persons—belongs to one world with one form. In the pure immanence (after phenomenological reduction), no "we," no plurality of subjects is first given—only the stream of experiences and the pure ego of this stream. "Foreign" subjects are objects intentionally announcing themselves within my consciousness-stream. So first we have immanent time, then the problem of constituting an "objective" non-immanent time, then a time constituting foreign and own consciousness as human, intersubjectively identifiable. The question is: how do the different times separating within pure immanence relate to each other? They have their own objectivity—forms of individuals constituted in immanence. It seems all "covering" times really constitute themselves as one identical time, and all series of individuals have simultaneity in this time.

#### **§ 5\. Duration as Objective Time-Stretch and Subjective Givenness**

Husserl clarifies the concepts. When we say an immanent datum has a time-extension and is an event filling a stretch in time, we understand time and time-stretch as something objective—the same whether I now hear the tone or recall it. The time-stretch belongs to its essence. In contrast, "duration" (*Dauer*) can mean the time-stretch is in statu nascendi, in becoming, presently taking place. "Duration" has a non-accidental ambiguity: the living duration of the still-becoming versus the elapsed duration that is identical through all modes of givenness. This identical thing—which has its first positing in the modality of the present and in all further transformations points back to this primal givenness—is the time-object, and to it belongs the objective time as essential time.

#### **§ 6\. Time as Encompassing Rigid Form and the Flowing Now. Time-Modes and Givenness-Modes of Time**

Time as the encompassing form of the objective time-stretch is rigid and does not flow; but the Now flows, and the time-modalities flow. To every time-point and time-stretch belong modalities that are the time-objectivities' own in their truth—they have their own objectivity, an objectivity in constant flow that is nevertheless objectivity of a validity in itself. Time itself has something constantly flowing about it, although it is itself a rigid thing—rigid identity in flow, nothing without this flow or beside it. It is a system of determinations for the flow regarding its identical component. Must we not say: "Time" is the flow of all presents, the constant appearing and sinking of all presents? But "time" is also used as the identical continuous linear order constituting the identically persisting time-points and time-stretches. The time-modes must be distinguished from givenness-modes of time. Only when the time-mode is originally given do we have evidence for its determinate belonging to the relevant time. Most primordial givenness (in the mode of the present) is the only givenness in which the being of the time-stretch and what endures in it is given in living duration, living becoming—but this is not yet originary givenness of objective time-points and time-stretches, which constitute themselves only as the whole become duration sinks into the past.

#### **§ 7\. Supplement. The Time-Modalities in Inner and Outer Perception, in Recollection and Phantasy**

In the immanent sphere, the time-modalities of the immanent tone coincide with the givenness-modes of the tone-time itself (when we understand givenness-modes as original ones in flowing retention). In recollection, each phase of duration gains the "Now" character only by occupying its place in the originally time-constituting process. A genuine recollection would demand the entire preceding consciousness renewed. This makes the enormous difference between phantasy and perception. In phantasy, the horizon is generally empty; in recollection, it is contentually more or less determinate and explicable. Isolated recollections do not suffice for time-relations.

#### **§ 8\. The Temporality of the Time-Modalities in the Living Present, in Retention, and in Recollection**

Do the time-modalities themselves have position in time? A tone's living present lasts as long as the event's objective duration. The present-process and the event-process are not identical: the modality-process is itself an objective something that was present and then past. How does the time in which the flow of modalities runs relate to the rigid time? The process of sinking into the past is a constant becoming of new modality-levels. This process itself has its present, its pasts, its identity. The originally presenting process and its correlate (original presence) are bounded though "flowing" at the boundaries. How do we come to posit the flow of time and time itself as infinite? The problem of phenomenological time's "shrinking"—how each shrunk thing un-shrinks in recollection. Adequate, perfect recollection as an "idea" never fully realizable.

## **Text Nr. 8**

### **Description of the Time-Modalities in Noematic Regard**

(*Deskription der Zeitmodalitäten in noematischer Hinsicht*)

#### **§ 1\. Modification-Levels of the Now in Past and Future. Two Concepts of Temporal Modification. Streaming Consciousness of a Time-Object and Consciousness of Streaming**

A time-object is originally conscious ("perceived"): a time-point appears, becomes conscious as "new," as Now; afterwards as just-having-been; a more or less determinate pre-consciousness referred to it before its appearance as "just coming." In consciousness, the time-points are conscious in the noematic forms "now," "just-past," "just-coming." The form "Now" stands in a peculiar relation to the others: it is the "original or ground-form," in relation to which all others are "modifications." "Just-past" means "just-past Now"; "coming" means "coming Now." In the forms as noematic "sense"-forms, the sense "now" lies "modified" enclosed. The modifications are by their own sense continuous and gradually increasing/decreasing—continuous level-sequences whose null-limit is the form-moment Now. Every modification-form can itself be taken as a relative starting-point for further modifications: every past is not only past Now but also past past relative to any past closer to the Now. Two senses of "modification" must be distinguished: (1) the immanent relation of every noema regarding its pastness-mode to a continuum of other past modes—containing nothing of streaming; (2) the streaming in whose continuous transformation the modes are successively grown together, the null of modification (ur-present) continuously transforming into ever-further past.

Husserl describes the primal consciousness as a stream or stream-stretch, a linear continuum like the time-object's time-points. In each stream-phase, not merely one time-phase but the whole time-object with all its time-phases is conscious, though in different givenness-modes. One and only one time-point is conscious as Now; all "preceding" ones as "past," in a continuous gradation from zero (Now) to ever-higher levels. The time itself has an order (earlier-later) that is distinct from past and future—the latter concern modes of time-givenness in the stream. The law connecting both is described: the earlier a time-point relative to the Now-point, the higher-level its modification of "just-past." The future side is treated analogously: from the ur-Now, a future-horizon extends as a continuum of noematic modifications of the type "originally coming" in continuous gradations. Both branches meet at the common null-point (the Now). The total noema of each stream-phase is a double linear continuum—what Husserl calls "primal-stream atoms" (*Urstrom-Atome*). But the two branches are not simply two rows of analogously structured modifications; very difficult descriptions are needed. The streaming itself must be described: in the streaming, a consciousness of the transition from stream-phase to stream-phase exists. In the continuous succession, each ur-present time-point loses its ur-present form and takes on the modified form of pastness of lowest rank, then in the next phase the next level of pastness, and so on continuously. The identical content "streams" through the form-system of pastness-modifications, which as a fixed framework remains standing. The future-horizon also remains as its own form-system, with ever-new content flowing through: what was conscious as "immediately coming" transforms into the noematic form of lighting-up ur-present.

#### **§ 2\. The Stream and the Adumbration-Forms of Time-Objects and Time-Stretches. Time and Space. (On Terminology)**

The stream is a continuum of stream-phases. Each stream-phase is an aspect of the time-object—a "time-objectual aspect" as opposed to a spatial aspect. The stream is noematically the successive continuum of time-objectual aspects. Each individual adumbration/aspect is itself a continuum: the whole time-stretch adumbrates itself through each of its points adumbrating in the running-off modes. Husserl introduces terminological distinctions (point-adumbrations, stretch-adumbrations \= aspects, point-adumbration-continuum) and draws analogies and disanalogies with spatial schemata: the time-shape is a piece of time itself (unlike the spatial shape, which is not a piece of space but moves through congruent space-pieces). In time, "time-piece" and "time-body" coincide—there is no analogue of the spatial body.

#### **§ 3\. Closer Investigation of the Noematic and Noetic Structure of Temporal Objectivities and Time-Schematic Aspects. Is the Model of Apprehension and Apprehended Content as Appropriate for Time-Constitution as for the Constitution of Spatial Objects?**

The main question: whether the apprehension-content/apprehension schema abstracted from spatial objects can be carried through here, down to the point-adumbrations. Is each stream-phase noematically an "aspect" because it harbors a continuum of sensory data ("ur-data," "primal soundings," "fadings") accompanied by "animating apprehensions"? It is certain that in the pastness-consciousness we have an intuitiveness-stretch in which the "appearing" past time-content and a how of appearance must be distinguished. The givenness-mode of the past time-point does not differ from the ur-present merely by the same sensory content being placed in a different form. In the ur-present mode, the tone-point is conscious "in the original"—the content is in the mode of originality, while in later stream-phases, a modified content ("fading" of the original) functions as representative, through which we "mean" the original. Husserl works through the noematic structure carefully: the intentional object with its time-schema and content (logical form "something" with determinations), the noematic sense as the identical through the streaming noesis, the givenness-modes of both time-schema and content. He insists the sense as intentional unity constituting itself in successive covering is not "reell" contained in consciousness. Then he introduces the critical distinction between immanent and transient objects: for immanent objects (sensation-objects like a sounding tone), how is the tone-point conscious when its schema runs through the modes of ur-future, ur-present, ur-past? Each mode involves a different givenness. The temptation is to say: in the ur-present mode, the tone-point's original is a real (reell) moment of consciousness; in other phases, "fadings" of this original function as representatives. But Husserl warns against simply identifying being-in-the-original with being-a-real-component-of-consciousness—otherwise every outer perception would contain its object as a real datum. He draws the parallel with spatial-object adumbrations: the shading-moment is not noema but functions in the noema through a peculiar covering (resembling yet distinct from image-depiction). The darstellende moments are in consciousness as functioning hyletic data, not as noematic data—an ideal transformation of them enters the noema.

### **Supplement IV (to § 3 of Text Nr. 8): Is the Presently Originally Given Tone-Point a Real Content of Consciousness? On the Problematic of Noematic Time-Objects and of Apprehension in Time-Consciousness**

Husserl probes deeper: in the moment of ur-present, the tone-point is conscious "in the original"—but does this mean the tone-point is a real (reell) moment of consciousness? Even for immanent objects, the constituted unity of consciousness can never be a consciousness-piece in the constituting consciousness. Just as the intentional object, so too the "intentional object as such" and its how of givenness—everything noematic—is not a consciousness-piece. But corresponding to the objective datum in phenomenological time, there is something real in the stream of experiencing. For the present-consciousness we must distinguish: the moment relating to the formal as consciousness-of-originality, and what relates to the content that is conscious in the original. We have in the flowing consciousness a "tone-sensing"—a life-moment that does indeed flow, appearing and disappearing—a "real hyletic datum" that is a component of consciousness itself and not of the conscious object (the so-called immanant tone of immanent time). In retentional consciousness, the modified hyletic datum functions (and becomes objectual only in reflection). Can we then apply the apprehension-content/apprehension schema? Not as in outer-object consciousness (where the apprehension-content is itself a constituted object), but the hyletic moment is a non-independent consciousness-core clothed with functions. What matters most is to systematically extract through all possible reflections the structures lying in the intentional object as such (ontologically), in the noematic, and in consciousness itself (reell). Husserl ends with a remarkable set of open questions: whether the noesis-noema distinction is absolute or merely relative; whether something absolute must remain in the final analysis; and the conviction that the primal consciousness can only properly be described through its noematic constituents, to which one must attend level by level. This passage closes Part II and marks the transition to Part III's systematic investigation of the apprehension/content model and the danger of infinite regress.

# **Part III: On the Application of the Model of Content and Apprehension in the Analysis of Primal Time-Consciousness, and on the Danger of Infinite Regress**

(*III. Zur Anwendung des Modells von Inhalt und Auffassung in der Analyse des ursprünglichen Zeitbewußtseins sowie zur Gefahr des unendlichen Regresses*)

## **Text Nr. 9**

### **Apprehension and Apprehended Content in Outer and Immanent Perception**

(*Auffassung und aufgefasster Inhalt in der äußeren und immanenten Wahrnehmung*)

#### **§ 1\. Outer Perception Is Founded in the Time-Objects of Immanent Perception**

Husserl begins Part III by recalling the apprehension/content schema as it operates in transcending consciousness, paradigmatically in outer perception. The transcendent object "adumbrates" itself in hyletic data that are not themselves components of the perceived object but "represent" it—and the "representing" is the apprehending consciousness. The hyletic data have their immanent being as individual data in the form of phenomenological time. The perceptual experience is a continuous unity of succession; through this succession runs an intentional unity—the apprehensions of the sensory data are in continuous "covering" (*Deckung*), and an enduringly selfsame thing given in different appearance-modes constitutes itself. Every "outer perception" is thus founded in immanent experiences. This means something quite different from the synthetic founding of spontaneous acts in spontaneous acts: it does not mean immanent givennesses must first be grasped, then the founded ones built upon them. Rather, for a transcendent object to come to original givenness, the giving experiences must have a determinate structure—an immanent streaming of hyletic data and their apprehensions running in "phenomenological time." Every outer perception is an interweaving of a double objectivation: outer perception is in its essence a continuous course of "inner" perceptions (perceptions of immanent temporalities), through which a second intentionality runs, constituting the outer object in its transcendence and objective time through "representation."

#### **§ 2\. Living Functioning and Apprehending Grasping of Differently Constituted Immanent Time-Objects**

Husserl examines the immanent perceptions of hyletic data and distinguishes the various reflections we perform. The gaze on hyletic data goes through the ur-presentations, which function as fulfillments of protentions and transitions to de-fulfillment; the gaze goes toward the coming, grasps the same, sees it sink, but continually pursues the new. The whole constituting consciousness is in action; the grasping encompasses all its intentionalities in a determinate way—not that the acts are themselves struck by the gaze, but the ur-data of the ur-presentations are also not grasped as such; they "function," and so functioning in the grasping, the immanent tone (which is no component of the process) is grasped. Similarly for the reflective grasping of the perception and of the process itself—different attentional configurations illuminate different intentional lines and interweavings. This leads to a refined account of "living functioning" versus "apprehending": under all circumstances, the stream of experiencing with all its intentionalities and constitutions is in flow, whether we are grasping or not. Only attentive turning, bestowing a particular performance-animation (*Vollzugsbeseelung*) on the constitutive system, actualizes it into a perception of this or that object. What is "present" but ungrasped becomes actual object only through the ego's grasping. Acts can also function as contents of acts (as in Lockean reflection on perception). Genuine apprehension of hyletic core-moments occurs only when the attentional mode of grasping targets the relevant objects and they exercise "real function." Beyond this, speaking of apprehension and apprehension-contents designates the corresponding potentiality of grasping.

#### **§ 3\. Various Concepts of Apprehension in Outer and Immanent Perception. The Intentional Modification of Retention, Phantasy, or Recollection Implies No New Apprehension**

Husserl carefully differentiates multiple senses of "apprehension" (*Auffassung*): (a) in outer perception, where sensory data as apprehension-contents represent a transcendent object through animating apprehension-characters; (b) in cases like recognizing a signal, a tool, or a remembered object—all of which involve founded acts. He considers whether immanent perceiving can itself be called apprehending—but in the same sense, no longer: there is no concrete consciousness with a still-deeper constituted object lying beneath the sensation-datum. Then he makes the decisive claim: no consciousness that in itself modifies (by its own intentional nature) is an apprehending regarding its modificate. It was wrong to regard phantasy as a peculiar apprehending with "phantasmata" as apprehension-contents. Phantasy is a modification of the corresponding perception; phantasy-contents are modificates of corresponding sensation-data, not themselves sensation-data "only otherwise apprehended." Likewise retention, recollection, and anticipation are not apprehending but at best modification of an apprehending. Every "modification" is characterized by the fact that the relation to another consciousness (of which it is a modification) is enclosed in it—a consciousness not really contained in it but graspable through a suitably directed reflection.

#### **§ 4\. Does the Relation of the Time-Constituting Flow to the Core-Data Still Have the Form of an Apprehension of a Reell Content?**

Returning to the first phenomenological level (immanent sensation-data under abstraction from transcendent apprehensions), Husserl asks about the structure of immanent perception. He finds continua of intentionalities whose phases are wholly non-independent, among which are sensory ur-data (ur-sensation-data) with merely punctual and fleeting existence—not the concrete existence of sensation-data proper, which are subsisting unities in immanent time. Alongside are protentions and retentions, which are modifications and cannot be called "apprehensions." One might try to parallel: just as in outer perceiving the hyletic data are reell components of the experience, animated by apprehension, so too the ur-present data in inner consciousness are reell-contained and "animated" by the ur-presenting consciousness. But the "reell" here means nothing other than "conscious in an unmodified, purely originally giving consciousness (ur-presence)," and does not mean that the reell of the innermost sphere is a sensation-datum already constituted before the "apprehending" consciousness. The reell of the innermost sphere is a last, no-longer-constituted something—only as "content," as the reell core of the ur-presenting consciousness, without which it is unthinkable. And the ur-presenting consciousness is not a concrete experience but an abstractly non-independent punctual phase. Nothing prevents calling the core-data-bearing consciousness an "apprehending"—but this is then not in the old sense, since the core-data are not first there and then taken up by an additional consciousness, but are non-independent and only thinkable as so borne. The main question remains: may we speak of ur-data "representing" objective time-points, or are ur-presentations not themselves what present, with their cores being the time-objects themselves (identifiable only through identification)?

## **Text Nr. 10**

### **On the Question of the Temporality and Perceivability of the Ultimately Constituting Primal Process. The Hypothesis of a Non-Temporal and Unconscious Primal Process**

(*Zur Frage der Zeitlichkeit und Wahrnehmbarkeit des letztkonstituierenden Urprozesses. Die Hypothese eines unzeitlichen und unbewußten Urprozesses*)

#### **§ 1\. (Objective) Time and (Subjective) Time-Modalities (Orientation). Important Time-Ontological Axioms**

Husserl lays out fundamental ontological axioms about time. Time is an objective form; each time-point is objective. A time-point is a time-point of an individual that fills it. The essence of the individual contains nothing of "now, past, future." Time in itself is not present and was not and will not be. Each time-point "runs through" each mode of pastness and futurity once and the mode of present once. The temporal object and its phases are, with "their" time—but to the object's own essence belongs no moment of now-present, past, or future. Yet the object necessarily has a givenness-relation to the knowing subject: it "happens" presently, or has happened, or is anticipated. "Event" is thus a subjective givenness-concept. Time and its objects do not flow; they are, and the "is" is rigid. The time-flow is not the flow of time but of the givenness-modes of time and its objects.

#### **§ 2\. Objective Time, Phenomenological Time, and the Ultimately Constituting Consciousness-Stream. The Danger of Infinite Regress**

Phenomenological time is the first and most primordial time-form. Behind it lies a further transcendental-subjective sphere—"experiences" of a new level in which this temporality constitutes itself, experiences that are themselves not temporal (neither objectively nor in the sense of first-level transcendental time). But the question arises: is the stream of experiences constituting phenomenological temporality not itself in phenomenological time? This seems to lead to an infinite regress of stacked times. The real question is: how far does actual description reach? From objective time to sensation-data (first-level transcendental time), then to givenness-modes of these data (second-level), then: can we further reflect? Do we find givenness-modes of givenness-modes that are genuinely new? No—we cannot find differences in the exhibiting description, let alone new levels of differences. But is there not a problem: must the flow not constitute itself as unity, and if so, through new givenness-modes? This is indeed the problem. Furthermore: can an experience-process run without being simultaneously consciousness of the experience-process? Can there be an ur-life process that is not already consciousness-of?

#### **§ 3\. The Perceivability of Temporal Objects of Various Levels. Perception with and without Grasping**

Husserl explores conditions of possible perception for temporal objects at all levels. Essential is the distinction between grasping perception and perception without grasping (consciousness giving an object without the grasping attention-ray). Grasping perception presupposes the possibility of non-grasping perception. For immanent objects, esse \= percipi (in this non-grasping sense): their being is inseparable from the being of their constituting process. But for the constitutive processes themselves (the time-objects of the next ur-phenomenal level): their perception seems to require yet another constituting process—threatening an infinite regress. Unless: the process constituting immanent objects of the first level is not only itself constituted/conscious but is such that it is in itself, without requiring new processes, conscious of itself and its temporality—a last primal process whose being is consciousness and consciousness of itself. How is this possible?

#### **§ 4\. Phenomenological Idealism. Real and Ideally Possible Subjects and the Perceivability of Time-Objects**

Husserl connects the time-problems with phenomenological idealism. The perceivability of transcendent objects does not require that they be "immanent." Spatial objects can be without being actually perceived. The ideal possibility of perception of eidetic objects yields their actuality (excluding contradictory possibilities), while the real existence of a real world requires not merely ideal possibility of perceiving subjects but their actuality. For time-objects: every temporal object is rationally positable only as either now-being, having-been, or going-to-be, and thus seems not thinkable without some actual subject. But attempting to interpret perceivability as a purely ideal possibility of "logically" possible subjects leads to difficulties—every possible object has a time-position in a possible subject's time-field, which is trivially true and does not establish actual existence.

#### **§ 5\. Does the Constitution of Time-Objects Require a Grasping Perception? Is There a Non-Temporal and Unconscious Primal Experience?**

Husserl raises the question whether the subject "experiences" things without them being constituted as temporal: two possibilities. (1) The primal process is constantly constituting phenomenological time with its time-filling immanent objects, even without grasping—reflection is then a mere redirection of gaze through already-constituted intentionalities. (2) Streams of primal life run without constituted experiences (temporal unities) actually in them—only a potentiality exists, and reflection means a new performance of "apprehensions," a bringing-in of time-constituting that was not already at play. Husserl probes both possibilities, noting that what reflection grasps always has its temporal form, every act of reflecting is itself a streaming perceiving. Are these life-series not there whether we attend to them or not? Are they not in themselves temporality-constituting? The "first possibility" is that before any grasping, a constitutive process already runs wherein events are intentionally objectual—constituted as time-objects without the favor of attention. The "second possibility" is that ur-experiences appear in sequence but lack everything that would make a unitary consciousness of identical time-points possible—"apprehension" is missing.

#### **§ 6\. Further Consideration of the Hypothesis That the Constitution of Temporality Is a Matter of a Subsequent Apprehension of a Non-Temporal and Unconscious Primal Process**

Husserl tests the hypothesis of "unconscious," non-intentional, non-constituted processes. If a merely unconscious succession of life-moments is supposed, then when one moment passes and a new comes, the old is absent for any apprehension. We get a succession of apprehensions where the preceding one cannot persist. If we try to say every datum is accompanied by modifications as well as apprehensions, and these apprehensions themselves are ur-data requiring further apprehensions, we get the regress. The decisive objection: the moment of novelty (of ur-novelty, not yet modification) is itself a moment of every primal experience; if it is apprehended as Now, this moment of novelty would itself need to be apprehended, requiring a new moment of novelty and so in infinitum. Every modification also appears as new—requiring a moment for "newly appearing" and so in infinitum. Recurrence to "new" moments and "modification-moments" for the apprehension that is supposed to yield time is therefore "fundamentally wrong" (*grundverkehrt*).

### **Supplement V (to §§ 5 and 6 of Text Nr. 10): On the Question of the Consciousness of the Primal Process**

#### **a) Precision of the Question**

The two possibilities are restated with precision. Under (1), the primal process is constantly time-constituting—a consciousness-process of intentional experiences constituting time with time-filling objects even without grasping. Under (2), streams of primal life run without actually constituting experiences as temporal unities. Husserl argues that what we find in reflection is a real consciousness-process already constituted as a process before the reflection—there is evidence that being and being-constituted are inseparable for it. The reflection is a mere change of gaze through already-accomplished constitution. If the process were in itself unconstituted (unconscious), its perception would require a new, different process of constitution—which would have to be of a different kind from the reflection that finds the process already constituted. The conclusion presses: a primal process not constituted for itself as a process (not conscious of itself) is unthinkable. Every experience must be conscious, and the consciousness of it must itself be conscious. Everything depends on clarifying the self-relatedness of the process constituting first-level experiences—which sounds like Münchhausen pulling himself from the swamp by his own hair, yet cannot be abandoned if infinite regresses are to be avoided.

#### **b) Modes of Grasping of Outer Objects and Immanent Objects of Various Levels**

Husserl considers how different kinds of objects are originally "perceivable." For outer objects, no difficulty. For immanent objects, we have unnoticed backgrounds; when we turn to them, we grasp them, with the retentional consciousness that they were un-grasped, the same. For immanent objects of the second transcendental level (constitutive processes), there is no persisting individual being—but there is the persisting of identical processes through their givenness-modes. The original givenness-mode for such objects is reflection, which grasps the process as "object" of a constituting process—but not as something persisting in the sense given above.

## **Text Nr. 11**

### **Content and Apprehension in Ur-Presentation, Retention, and Protention**

(*Inhalt und Auffassung in Urpräsentation, Retention und Protention*)

#### **§ 1\. The Fundamental Facts of Time-Consciousness**

Husserl lays out four fundamental facts: (1) There is ur-present consciousness in which something is conscious as ur-present, as now being. A content is conscious as Now, and this Now is a form of this content. (2) Ur-present consciousness transforms into the continuum of pastness-consciousness; in this continuum, the same content is conscious in continuously modifying form. The ur-present is what it is in transition to having-been. (3) Ur-present can continuously join into the unity of an enduring ur-present (e.g., a continuously sounding tone), and to each phase belongs the continuum of pastnesses. (4) A discrete new ur-present can follow a continuous one—but every ur-present is only possible as "duration." The two "springings-forth"—the continuous one in which ur-presences arise and the one in which pastnesses arise—are inseparably one: consciousness of the continuously new ur-present is possible only through the sinking of each phase and stretch into pastness.

#### **§ 2\. Ur-Presentation, Retentional Modification, Phantasy-Consciousness**

Entering the structure of the time-constituting consciousness, we reach the primal fact of a process that is by constituting itself as process. We find phases: each consisting of a phase of unmodified consciousness and a continuity of further phases that are modified consciousness (of the earlier unmodified phases), at continuously different levels. As the ur-presentation has core-data in itself "reell" (unmodified), each ur-retention has core-data but not reell—it has them as those of the other consciousness, in modified and non-reell fashion. The modification has a graduality of clarity and darkness, analogous to phantasy. In our case, the graduality follows the graduality of retentional modification. There is a continuous sensory content-continuity from the ur-presentation through the retentions—which seems to support the idea that sensation-data are reell throughout. But one objects: only one present-point of the event is "really given" at each Now; yet we find a continuum of core-data in living intuitiveness. We seem forced to say this intuitive stretch is "still" in perception—grounding the talk of "perception" on a continuum of sensation-data functioning as representatives of the pastness-apprehension. Against this: it is certainly not mere reproduction or phantasy. In the case of retention, the difficulty is the continuous transition of retentional core-data into ur-data.

#### **§ 3\. Ur-Presentation and Retention as Apprehensions of Reell-Contained Data**

Husserl works through the hypothesis that not only in the ur-presenting moment but also in each retentional phase, a sensory content is reell contained—a continuity of sensory content "animated" by a continuity of "apprehension," through which objectual constitution comes about. But this cannot be apprehension in the ordinary, object-presupposing sense. A concrete retention immediately following an event is built from ur-retentions of the same kind as those functioning within the perceiving consciousness of a tone-event. If the concrete consciousness is founded, then so is each phase; but the ur-retentions are certainly not founded—we have no apprehension-data and no founded apprehending in the rejected sense. If we assume a continuity of ur-sensation-data (the ur-datum for the ur-presentation, "fadings" for the retentions), these cannot function as representatives. What then does "sensation-datum" mean for the cores of primal time-consciousness?

#### **§ 4\. The Levels of the Retentional Fadings as Levels of the Apprehensions of a Reell Content**

Husserl tries to carry through the view: as a new fading appears (a new present), it is retentionally apprehended in relation to its predecessor. This retentional apprehension is itself a new present that fades and is again apprehended—building an ever-higher structure of retentions of retentions. The formalism works: E₀ goes over into R\[E₀'\], this into R\[{R\[E₀'\]}'\], etc. This is analogous to continuous image-likeness—one can consistently carry it through. But barely soluble difficulties arise when the sequence of ur-present data exactly matches the fading-series, and when we ask why certain fadings fuse with the ur-data and others don't.

#### **§ 5\. Counter-View: Retention Is Not Founded in Reell Given, Present Data**

The counter-view denies that retentional consciousness involves founding by a present consciousness and does not insert a sensation-present as momentary-present. The view would be: ur-presence is a consciousness of a "content," a non-independent consciousness that cannot be without extension. The content is also non-independent—only as "present," i.e., conscious in this peculiarity. This consciousness with its content is not itself again content of a further presenting consciousness. This consciousness-with-content continuously transforms into another consciousness that is a "modification" of the earlier.

#### **§ 6\. Originary Consciousness and Non-Originary Consciousness. Consciousness and Grasping**

Husserl works through the distinctions: X perceived \= X given in the original; versus X phantasied, X recollected, X otherwise re-presented. Every experience is "perceivable," present and graspable in its presence. In the original to grasp an individual is to perceive it and have it given as presently being. Before grasping, the experience is a real (original) experience. We must separate originary consciousness from non-originary; in the primal process, ur-originary phases versus non-originary phases. The non-originary phases are themselves originary experience-moments, only they are not originally giving regarding their "object" (the pastness-phases of the time-object). "Conscious" does not mean "grasped" or "attended to."

#### **§ 7\. The Danger of Infinite Regress in Originary Consciousness of the Present Primal Process. The Question of the Grasping of a Non-Intentional Consciousness**

If every process-phase is conscious, then each phase is consciousness of the phase. Must not this consciousness again be originarily conscious, and so in infinitum? This is the difficulty—the ultimate, reduced form of the old difficulty of "inner consciousness." Husserl considers the attempt to appeal to a continuing potentiality of establishing perceptions (each of which, as a life-occurrence, is a process). He also considers whether grasping itself is what constitutes the "present"—but objects that retentional consciousness can reflect on the having-been-present even where it was not grasped. The deeper difficulty: if the ur-process and its phases are not intentional experiences, how can the grasping reach them? How can the equivalence between grasping something intentionally conscious and grasping something "simply there" be understood? "Whoever finds no stumbling-block here has not understood what is proper to intentionality."

#### **§ 8\. Is There Also the Danger of Infinite Regress with Protentions?**

Husserl raises whether protentions and their fulfillments, being themselves present, also require protentions directed at them—and so in infinitum.

#### **§ 9\. Attempted Solution: Immediate Consciousness of the Present of the Primal Process as Limit of the Two-Sided Continuity of Retentional and Protentional Mediacy**

The difficulty is not insuperable. In the primal process, fulfillment is every Now and fulfillment of every past as past. The whole primal process is a stream of protentions and fulfillment of protentions, equally a stream of retentions and de-fulfillments. Each phase can be described: R(∞.....0.....Eᴋ) P(Eᴋ.....0.....∞). Each phase has a continuity of intuitiveness and one of non-intuitiveness, double-sided and symmetric—a retentional side and a protentional side. The limit between the intuitive and non-intuitive is Null (of intuitiveness); the culmination is the present-point Eᴋ (maximal fullness). The ordering is a continuous series with mediacy increasing from Eᴋ outward in both directions: absolute immediacy is an idea with its limit at Eᴋ—the Now-consciousness, immediate consciousness of Eᴋ, is retention only as the limit of continuity of retention, which conversely becomes ever more mediate toward zero and infinity. Exactly this analysis transfers to the protentional side. At the transition-point Eᴋ, where the counter-directed stretches meet and close into a unity of one stretch: a single consciousness is here signified as momentary consciousness, with two extensions of immanent intentionality. Here lies the zero-point of mediacy, also of protention.

#### **§ 10\. Attempt to Differentiate the Graphic Representation (Diagram)**

Husserl describes the momentary consciousness as a continuum with two extensions—two continuous extensions of intentionality. Further descriptions clarify the lawfulness governing the succession of the generally coinciding momentary phases. The new, complete diagram of the process is introduced (though time-perspective cannot be given diagrammatic-sensory expression). Each U(x) is itself a zero-point for a continuum of retentions (the vertical lines left of it) and protentions (right of it). The strong vertical U(x) is the zero-point; its retentions are the vertical-continuum to its left, its protentions to the right. Each U(x) is protentionally directed at all following ones and retentionally at all preceding, with intuitiveness-bounds on both sides. U(x) "grows" upward toward infinity while fulfilling itself, and in its full form encompasses the entire half-plane to its right. Each U(x) is not only arisen through modification of an earlier but is also "expectation" of a similar future.

### **Supplement VI (to §§ 9–10 of Text Nr. 11): Difficulties of the Solution-Model of Increasing Indifferentiation and Mediacy. Differences in Retentional Sinking**

The distinction between differentiated and indifferentiated pastness-consciousness creates difficulties. Husserl uses the example of hearing a melody: within the sphere of differentiated past-consciousness, differentiation decreases toward the past, ideally toward zero—but not all differentiations vanish simultaneously. The constituted unities (tones, measures, strophes) have their own closure, prominence, and force of self-preservation in further concrete retention. As constituted unities, they retain unity of sense but can lose differentiation of their "apprehension-contents."

### **Supplement VII (to Text Nr. 11): On the Designation of Pastness-Consciousness and Its Modification**

Husserl introduces formal notation: B(E) \= consciousness of E; V \= pastness-modification (retention); the law excluding infinite regress: B(B(E)) \= B(E); and the designation-law VK(VΛ(E)) \= VK+Λ(E). With upper index zero, V⁰(E) \= E—no modification.

### **Supplement VIII (to Text Nr. 11): Representation of the Continuum of Time-Positions and Pastnesses in the Diagram**

Husserl works through the formal correspondence between time-positions and pastness-indices. The pastness-index of a point is determined by its distance from the point currently in the Now. The various symbolic conventions for the diagram are elaborated, including the 45° representation of the correspondence between time-position distances and pastness-modification levels.

### **Supplement IX (to Text Nr. 11): The Form-System of the Primal Life-Stream and Its Continuous Modifications in the Diagram**

A very important (though unfinished and fictive) analysis. Husserl distinguishes "form" and "content" in the primal life-stream. A formal moment of all primal experiences grades itself in a linear continuum from a point of ur-novelty (I) toward an ideal infinitesimally reachable limit (zero). A second dimension of individuality (indexed by z) enters—a two-sidedly infinite linear continuum giving each life-moment its individual character. The "flow" is the same for all life-moments: all moments of form Iz pass into the continuously new forms I...0 in the same way and "equally fast." Each new I must have simultaneously all modifications I...0 as modifications of the "preceding" Iz. The old diagram serves with appropriate indexing.

### **Supplement X (to Text Nr. 11): Ur-Present and Pastness as Form and as Individual Givenness. Representation of the Retentional Modification in Formulas**

Husserl separates the general form of ur-present from the individual ur-present (an absolutely unique, once-only thing). Each time t has one Now, one unique ur-present. The particular pastnesses are bound to specific J-indices: V is bound to J₁, V(V) to J₂, V(V(V)) to J₃. The notation JK VK designates both a continuously new V-modification at a continuously new position in the flow and the cumulative VK \= V(V(V...)) modification. The succession of ur-presents J₀...JK is called a "depth-phase-continuity" (*Tiefenphasenkontinuität*).

## **Text Nr. 12**

### **An Attempt to Analyze the Primal Process without the Model of Apprehension and Apprehended Content**

(*Ein Versuch, den Urprozeß ohne das Modell von Auffassung und aufgefaßtem Inhalt zu analysieren*)

#### **§ 1\. Outline of the Analysis of the Flow of Ur-Impressional Givennesses and Retentional Fading-Modifications Using the Model of Apprehension and Apprehended Content. The Objection of the Infinite Regress**

Husserl recapitulates: fading belongs to the primal lawfulness of all data of transcendental consciousness. All transcendental events of phenomenological time are constituted unities pointing back to two constitutive transformations: ur-springing impression (ever new "ur-data") and ur-springing retention (each ur-datum transforms into a "fading," which itself undergoes the same fading-modification, and so on). This primal process is the seat of the most primordial intentionality, from which the first "objective" time with the first transcendental events arises. In transcendental reflection we find ur-impressional data, the fading-modifications, and also the apprehension-functions. But if the apprehension-moments are themselves data requiring further apprehensions of a still-deeper level—we arrive at a regressus in infinitum.

#### **§ 2\. Development of the Alternative Model: The Primal Process in Its Arising and Modifying Fading Implies No Apprehension. Only the Reflectively Perceiving Grasping of Ur-Data and Their Fadings by the Ego—i.e., the Constitution of Temporal Objectivities—Requires an Intentional Apprehension. Peculiarities of the Perception of Fading-Modifications**

Husserl develops the alternative: before any grasping, the primal process is a mere process of ur-arising and fading without any apprehension or representation. (He notes that if a genuine constituting time-consciousness has already developed, a background apprehension may operate through "secondary sensibility.") Genetically, an ur-beginning is conceivable: a primordially sleeping consciousness that has never been awake, or spheres in which primal processes run without being time-constituting. When an ur-datum appears and stimulates the ego to grasping, this is the beginning of the constitution of a time-event-consciousness. The ego grasps the continuously newly springing; this grasping undergoes the modification of fading—and in this fading, the character of pastness-consciousness necessarily arises because the steady modification carries a continuous covering of the intentional that holds what was once posited. Without grasping, we would have the mere primal process without any time-constituting apprehensions and thus no really constituted temporal event, no phenomenological time. All objecthood, even time itself, is a product of constitutive functions—of spontaneities. But once time-objects have been constituted, the law of secondary sensibility acts, so that even where no genuine time-event has been constituted, a primal process can stimulate a unity-apprehension reproductively. Husserl then returns to the question of "fadings" as representatives: the real after-sounding of a tone is itself present, genuinely heard, not a time-representative. The retentional "fading" is something else—a necessary modification of every experience-moment, making time-apprehension possible. This modification can be taken as a present representative (itself as present), analogous to a reproductive modification.

#### **§ 3\. Investigation of the Aporias of the Constitution of Ur-Data and Fadings as Time-Objects (in Both Competing Models). The Danger of the Infinite Regress**

The fadings themselves are time-objects; their continuum is itself a temporal event. If we assume every original datum and its fading-continuum necessarily receive apprehension (before any grasping), then where are the apprehensions through which the fadings themselves become objects? The supposed apprehensions are themselves ur-consciousness-data requiring yet further ur-presentations and modifications—in infinitum. The alternative interpretation avoids this: every original life-datum fades; the primal life is a stream of data (ur-data and fadings), all before any apprehension. But the law holds: every datum of this primal life is graspable as "immediately bodily" given—and once grasped, the grasping itself fades, and retentional consciousness ensues. This is an infinitely repeatable potentiality but does not presuppose an infinity of acts and representatives in advance—all difficulty seems removed by this interpretation. What is really present are the respective primal processes; whatever functions of perception we exercise are themselves in the form of primal processes. The existence of an experience as a time-event before grasping and actual constitution means nothing other than the ideal possibility for the ego to exercise the spontaneous functions on the basis of the pre-objectivating primal process.

## **Text Nr. 13**

### **Averting the Infinite Regress in Time-Constitution through the Demonstration of the Retentional Self-Reference of Time-Constituting Consciousness**

(*Abwendung des unendlichen Regresses in der Zeitkonstitution durch den Nachweis der retentionalen Rückbeziehung des zeitkonstituierenden Bewußtseins auf sich selbst*)

#### **§ 1\. Recapitulation of the State of the Problem: The Immanent Time-Object and the Change of Its Givenness-Modes**

Husserl recapitulates: an immanent datum endures, fills a time-stretch, passes. In the perception of the event, we distinguish perceiving the beginning (the onset-point given in the mode of present), then the continuous new event-point in the mode of present with the continuum of pastness-consciousness of the earlier phases. Each event-point has changing givenness-modes. In reflection, we find the "fading-content" and the "apprehension" that animates it—the same event-point through different representations as pastness-representations of the same, entirely analogous to the adumbration of a spatial surface.

#### **§ 2\. The Objection of the Infinite Regress: Are the Experiences Constituting the Event-Phases Not Themselves Again Constituted?**

From the perceived event we went back to the "constituting" consciousness-experiences. These are not invented; we perceived them. We had continuous sequences of ur-giving experiences for event-phases: they appear in absolute selfhood, immediately losing this selfhood as ur-givenness. We had successions and coexistences. But from the standpoint of consciousness: a primal process with springing-forth and flowing-away. All words seem to carry temporal meaning. Are the constituting experiences not themselves in phenomenological time, thus requiring further constituting experiences, and so in infinitum? The diagram shows: not only does the continuous time-stretch E₀–Eₙ constitute itself therein, but also the sequence of constituting experiences of the second transcendence-level. If C₀ goes into C₁, through V¹(E₀) we are conscious of C₀ as just-past. Thus exactly as the E-series is perceptually conscious through the pastness-modifications, so too is the C-series—we arrive at no infinite regress.

#### **§ 3\. Two Directions of Transcendental Reflection: On the Stream of the Constituting Flow and on the Sequence of Constituted Events**

Husserl distinguishes the flow of constituting experiencing (running whether grasping occurs or not) from this experiencing as complicated by reflective-attentional modification. In the phenomenological perception of E, the grasping ray from the ego goes through E₀ E₁ E₂... continuously, holding what was once grasped through all modification. In the deeper transcendental reflection (second level), the gaze turns to the transverse rows (the continua of givenness-modes) and holds them through their modifications. The two reflection-diagrams are contrasted: Diagram I (gaze following the E-series through V-modifications) and Diagram II (gaze directed at the transverse rows as wholes). A remarkable multi-dimensionality of attention emerges. What the reflection on the transverse rows targets is the transverse row of givenness-modes of the E—not of "acts."

#### **§ 4\. Unity of the Functioning Primal Process of Constituting Consciousness and Its Doubling in Subsequent Reflection. Constituted Immanent Temporal Objectivities of First and Second Level**

The question: how does the first phenomenological time (in which a transcendent event constitutes itself as a filled time-stretch) relate to the phenomenological time in which the constituting series constitutes itself as a stretch? The constituting event and the constituted event are constituted in one and the same constitution. The constituting sequence as sequence is peculiarly implied in the constituting sequence itself. The simultaneity has a different sense from objective simultaneity—the phenomenological time is in one sense one (admitting two attitudes) but double insofar as an objective event-series constitutes itself and then the constituting itself, in reflection, must present itself as a series and time-succession that cannot be called identical with the first despite essential interweaving. The transcendental primal living is a stream of ur-consciousness, an unceasing arising and passing (modification). It belongs to the ur-peculiarities of consciousness that: (1) a connection, a creation of consciousness-unity, shows itself in the diagram as the unity of a continuous transverse row—the unity in the ur-simultaneous; (2) a consciousness-unity prevails in the continuous ur-succession, holding through the succession the perceived sequence unitarily "enduringly." Reflections are new events—noetic rays from the ego penetrating the primal-life-data in various ways.

### **Supplement XI (to Text Nr. 13): Recapitulation and New Possibility of Infinite Regresses in Time-Constitution. The Problem of the Temporality of the Primal Process**

Husserl recapitulates all that was described and asks: does not the time-form presuppose itself? All that we grasp in time-consciousness must itself be subject to temporal apprehension—what we behold as time-constituting must itself be a temporally constituted thing. This seems to demand an infinite regress. The ur-consciousness of E₀ transforms into V(E₀), this into V(V(E₀))...; V(E₀) is itself a new event-point (a present), requiring U(V(E₀)), which transforms—yielding new event-series and multiple infinite regresses. But: (1) when an ur-datum newly appears, the consciousness of this ur-datum is not itself newly appearing in the same sense, not itself an ur-datum requiring yet further consciousness in infinitum. (2) When E₀ E₁... arises in ur-present, this is not yet the consciousness of the event E₀-Eₖ; for that, with the newly present E₁ there must simultaneously be present V(E₀), etc. The primal succession in the double generation of (1) new Eₖ and (2) pastness-modifications is event-constituting. In what sense is it itself an event? The diagrams address this.

### **Supplement XII (to Text Nr. 13): Alternative Solution-Attempt for the Problem of the Infinite Regress: The Consciousness of Temporal Objectivity and the "Perception" of This Consciousness Are Necessarily Connected**

From the alternative standpoint (Text Nr. 12): "I perceive an event of phenomenological time"—I then reflect and describe the How of its givenness. Each perception is itself again a transcendental event, really given only in a second-level perception, and so in infinitum. But experiences that the subject does not perceive still are and run off—they seem to differ from specifically perceived events only in that the subject does not look at them. This seems to require infinities of apprehensions. The alternative (Text Nr. 12\) tries to show that the event as event in original propriety arises and consists only in perception—without actual perception, all that constitutive apparatus is not really present.

# **Part IV: Egoic and Hyletic Temporality in Genetic Consideration**

(*IV. Ichliche und hyletische Zeitlichkeit in genetischer Betrachtung*)

---

## **Text Nr. 14**

### **My Stream of Experience and I**

(*Mein Erlebnisstrom und Ich*)

#### **§ 1\. The Reduction to the Temporality of the Primal, Egoless Sensuality**

Husserl begins from the phenomenological reduction, considering the stream of experiences: a "living," necessarily mobile "present" with its structure of ur-presence and "horizon" of just-having-been and a future. Penetrating this horizon, one grasps in "evidence" a continuity of such subjective presents — an endless past-continuity and an indeterminate coming continuity. The points of ur-presence form a streaming continuum of a fixed form: the form of immanent time. To each point of immanent time belongs an objectivated full presence. The continuity of living presents forms an "objective," repeatedly identifiable stock in a fixed immanent time-form — initially to be distinguished from the time-form of the ur-presences, but "in covering" with it. Here the problem of infinite regress recurs (is not every full presence, when I look at it, the center of a horizon?). In any case, we have several immanent time-orders, among them a necessarily first one: the form of temporal being whose content contains nothing of the intentionality through which temporal being constitutes itself as temporal.

Husserl introduces a crucial methodological reduction within the phenomenological reduction: the reduction to "primal sensuality" (*ursprüngliche Sensualität*). This is an abstraction from the ego and everything egoic — though only an abstraction, an important one. In the first immanent time-order, we then find sensation-data and sensory feelings. Sensory drives are affections toward the ego, passive reactions — but we now also exclude these, since they bring the ego into play. We distinguish the "completely egoless" sensory tendencies: sensory tendencies of association and reproduction, and the horizon-formations they determine. This yields a first, "abstractly" isolable structure: the passivity of primal sensuality. Three layers emerge: (a) the passivity of primal sensuality (egoless); (b) irritability — the realm of affections and reactions (presupposing the first level); (c) the *intellectus agens* — the realm of active ego-spontaneity (presupposing both lower levels). Within (b) and (c), distinctions of attention and wakefulness arise: the ego's various ways of being directed — grasping, feeling, desiring, willing. A theory of attention and wakefulness is needed, separating passivity within wakefulness (yielding) from spontaneity in the pregnant sense (performing specific ego-acts). New layers thus enter the stream of experience, each with its own immanent time-order — not separate from the egolessly considered layers but new structures upon them, also indicating modes of transformation of the old (functional transformations through dependence).

#### **§ 2\. The Identical, Non-Objectual and Non-Temporal Ego as Functioning Pole (Primal-Stand) of the Stream of Experience**

We have attempted to construct the universal realm of immanent time. Seemingly we "have" all that is subjective — yet not everything subjective is temporal, individualized by a unique time-position. What we above all do not have in the stream of experience is the ego itself: the identical center, the pole to which the entire content of the experience-stream is referred — the ego that is affected, that actively behaves, that shapes. We have spoken of all this and found a place for it in the stream, but now it must be discussed that the ego as identical pole for all experiences and everything ontically enclosed in the intentionality of experiences is the pole for all time-series and is necessarily "above"-temporal: the ego for which time constitutes itself, for which temporality and individually singular objecthood exist in the intentionality of the experience-sphere, but which is not itself temporal. In this sense it is not "being" (*Seiendes*) but the counterpart (*Gegenstück*) for all being, not an object (*Gegenstand*) but primal-stand (*Urstand*) for all objecthood. The ego should not even be called "ego" — it is the nameless above all graspable, not standing above, not hovering, not being, but "functioning" (*fungierend*): as grasping, as valuing, etc.

Must not the ego's experiencing of stimuli and responding to them also be non-temporal, insofar as we take the ego as the functioning one? But in functioning and actively attending to the constituted, a temporal stock necessarily accrues — always new, temporally changing or relatively persisting. This stock, as arising from ego-function, directs reflection toward the functioning ego, which becomes objectual in reflection as the identical function-center, the achiever of all these achievements. But how can what is not an object become objectual? How can what is non-temporal be grasped, when grasping can only find what is temporal? It is given essentially that in the experience-stream something arises that has a totally different becoming from other experiential things — given not merely as "something happens" but as "I do something," "I suffer from something." Before that: something is given with an irritating character (stimulus) or with the character of an "accomplishment" (*Gebilde*) — pointing back to a correlate, a co-being in a new dimension: "I do this," "I achieve the achievement" — and there we encounter the pole, an identical something that is not itself temporal.

Husserl contrasts this ego-pole with the objectual poles (intentional unities). Every object "constitutes itself" as unity of manifolds of "consciousness" of it. In the experience-stream (with its parallel, covering time-series), we find separate experiences of the cogito-form. Through reproductions and identifications, the reproduced experiences are separate, their ego is identically the same. The ego is "standing and remaining" — not arising and passing like an experience, not temporally extended (with different phases like something changing or enduring). It is identical even in discontinuity. The experience-stream is continuous, and the ego is continuously "there" through it. But what endures has in each phase of duration a new content; the ego has in time no content at all — nothing different and nothing alike, nothing "intuitive," perceivable, experienceable. Only the affections and acts that have entered time have their contents and durations. Every act has the act-pole, the ego, which is not only numerically identical but has for all time-points the same absolutely identical sense "ego" — an identical of form, a kind of ideally identical, always again temporally "localized" through its acts and states, yet not really temporal.

## **Text Nr. 15**

### **The Time-Relation of the Pure Ego**

(*Die Zeitbeziehung des reinen Ich*)

#### **§ 1\. The Eidetic Shape of Psychic Interiority. Temporal Ur-Genesis, Ego, and Hyle**

Husserl characterizes the general essence without which a unified interiority is unthinkable. Through phenomenological reduction: the return to the stream of pure experiences and the pure ego, to immanent being whose necessary form is "phenomenological time" — and then the further return to the primal consciousness constituting immanent temporal objectivities. The essential lawfulness of this time-constituting consciousness is the primordially first and deepest lawfulness of a consciousness-genesis and simultaneously of a genesis as primal constitution of objectivities. Everything exhibitable in an interiority must fall under the contrast: ego as subject-pole of comportments, related through them to all other immanent things (its "environment"). The form of immanent time is the form of all environment-objects and of all ego-comportments. The ego itself as subject of comportments constitutes itself for itself as temporal with the comportments. A non-reflexive environment is necessary. To it necessarily belongs a hyle — a "hyletic environment" as an unconditionally first necessity. No point of immanent time without this first objective content: in oriented immanent time, no Now without a hyletic ur-impression. Further essential laws follow: hyletic unities cannot be punctual; they must join into enduring time-objects. Not only is there no hyleticallyempty time-stretch, but every hyle has its qualitative stock, which cannot change discontinuously from time-point to time-point.

Ur-genesis (as eidetic form) — the form of the constitution of immanent hyletic objects — underlies every further genesis. All genesis takes place in the ur-form of time-constituting consciousness. Everything constituting itself as individual — such that it cannot be given twice absolutely identically (originally experienced, perceived) in the environment — must be given as something temporal. The goal: working out the essential form of interiority or the psychic as a development (thus eidetically necessary development), in which a purely hyletic environment as the first immanent temporal objectivity and the corresponding ego of the lowest level co-develop — the latter up to the empirical ego that has a transcendent objective world over against it.

#### **§ 2\. The Pure Ego and Time. The Ego as All-Temporal Individual and as Secondary Time-Object**

Is the ego of the cogito, like a hyletic datum and other objects, a unity constituting itself in the consciousness-stream — itself a datum and then an individual datum? Everything belonging to an interiority is an actual or possible datum of the ego of this interiority. The ego in a new cogito relates to the old cogito; in yet another cogito, the second-level cogito can be grasped, etc. The ego becomes objectual and graspable, identifiable as the same across all levels of reflection. But the reflecting ego only becomes object through a new reflection that has it as unreflected subject. Every reflection becomes visible in the higher reflection as entering and proceeding in immanent time. The ego appears as active in time, holding itself through the stretch of activity. The ego of separated cogitos is evidently the same.

Reflection finds, before the cogito, stretches of affection — stimuli of ungrasped objectivities on the ego. The ego is not continuously a grasping ego through its entire immanent time; even where it is, a horizon of the ungrasped always remains. To the essence of a pure interiority or pure consciousness-stream belongs that everything therein can change within the formal law of filled immanent time — everything except the ego. It is the necessary immanent individuality. While everything else — what affections come, what turnings occur — can vary (is "contingent" within the essential form), the ego is the necessary individual and the only one. Otherwise only this is necessary: that there are individuals and individual occurrences, and that they fill time.

Returning to the primally constituting life-stream: it has an essential structure according to which sensory ur-impressions arise in unceasing ur-arising, with contingent content (bounded only by formal laws). Correlatively, a single ur-ego belongs to the stream — not contingently entering like an objective datum but necessarily present as the numerically unique subject-pole of ego-affections and ego-comportments. The ego runs steadily and necessarily through all of immanent time, identical regardless of what may affect or become the theme of its temporal turnings. Immanent being appears in the form of immanent time: a basic series of contingent "objects" fills this time, and upon it another series of temporally enduring objects (affections, turnings, comportments) is founded, having their necessary unity in the "eternal" ego — the all-temporal individual that relates through these comportments to all objects and can also relate to its own comportments and itself. It finds itself in time and is thus itself a time-object — but in a secondary way.

While every immanent "object" is directly perceivable for the subject, the ego is perceivable only reflectively and only as after-coming. As the living ego, it performs acts and experiences affections that enter time. But the living source-point of this entering — the living being-point with which the ego itself becomes temporal and enduring — is in principle not directly perceivable. Only in reflection, which is something after-coming, and only as the limit of what flows away in the time-flow, is the ego graspable — by itself as grasping and graspable originary ego. The primally living ego is the constant and absolutely necessary correlate of all objects. Its being is totally different from that of all objects: it is subject-being, living in a primal life that hovers above all temporal — a life that immediately enters temporality and provides the ego as subject-pole of its experiences a position and duration in time in a secondary way. Every ego-act is a new content; all ego-acts have a peculiar unity without sharing a "content." The identity-point of all acts gives all this subjective a unity that is not a contentual (extensive) unity but through the identity of an ego touching each time-point equally has a quasi-temporal extension. The ego lives its primal life as a steadily extending experiencing of ever-new content — but the unity-giving identical is a content-less life-subject, not a substrate, but one that comports itself toward objects, toward what is ego-foreign, in such-and-such ways.

# **Part V: On the Phenomenology of Individuation: The Temporality of Experience-Objects, Phantasy-Objects, and Ideal Objects**

(*V. Zur Phänomenologie der Individuation: Die Zeitlichkeit von Erfahrungsgegenständen, Phantasiegegenständen und idealen Gegenständen*)

---

## **Text Nr. 16**

### **Temporal Streaming and the Constitution of Individual Objectual Being**

(*Das zeitliche Strömen und die Konstitution von individuellem gegenständlichem Sein*)

#### **§ 1\. Individuality and Identity of Time-Objects (Fact and Essence): Ur-Presentation and the Individuality of Present Givenness, the Continuous Pastness-Modification and the Identical Time-Position**

Husserl opens Part V by asking what "the same object" means when we say the same object could be either experienced or merely phantasied. What is identical across experience and phantasy is a "full sense" — the noematic essential stock — that in experience has the character "actual" and in phantasy the character "phantasied" (quasi-actual). This full sense is the individual essence (*individuelles Wesen*) of the object, encompassing its identical time-duration and the distribution of its temporal filling over this duration. But how is this "individual essence" a universal? It is one in that it is an identical general that particularizes itself here and there, as individual actuality or individual possibility. Husserl then turns to the case of two experienced objects given in a single presence. In the process of original experience — a process of steadily constituting and positing content — each enduring thing arises at a place in an encompassing process: each new positing (Now-positing) posits its content in the form of a new time-point. The individual difference of the time-point is the correlate of a certain primal instituting (*Urstiftung*) by a givenness-mode that, through the continuous transformation of retentions, maintains an identical correlate. Here lies the origin-point of individuality, factuality, and the difference of existence (*Dasein*). The most primordial having or grasping of a content as fact occurs in the actuality of original presentation and in the consciousness of the originary present of the content. The content is conscious in the mode Now, and in this mode it is individual, unique of this content. The first and most radical character of individual existence appears in the form of Now-being. Every Now is, as originary existence-character, the source-point of an infinite continuum of pastnesses; the whole of pastnesses is structured so that they all lead back to the one process of original presentation.

#### **§ 2\. The Arising and Passing of the Present and the Constitution of Objectively Identical Time-Position, Time-Duration, and Time-Order**

What is the identity of the time-position as against the two-dimensional continuum of ever-streaming pastnesses with its single source-point of momentary present? Each line of pastnesses designates a time-point; the continuum of such lines designates the continuum of the one "objective" time. Every time-point is the form of the identity of the same existence that constitutes itself throughout in a system of pastnesses streaming from the same source-point "Now." The essence of factual being as constituted in time-consciousness is: to be as entering and passing, once and for all entering and in ever-lasting passing. Husserl identifies two fundamental processes that are inseparable sides of one concrete total process: (1) the continuous appearing of a new punctual present, in which the existent as becoming always again enters the present; (2) the continuous passing of every present-point, in which the identical same time-point is constituted. Duration constitutes itself in the flow of ever-new becoming: it is in continuous arising and passing. A persisting substrate constitutes itself as identical — enduring its time so long as each illuminating point of the new present, sinking through the modes of pastness, constitutes its objective time-position through all these modes.

#### **§ 3\. Time-Modes and Modes of Belief or Being-Mode: Are Time-Modes Further Differentiable Existence-Modes According to Fact and Essence?**

Can what one calls time-modes (present, past) be referred to judgment and the ur-doxa as correlates of modes of judgment? Do time-modes designate existence-modes? Husserl argues that the temporal modalities (present, past, future) are modes of the existent, of the individually being as temporally being. Originally given is the individually existent in the transformation of these time-modalities — in the original presence. A necessary modification finds place in originary consciousness where existence is at issue, but it concerns the whole noetic-noematic structure, not specifically what makes it doxic. The time-modes are "existence"-modalities only in the sense that "existence" means individual being (*Dasein*). Husserl contrasts the one-dimensional identical (objective) time — an objectivation that does not fully exhaust what we understand by time — with the modes of present and the continuum of pastnesses, which our everyday and scientific predications necessarily refer to.

## **Text Nr. 17**

### **On the Phenomenology of Individuation**

(*Zur Phänomenologie der Individuation*)

#### **§ 1\. Ideas-Relations and Facts-Relations \= Relations of Specific Essence and Relations of the τόδε τι. The A Priori of the Individualizing Form of Nature and the A Priori of the Individualized, Qualifying Matter of Nature**

Husserl takes up the Humean distinction between relations of ideas and relations of fact as a problem for phenomenology. The a priori of nature divides into: (1) the a priori of the united form time-space; (2) the a priori prescribing to nature the higher form of real-causal nature (Kant's discovery). Every existent has its essence and existence, its So-being and There-being. But the essentia of the substrate is twofold: on the one hand, concrete specific essence (a "repeatable"); on the other, the τόδε τι — what individually particularizes the lowest, no-longer-specifically-differentiable species. The τόδε τι has its own generalities and a general form that specializes, but this specialization is individual particularization, not specific differentiation. Thus "genus" becomes ambiguous: (a) generalization in transition to the individual form; (b) generalization as specification of specific moments. The individual difference is not something "added on" — a specifically lowest moment is already individual, individual through its particularization, which is a last. The axioms of space and time are the essential laws through which space and time are totalities. The manifold of possible τόδε τι is a definite manifold — from finitely many given τόδε τι, all others are uniquely determined. Causality is the dependence of individuals through their qualities; causal laws must supplement the definite place-system into a definite system of concrete individuals.

#### **§ 2\. The τόδε τι. The Concrete, Temporally Individuated Essence and Its Temporal Extension. Division and Extension of Temporal Extension in Formal Consideration (Time-Axioms)**

Husserl returns to the *Ideas I* distinction between sachhaltig essence and τόδε τι. We are led back to individuals whose essence is concrete. Concrete essence contains abstract (non-independent) essences; every essence is individualized through the τόδε τι. The τόδε τι is for each concretum a manifold insofar as each can be infinitely often individuated. The expression "τόδε τι" is nothing other than a designation for the necessary state of affairs that to the logical essence of essence belongs being an identical universal in relation to an open pure possibility of an infinite range of particulars — each an absolute once-only this-here. Individual being and temporal being are equivalent concepts. To the formal essence of the concretum belongs temporal extension — and temporal extension, as moment of an essence, is itself an abstract essence occupying a distinguished position: every other abstractum is temporally extended (spreads over the temporal extension). In the concretum we distinguish "form" and "matter": form \= extension-determinacy as such (duration); matter \= what extends, what endures. Temporal extension is infinitely divisible, and with the division of temporal extension comes division of filling moments. Husserl then lays out time-axioms: every part has its neighbor, sharing a boundary-point; every part admits further division; infinite divisibility follows as a consequence. On the infinity of time: every concretum can be temporally extended, and the extension is iterable. Concreta can be "seamless" (in themselves separation-free) or can contain separated concreta joined as "concrete connections." The lex continui of time-filling governs the continuity requirements.

## **Text Nr. 18**

### **The Time-Form of Ideal Objects. Givenness-Time and Objective Time-Form**

(*Die Zeitform der idealen Gegenstände. Gegebenheitszeit und objektive Zeitform*)

#### **§ 1\. The Difference between the Temporal Extension of Individual and General Objects**

Every consciousness has its continuous spreading — it is in becoming, in an inner flow. The intentional objectivity has a temporal spreading corresponding to the continuity of the inner flow. But of an essence or essential connection, it is said to be timeless, with no position in time, no duration. Husserl responds: in "viewing together" the general on the basis of temporal being, we grasp the general as an identical that particularizes in what is alike. It gives itself in a time-duration, but if grasped in repeated acts, not different generalities distinguished by time-position are grasped — the general gives itself in total identification as identically the same, independent of the length of duration. The general does not spread into time in the proper sense; it does not grow, develop, integrate through phases of ever-new content. Immanent time is the givenness-form of all objects, but general objects have their omnipresence (*Allgegenwart*) in time — they can be given at any time-position. Yet the general is nowhere; related to any time, it is always absolutely the same, experiencing no temporal differentiation and (equivalently) no extension in time in the proper sense. In experiencing consciousness, each phase has its objectual correlate joining into the unity of one object — the grasping is multi-dimensional. By contrast, the essence-intuiting consciousness is, in the single moment, already complete; in the continuous spreading, no integration takes place, each new phase brings the same (not merely an equal), so the consciousness is a continuity of covering in the manner of absolute identity-covering.

#### **§ 2\. The One Time and the Many Times in Sensation-Objects, Natural Objects, and General Objects**

Husserl presses the question whether essences truly appear in the form of time and whether their givenness-time is a "real" time. The coverings (*Deckungen*) with the time of simultaneous individual objects and of the constituting acts are genuine. But time for essences is a givenness-form, not an objective form constitutive of their own being. The crucial distinction: for sensation-objects, givenness-time is simultaneously essence-time (*Wesenszeit*) — they are in the givenness-time, have in it not merely a givenness-form but a constitutional being-form. Natural objects have their givenness-time and their nature-time — the encompassing form proper to their being. But for essence-objects, time is a givenness-form only: no constitutive moment of theirs is a duration in the sense in which an individual endures. Their givenness-time is not their objective time. The phantasy-time of a phantasy-process is a quasi-time, constitutively belonging to the quasi-present of phantasy; the givenness-time of the possible object is not the time of that object itself (which is only a possible time).

#### **§ 3\. The Temporal Essential Determinations of the Constituting Consciousness and of the (Individual and General) Constituted Objects**

Every experience of every consciousness stands under the primal law of flow: a continuity of transformations that show themselves in the intentional correlate. Every concrete experience is a unity of becoming, constituting itself as object in inner consciousness in the form of time. What influence does the necessary time-constitution of experiences have on their intentional objects? When must the constituted object necessarily take on a time-form belonging to its own essential content? Not always — as we see with general objects. Husserl distinguishes: ego-foreign, unreflected objects (sensation-objects constituted immediately, spatial objects constituted mediately through apprehension), and reflected objects. The directly constituted objects (both those of inner consciousness and the ego-foreign hyletic objects) necessarily have a time-form belonging to their being. When objects are mediately constituted (e.g., "physical" spatial objects), immanent time serves as apprehensive representative — and through this representation, an "objective" apprehended time accrues. But there are also higher-level intentionalities (like essence-intuition) where the times constituted in the lower-level objects have no representing significance for the higher objects. In essence-intuiting acts, the essence of a duration is intuited on the basis of given durations — but the constituted object is itself no duration and contains no duration as part.

### **Supplement XIII (to § 1 of Text Nr. 18): Acts as Events in Phenomenological Time. Ideal Objects and Their Supra-Temporality (in Distinction from the Temporality of Individual Objects)**

A polytethic act (constituting a predicative state of affairs) is a process in phenomenological time. In a certain way, this holds also for eidetic objects and states of affairs. But their supra-temporality (*Überzeitlichkeit*) does not mean lack of relation to time; rather, it belongs to their essence that they can be originally constituted at any time, and an identifying consciousness can span repeated constitutions, so that what is constituted at different time-positions is identically the same. The individual time-positions enter into the individual object but not into the supra-temporal object. It lies "accidentally" (*κατὰ συμβεβηκός*) in time — ideally its duration is any whatsoever, i.e., it has no duration as a determination belonging to its essence.

### **Supplement XIV (to § 1 of Text Nr. 18): Problems. Various Forms of Temporal Unity. The Temporal Validity of State-of-Affairs Statements about Individual Objects**

Husserl distinguishes: (1) the unity of a tone that endures or changes (identical substrate of non-change or change in a time-stretch); (2) the unity of the event (the filled time-stretch as continuum of phases); (3) unities like a judgment or state of affairs. An individual state of affairs has merely temporal validity; an eidetic state of affairs has supra-individual validity. The individual constitutes itself in time as enduring substrate of an event. The state of affairs constitutes itself in steps and lies in time in a certain way, but its temporality is not the temporality of its object.

### **Supplement XV (to Text Nr. 18): The Time-Relation of States of Affairs. The Non-Temporality of Ideally Identical Objects and Their Temporal Realization**

Husserl explores the radical difference between the duration of a "consciousness-of" (and especially an active act) and the duration of a hyletic datum. The thought, the thought state-of-affairs, is "ideal" objecthood, belonging to the hyletic data in an individually unique way. The "non-temporal" ideal is an absolutely identical that gives itself in temporal experience in a time-shape that does not determine, does not individuate its own identical essence — only its "appearance-form." The identity of the ideal is not an equality. Husserl also analyzes the structure of the immanent time of hyletic data, orientation-modes, and the apparently infinite regress of retentional layering — but notes that the thought state-of-affairs, though receiving orientation-givenness (Now, just-past), does not have a "real being in time" but a temporal "realization" (*Verwirklichung*) that does not prevent the identity of what is variously realized.

## **Text Nr. 19**

### **Time-Duration of Phantasy-Objects. On Time-Position, Time-Connection, and Individuation in the Time of the Experience-World and the Quasi-Times of Phantasy-Worlds**

(*Zeitdauer von Phantasiegegenständen. Über Zeitlage, Zeitzusammenhang und Individuation in der Zeit der Erfahrungswelt und den Quasi-Zeiten der Phantasiewelten*)

#### **§ 1\. The Absolute Time-Position of an Actual Object and Its Absence in Phantasy-Objects. Actual Time-Position and Individuation, Uniqueness and Equality, the Temporal Unity of All Experiences of the Pure Ego**

A phantasied centaur has its time, yet is "in no time." The time-duration of the phantasied is modified in the same way as its quasi-coloring. But one thing necessarily lacks in pure fiction: absolute time-position — "actual" time. Phenomenal positions and relative positional relations are given intuitively in phantasy, but they are not positions that can be identified in the sense of an "in itself." Husserl restricts to the immanent sphere. All immanent perceptions give originally and "adequately" immanent objects to the perceiving pure ego. In the continuity of immanent self-life, new immanent objects arise and connect. Everything sunk or submerged remains in the ego's dominion — free recollections can reach back. A priori, every here-arising object is conscious in some ego-experience and has a kind of position within the connection of ego-experiences. It turns out necessarily that every immanent content has a determinate objectivity, is a καθ'αυτό — recognizable in repeated recollections, and distinguishable from a completely equal other. This is what "absolute time" achieves: at each point, it is decided for every pure ego what its actual immanent objects are. All "hitherto" past immanent contents have a connection to the unity of an overall objecthood, with the connecting-form of time — so that every immanent object endures, and this duration in all its phases is an absolute, unique, non-repeatable thing. To re-recognize an immanent content in its individuality is to re-recognize it in its absolute time-position.

#### **§ 2\. Phantasy-Acts and Their Connection with Other Phantasy-Acts or Acts of Actuality-Positing Like Perception and Recollection**

In free phantasy (entirely free from actuality-positing), objects have no absolute position, no genuine uniqueness. We can present a red triangle in arbitrarily many disconnected phantasies in complete equality; each is distinguished as content of a different phantasy-consciousness but not as individual object. If the phantasies are truly disconnected, there is no possibility of speaking of several objects or even of the same one object repeatedly presented. But as soon as phantasies are given connection — with perception or actuality-positing — the situation changes: identification with recollection or future perception yields absolute and actual individuation. A chain of intentionally connected phantasies (each relating to the earlier as quasi-recollection relates to earlier perception) yields a unity of interconnected phantasies that can be converted into a unity of connected recollections in which the manifoldly intuited is conscious as the same.

#### **§ 3\. The Unitary Connection of a Phantasy or Phantasy-World and Its Unitary Time. Every Phantasy-World Has Its Own Coherence and Its Own Time**

Husserl defines "unity of a phantasy" as any connection of phantasies that through their own sense go together into a possible, intuitively unitary phantasy in which a unitary phantasy-world constitutes itself concordantly as correlate. Within such a phantasy-world, every individual phantasy-object has an "individual" particularization for every time-point and every time-duration. But passing from one phantasy-world to an unrelated other: the "things" of one world have "nothing to do" with those of the other, because the fulfillments and disappointments constitutive of one world can never reach into those constitutive of another. The unity of time, as condition of the possibility of a unity of world, plays its special role. Between different phantasy-worlds, one can speak of equality and similarity but never of identity — no connecting incompatibilities can arise. It has no sense to ask whether Gretel in one fairy-tale and Gretel in another are the same. In the actual world, nothing remains open — it is as it is. The phantasy-world "is" only so far as phantasied, and every phantasy leaves free elaboration open. But essential restrictions lie in the essence of the unity of the phantasy, finding expression in a form of the corresponding phantasy-time.

#### **§ 4\. The Distinction between the Particularization of a General Concept and the Spatio-Temporal Individuation in the Unitary Connection of an Actual Experience or Phantasy**

Husserl contrasts the possible particularization of a conceptual universal in its extension with the individual particularization of an individual-concept in equal exemplars coexisting in a world. The range of the concept "duration" encompasses all disconnectedly phantasizable or actually experienced durations — but this totality offers no individuation of the species "duration," just as the totality of phantasy-colors offers no individual colors. Within a phantasy and within extensions maintaining the unity of a phantasy-world, a further differentiation enters that is not specific and does not extend beyond this world. What ultimately differentiates a lowest difference of color within a world — individuates it — is the hie et nunc. Individual differentiation exists only so far as "one world" reaches. The logical-conceptual particularization is not particularization to an objectively identifiable — logical individuality stands under conditions of time, under the demand for coherent, verifiable intuition. Every phantasy-system is the idea of a system of free phantasies continuing the begun world concordantly. Multiple such worlds as actualities: must one swallow all others? All these worlds are mutually incompatible — if one exists, it excludes all others.

### **Supplement XVI (to § 4 of Text Nr. 19): Essential Insights and Phantasy-Insights. Possibility-Consciousness and the Orientation toward Actual and Possible Objects as Examples. The One Actual World and the Multiplicity of Possible Phantasy-Worlds**

Insights valid for phantasied objects as such — gained by moving within phantasy, freely varying objects, recognizing what holds unconditionally for objects of this kind — hold also for the given experience and every possible experience. Conversely, starting from actual experience and using the given as mere example for pure generalizations: our thinking moves in free phantasy, the actuality becomes an example with its thesis neutralized. Phantasy is the consciousness with the character "as if"; the phantasy-modification allows conversion into a possibility-positing. The possible is taken as an arbitrary exemplar. The universal, the essence, has a range of possible objects. All these possible worlds form a multiplicity, because each possible unity of experience constitutes its world and each other possible unity another. But it is an essential law that all acts positing individual existence, insofar as they proceed from a positing ego, necessarily constitute the existent in its totality as belonging to one time. Only one time can be — for all co-existing worlds.

## **Text Nr. 20**

### **Time in Phantasy and in Actual Experience**

(*Die Zeit in der Phantasie und in der aktuellen Erfahrung*)

#### **§ 1\. Essential Identity between the Intentional Sense of Experience and Phantasy. Experience-Possibility and Phantasy-Possibility**

Every actual experiencing act has its experienced-as-such, identical for all experiencing acts with which the given act covers or could cover in the unity of a concordant identity-consciousness. Every act of a such a group has "the same" objectual direction (the completely equal one), to which corresponds the identical essence. Correlatively in the noema: the same experienced object as such, a numerical identity. For any phantasying quasi-experiencing act: an analogous group of quasi-experiences covering to yield identity of the quasi-experienced. The objectual sense is the same in both cases — but once belonging to actual experiences, once to quasi-experiences, once with simple scare-quotes, once with double (for the phantasy-modification). Any actual experience, taken as particularization of an essence, can be paired with an open infinity of phantasy-possibilities in concrete essential community. The idea of actuality is the idea of a determinate kind of possible experiential connection in the unity of an experiencing subject (and for intersubjective actuality, a concordance of multiple subjects).

#### **§ 2\. The Time-Position in Perception and Pure Phantasy. The Unitary Time-Order of Experience-Objects and the Various Time-Orders of Phantasy-Objects**

Husserl presses the radical difficulty: every experienced individual is in its sense time-determined — but can one really say a perception and a precisely equally directed phantasy are directed at the same stretch of time? Perceptions of otherwise completely equal sense necessarily have different intentional times (assuming they belong to the same subject). Between phantasies: can one say each is phantasied with a different time-determination? Only if the phantasies have "connection" — are related to a phantasy-ego and its coherent consciousness-connection. Arbitrary perceptions within one ego-consciousness necessarily have connection (each has its retentional horizon leading back through recollections to all objects of prior perceptions), but phantasies have no such connection in their objectual reference. A phantasied centaur and a previously phantasied hippopotamus have no time-position relative to each other.

#### **§ 3\. Time as "Form" of "Sensibility" (and as Form of the World of Experience-Objects: The Constitution of a Unitary Connection of Experiences and Phantasies and Their Intentional Correlates through Inner Time-Consciousness)**

Husserl vindicates the inner truth of the Kantian thesis: time is the form of sensibility, and therefore the form of every possible world of objective experience. Before all questions of objective actuality, there stands the essential peculiarity of all "appearances" that they are time-giving and so that all given times fit into one time. It is a main problem of phenomenology to clarify how every experience (e.g., every recollection) comes to have that connection with every other experience of the same ego which creates the connection of everything experienced in the one time. One must be careful not to reason in a circle — speaking of "consciousness-stream" already presupposes infinite time. All simultaneous quasi-perceptions (phantasies of the present) and all those arising in a connected course in inner consciousness prescribe an order of phantasy-time for phantasy-objects. Every perception and every recollection must yield a time-relation for their objects. Differently for phantasies: they do not, by essence, arise in continuous connection. If they did, phantasy-perceptions would constitute a unity of phantasy-time. But they do not. Disconnected phantasies a priori have no necessary connection. It has therefore no sense to ask whether the object of one comes before or after the other. Each disconnected phantasy offers its own imaginary time — there are as many such non-comparable times as there are or can be such phantasies, thus infinitely many. No absolute position of one can be identical with that of another.

#### **§ 4\. Difference between the Connection of Experiences and of Phantasies. The One Experience-Time and the Many Phantasy-Times**

The ground of the different status of perceptions and phantasies: all intentional connection is actual in perception — immediately given, immediately constituted. All non-perceptual intuitive connection points back to concatenations in the unity of actual intuition, leading to continuous recollections (or quasi-recollections). It belongs to the essence of the perceptions of one ego that they arise only in continuous concatenation; the unity of one ego reaches exactly as far as the unity of one inner consciousness, and all perceptions therein must constitute a time-connection covering the immanent time of the acts. But phantasies, though ordered in one inner consciousness as all acts are, do not have objectual connection by essence. If inner consciousness were accompanied by a continuous inner phantasy, then phantasy-perceptions would constitute a unity of phantasy-time. But it is not so. Disconnected phantasies a priori have no necessary connection; each offers its own imaginary time (*Einbildungszeit*), with as many incomparable times as there are disconnected phantasies.

#### **§ 5\. The "Covering" between Experience-Time and Phantasy-Time and between Different Time-Moments of Experience. Covering Does Not Yet Signify Temporal Identity**

The time of a fictum "covers" with the time of the actual present — but the time of an intuitive recollection also "covers" with actual present-time, and likewise the time of an expected objectivity. In covering lies no actual identification. Covering is covering of "content" (possibly the whole concrete essence, at minimum the time-moment), yielding mere equality. Actual identity-covering occurs where an object (in different stretches of its duration) is conscious in two acts — as when I identify a box I now see with one I saw earlier, or when I have recollection-repetitions of the same object at the same time-point of its existence. There the intended objects are identified as absolutely the same; the times are identified as absolutely the same. In the other cases (phantasy-time and experience-time, time of different constitutive levels), we have only necessary mutual reference of two objectivities each having its time, with these two times correspondingly covering — only the essence is truly identical.

# **Part VI: On the Phenomenology of Recollection**

(*VI. Zur Phänomenologie der Wiedererinnerung*)

---

## **Text Nr. 21**

### **Various Kinds of Recollection and Their Repetition**

(*Verschiedene Arten der Erinnerung und ihre Wiederholung*)

#### **§ 1\. Repeated Constitutive Recollections, Incidental Recollections, and Dark Sedimentations with Their Stimulus toward Renewed Turning**

Husserl distinguishes the formal levels of repeated recollection. (1) I perceive a — I originally grasp a in its "present," its original setting-in as Now, its sinking back into the just-been, its continuous original enduring. Even without grasping, this original enduring constitutes itself in the primal stream. (2) I recollect a: E₁(a) — a new experience in which a is conscious in the character of "recollected" or earlier pastness. This recollection-experience is in itself a modification of the perception of a. (3) I recollect a a second time: E₂(a), which is simultaneously E₂(W(a)) and E₂(E₁(a)) — if characterized as repeated recollection, I also recollect the prior recollection. Husserl develops formulas of increasing complexity for third, fourth, etc. recollections. But he then asks whether these formal representations are sufficient. They are not, because the phenomena are far more complex than the "complications" suggest. Husserl distinguishes the "living" or genuinely reproducing, time-object re-constituting recollection from the "incidental" recollection (*einfallende Wiedererinnerung*) — a counterpart to the living one. In the living recollection, the object "again" begins as Now, "again" endures. Opposed to this is the mode of recollection as incidental appearance: after a melody has entirely sunk into darkness, it can still "be there" in a certain way, exercising its stimulus from the dark on the subject. This being-in-the-dark is not recollection but belongs to the horizon of the original intention — a mode of retention where the object is a "dead formation" (*totes Gebilde*), present at once and harboring the originally constituted only as potentiality for a re-production. Everything constituted (whether in primal time-constitution or in synthetic act-connections) has, beside its originally constituting mode, a mode of secondary intentionality — a mode of dark or confused static sedimentation (*statischer Niederschlag*), graspable in a ray of momentary attention in the peculiar sense of a "that" whose real content is displayed in a new productive, dynamic constitution. A chain of dead, incidental recollections can function as a unified stimulus and pass over into a chain of constituting recollections.

#### **§ 2\. Identity-Covering between Multiple, Various Recollections of an Individually Selfsame Past Object**

Husserl analyzes the peculiar phenomena of identity-covering (*Identitätsdeckung*) between dead and living-constituting recollections. The covering-phenomenon is hard to describe in all cases where in repeated acts an individual is conscious. On the act-side, the experiences have their places in the same time; but regarding the "objects," we do not have a chain plus some "identification" — there is only one object appearing multiply in different experiences. It is not like a "sensory" equality-consciousness (three equal bell-strokes), where three phenomena and three objects are involved. When I hear a tone and return to it multiply, the acts differ, but the tone does not appear three times — only once, in different modes of appearance. Husserl wrestles with the difficulty: in the direction of the objects, I find only one; reflecting on the experiences in their succession, I find several. Living in the identifying, I am in the consciousness spanning the individual recollections, each having the mode of direction toward the past — and that, by the sense of identifying, is one. Reflecting, I find multiple experiences, each with its intentional content, the remembered, the direction toward it, and spanning them the unity of the consciousness "the same." An individual unity is nothing other than the correlate of a perception together with a system of ideal possibilities of interconnected open chains of identifying re-presentations.

#### **§ 3\. Summary: Various Kinds of Repetition of the Recollection of a Selfsame Event**

Husserl systematizes: a chain of repeated recollections of the same can be: (1) a chain of dark incidental recollections; (2) a chain of repeated constituting recollections, each arising of itself and involving back-reference to the preceding; (3) a recollection coming as an incidental that immediately passes into constituting recollection, which simultaneously "awakens" an earlier recollection of the same. These distinctions show that the formulas given earlier do not suffice — the possible and important complications are not accounted for.

### **Supplement XVII (to Text Nr. 21): Protention and Retention within the Process of Recollection. Infinite Past and Future**

Recollection is not merely a quasi-renewed running-through of a sequence: during the quasi-running-through, each earlier phase is protentionally directed at the following. This is not recollection "of earlier protentions" but belongs to the recollection as process. The recollected sequence has the character of flowing in the mode of constantly fulfilled "expectation." This leads to the thesis that protentional motivation belongs to the essential ur-form of consciousness — hence "immortality" of the life-movement (*Die Unendlichkeit der Lebensbewegung*), with a reference to Plato's *Phaedrus* 245c: ψυχὴ πᾶσα ἀθάνατος. τὸ γὰρ ἀεὶ κινητὸν ἀθάνατον.

### **Supplement XVIII (to § 1 of Text Nr. 21): Important Note on the Doctrine of Recollection: The Emergence of the Earlier**

Every recollection points back to original retention and perception. The originally given, having the "subjective" mode of the Now, passes into the mode of the just-been and then into the mode of the earlier-been, becoming "unconscious" in this continuously changing mode. Affecting the ego and "consciously re-emerging," it appears in the mode of recollection. The essence of recollection is that it necessarily first has the shape of emergence (*Auftauchen*): in the moment of emergence, the whole earlier — a whole duration in the time-mode of the earlier — is conscious in undifferentiated unity. To the essence of an emerging earlier belongs that it can be explicated, transformed into a quasi-original constituting perception-present-as-if. Every recollection has two modes: the mode of emergence (confused, unexplicit recollection) and the mode of explicit recollection (the reproduced, renewed perception). Necessarily, emergence comes first.

### **Supplement XIX (to § 2 of Text Nr. 21): Essential Insights and Phantasy-Insights. Ideation and Intuition of Identical-Exact Essences. The Evidence of Repeated Recollection as Presupposition of the Evidence of the Consciousness of Identity**

Husserl raises searching doubts about the evidence of recollection. When I grasp an eidos that particularizes in a persisting or equal moment, how can I be certain the equality is complete and that I have a determinate identical essence? The lowest difference and the determinate individual moment are "ideas" (*Ideen*). Every equality found in perception gives itself through "covering" in which no gap stands out — but gap-less covering is a limit, an idea. How can I know that the repeatedly recollected a really offers itself as exactly the same? Whence does the distinction between "real identity" and merely meant identity arise, and how does the intuition of identity prove itself as genuinely originarily giving?

### **Supplement XX (to Text Nr. 21): Repetition and Recollection: Various Kinds of Recollection-Givenness and Re-Givenness on the Basis of the Continuity of Time-Constituting Consciousness**

Husserl distinguishes: (1) re-givenness as reproductive givenness (the self is not actual but re-presented self); (2) re-givenness as actual originary givenness with the consciousness of being given for the second, repeated time (re-recognized). For individual objects: repeated consideration of one and the same object, where unity of time spans both. For essence-objects: repeated givenness with consciousness of recognition — the given is in time but not itself temporal. The essential insight: to every time-point belongs an infinitely modifying course-mode; to every recollection, the character of "returning" to something already temporally constituted; to two recollections of the same, that the recollected has its objective course-mode changed precisely by the immanent "time-distance" the recollections have from each other as actual time-events.

### **Supplement XXI (to Text Nr. 21): Givenness of the Unity of the Consciousness-Stream. The Motivation of Series of Possible Recollections and Expectations**

"Past immanent" is not a title for mere phantasy-possibilities of producing recollections, but for modified possibilities. A single possible recollection-series is distinguished as the series of true and actual recollections — those to which a past as perceived-by-me corresponds. To the essence of every experiential present belongs a past-horizon and the essential law that this horizon is in idea evidently explicable: a motivated possibility exists of producing ordered series of recollections that confirm each other. A priori it is certain that to every perception, taken in its full concretion, only a single fully determinate recollection-series is possible corresponding to the idea of an all-sidedly confirming recollection terminating in the Now. Parallel analyses are needed for the future and expectation. The recollection points backward, the expectation forward — but the future can only be awaited, not gained by mere expecting.

## **Text Nr. 22**

### **Phenomenological Analysis of Recollection as Fulfillment**

(*Phänomenologische Analyse der Erinnerung als Erfüllung*)

#### **§ 1\. The Gradual Evidence of the Recollection of an Individual Process: The Intention toward Closer Determination and Its Ideal Limit**

Husserl analyzes the fundamental phenomenon of "closer-bringing" (*Näherbringung*) in recollection through repeated and thereby chained recollections. In the chain, what was merely intended becomes better determined: the man I remember had not black but brown hair — or, looked at more closely (more closely remembered), blonde hair. The acts form a chain constituting a proper "field of view" or time-field. At the recollected whole, we distinguish phenomenologically what is "properly remembered" and what has the character of a "gap-filler" (*Lückenbüßer*). The closer-bringing reveals a graduality of intention and possible fulfillment. The passive intention can be activated into intensive striving — we can penetrate deeper into the recollection, "bring closer" the remembered in ever "more perfect" recollections. So long as the consciousness is that of mere approximation (greater or lesser distance or "nearness"), its correlate is not yet characterized as "actually so-having-been." The true and actually-having-been is an "idea" — approached through convergent series of apprehensions of increasing perfection, each revealing the previous as "only an approximation." This graduality is analogous to the way we apprehend a color under the idea of pure red, or a line as approaching genuine straightness. An infinite openness belongs to every limit.

#### **§ 2\. Various Kinds of Unclarity and Indeterminateness in the Fulfillment-Process of Recollection. Passive and Active Progress in Recollection**

Husserl distinguishes a further graduality: clarity versus darkness of recollection. Perfect clarity is itself an idea. "Unclear" recollection can mean: I have a vivid "image" but what is vivid does not give itself as the remembered; the image may waver or switch to another. The indeterminateness of recollection differs fundamentally from that of phantasy: in phantasy, indeterminateness can be filled arbitrarily; in recollection, indeterminateness indicates a dimension of possible fulfillment in a determinate direction that has not yet emerged. "It has in reality a determinate color, and I can still remember it." The indeterminateness is a determinability. The intention has a "bound marching-route" — it prescribes the path of illustration and closer-bringing, directed at a fully determinate clarity-pole: the having-been as perceived-having-been. The unfulfillednesses that the earlier perception itself contained remain from the standpoint of recollection open indeterminatnesses — the "perfect recollection" is itself the idea of the consciousness of past perception as such. The process of closer-bringing can be passive (recollection comes as incidental, progressing from incidental to incidental) or active (the ego freely penetrates the intention, lays out its sense, progresses toward the remembered itself). To recollection belongs essentially the idea of an infinite series of approximation to the "completed" recollection — and to this belongs the further idea of a possible freedom and possible exertion of force to overcome all counter-tendencies.

#### **§ 3\. The General Difference between Practical and Exact Orientation in Approaching an Object in Recollection and in Perception. Different Interests Correspond to Different Forms of Adequation**

Husserl draws a fundamental distinction between the practical and the exact orientation. The object itself, the actual object, is an idea — correlate of the idea of perfect attainment. But we must distinguish the practical stance (aiming at the object as practical idea, practical telos) and the exact stance (aiming at the object in "strict," "exact," "ultimate" truth, as idea in the Kantian sense). For perception: the practically oriented person sees the house "perfectly" when seeing what matters for current practical interests — closer inspection adding only "insignificant" differences. The theoretical orientation encompasses the optima of all possible practical orientations, embracing all determinations with equal interest — an interest in the object purely as such, as substrate of properties. From this standpoint every perception becomes imperfect and the object becomes a goal lying as idea at infinity. The analogy with recollection holds: the perfect recollection is the idea of perfect approximation to the remembered as it was perceived. For logic: the sphere of the exact, an essential doctrine of what is in the exact sense. For practical equality, a=b=c does not entail a=c — from irrelevant differences relevant ones can accumulate.

### **Supplement XXII (to § 1 of Text Nr. 22): Retention as a Kind of Perception and the Evidence of Recollection**

The evidence that pastness was past present says that a "transposition" into the originally retentionally conscious past is possible through a recollection connecting in a covering-consciousness. Within the presence-field, the ur-presented present-point is continuously mediated with any retentional past-point. This stretch within presence serves as "apprehension-content" for the stretch of "objective" time. The perception of the time-real takes place in passive identification or covering in the flow of continuous ur-presentation, with retentions sinking continuously. Every retentional stretch is "perception" of pastness — not in its abstraction in the momentary presence, but in the continuous covering of momentary presences. Perception in the normal sense \= originally giving consciousness for a time-object in the mode of present. But perception in the wider sense \= originary consciousness of the real in its temporal modality — not only present-perception is perception but also retention is perception: the perception of the past in its pastness-modality.
