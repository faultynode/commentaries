# ***Studien zur Struktur des Bewusstseins*****, Teilband II: *Gefühl und Wert*** 

## Hua XLIII/2

## **I. Valuing and Value. Toward a Theory of Value**

### **§ 1\. Determinations of the Thing and Determinations of Value**

Husserl opens with a question that will set the tempo for the entire investigation: in valuing (*Werten*), are values *objects*? When something pleases me — when I take pleasure in a landscape, a melody, a person — is the pleasing-as-such itself an object that stands before me in the act of pleasure? The natural first answer of his earlier framework is *no*: pleasure does not objectify; only presentations and judgments — the so-called "objectifying acts" — objectify. Husserl accepts this in a preliminary way but immediately destabilizes it. For an objectivation must indeed underlie the pleasure: what already stands there as objectified (the thing that pleases) stands in the pleasure as *pleasing*, as *beautiful*, as *good*. But, he asks, in the pleasure *alone*? "Beautiful" and "good" are predicates. Must not a further objectivation join the lower one to bring such predicates to constitution? The whole strategy of the section is condensed into this displacement: the orthodox doctrine that feeling does not objectify is right about the *act-character* of feeling, but it leaves unexplained how a value-predicate like "beautiful" can come to attach to an object at all. Husserl is opening the room in which a separate kind of apperception — *Wertapperzeption*, value-apperception — will eventually have to be installed.

He then asks what these predicates ("beautiful," "good") really *say*. Do they say that "the pleasure belongs to the thing"? No: the object that stands there as beautiful is *not* thereby related back to my pleasure. When an object stands there as beautiful, it does not have the relative property of being-something-pleasing-to-me (or to anyone), nor of being-something-that-ought-to-please. The analogy he uses is decisive: when a red object stands there, it stands there *as red*, not as the object that is to be apprehended as red by me or by someone else. The redness is not a relational predicate folding the perceiver into the object's content. *Mutatis mutandis*, beauty is not a relational predicate folding the feeler into the object. Yet, on the other hand, when we speak of "redness" as a property of the object and "beauty" as a property of the object, it is plain that these are *fundamentally different* kinds of "properties." The first belongs to what Husserl will call *theoretical* properties (or rein-sachliche, real properties in the broadest sense); the second belongs to *value-properties* (Werteigenschaften). In a footnote he flags an important caveat: this opposition cannot run parallel to the opposition of intellect and feeling, because predicates such as "true," "probable," "questionable" sit on the same side as "good" and "beautiful" — they too are not bare "real" predicates of the thing in itself.

The next move concerns the meaning of "theoretical." Husserl notices that we call a thing beautiful, but we also call a *theory* beautiful, a person's *conduct* beautiful, his *feelings* beautiful, his *resolves* good, and so forth. So whatever the "object" is — whether a sensible thing, a theoretical structure, a comportment, an act — "beautiful" and "good" presuppose a *full* object. The value-predicate sits on top of an already-given object. By the same token, valuing presupposes the object on which it bears: it is *founded* in a presentation, in another, "objectifying" act. He notes a possible complication — that this underlying objectivation can itself contain a valuation, in which case one must look for *yet another* underlying objectivation — but maintains that ultimately we arrive at "bare objectivations" (mere empirical apperceptions) that contain nothing of valuing in themselves. Comparative or preferential predicates ("better," "more beautiful than") also presuppose what has already been valued as good or beautiful.

The schema, then, is this: first an object, then a pleasure in it, and *with* this pleasure — only thereby — the predicate "pleasing," the *value of the object*, comes to constitution. Crucially, this value is not something that exists for itself. Rather, "through" the pleasure an *expanded* object is constituted: the object that *now has* the value-predicate enclosed within itself. With reference to a new valuing, the object can take on yet further predicates, and so on indefinitely. We thus distinguish properties that belong to an object "from the outset" and properties that accrue to it "only through valuing," only by virtue of the work of feeling (*des Gemüts*). Husserl raises immediately the obvious worry: but values are supposed to belong to the object *objectively*, regardless of whether anyone values it or not — what then is the point of this distinction? His answer is precise: the distinction is not between subjective and objective; it is the recognition that *value-predicates are in principle different from all other predicates*. What an object *is* (in itself or in relation to other objects) and what an object *is worth* (which value-predicates accrue to it) are two different things: the *being* of objects and the *value-being* of objects.

Husserl closes the section with the architectonic claim that will govern much of what follows: objects constitute themselves as what they are, and "before" all value-being, *through the understanding* (*durch den Verstand*); they constitute themselves with respect to their value-determinations *through feeling* (*durch das Gemüt*). Value can be nothing that is not already, apart from value, *something*. An object has its nature, and only through this nature does it have value. The remaining question — and this is the cliffhanger that opens § 2 — is what lies behind all this and how it is to be clarified at a deeper level. Already in this opening section the entire architectonic of Hua XLIII/2 is in place: the parallel between *Verstand* and *Gemüt*, between objectifying and valuing acts, between Sein and Wertsein; the founding-relation of valuing on objectivating presentation; and the as-yet-unresolved question of whether feeling itself has its own kind of apperception — its own *Materie* — by which the value-predicate is built up, or whether it remains a mere "modus" attached to an objectivation that does all the constituting work.

### **§ 2\. Empirical Apperception and Apperception of Feeling. Do Belief and Pleasure Stand on the Same Level?**

Husserl now reframes the inquiry from the standpoint of formal logic. Formal logic, as a formal theory of objects (or theory of validity of meanings), bears in pure generality on objects in general — on objects insofar as they are *anything at all* and have *any* predicates. With this scope established, he turns to *individual* objects and divides the ways they can be determined into two: (I) they can be determined *ousiologically* — they are ousiological unities and as such have, in the broadest sense, a *nature*, a being in the sense of οὐσία; (II) they can be determined *axiologically* — they have, as so determined, a *value*. The relationship between the two has a specific direction: in a certain way value is founded *in nature*, not nature in value. He flags in a footnote that this distinction is sometimes relative and sometimes absolute (in the latter case "nature" is taken so that all value-determinations are excluded).

He then unpacks side (I) — the constitution of objects as objects of *nature*, in abstraction from value. Such objects are objects of experience: of sensible perception, of memory, of expectation, whether immanent or transient. As experiential objects they have their time (their duration), they change or remain unchanged, and as temporal they constitute themselves in "primal association." Insofar as they are transient objects, they constitute themselves through different associative intentions and associative motivational connections among themselves. On this base rests determinative thinking, which objectifies these objects as *objects of nature in the narrower sense* — with objective natural time, space, causal properties, and so on. The unity of natural objects in this stricter sense is sustained throughout connected perception, in that the intentional components of perception fulfill, confirm, corroborate themselves in the course of continuous perception, always in a simply unifying way. This is the domain of the theory of experience as a theory of the constitution of experiential unities and of their determination in the form of natural science (physical and psychophysical).

He turns then to side (II), the constitution of axiological objects, the axiological determinations of objects already constituted in experience. Here, Husserl says, *Erwertung* corresponds to *Erfahrung*. As, in simple sensible experience, the object appears as a natural object, so in *Erwertung* the *value* appears — or, more precisely, in the valuing that is built phanseologically on experience, the natural object appears *in its value*. He coins a parallel: to *Wahrnehmung* (perception, "taking-true") corresponds *Wertnehmung* — the immediately constituting and, as it were, *giving* act of value. This parallelism between perceptual and valuing acts will become a structuring backbone of the entire volume, but Husserl is at pains not to let the analogy collapse the difference. The "value-appearance" is not appearance in the empirical sense of "sensible" appearance. The unity of sensible intentions in empirical appearance, and the unity of empirical appearance in the empirical perceptual nexus, is characterized as *associative* motivational nexus — its intentions are associative intentions that find their characteristic saturation. When, on the basis of an empirical appearance in which the empirical object "stands there," a value-being constitutes itself, the constituting and unity-giving for the value is *not* a sensational, empirical apperception, not an associative grasping. To empirical sensations there correspond *feeling-sensations* (*Gefühlsempfindungen*).

But — and this is decisive — one must not think that to empirical sensations there correspond merely feeling-sensations that for their own part gain an analogous intentional unity to that of the empirical sensations. He patiently rules out two tempting models. (a) One might think that certain feeling-tones belong to empirical sensations the way colors belong to surfaces, so that to empirical unities feeling-unities correspond, the sensible adumbrations of appearing thingly features being "covered" with fused feeling-unities, which then carry intentions parallel to the empirical course of adumbrations and refer to a privileged adumbration (a privileged orientation of the object) in which the feeling-impression would be "the most favorable." On this picture, the intention belonging to the feelings would be an *empirical* one, not essentially distinguished from outer thing-apperception as experiential apperception; through the motivating ocular-motor and other circumstances, the feeling-moments would be co-motivated like other sensible moments, and the determinations constituted through them would belong to the object exactly like color and warmth. (b) Nor can it be the way it is with the constitution of *adherent* determinations and *effect*\-determinations. To be sure, in this way the pleasant taste of a food is also apperceived — analogously to how the ringing of a thing (a struck bell) is apperceived. There do constitute themselves effect-determinations that grow out of empirical apperception (the associative apperception) of feeling-sensations and feeling-complexes — in that feelings get apprehended as *aroused* by the objects.

Husserl grants that there are indeed *empirical feeling-properties* of this adherent kind: just as a thing is now warm, now cold, now sounds and now does not, so it is now pleasant, now unpleasant, now arousing pleasure and now not. But from these *experience-apperceptions*, in which feeling-sensations function as empirical sensations (where "empirical sensations" includes not just colors but any experience that functions empirically — say, as "inner sensibility"), one must sharply distinguish *valuation-apperceptions*. The feelings of "pleasure" and "displeasure" are sensations like other sensations. Only the *acts* of liking or disliking bring what is essential for value-apperception. (He concedes parenthetically that even these acts can function empirically, in empirical apperception, as empirical "representatives" or sensations — psychologically and psychophysically.)

He elaborates: the feeling-colorations belonging to the lowest empirical sensations (the primary contents of "outer sensibility"), apperceived as a unity, may ground a liking or disliking *of the object*; or the object, considered with respect to feeling-determinations that accrue to it in certain experiential nexuses, may *arouse* liking. Liking can also ground itself on certain sides, certain "views" of the object, or on certain manners in which certain appearances run their course out of the object's encompassing manifold of appearances — so that it is not the object itself and primarily but rather such *views* of it that liking is referred to.

A new register opens: *immediate givenness of an intrinsic value*, *immediate givenness of a means-value as such*, *value-meaning*, *value-intentions*, and *value-fulfillments*. What does "value-intention" mean? Belief-intentions directed at value-being? No. One must not confuse unmodified intentions (*Impressionen*) with *Glaube*. (A footnote here flags an architectonic point: value-apperception is *also* an apperception, and apperception is not specifically a matter of "Gemüt" any more than of "Verstand"; apperception is "the root of everything logical," makes presentation possible in a certain sense; but the distinction between empirical apperception and value-apperception is a *fundamental* distinction *within* apperception.)

Husserl then sketches a typology of intuition: (a) empirical intuition (sensible intuition in the ordinary sense), e.g. empirical perception and memory; (b) value-intuition. On both sides we distinguish immanent and transient intuition. We speak of immanent perception and memory, directed at the immanent, at primary contents. Everything immanent can undergo empirical apprehension and there function as "sensation." To simple sensible intuition is opposed *categorial intuition* in the sense of the *Logical Investigations* — an affair of thinking; the categorial givennesses are think-givennesses, and thinking can of course refer in the same way to what is empirically given and to what is axiologically given.

He pushes the point with a concrete case. If on the basis of sensible intuition I judge "S is P\!", I can extract an object-about-which (the state of affairs as subject of further predications). If I have the judgment "S is P\!" before me, I can take *delight* in it (*ich kann mich dabei freuen*). I can then again, thinkingly, objectify: I can assert "*That S is P is delightful*." The state of affairs receives a feeling-predicate, and possibly this is an objectively valid value-predicate. But who is doing the objectivating here — the judgment, or the feeling-act? Thinking, judging, is not creative on its own, insofar as it presupposes a "presented" content that it grasps and forms. The form of predication and the predicate as such stem from thinking, but the *content* of the predicate does not. The object in the proper sense — that which is and is identical with itself, sometimes this and sometimes that — is the affair of thinking, but of thinking on the basis of a given *presentation* (or in relation to a presented). And presenting cannot be merely sensible. Is it not *the feeling* that, on the basis of a sensible presentation, accomplishes the *Wertnehmung*, the simple "value-meant-ness," and so makes the underlay for the thinking-objectivation?

Of belief-acts we say they have a relation to the objective. We say the same of feeling-acts. Is the speech in the same sense on both sides? What is "belief-act"? Judging in respect of its certainty? But mere belief *does not objectify*; it "posits" the meant state of affairs (footnote: "Belief needs an 'appearance.'"). In the case of perception, too, we speak of the modus of belief: there too belief does not objectify *as this moment* but rather posits the object — and the object-positing requires "matter," apperception. So it is also with the feeling-act, that is, with the *feeling-character* (*Gemütscharakter*), which is the *pendant and analogon* of belief. It bestows the "feeling-coloration," the pleasure-coloration, on a presented content. In the apperception of feeling the sensibly apperceived or categorial object is *clothed* with the feeling-predicate (or appears, stands there, in such characterization), and in the subsequent judgment the predicate "beautiful," "good," etc. is predicatively assigned to it.

But — Husserl steps back — he cannot quite *understand* this. The perceived stands there as actual (self-presently impressional) and as truly being. It pleases me. Is *this* pleasing to be treated on the same level as belief as the consciousness of true-being (of being in the sense of truth)? Or, when I judge "S is P\!" ("the weather is stormy") and *wish* "may S be P," do the character of true-being on the one side and that of wish on the other stand on quite the same footing? In judging "S is P\!" I can perform a transformation that converts the being-character, so to speak, into a predicate: "That S is P, is actually so," or "the judgment is true." Wishing that S be P, I can say "that S be P is wish-worthy," but also "that S may be P, that is right" or "the 'wish' is justified." That correctness here and there (correctness of the wish, correctness of the judgment) corresponds *exactly* — that is beyond doubt. But it stands differently with the *correspondence* of "belief" with wishing.

If we parallelize empirical apperception and feeling-apperception, the former, according to its matter, constitutes the *being-predicates* (the not-yet-thinkingly-grasped being-content of the object); the feeling-apperception constitutes the *not-yet-thinkingly-grasped value-content* of the object, its value-properties. What then is empirical apperception? It is nothing but the whole content of empirical intuition (perception or memory) and of empirical presentation in general, just abstracted from the modus of belief, of doubt, and so on. And so feeling-apperception, too, is the feeling-act itself (as built in its concretion on empirical apperception and empirical thinking), just abstracted from its modus — and there are parallel modi here as well: wish-certainty, wish-inclination, and so on. Now it was said above that the empirical object, the state of affairs, receives a feeling-coloration. The object stands there as delightful or undelightful. The thought state of affairs stands there as ought-to-be.

On the other hand, *belief* does *not* color. Color colors; color is a determination of the objective, belongs to its content; the color-meaning or \-appearance is a constituent of the object-apperception. Belief or unbelief is a mere "modus." Must we then say that pleasure and belief do *not* stand on the same level? Feeling-predicates arise through the fact that a *new* apperception constitutes the feeling-coloration of the object. And yet doubts remain. "Actual-being" does not stand on the same level as "delightful." But do not *being* and *ought-to-be* stand on one level? Is pleasure and wishing therefore not to be separated? Or pleasure as valuing and pleasure as agreeable-experiencing, taking-delight, etc.?

The section closes with an architectonic catalogue. There are objectivations with the modi of belief, and objectivation presupposes some modus and a *what* — a "matter." On this *what*, which we call apperception (apprehension), now bear fundamental distinctions: (1) the distinctions of empirical-sensible, "theoretical," and "feeling-" apperceptions; (2) the distinctions of *intuitivity* (intuitive, imperfectly intuitive, non-intuitive empty apperceptions); (3) within intuition, the distinctions between *simple* and *non-simple* — simple: empirical intuitions (sensible in the ordinary sense), empirical sensations with possible transient interpretations built on them, saturations of these interpretations corresponding to the distinctions of immanence and transcendence; on the other hand, feeling-intuitions, feeling-sensations and feeling-interpretations, the one as immanent colorations of sensible sensations, the other as transient feeling-colorations; non-simple: categorial empirical intuitions, feeling-syntheses of preferring (?), categorial structures referred to sensible or feeling-intuition, sensibly-empirical "theoretical" predicates — feeling-predicates; (4) distinctions of immanent and transient apperception.

A worry surfaces at the end: what really *are* "theoretical" apperceptions? Does not the specifically theoretical lie in the *apophantically categorial* — in the functions of synthesis (meaning and its categorial formations)? Then we should not speak of "theoretical" but of *sensible* or *empirical apperceptions* and *feeling-apperceptions*. ("Sensible" here covers both inner and outer sensibility.) Hence we must carefully distinguish the simple immanent feeling-moment (and in general the "sensed" feeling that can undergo sensible apperception — e.g. in the formation of empirical predicates of objects like "awakening pleasure," "arousing pleasure in us," and in this sense charming, attractive) from *actual value-apperception*. The "value" refers necessarily to *contexts of value-motivation*; it constitutes itself only therein. Feeling-apperceptions necessarily presuppose empirically sensible ones — they are founded in them. Empirical objectivity receives "colorations"; it gets apperceived in feeling-fashion (but not again empirically). Here is the domain of *Gemütsmotivationen*. Associative motivation lies, as belonging to the empirical object, *at the basis* of the valuing of this object. But even though the apperception of feeling rests upon associative motivation and is determined by it, feeling-motivations are *not* to be reduced to empirical-associative ones. (Corresponding to this is the distinction of ontologies: apart from the ontology of the immanent we have the ontology of "real" being, of being in the narrower sense of οὐσία, and the ontology of *value-being*.)

The section thus accomplishes three things at once. It *parallelizes* feeling and intellect (Wertnehmung beside Wahrnehmung) but with a crucial asymmetry: belief is a mere modus that does not color, whereas feeling-apperception genuinely *colors* the object with a value-predicate; yet, on reflection, the parallel may need to be redrawn as one not between belief and feeling but between *being* (Sein) and *ought-to-be* (Sein-sollen). It introduces the two ontologies — *ousiology* and *axiology* — that will run as the great architectonic counterpoint of the work. And it leaves Husserl's own position openly aporetic: he has not yet decided whether feeling- and belief-acts stand on the *same* level, and the problem will continue to drive the next sections.

### **§ 3\. Willing Is Not Value-Apperception. The Evaluability of Willing**

Husserl now turns to the question whether the *will* fits into the schema developed so far. His answer is unambiguous: the will is no apperception, just as little as "belief" and its modi. In valuing, I see the object as valuable, and in willing — only then *can* I will — a presented content stands there as valuable. But the willing does not give it a *predicate*. Or should we say it gives the object the predicate of "ought-to-be" (*Seinsollens*)? That is more accurate: rather, it is *that the object be* (which is presented or thought as not-being) that is the *ought-to-be*. That the object should be — *that* is here the value, the "what would be beautiful." But the willing itself does not give it the "coloration"; the *valuing* does.

Of course, willing too can be drawn into apperceptions — for example in the perception of action. But the action is not an objectivity that stands on the same level as "value." Action is, first of all, a certain natural process (running its course, e.g., in perception). But it is more: this process runs *willingly* in a continuous *fiat*. As against the mere process — the mere becoming in the empirical sense — there is constituted "effecting" (*Wirken*), the creative becoming, that is, action and its end-result, the work. This is a distinctive objectity, just as the apperception belonging to it is a distinctive apperception. But it is not a value-apperception. It can underlie a value-apperception and value-judgment, as the mere empirical apperception and empirical thing-positing can. But the question "is this a deed, an action, a work?" is different from the question "is the thing good, valuable, is the deed a good one?" Alongside the thing-objectivity that constitutes itself in empirical apperception there constitute themselves yet other objectivities (founded *in* but not produced *by* empirical apperception), and among them the value-objectivity. From the "feeling-sphere" arise *new transient predicates of pre-given (pre-constituted) objects* — predicates representing a transience of a peculiar type.

Husserl revisits the question: willing as such, he had said, is not valuing — it is not value-constituting. Is willing then a *modus* of valuing apperception? But should the will be coordinated with the modi in which apperceptions must be outfitted to concretion — the modi of belief? That cannot be done. Into this series the will manifestly does not belong. It belongs rather in a series with *wishing*, *desiring*, *fleeing*, and equally *saturated joy* and its opposite, *grief*.

But — does there not lie a pleasure, a valuing, in joy? And likewise everywhere, in wishing, in fleeing? Certainly, but the wish *as such* brings something new with it. In the wish the wished-for is not "colored," it is not "perceived" in a wish-coloration nor is it to-be-perceived in such. The wished-for would be "perceived" in this sense as value, *if* the same — the wished-for matter — were given "in" its value, that is, were standing there in its appraising valuation. So should we say: wish and will, and likewise joy, belong together, and they line up alongside the complex of belief-modi? But how should we carry that out?

Husserl now turns the analysis self-reflexively. The will can be judged as good and beautiful — it itself is subject to valuation. So too can belief be judged as a value: it is valuable or worthless. Belief is to be ratified as right insofar as it is directed at truth. Is a belief that hits the truth not a *value*? (It is *not* the truth itself, *as such* — i.e., the existing state of affairs — that is a value.) A determinate state of affairs *can* be a value, but not the existing-state-of-affairs as such. Rather, it is the *evident having-been-given* of the state of affairs, and the right judging that is to be brought into such intuition, that is a value. The will that hits a value is itself a value. A personality whose willing is habitually directed at values in the sense of the "highest practical good" is a value. All human willings can be ordered into a system of will in which, as a whole, the best possible, the most valuable in a human community, is realized.

The editors note in a footnote that beside the next stretch of text Husserl wrote a "0" with the marginal note: "no continuation. No coherent treatment any more\!" — a candid editorial signal that the manuscript trails off. The text continues: leaving-undecided, doubting, suspecting Husserl had intended to grasp under the title "belief-modi"; naturally also questioning. How is it with *acknowledging* and *rejecting*, with *approving* and *disapproving*, with "valuing as true and false, as good and bad"? An action I value as good. Whether I "see" it, or whether I "value it in presentation," I "see" that it is a "right" action, a rational one, and value it as rational and as good (as is every rational one). Just so I value a judgment as true. I judge that the judgment is right and value it as a good (every "right" counts for me here as good).

The architectural yield of § 3 is the firm placement of the will *outside* the line of value-apperception and *outside* the modi of belief: the will belongs in the family of wish, desire, fleeing, joy, grief — but, as the next sections will show, with its own twist, since the will is *evaluable* without thereby being a value-apperception. The will is evaluable because of its directedness; but its directedness is on *Seinsollen*, on *ought-to-be*, and the schema of "value-coloration" the *valuing* gives the object cannot be transferred to the will-modus. This sets up Beilage II, where Husserl will ask whether there are any *spontaneous* feeling-acts at all — i.e., feeling-acts that are not founded in but *coordinate with* theoretically determining thinking-acts.

### **§ 4\. Feeling-Motivation in Distinction from Empirical-Associative Motivation**

Husserl opens with a taxonomy: "acts" (meanings, intentional acts) refer to (are directed at) objects. Among them: believing, suspecting, doubting, questioning, liking, taking-joy, wishing, willing, and their possible negatives. Perceiving and remembering belong, on one side, with judging as predicating, predicative questioning, suspecting — these all belong in the first row: by their general modus they are belief etc., only the "matter" is different. Objects appear, are presented, are thought. They can be considered "theoretically," determined as to essence or actuality, and scientifically explained. Then there is the *feeling-comportment* (*Gemütsverhalten*) toward presented (appearing, thought) objectivities in the broadest sense — and here it is *indifferent* whether the objective constitutes itself on the basis of an empirical or a feeling-apperception. Here the objects are *not determined*; insofar as no willing comportment as in cognition is leading, *correctness of judgment*, *grounding*, are not the aim. (In other words: in voluntary cognition the will is directed at the value of right and grounded judging — but that is not the only value.) Rather: the objects please or displease, in themselves or for the sake of something else. They please when they please *in themselves* — for the sake of this or that of their objective determinations or for the sake of their mode of appearance, or because they can be brought to such a primarily pleasing appearance. Objects are *objects of joy*: joy over the being of objects, over the holding of states of affairs, etc. Wishes "go to" objectivities. Wishes are directed at the being, e.g., of things, at the occurrence of processes, at the showing-itself of cognitions, at the eventuality that in theoretical thinking a theory of just this form may turn out to be true, etc. Willings are directed at *realization*: at the production of things from things, at the carrying out of bodily movements, etc.

The fundamental acts — so called because all other acts presuppose them as "foundations" — are the apperceptions, accomplished in the modus of belief or in one of the various coordinate modi (unbelief, doubt, etc.). As for the other "acts" — feelings, desirings, willings — they can be drawn into experience-apperceptions. In a certain way every act can be drawn in — even perceiving and judging, when I say "I perceive," "I judge that …."

Husserl works through a worry. He had said: in believing, the believed does not stand there in a "belief-coloration"; it stands there in the sense of the corresponding object-apperception. One could say that fiction stands there differently, "appears differently to me," than an object held to be actual (e.g., the *Rohns*, a Göttingen inn near the Hainberg — the editors' charming aside). But strictly speaking, the object does not appear in different determinations, in different objective characters that would then appear as predicates of it.

Now: if an object pleases me, if I regard it with pleasure, then it has the agreeable feeling-coloration. The pleasure is referred to the object — therein lies its "intentionality." It is not an arbitrary, newly-arising consciousness-moment but is referred to the (transient) object that stands there as pleasing. Husserl gives a beautifully concrete example: a noble horse pleases me — its form in its beautiful lines, the expression of energy in the eye, the grace and force of the movements. Of the experiential object and the experiential determinations underlying the appraisal, only some are given, and not adequately given; but everything is empirically apperceived, and on this rest the feeling-moments and feeling-intentions which, in their connection, in their harmonious unity, *found* the pleasure. These intentions would harmoniously fulfill themselves in a fitting course of empirical appearances of the horse, as far as they are not actually fulfilled in the partial actual course of the perceptions I have of the horse. Here we have, on the one hand, experience-connections (associative ones) and experience-motivations. But not only such. Alongside the motivations belonging to the constitution of the thing — first to the inner space-filling, primarily thing-constituting properties, then to the adherent, the effect-properties — we have the *feeling-characters*. Husserl is precise: he does *not* mean the "feeling-effects" of the object. Insofar as I apprehend them — and I can perform such an effect-apperception at any time — I have nothing new, or rather I have only something *secondary*.

He elaborates the contrast. The thing arouses warmth in me, arouses pleasure in me, etc. It is not just a matter of memories of feelings being awakened alongside actual feelings (which are experiences), or of indications being there of feelings that would actually set in if such-and-such appearance-changes occurred. *Those* would be "aroused associations," experience-intentions; insofar as they appear in the total apperception, they constitute sensible properties — *empirical feeling-properties as effect-properties*. To that extent, feelings would be there *in the function of empirical sensations* (sensible representatives). So feelings *can* function. They do, for example, when I follow the various situations of the objective, attend to the various appearances, and address the feeling-effects to be hoped for under those situations as effect-properties of the thing. But that is *not* their function when we live in the pleasing.

The example is sharpened. The play of the horse's expression is an indication of its energy, which I empathize into the horse. This energy is the condition for valued performances — in a race, say. But the play of expression does not work *merely* as an indication of something else that pleases — or, better: in the play of expression I do not see merely the energy, and the energy does not merely "remind" of performances which, were they present, would please. Rather, the play of expression and the energy *themselves please* for the sake of this connection of remembrance. The slender legs and the proportions of the build please as indications of swiftness. Swiftness I value — it pleases me as actual performance. But the *indication* of possible swiftness pleases itself and as such. The whole animal as it appears to me is a system of indications for various possible pleasingnesses, but is itself pleasing for the sake of them. And the individual pleasing thing motivates, in this system that is a *harmony*, a higher pleasing. *This is no mere association; here a new motivation arises.*

Now, one might say: living in the pleasing, the object — the horse — appears, and it pleases. That does not mean that a system of pleasure-motivations becomes somehow objective. To be sure: as the horse pleases me, the various lines of empirical association are aroused, the tendencies toward the appearance-manifolds that would awaken pleasing of such-and-such kind. And with these empirical tendencies, with the empirical intentions, are interwoven *pleasure-intentions*. But this is not to be understood as if I had presentations of appearances I do not have but might possibly have. I have just *this* perception or perceptual continuity. The horse stands there from this side, then from such-and-such altered sides. And in this stick manifold "experience-intentions." Now likewise on the basis of this perception I have an actual feeling, the actual pleasing, and in *it* stick manifold pleasure-intentions. *As perception, so the pleasing too has its transcendence.* As the perception-intentions united in the actual perception, so the pleasure-intentions united in the actual pleasing point to *possible fulfillments*, on both sides not just the singular ones but, in their harmonious interweavings, the complexes — and all this in accordance with the manner of the connection and the course. The fulfillments are *new acts* and are *mere act-possibilities*. They are not presented. What is presented is simply the horse.

A reproductive presentation of the horse in such-and-such situations — in the race, etc. — may possibly arise. To these presented situations there attaches a "pleasing in presentation": they stand there as pleasing analogously to how the now-actually-perceived horse stands there as pleasing. Here as there the pleasing as such is *not* objective — just as little as the perceiving as such is, or empirical acts as such are. But on both sides reproduced appearance, or imaging possible appearance (horse in the race), and corresponding feeling-coloration do not stand isolated, without connection to the actual horse-appearance in perception and its feeling-character. They are *motivated* appearances; they belong to the object that is the just-perceived object. The pleasure-characters, too, belong to this object — to the "horse in the race," the object in this situation. But that is "the same" horse I now perceive, only thought in a possible and motivated situation.

The pleasure-characters are, to be sure, pleasure-characters "in fantasy," and the pleasure-acts are pleasure-acts of a *modified* kind. On the other hand, it is not as if I were thinking that something pleases me — namely, what is fantasy-presented (what is actual through illustrative presentification) — but that "in truth" does not please me at all. There *is* therefore an in-truth pleasing in fantasy. The pleasing is really and properly grounded in the fantasy-presenting and refers "properly" to the fantasy-object; and the fantasy-object is not "merely a fantasy-product" but presents to me what is now perceived in a possible situation that is motivated as a real possibility. The pleasing belonging to it has, for its part, "grounding," motivating relation to the actual pleasing on the basis of the perception.

But — Husserl presses the question — if the object pleases me, does it not stand there *as pleasing*, *as beautiful*, *as agreeable*, without further ado? Does it require a new apperception? Is it not the case that not only the object that pleases stands there before my eyes, but, without further ado, *the object as pleasing*? It is, so to speak, clothed with a pleasingness-character, and indeed with regard to certain pleasing moments. In what then could the doubt consist? I do not require a new "objectivating" act and a new objectivating apprehension, as when I move from mere perception to the apprehension "I see the object and I see it just in this mode of appearance, etc., and I feel pleasure, and the object arouses pleasure in me." But in plain empirical perceiving and pleasing I already have *the double apperception*.

Husserl admits the cautious version: even granting that a *feeling* could occur without already being so related to the otherwise appearing object as to lend it a pleasingness-character (in three editorial footnotes Husserl refines this — feeling without a meaning that takes hold of the pleasing object; the question whether such a feeling must be founded in presentations; and so on) — granted all that, *one thing is sure*: wherever we speak of values and wherever we speak determinately of joy-over, of grief-over, we have the *appearing* or otherwise presented object in a corresponding character. The object stands there as agreeable, nice, charming, pretty, etc., or as repulsive, boring, ugly — a state of affairs as delightful or undelightful etc. (Although in joy and grief, as in wish and will, this "appearing" is to be understood differently.) In all such "valuing," then, a *new apperception* seems to come in, namely in accordance with a new motivation, the *Gemütsmotivation*, to which the "for-the-sake-of" (*umwillen*) belongs. It is not, in the manifold of appearances and within the moments of each appearance, that one points to another merely empirically (associatively); rather, a new motivation arises, and motivation is also what links each pleasure-moment with its objective underlay.

He states the central thesis in italics: the feeling-character does *not* belong to its objective the way warm belongs to a warm body or sound to a sounding object — i.e., merely empirically — but belongs to it through *a peculiar relation of grounding, phanseologically through a peculiar motivation*. The presentation-underlay motivates the feeling quite differently from the way another presentation motivates. It is something completely new, this peculiar way in which, in the course of sensible appearances, through a certain layer of these, a *feeling-course* is motivated and a *feeling-unity* is constituted. The object has its value insofar as it belongs into certain real or possible experience-connections, and insofar as to the appearance-manifolds constituting these there belong certain *privileged appearance-possibilities* that constitute determinate feeling-unities. When I consider the object as it is, I move within the appearances belonging together, and I mean now this, now that objective moment, following its unity in the appearance-course; apart from the actual appearances I can pursue the unity in possible motivated (associative) appearances and then, thinking further, determine the moment as feature, as property, and so determine the object in various directions. But when I live in *valuing*, certain appearance-connections and appearance-possibilities, and corresponding thought-possibilities, are *privileged*; only in them do I move meaningly, in order to bring the unity of the feeling-character — i.e., the unity of the value-predicate — to givenness. It is now no longer the unity of the object as such that matters, with its features and the determination of these features, but only what is *founding* for the value, and as far as it is so.

The value-predicate, then, is nothing that constitutes itself in the empirical connection as such, nor anything sensibly-naturally-effected (as a psychic state caused in me, which has a quite different unity from the value-unity — the latter being a unity that cuts through manifold possible feeling-states and is only manifested in them). Rather: on an empirical object-consciousness there grounds itself a *valuing consciousness*, which is *not* united with that empirical consciousness in the way an empirical object-consciousness is united with another empirical object-consciousness — rather, in a *completely new way*. And this valuing consciousness, founded in the empirical, carries within itself a new unity, which can be referred to the empirical object as a new kind of predicate that we call *value*. The value belongs to the object: the empirical object-consciousness *founds* a value-consciousness that can find its intentional fulfillment, in such a way that a determinate unfolding of the object-consciousness is the steady foundation for a determinately motivated nexus of a unity-giving value-consciousness — which fulfills the originally founded feeling-intention. This fulfillment is *Gemütserfüllung*, fulfillment in valuing.

In the logical apperception — the predicative synthesis and verbal conception that makes the value a predicate of an assertion — the fulfillment functions at the same time as *confirmation* (*Bestätigung*). The logical intention "directs" itself co-toward the *Gemütserfüllung*; if the fulfillment sets in, the objective value-intention is *confirmed as true*. If one asks how the logical-predicative intention makes the value a predicate, the answer is: first the value is attended to. As I can attend to the *form* of an object, I can equally attend to the beauty that accrues to it on the basis of the form and other determinations. The whole empirical-axiological apperception in its complication can be ensouled in various ways by a meaning; the noticing, attending, and meaning-out can be active here in various directions, as it can already be in the lower level of mere empirical apperception. (Husserl notes — in a long footnote later supplemented in 1910 — that this *meaning* is what is *properly objectifying*, in both the empirical and the non-empirical sphere; the bare thing-apperception is itself not yet objectivation and stands fully on a par with the axiological apperception. The meaning can be simple or synthetic, and all forms of synthesis belong to it, while the matter stems from the apperceptions and, with that, the special relations: empirical relations, value-relation, and relation between empirical object and value. "Meaning" is a positing, a "believing" and so belongs to a peculiar series.) I can give priority to various properties (before their conceptual grasp). The whole is of course meant in a certain way, but in this whole the corresponding objective moment is meant. So too when we add the axiological apperception. What is meant is the whole empirical object — or rather the empirical-axiological object, as it were — but through the meaning the apperception-layers that are matters of valuing are now privileged: the empirical layers underlying the valuing (grounding it in the special sense), and the valuing apperception itself. And with that, the *value of the object* — value because of such-and-such moments or empirical connections — is what is meant.

The closing thesis: only in feeling-acts can the *sense of value-predicates* be found, but feeling-acts alone do not objectivate. (That can mean only that to objectifying belongs *meaning* and *thinking determination*.) A value is given and judgmentally set in an evidence, and this evidence is, not as evidence but by virtue of its content, of its *sense*, different from other evidences — from non-axiological ones. Evidence, apart from judgment, is a *givenness-consciousness*, and *givenness-consciousness is everywhere generically the same*. We can also say: a value is intuited, is perceived. (In general value is a transcendent predicate and as such refers to empirical thingnesses. But immanent valuation is also possible, e.g., the valuation of a beautiful sequence of tones: it is beautiful.)

The architectonic of § 4 is now fully visible. Husserl has shown (i) that feeling-comportment is not aimed at *determination* but at *pleasing/displeasing*; (ii) that feelings can either function as *empirical sensations* (in the apperception of effect-properties of the thing) or function in their own register, where they live in pleasing; (iii) that the latter presupposes a *new motivation*, not reducible to associative empirical motivation, and centered on the "for-the-sake-of"; (iv) that this *Gemütsmotivation* founds a *value-consciousness* with its own unity and its own kind of fulfillment, distinct from but parallel to perceptual fulfillment; and (v) that the value-predicate emerges not from feeling alone but from the meaningly-directed thinking that operates *upon* the feeling-apperception, which alone supplies the matter. This is the core constructive position of Hua XLIII/2: feeling has its own apperception with its own motivational structure, but objectivation requires the cooperation of meaning-thinking; value-predicates are the *thinkable correlates* of value-apperceptions, just as being-predicates are the thinkable correlates of empirical apperceptions.

### **§ 5\. Valuing as an Act Founded in Perceiving. Fulfillment of the Value-Meaning. Immediate and Mediate Values**

The editors flag this section as "fundamental" in a footnote — "concerning the doctrine of the constitution of real 'empirical' values through transient value-appearances." Husserl reopens the question: what *are* feeling-acts? Are they not, perhaps, *Gemütsapperzeptionen*? He turns to a pivotal example: a cigar. He performs a pleasing-act on a cigar; he immerses himself in it and brings to clarity, going beyond the simple pleasing, *what* it is in the cigar that pleases him and *why* it pleases him. He imagines smoking the cigar. He "as it were" senses the smell and taste, a certain animation of his train of thought and his mood. And not only does he "as it were" perceive this — he also perceives the *noble smell*, the *beautiful taste*, the *agreeable feeling* of the free, cheerful mood. All this belongs to the cigar (so far as he apprehends it) — that it is suited to smoking, that under the circumstances of actual smoking it has such-and-such taste-properties, characterized in such-and-such ways feeling-wise. The cigar pleases him *for the sake of* this; better: *for the sake of this* it is *valuable* for him, is a *beautiful*, a *good* thing. It is so for the sake of these *real possibilities*.

The actual pleasure with which he now considers the cigar, and the actual empirical perception he has of it, contains nothing of all this. But just as in sensible perceiving manifold "intentions" are enclosed that "point to" "perceptual possibilities," so also in sensible-and-feeling-fashioned apperceiving certain *feeling-possibilities* are enclosed: there are "indications" of such-and-such empirical-sensible appearance-connections and of the feelings and feeling-connections founded in them. He has the sensible perception now: the sensible sensation-material in the complex of partly properly perceptive, partly improper intentions. In this example, no feelings hang on the properly perceptive content; feeling-intentions belong to the *improper* intentions, in such a way that their realization (fulfillment) leads to appearance-series that in a certain way *carry phansic feelings*. These too have their intentional unification. But now he has not merely the perception with all these sensual and feeling-intentional components — he has not merely the perception-apperception which presents the cigar at once as the spatial thing and as the thing capable of being smoked, smelling, tasting in such-and-such ways, *and also* as the *good-tasting* one — rather, he has a *valuing of the cigar as so perceived, as so apperceived*. This valuing is a *new act founded in perceiving* (an act-impression), in which this cigar "stands there" as a value, more precisely: is *meant* as a value. He values it, esteems it, performs an *appraisal* on the basis of that sensible-feeling apperception, namely perception.

He elaborates: he performs an appraisal — the cigar pleases him; a pleasure-act is performed on the basis of that apperception. But the cigar now stands there as *valuable*. The pleasure as pleasing-in is a *value-meaning*, and so the object stands there not only as that as which it appears and is set in mere perception (perception in the wider sense, that includes feeling-properties — pleasant taste, etc.), but, by virtue of the *actual* pleasing, it stands there *as valuable* (actual having-pleasing is nothing other than consciousness of a pleasing-thing). But this *standing-there* is not a perceiving, not a second-stage perceiving. The value is *not* "given," and in no way given; it is not as in perceiving where the object, even if only imperfectly, one-sidedly, perhaps only as to visual spatial determinations of one side, *is given*. And this concerns both the object itself and any determination of it.

Husserl sharpens the negative thesis: one must *not* say that the perceived object — *that* is what has value — is what is given, while the value itself is not given but only *improperly co-perceived*, like so many other determinations of the object that are co-meant but not properly perceived. For one must mark: co-meant, improperly co-perceived moments of the perceived as such come *to givenness* in *new* perceptions belonging to the fulfillment-series of the perception just performed. But the value of the cigar does *not* come to givenness perceptively (not "perceptively" in the sense of sensible sensation-display; but, an editorial footnote crucially adds, *yet* "perceptively" insofar as the addition of feeling-motivation constitutes a *founded objectity that has its "perception"*).

When I move perceptively from appearance to appearance, I do not arrive at an appearance that *brings* the value; rather, the pleasing I perform — the appraising — *manifests itself* in the transition from appearance to appearance, namely in the fitting selection of appearances belonging to the object, in the realization of those appearances that bring to givenness the founding (so to speak motivating) feeling-properties of the object. So the appraisal turns out to be "justified" and the object really has the value. It has not only really the corresponding properties — pleasant taste, etc. (these *ground* its value but are not themselves its value) — but the pleasing-in (the special pleasingness that is co-meant in the now-to-be-tested appraisal) is, because the founding properties are exhibited as present, in turn exhibited as justified; the value really accrues to the object because it has the properties that are supposed to be value-grounding. The value is *demonstrated*; the appraisal is justified and "evidently" justified. But it does not have the character of a *perceiving*, let alone of *thing-perceiving*\! It is *an act of higher level*. (A footnote here rightly demands a fundamental distinction between *perceiving as givenness-consciousness in a sensible apperception* and *perceiving as givenness-consciousness in general*, e.g., in a feeling-apperception.)

The simplest pleasing-acts are the immanent ones: he sees, say, an immanent red expanse of a quality that grounds a pleasing. *This* red is "in itself beautiful." This is a simple pleasing and one that has the character of *sensing* and "perceiving." The red is given to him, sensibly given, and its pleasingness, its beauty, is just as simply given to him; all in all, it stands simply as *pleasing red*. The matter is no longer so simple if he takes an *oculomotor* red. This is already an empirical unity, and there corresponds to it an empirical feeling-unity: the pleasing red exhibits itself in the oculomotor process of looking around, in which all sensible and feeling-intentions fulfill themselves at once and pass into one another in unifying way, constituting unity. Here we already have a transient (though fully intuitive) sensible-and-feeling perception. The new thing the higher pleasing brings (which we have here always called *appraisal*, *valuing*) — is that something else than a valuing of the object *for the sake of the immediate or otherwise immediate beauties* belonging to certain of its properties?

If he savors the cigar, the noble Havana taste is beautiful, "noble" in itself — and this is *immediately perceived*. The cigar itself, when he now appraises it (designates it as good if he expresses it in words), is not merely *meant* as good but he "sees," as it were, *that* it is good *as having the noble taste*, and this "seeing" *fulfills* the appraisal of the cigar as good when he is not actually savoring it. But the way in which this *mediate* pleasingness, this mediate beauty *for the sake of* the beauty of the property, accrues to the thing is essentially different from the way the property itself accrues to it. And here we have *not* a "value"-property "perceived" *of* the object as a proper property like red. In this sense the pleasant taste *is* a property; and even though the beauty of the taste does not belong to the object as a self-standing property, it belongs to it as a component of a complex property. Yet that too is not exact. Properly speaking, only the *taste* is perceived (sensibly perceived as a thingly property), and *directly* on it given, as founded in it, the pleasingness of the taste, and now the object itself in a new way, in higher level, in the motivation of the *for-the-sake-of*, as "good" — *value-perceived in a value-perception*, as the editor-footnote rightly says.

Husserl now considers the case where one *actually* values an object *without* having the properties for the sake of which it is valuable given in their beauty, and so without intuiting the proper value-grounding. Does the actual valuing one performs there relate to the actual valuing that builds up properly on the value-grounds in their givenness analogously to how a *perceptual meaning* with respect to non-appearing determinations relates to the *fulfilling perception* in which the merely co-meant determinations are really given perceptively? Yes and no. There presses urgently a need to distinguish sharply between *co-meant-nesses* that one must *essentially* count as belonging to transcendent thing-perception (without which the thing-object could not stand there as *given*) and *other* co-meant-nesses, which yet should not have the character of *thought*\-meant-nesses, of thoughts. (Footnote: "Co-meaning of properties of the type of *capacities* in a perception: such co-meaning is itself not proper perceptual content.")

Perceiving in the proper sense is a *self-grasping, allegedly self-grasping* meaning, and indeed of an individual temporal object and with respect to its temporal fullness — that is, an alleged grasping of a self-presence. The capacity of an empirical object, e.g., a physical thing, *to be able to perform certain functions under certain circumstances*, I can co-mean in unity with the perception of the object, but this co-meaning does not itself belong *to* the perceiving. What does "capacity" mean — what does "to be able to perform under certain circumstances" generally mean? Therein lies: such-and-such circumstances assumed, such-and-such success "must" or will set in.

The example continues: he perceives the cigar, and there immediately attaches the presentation "I light it," and given this assumption, it will burn, and if he smokes, will develop the well-known taste, etc. All this in such a way that he need perform no conceptual thinking at all, and in general such conceptual thinking will not be present. This whole can run "intuitively"; only individual things may become clear, and yet *that is the meaning*. And this capacity of the cigar to be smokable and tastable belongs to it; it is a determination of the cigar and is something that can be co-meant at the sight of it. But *assumption and consequent positing* are not constituents of perceptions. The intuition that takes place here is not a merely sensible intuition of simple kind but already an act of higher level, lying on the line toward the categorial (proper synthetic "thinking").

When seeing the cigar he apprehends it as tasting such-and-such — e.g., a Sumatra as having a light, fleeting aroma, a Havana as "heavy" — this apprehension is no longer a matter of perception. And when it confirms itself first in the clear properization on the basis of the intuition (a kind of memory) and then through actual smoking, the confirming act is no perception but an act of higher level grounded in perception. He can perceive only the lighting, smoking, and tasting; not that, under the assumption of lighting and "drawing," smoke develops and taste of such-and-such kind sets in (must) — though Husserl himself notes in a long footnote that this is only useful as an aporia, since every thing-perception leads back to "assumption and consequent positing"; effect-properties and physical properties are essential to a thing, and one would not be able to speak of thing-perception if their exhibition were not also called perception. Thing-perception, then, has its *levels*; we are led to acts of higher level. So one must distinguish *thing-perception* (matter-perception) with its various levels and *the perception of the higher objectivity*: thing-value, good, use-object, etc.

This naturally transfers to the value-meaning that attaches to the perception of the cigar and the co-meaning of its capacity to develop a certain pleasant taste. Here a pleasing-in "the taste to be developed under circumstances" enters into co-meaning, namely as pleasing under the circumstances and their assumption; and naturally, just as this "under assumption" — and every property of the type of a capacity, and *a fortiori* the value — does not belong to the perceivablenesses in the proper sense (i.e., to thing-perceptions). (Editorial footnote: "All these foregoing important elaborations show that *transient values* constitute themselves through *transient apperceptions*, analogously to how thing-realities, real being, constitute themselves through thing-apperceptions; and the constitution of the transcendent value is *not* such that to every thing-appearance a valuation-moment is correlated in the value-consciousness, so that we have here in fact a new kind of unity that constitutes itself purely in the valuing on the ground of the thing-unity.")

Husserl gives the parallel example of a violin — its construction, its beautiful form, etc., as indications of the noble tone it would develop in playing. The value-meaning as actual *holding-as-valuable* confirms itself in actual playing. But this *self-confirming, self-fulfilling* is something quite different from the bare self-fulfilling of a simple empirical intention. First the positing-under-assumption confirms itself: under the assumption of playing, the sounding of well-known kind really sets in, and it is really a "noble tone." It confirms itself that under this assumption not only the *empirical consequence* but also *the pleasing founded in it* sets in. The latter confirms itself differently from the former — namely insofar as it is *co-posited* differently. The empirical consequence is the first thing. But the pleasing is founded in the perceiving of tone-peculiarities of certain kind, which as a perceiving of such an (immanent) content founds, properly and really, a pleasing, a finding-beautiful. This is not itself an empirical consequence but a *necessary co-consequence*. But of course, since it is one with the empirical, it is also empirically co-motivated: the beautiful tone is posited under assumption and confirms itself, in the case of the realization of this assumption, as actually setting in.

And now the value-positing too confirms itself, only in a *Wertnehmung* as *Wertwahrnehmung*. The violin as actually endowed with the property of producing this tone (this endowment having been confirmed) is *really worth*. The valuing pleasing is, to be sure, a momentary act, but the value belongs *to the object*; namely, to the essence of the whole state-of-affairs in which the object as so-constituted participates and for the sake of which it has the such-and-such value-grounding empirical-axiological properties belongs the value-predicate; correspondingly to the constituting acts of such-and-such essences (in which essentially such properties constitute themselves) belongs again essentially the *possibility of properly performable* pleasing. And again: the pleasing attached to the "improper" presentation, to the object-presentation and to improper "co-meaning," is "right" if these meanings are of such an essence and of such a factual ordering that *fulfillability* obtains.

The pleasing — i.e., the holding-as-valuable of the object as bearer of such empirical and axiological determinations — is *not yet* a judging-as-valuable in the predicative-conceptual sense. It is an act of founded apperception rooted in the *Gemütssphäre*. Such not-yet-conceptually-founded apperceptions, that have nothing to do with feeling, we find plenty of in the sphere of "intellect": he recognizes a picture as noble that he saw in the Uffizi; he sees a picture, and in memory another stands there that resembles it, and he grasps the resemblance — a whole *categorial* layer, yet apparently still independent of concept and word. (Husserl muses: but ultimately, does conceptual thinking not also belong on the same line? — yet not, insofar as it stands *opposite* all simple apperceptions, even founded ones.)

In the previous discussions Husserl had complicated cases in view, where things are valued for the sake of certain capacities, abilities for performances. But concrete things can also be valuable on a *simpler basis*: a thing is valuable for the sake of its beautiful form; a house is valuable for the sake of its beautiful façade. The value of the thing can be determined by such-and-such appearance-moments — by perceivable moments — to which a beauty-valuation attaches. The moments please *immediately*; they are immediately and through their own essence beautiful. But they are *moments* of the thing, and the thing has its value for the sake of the beautiful moments it can present in appearance. The beauty of the form will of course belong not merely to a single sight but to manifold beautiful sights that, in the manner of their course, can ground a *higher beauty* — which is uniformly apprehended as the beauty "of" the form. Likewise the coloration of a fabric can be splendid in individual fold-formations, but every turning of the sight can yield new beauty-moments for the unifying beauty-valuation that combines all these moments to the unity of *one* beauty. The object is, however, a concrete value *for the sake of* this property, this beautiful property.

Every thing, every concrete object is valued and is a value for the sake of certain belonging-to-it properties or modes-of-appearance, and ultimately we are led back to *immanent* valuations of intuitive moments and to *unity*\-valuations of intuitive unities of continuously interpenetrating, continuously connected moments. With this, Husserl says, we have studied a determinate concept of value, one that constitutes itself in the valuation of *concrete transient objects* (perceivable objects). In this concept there essentially sticks an axiological motivation of the *for-the-sake-of* type. It therefore presupposes another value-concept. *Primary* values are *immanent* values — values that hang directly on something immanent. Likewise *unity-values* — values that constitute themselves as value-unities on the basis of simple unities (which as such can come to unfolding in simple intuition). These too as *immediate* values; these are already *second* values, but not *mediate* values.

*Mediate* values are concrete (transient) thing-values, values of transient things for the sake of certain value-unities that attach to some property-unity, and values of things for the sake of certain capacities. (Goods-values lead already into the will-domain, of which there is here as yet no talk.) In the lectures (the editors note: probably the 1908/09 ethics lectures, Hua XXVIII) Husserl had spoken of *beauty-values*, but he lacks fitting names. "Concrete thing-values" — but he cannot speak of "property-values," since it is not the property as such that is in question but the *content* of the property. Should we say "content-values"? Or simply: simple values and founded values, primitive and derived?

In any case, the distinction stands: things as values, and the axiological predicate, the predicate of value-being of things — *not* to be confused with what one might naturally also call value-predicate, namely the determinate predicates of the thing that ground the value of the thing for the sake of which it is value. These latter, in case of demonstrability, are again led back to immanent and unity-values, which are foundational for all *mediacy* in the value-holding.

To valuing belongs immediately *preferring*. A pleases me (I value A); B pleases me — *A pleases me more*. The two value-meanings *found* a preference, whose correlate is the relative priority of A over B. The underlying pleasing-acts can be "evident" or not; they can be in need of appraisal or not. And the same goes correspondingly for preferring. According to Brentano the act of preferring is a *Gemütsakt*. Naturally the preferring belongs to the feeling-sphere insofar as it is a unifying meaning founded in the simple pleasing-acts, necessarily so, in the strict sense. But should that "belonging-to-the-feeling-sphere" mean more? Naturally a perception-act that directs itself at the two value-acts unifies them quite differently — unifies primarily the perceptions of them — and is not in principle founded in them. In principle, perception of two acts is everywhere the same, not just of two valuation-acts. But on the other hand, is not the preferring of a *probability* in principle the same preferring as this preferring of feeling? Is it not ultimately a *gradation-relation* that is grasped, here between values, there between probabilities? But of course the grasping presupposes one time the living in perceptions, another time the living in suspectings, the third time living in pleasing-acts\! And does *that* make essential differences?

The architectonic of § 5 is this: the cigar-and-violin examples ground the central distinction between *value-perception* (Wertnehmung as Wertwahrnehmung) and ordinary perceptual perception, by showing that the value-property is not "given" the way a side of a thing is given but is *exhibited* in the harmonious fulfillment of feeling-intentions during the realization of the appearance-conditions under which the founding sensible properties come to givenness. This permits Husserl to introduce the distinction between *immediate values* (immanent and unity values, which are immediately given) and *mediate values* (transient thing-values, which require a "for-the-sake-of" motivation). Preferring, as a value-act founded in two value-acts, is then placed in formal parallel with the comparison of probabilities — preserving Husserl's commitment to a robust isomorphism between intellectual and feeling reason.

### **§ 6\. Sensing and Apperceptive Objectivation in Acts of Perceiving and Pleasing**

Husserl now turns directly to the question pressed by §§ 1–5: are *Gemütsakte* themselves *objectivating acts*? He gives a careful exposition of what "objectivation" means. *Object* — the existing, the self-identical: identical "in presentation-consciousness." For the object stands there as the *one*, e.g., as the one thing in the manifold of "uniting" appearances, including in the transition of various kinds of intuition — perceptions, memories, fantasies, etc. The continuously-one becomes synthetically, in discursive and conceptual thinking, an *identified*. "Presentation-consciousness," then, can be understood as *unity-consciousness* in a double sense: as continuous unity-consciousness, and as a synthetic consciousness of the *identical* setting itself off against its background; though this latter is presumably not yet to be regarded *eo ipso* as conceptually predicating, so that *that* would still be a new step. *Presentation in the broadest sense is the act* (Husserl flags in a footnote his hesitation: "the act?\! But isn't every act, since in every act something is made conscious that can be made into an object-about-which, a presentation?") — that constitutes intentional *unity*, which as identical and distinct from others is determinable in logical thinking. Presentation has its correlate in the *object*; every object constitutes itself in presentations and, according to its category, in presentations of corresponding presentation-category.

How then does it stand with *value-objectivation*? To it belong determinations like "pleasing, agreeable, beautiful, valuable." Two layers: (1) underlying objectivation of the object that pleases; (2) the pleasing in it with its "new intentional mode of relating." But — Husserl recalls his own earlier formulation in the original sheets — "living in pleasing, I objectivate nothing." There he had only one *Objektität*, the one of the object. There would only come *afterward* a new objectivation that apprehends the object as either pleasing or as pleased-in, valuable. The one time the pleasing is referred to the *subject*, the other time the *object itself* would appear as the bearer of the pleasure-character. This character would be *nothing constituting itself in the empirical manifold as a unity* (an empirical unity); thus no *thingly* (no empirical) property of the empirical object. First the empirical object, ready-made as it were, and *this* is then pleasing. It has a feeling-character, an "adherent" determination, but not a physical one like the sounding of the object.

Husserl had also spoken of the object being given one time under such circumstances that the pleasingness-predicate accrues to it actually or directly — say, the apple in the eating; the other time *indirectly*: the apple as possible object of eating and thereby carrying a pleasing-character. If he apprehends the pleasing as *his* pleasing, as his state, then he says: the object is the object *of my* pleasing, i.e., it pleases me, exercises a pleasing-stimulus on me. If the relation to *the I* remains out of play, then the object itself stands there *as pleasing, agreeable, beautiful*. But it stands there as that *not merely on the basis of* the pleasing, but by virtue of an *observing, perceiving, beholding*. *These would be the objectivating acts*, which build themselves on the feeling-act, and only they would draw the feeling into the object-consciousness, as its stuff, and constitute the *pleasing object*. The object presented (perceived) in the underlying mere presentation is apprehended as *having this pleasingness-property*. Only thus, then, could *value* constitute itself — namely as value of the object, as predicate of it, and in fact not as the bare predicate "to be pleasing." (Footnote: and these stand parallel to "actually and truly being," if one pairs valuing acts with judgment-acts, being-positing acts.)

Husserl now says this conception still requires renewed reflection. What is the "intentional relation" of the pleasing to the presented object supposed to mean, if not that *in the pleasing as a consciousness the object is "meant" as pleasing*? But what does "meaning" say there, what does "consciousness" say? We have, of course, the differences in the *direction of meaning-out*. He gives a fine example: a real Havana or a beautiful old violin he can regard with corresponding reverence; meanwhile, however, some structure of the wood may interest him — what is that there, this stripe? — and while he still has pleasure, he can specifically live in *the underlying objectivation* of the pleasing, and *mean out* something special in the object as empirical object. Just as the whole object "stands there," but precisely *this* moment of the object — this structure of this part of the surface — is what is specifically meant, so the value-piece as such *still stands there*, the noble violin, although he is *not* living in the value-consciousness and (which is the same thing) not attending to the value-being. Naturally he can attend now to the value-being of the object, now to the fact that it pleases him or that the object stimulates him to valuing. Finally he can also say the object compels him to *judgment*, just as we likewise say the object arouses in us the corresponding appearance.

When we *refer* the act to the I and establish a relation between act, object, and I, the "act" *in relation to the object* is already presupposed — i.e., that the object stands there as so-and-so determined is *presupposed*. Likewise, when we refer the *pleasing of the object* to the *subject*, it is presupposed that the object stands there *as pleasing*. In phenomenological reflection we then have the object-appearance and *the plus of the pleasing*, which builds itself on the object-appearance. And as object-appearance is in itself referred to the object — insofar as the determinate change of "stance" is what lets the *look-at* the appearance proceed *out of the appearance-consciousness itself* — so the pleasing is *in itself* "consciousness" of the *pleasing as such*, and the same change of stance (the reflective look at the pleasing) makes the pleasing into an object, while previously a special look at the pleasing was *not* required, because the pleasing *itself* is consciousness of the pleasing. (Footnote: "Yes — but here is the question: is the pleasing-attention already an *Gegenstandssetzung*, already a *Gewahrung* in the sense of being-taking?") Reflection is *perception*: there is required a special perception in order to bring to consciousness in the specific sense what was not (specifically) conscious.

Perhaps it is clearer to say: *meaning* in a quite specific sense — as *having-it-aimed-at, looking-at, meaning-out* — is something that combines with various "consciousnesses" and calls forth in each of them various articulations, privilegings, and so on. If we call meaning in this sense *objectivating* (= *attending*), then it is everywhere one and the same, and has perhaps only various *modi*. (Footnote: "But compare the next sheet. *Objectivating in the proper sense is more, is thinking objectivating.*") The differences of "acts," then, consist in *that which the specific meaning presupposes*: in the "consciousness," in *various* "apperception" (footnote: "the word 'apperception' is meaningless"). Thus the immanent apperceiving, the line of phanseological flux, in which independently of the specific meaning the unity of the *tone* constitutes itself — but which is only object in the proper sense when the meaning lives in this flux and the tone is *now what is meant*. Or thing-perception in its courses of intermerging appearances: the thing stands there as self-present. But it is *object in the proper sense* only when the meaning lives in this course. The object is the *meant*. Likewise my pleasing. The meaning must live in the pleasing, and *then* the pleasing-as-such is not only conscious but *meant*.

There remains to be investigated, Husserl says, whether the *identifying and distinguishing*, and all the predicating built thereon (including conceptual cognition), belong in the sphere of the *specific meaning as such* — i.e., are *modi of meaning*, modi of objectivating in a *privileged* sense. The this-meaning, the meaning-as-an-A, the meaning *that A is b*, etc. But in *grasping* (*Begreifen*) and *thinking*, is there not the *self-constitution of new objectivities* that can in turn be meant out? Mere meaning-out is no apperceiving. But synthetic comprehending, predicating, *is* apperceiving — there lies the difficulty (footnote: "It is creative apperceiving").

Husserl concedes: it is naturally right that *objectivating in the proper sense* (the being-positing making-into-object) presupposes a *meaning*, and a *thinking* meaning, a determining and grounding meaning. The true object is the object that *can sustain its identity*, or the appearing object that is determined-thinkingly in such a way that *the so-determined* can be retained, under all cognition-circumstances and in every cognition-situation, as *this same so-determined*. This holds also of the valuable object: it must exhibit itself as a *value* and exhibit its identity *as valuable*. Objectivation occurs everywhere at the highest level through *identifying and determining thinking and theorizing*. So thinking must of course join itself to the pleasing (Wertnehmen) just as to mere perceiving (in general: to sensible presenting) (footnote: "yes, but perceiving is already a being-positing; the pleasing requires only a being-positing founded in *it*"). Are *Gemütsakte*, then — specifically valuing-, pleasing-acts — *presenting* acts? Does the title "presenting" thus comprise in the end *all* acts?

Of course the difference between presenting in a pregnant sense and pleasing should not be effaced. Presenting makes *non-values* into objects, or is the apperceptive source of value-free objects (objects that *can* be valuable but do not contain value), or presenting constitutes objects with respect to non-axiological predicates as *source*. Valuing constitutes, on the basis of presenting, the *axiological predicates*. Presenting is directed at "mere matters"; *Gemüt* is directed at *value-being of matters*, value-matters; *Gemüt* is *taking-part* in matters. In short, we have various *classes and layers of objectivities* with various sources of origin.

*Objectivating* acts in opposition to *valuing* acts? There would only remain: objectivations that presuppose no feeling-stance or apperception, and ones that do. The connection between *presenting* in the narrower sense (matter-constituting act) and *thinking* (positing) would have to be loosened. *Matter-positing* (*Sachstellen* \= matter-apperception) and *valuing* (*Wertstellen* \= value-apperception) would be on equal footing, only that every value-positing presupposes a matter-positing. And to both, *thinking* would stand in the same relation, referring to all merely matter-bound or higher-founded objectity in equal way (footnote: "this is not entirely correct, but in essence rightly meant").

Going back to the deepest *grounds of acts lying before all thinking* (apperceptions, but original, unmodified ones), we come back to: (a) the sensible sensations — sensations in the ordinary sense; (b) the simplest sensible feelings (feeling-sensations), the latter founded in the former. (Footnote: "but also (c) drive-sensations.") One may perhaps venture to say that already the most primitive feelings are real feeling-acts, founded in the manner of other feeling-acts — namely founded in the sensations — which presupposes that *sensations themselves are already "acts," already consciousness-of*. But the "intentionality in the full sense" completes itself only through the *specific meaning*, the proper objectivating in a certain way.

The sensible sensation-contents are unities of consciousness in which, when the meaning lives in them, the *enduring sensation-content as object-meant* constitutes itself (the immanent). The same goes for *all acts*: namely, on this conception they would be *constituted unities*, i.e., *Empfundenheiten* (sensed-nesses), sensed contents, and "reflection" on them would be nothing other than a *meaning that activates itself on the "sensed,"* through which the acts (pleasure, etc.) become *object*. But over against the primary sensible sensation-contents, these *act-sensednesses* would have the peculiarity that they are not only conscious but *consciousness-of*, and so can be not only *meant* but also have a meaning *living* in them and directing itself at *the object that is conscious in them*. Thus *appearing as immanent object*, and *the appearing object as appearing in the appearing*.

Sensations (or unitary sensation-contents) form the "raw stuff" of empirical apperceptions, above all of empirical sensible perceptions. Pictorially: sensation-contents — whether contents of outer or also inner sensibility — are "interpreted." We are dealing, then, with certain acts that build themselves on sensations in determinately-to-be-described manner. But in such a way that into these apperceptions (apart from the "sensation-underlay," where they may appear as psychologized feelings) *no feeling-acts enter*. They constitute *being*, not *value*. This is, then, an own group of apperceptions (these are *founded* consciousness-kinds, not mere "sensations"), and these make up a determinate concept of *intellect*.

Another group of apperceptions are the *feeling-apperceptions* (the valuings). They appear in their most primitive form already as *sensible feelings*: these are not merely sensations (they are also that, insofar as they are conscious) but already *consciousness-of*, namely *feeling-consciousness-of*, valuation of the most primitive kind — namely valuations of sensation-contents. Then we have, as valuation of sensation-contents, also valuation of "intellect-objects," i.e., of real objects, of psychic and physical occurrences (of nature). This is a new apperception, in which the primitive feelings (the sensible feelings) play an analogous role (to a certain degree) as the sensations for the intellect-apperception.

There remains the question whether feeling-apperception is now to be supplemented by *desiring-* and *willing-apperceptions* of in-principle just as peculiar groups, and whether to all these groups is *coordinated* and in a certain way *superordinate* the *logical apperception*, that of specifically *theoretical reason*.

Husserl pauses for a striking biographical remark: "It cost me infinite trouble to be able to carry through this conception. Since the nineties, when I noticed in all acts the *appearing-of*, I tried again and again, and since I saw no possibility, I could not finish with this *appearing*, *consciousness-of* in all acts." *Has the carrying-through really succeeded?* Everything in him bristles against giving this up: when he perceives a house, he has an intellective act, a perception-act. But he can equally *perceive* a feeling, a perceiving, an immanent red content, a willing, etc.; and he can *think* all this; and thinking seems to stand *closer* to intuitions, presentations than to feeling-acts. Both together (presentations and thinkings) he had taken as *objectivating*. Feeling-acts, he had thought, were materials for objectivations but not themselves objectivations. (Reference of feeling-acts to the subject in the psychological sense, etc.) And now in *opposition*: the essence of consciousness is precisely "presenting," objectivating, at least in a certain sense (footnote: "yes"). The modifications in fantasy, etc., every consciousness can undergo. Already the primitive sensation is consciousness-of, and every act consciousness-of, etc.\!

Earlier Husserl said: action is an objectity that ultimately comes about through "presentation" as the objectivating act. He needed an *own* objectivation. So (1) first the empirical apperception: *process*; (2) the *will*: the process is "willing," carried by the will; (3) apperception as *objectivation*, which posits the process as "proceeding from" the will, carried by it. — *In the other conception*, however: on a sensible-empirical apperception, through which the process constitutes itself, builds itself the consciousness of *creative working* that *willingly enlivens* the process. And this consciousness *is* consciousness of *action*, *deed*. He needs no new objectivation. (Therein lies precisely a new apperception, constituting the object.) The meaning *lives* in it, and that suffices to ratchet up in logical apperception: "*This* is an action," or to predicate otherwise. The theory may go ever so much against the grain — *I will have to accept it*.

To every apperception, then, belongs a peculiar *correctness*, a peculiar *evidence* — better, *fulfillment*, leaving "evidence" for the logical domain. Cognition then recognizes and formulates conditions of the possibility of fulfillment of the thing-apperception, i.e., conditions of the possibility of sustaining the thing-positing, conditions of the possibility of the fulfillment or sustaining of value-intentions, etc. Cognition referred to itself (to the logical sphere) recognizes and formulates also conditions of the possibility of confirmation of the thinking-apperceptions in general — *logic*.

The architectonic decision of § 6 is the great one of the manuscript: feeling-acts *are* objectivating acts. The feeling-character of an object is *not* an objectivity merely added on by a second-order apprehension that builds on top of a non-objectivating affect; rather, the pleasing *itself* is a *consciousness-of* the pleasing as such, *in itself* an apperception with its own intentional matter, and the further "objectifying" — the bringing-to-meaning, the predicating — is a second-order *meaning* that lives in the apperception. This was the conclusion Husserl had resisted since the 1890s; he records his concession explicitly, with characteristic candor.

### **§ 7\. The Relation of Joy, Wish, and Will to Valuing. The Foundation of Willing in Wishing**

Husserl now reaches the architectural question that already pressed in §§ 3 and 4: how do *valuing*, *wishing*, *willing*, and *joy* stand to one another? He recalls extensive considerations in which it had seemed to him that the concept of valuing might be drawn so widely as to encompass wishing and willing — a general concept of *Gemütsakt* and *Gemütsapperzeption* seemed to stand out, but its sharp delimitation did not succeed. In the latest considerations the talk was clearly of a *narrower* value-concept, and much depends on finding and securing sharp boundaries. The (still unclear) idea of *Gemüt* has its opposite in the idea of *intellect*: thinking as judging — but also questioning and suspecting seem to attach themselves closely to judging. And the sphere of *thinghood*, of nature? There we would have to say that the old faculty-division takes the world as pre-given and asks how consciousness can comport itself toward it: presentingly, thinkingly, taking-part-feelingly, and practically-willingly. There is a tendency, in place of this fourfold division, to perform a *twofold* division — to take presenting and thinking together as *intellect*, and to take taking-part as valuing, and desiring/willing as *Gemüt* (νοῦς, ὄρεξις).

Presenting is regarded as confused thinking — or as one says, presenting already contains unclearly intermingled thought-elements. And then likewise, by analogy, taking-part — pleasing, taking-joy, valuing — is somehow a lower stage for *wishing and willing*.

The will is directed at *realization of values*. The wish too directs itself at the held-as-valuable, at something that stands over against it as value, and directs itself at *that this be*. *How is this directedness at values and at the being of the valuable to be understood?* Judging is directed at *truth*; the fulfillment in evidence is, so to speak, its goal. But in judging there is nothing of the fulfilling evidence or of a thought of it. *Judging means "holding-as-true,"* but it is nothing further than just judging — a certain kind of meaning. So wishing is also a certain meaning, a certain consciousness-of. The judgment can build on empty or full presentations; so too the wish. (One must mention, on both sides, differences of liveliness — on one side a lively conviction, on the other the liveliness of the wish.)

If I have an *intuition* of what is judged, it is a problem how intuition really functions there — e.g., in "On the Hainberg stands the so-called Rohnsgasthof." In any case I have an intuition that is the underlay of a predicative articulation: I judge "as it were" (as if I were making a perceptual judgment — that is a fantasy-judgment), but I judge on the other hand really. Do I have something twofold here? Indeed not. I can have the *Einstellung* of "as-it-were-judging," but I have *the Einstellung of really-judging*. With the wish: if I imagine intuitively that I make a lottery win, while wishing I may make it, I can adopt a double *Einstellung* — the "may" is *this wish*, the other is *the fantasy-joy* over this win. If this quasi-joy is really performed, it would correspond to the fantasy-predication. But does a fantasy-predication (as quasi-predication) somehow stick in the judgment that intuitively presentifies its state-of-affairs? And again: does joy as fantasy-joy somehow stick in the intuitive wish?

Surely it is a great difference: dreaming oneself into a great lottery win and *enjoying* the joy in this dream (fantasy-enjoying), versus *not* enjoying in fantasy but, on the basis of the fantasy of the win, *missing* it — i.e., not enjoying joy but, in a contrary consciousness (one that does not present the enjoying), *feeling the lack* — and again, since this is something new, *wishing*, *desiring*. Can one find in wishing an *own component* like "valuing," to be distinguished from wishing? (Footnote: "valuing in wishing and taking-joy.")

Similarly with joy: joy is joy *that A is*; grief is grief that A is *not*. I cannot take joy not only without holding-for-actual but also without the A — meant as *truly being* — being *for me* worth as A. Does there stick in joy, then, a *valuing of A* and on top of that a *component referring to the being of A*? How does the taking-joy distribute itself between *the "content"* and *the moment of "actuality"*? Does there stick in it a valuing *independent of the question of actuality*?

And how is it with the will? Just as we say of joy and grief that they are directed on the one hand at *truly-being*, at "actuality," and on the other hand presuppose a valuing of what *is or is not* there — and just as we say the same of wishing — so too of the will. The will is, one would say, *not itself value-holding*, but presupposes such. But does the will not bring something *new* to all this?

Joy is *enjoyingly* directed at the valuable and value-fully-being, at the *good* (not real-being but *existing*). The wish is *desiringly* directed at the good (in the manner of a *Seinsollens*\-consciousness, i.e., directed *constitutively* at an *ought-to-be*, that, were it, would be good). How is the will? Is it directed in a similar sense — as directed not only at being but at *being-becoming*? (Footnote: "but being-becoming is not the same as the good. Being-becoming constitutes itself without will\!") I will that the work which floats before me *be*. *I will to carry it out\!* (Footnote: "the work is an ought-to-be, and it is a good. Realization of the good.") First one would like to say: there underlies a *wishing*, not a mere valuing. What I will is wished-for *for* me. And the will goes in the *fiat* at the *making, "creative," realizing* of the wished-for and so also of the good. The will is directed not only at the good but at *the work*, at the *deed*, and at the deed *through the medium of the "action."*

*The mere "resolve" is "empty will-intention"* — what does that mean here? I can have the resolve while having a clear presentation of what is to be realized; if the resolve is fully clear, I have the clear presentation of an *action* that *terminates in the goal*. Therein lies a *fiat* as starting-point and so all that further characterizes action and deed: only "presentationally." But now I have a *real fiat*, a real *I-will*. This is very similar to the case of the judgment that does not build itself on perception but on memory or other actual presentification: on the one hand I present the state-of-affairs and perform an as-it-were judging in the presentification, and on the other hand I judge *now* and really, as it were *through* the presentification-judgment, just as in the resolve I really and now will and as it were through the fantasy-willing and fantasy-action.

On the other hand: does anything correspond, on the judgment-side, to *executing willing*? Manifestly not. The judgment can be *justified* or *unjustified*; the *bare* judgment can be set against a *grounding* judgment (it should be noted that in this comparative consideration Husserl always parallelizes *experience-judgments* and *willings*, not, say, mathematical and in general essence-judgments where this all does not hold). But the resolve too can be justified or unjustified, and I can not only make it clear to me but make its *grounds of right* clear (footnote: "but realization is something different from right-exhibition"). The resolve is "executed," and the *action* is characterized as the *execution of the resolve*.

Does there really lie *wishing* in *willing*? In the resolve I am directed at the goal (that A be), but directed at it *through* the (more or less determinately) presented action. There is a *will-line* that runs through this presented process. The *wish* corresponding to the will went to the *being of A*. (Of course I can also wish an action: I wished I would do that, I would carry out the work. Even during the carrying-out I can wish that it would actually come to its end, that I would bring it to pass — i.e., regarding the not-yet-carried-out, although "set in the work.") So how are the relations here?

If I wish "to take a walk" — i.e., wish an action — I do not have to actually do it, although I "know that it is executable"; I have the consciousness "*I can*." How does the wish become a will? Naturally apart from *deliberations of motives*, from the cognition that what speaks against "does not come into consideration." Now simply: I will and *do*. I "resolve," say, first to finish such-and-such and then to go; or I simply *do*. The *fiat* sets the action in motion. The wish does not stick in it once again — wish is simply *replaced* by will in its continuous modifications, as belonging to the action as fiat etc.

But that was perhaps somewhat hasty or imprecise. I wished the action — going for a walk, going to the theater, visiting the friend, etc. I now act. Then through the action I *fulfill* the wish. As far as the action is not yet carried out, the wish is still alive. I am already getting dressed: this lies still *before* the action I really wished, and serves its "staging." It is, so to speak, the action I have to perform in order to carry out the action I wish. During the dressing, then, I still have the unfulfilled wish. Now I am already walking and have the *content of fulfillment*, but at the same time I still wish to walk *further*. It can also be that I want to go out in order to *visit a dear friend*: during the going I have *foretaste-fulfillment* insofar as I do hope to reach the goal and so far satisfaction; on the other hand still living wish with respect to the *visit itself*, which is not yet really fulfilled.

Must there not be in every will *wish for the goal* and *wish for the not-yet-realized way as way to the goal*? And likewise always something of joy: *for joy is real and anticipatory wish-fulfillment*, as Husserl once tried to formulate. And will-action, executing action, is always already wish-fulfillment. (Footnote: "wish-fulfillment — that is hasty. But yet a *good* lies in it: every phenomenon standing in the function of fulfillment is a *satedness* with respect to what stands on the side of mere intention. But the sated phenomenon need not always appear in a fulfillment-transition. Thus joy and wish would belong together as insightful judgment and uninsightful, as clear intuition and empty presentation.") The matter is no different when I just present an A-being and wish for it, in such a way that the wish carries no thought of an *action* terminating in A-being. If the thought enters and I execute, I feel *foretaste-joy*, joy-anticipation (hope-joy), and at the same time still "wish" directed at the goal. The foretaste-joy grows as I, acting, approach the goal. We would have, with respect to the relations of joy and wish, *as it were*: *joy \= sated wish; wish \= unsated joy*. (Footnote: "wish should however be *fully unsated joy*, analogous to *empty presentation* and *not* to imperfect intuition.")

Can one then say *the will is wish in the modus of fiat*? That will not work if "wish" is taken in the ordinary sense, since *then* "wish" presents no realization toward a goal. But if one takes "wish" generally as *desiring striving*, and distinguishes *bare wish* and *will*, where the desiring is modified by the founding "I can" *and* by the fact that it has taken the "I will," the *fiat*, into itself — then what? The answer will run: *the will, then, is founded in the wish, and through this it is directed at a held-for-good*. And it is so directed in the manner of *making*. The good as goal of making \= the practically good, the good *work*, the good *deed*. (Footnote: "but then it is to be said: the 'I will' is no mere modality of the wish — though the wish may have its base-character in the will — but a wholly new and in itself no 'meaning'.")

The fiat as such "fulfills" itself in the acting and ends, fulfilling itself, in the *work*, in the will-goal, in what is *meant as good*. The wish-underlay "fulfills" itself in *joy*. This is *pure* joy if it leaves nothing more in itself to be wished. *Then the good is given.* The will, to be sure, is directed at its goal in the *manner of realization*; but the will is also directed at the good *in another sense*: namely insofar as it (the pure will-moment of the fiat) is *non-self-sufficient*, founded in the described underlay. In any case, the concrete experience *will* (and *I-will*) is an "intentional experience" with the practical intention on a good *to be attained*. The problem is then to what extent the act-moment of the fiat itself can be designated as intentional toward the good. Manifestly the situation is by no means the same as for the intentionality of a presentation, or even of a wish, of a joy. And one will perhaps have to say: it is not a *proper* intentionality but an *acquired* one — namely such that *concrete phenomena as concrete* surely have an intentionality that is not merely that of components. The will "means" — by living itself out, by realizing — to realize *a good*. The will is right, is *pure will*, when the meant good *really is* good. Every will is "intention" toward a good *deed*. It wants, as it were, to be a *good will*. But this concerns it as "meaning" and concerns it analogously to every meaning. The "directedness-at" that lies in realization, although directedness at a *meant-as-good*, is yet to be distinguished from the "directedness at the truly good," which obtains when the good is *truly* good. The unbroken-running action *really attains* its goal; the goal that the will set itself, or whose attainment makes it up, is at the end of the action *actuality*. But in the other sense its meaning, *to realize a good*, has not also fulfilled itself (when, namely, the goal is *not* truly good, etc.).

The architectonic of § 7, which closes Part I, is the layered ordering of the *Gemüt*: at the base, *valuing* (Wertnehmen) as the pleasing-in that grasps the good in its being; on top of this, *wish* as desiring directedness at the *Seinsollen* of the good; on top of this, *will* as fiat and execution, founded in the wish but adding the new moment of *making*, of *realization*, with its own directedness at the *practical good*. Joy is sated wish, wish is unsated joy; will is wish-founded with the additional fiat — but the fiat itself has, Husserl carefully concedes, only an "acquired" intentionality, since concrete phenomena have an intentionality that is not just that of their components.

### **Appendix I. The Foundation of the Acts of Feeling as Apperception of Feeling and as Meaning of Feeling**

Husserl opens with a precision that his text in §§ 1–7 had been groping toward: *founded apperceptions* and *founded acts as meanings* are to be distinguished. We distinguish *experiencing* as a *self-experiencing-comporting* from *experiencing meaning* — i.e., from *perceiving, remembering*, etc., in which *attentiveness* and *Aufmerksamkeit als Hinsehen-auf* (looking-toward) hold sway. Likewise the *feeling-comportment* and *valuing meaning*. The (being-positing\!) meaning, he believes, can be regarded as the *properly theoretical fundamental function*. To meaning belong the various forms of synthesis, which in their purity unfold their lawfulness in formal logic. But to the full theoretical function belongs *signifying* (conceiving — proper signifying that makes word-sense), through which it comes to *predication*.

When it is said, then, that every *Gemütsakt* (pleasing, wishing, willing) is *founded*, the question is whether the foundation takes place as foundation of *apperception* by apperception, or also (since the first foundation is unavoidable) of *act* by act *with respect to meaning*. He gives a sharp example: he can live in an empirical apperception, exercise meaning in it, perceive the object (so being-positing), perceive on it this or that as belonging to it, etc., and *can in all this have pleasing* without *meaningly living* in the pleasing (footnote: "to live meaningly in the pleasing — that is *not* being-positing of the pleasing object"). Then the object does *not* stand there as pleasing for him; he sees the object but does not *mean* it as pleasing. In *joy* he takes joy *that* A is α: here he means *that*, and at the same time the feeling-state — and *if* he means, the feeling-meaning — is founded in the judgment (in the identifying synthesis).

It is to be investigated whether we must distinguish *bare looking-toward* from *making-into-the-this-about-which-of-a-(predicative, attributive)-synthesis*. He thinks not — that this cannot be separated, and that in the looking-toward already a "the-" or "this-" positing lies, only of course before the conceptive expression. What characterizes the *proper* this-positing is that the *this* has already become the subject of a predicative synthesis (or the like — the *nominale*); whereas a looking-toward, a *having-as-object*, is possible already *before* all that — as when he looks toward the wish or toward the joy without predicating about them. He resolves to investigate systematically and in aporias what "meaning" is when one is, perceiving, judging, taking-joy, etc., turned toward the perceived, the state-of-affairs, the "*so it is\!*", the *delightful-being*, etc.; how far one can equate all meaning with grasping or not; whether each *Stellungnahme* itself is the *meaning*, and to be distinguished from this is the *being-grasping, being-positing meaning of its correlate (value)*.

Beilage I thus refines the position of § 1–7 by introducing the distinction between *act-foundation at the level of apperception* and *act-foundation at the level of meaning*. It is possible to live in the lower-level pleasing without yet activating the meaning that would lift the pleasing into being-positing of the value. This is the seed of what will, in *Ideas I*, become the doctrine of the noetic-noematic structure of feeling-acts and the question of attentional rays cutting through founding apperceptions to objectivate value-correlates.

### **Appendix II. Are There Spontaneous *Gemütsakte* as a Class of Reason-Acts Distinct from Theoretically Determining Thought-Acts?**

The Beilage opens with a candid admission: here the problematic does not come to *clear delimitation*. Husserl now lays out the question schematically in numbered points. (1) Acts "direct themselves." First, theoretical acts of *determining thinking* "direct themselves" at objects, or rather at states-of-affairs. Already a question: is the directing-itself at *objects* (which are then determined) and at *states-of-affairs* a directing in the same sense? Are not objects "pre-given" to determining through "presenting" acts or through presenting apperceptions — through which a *ray of meaning* passes that brings their objects, *first*, to objectivation for determining thinking — and do not new objectivities then constitute themselves *through* the theoretically determining meaning *itself*, such that new graspings and theoretical determinations can then concern just these new objectivities? That concerns the merely theoretical acts. The further question is whether *parallel to* the theoretical (determining, logical, etc.) acts there run *Gemütsakte* that direct themselves in analogous way at objectivities, are *Spontaneitäten* in analogous sense, and constitute new (predicative) objectivities.

(2) The grasping and determining acts direct themselves at objects pre-given to them and ultimately at *simply* pre-given ones, e.g., perceptual objects. To these correspond the merely sensible presentations (apperceptions) possible already before the *Zuwendung*. The spontaneity of *determining* presupposes a *receptivity*. But in the sphere of receptivity we find the fundamental distinction of *being-apperceptions* (thinghood-apperceptions) and "*Gemüt*\-apperceptions." We have a value-constituting sensibility just as we have a matter-constituting one. Are now perhaps the spontaneous, *in-the-pregnant-sense-"directing"* feeling-acts nothing other than mere *Zuwendungen* to what is pre-given in such *Gemüt*\-apperceptions, what is constituted in advance — and the theoretical meanings possibly bearing on them? Does there really obtain an analogy between theoretical determining thinking (as a title for "intellect-acts") and own reason-acts of various further classes that we designate as practical, aesthetic, etc., reason? Or is there at bottom only *one* reason — namely *the theoretical* — so that all difference would lie in the simple apperceptions? Complications and syntheses might well occur in the feeling-sphere, but in such a way that no analogy and parallelism with the synthesis of the intellect-sphere would obtain.

(3) How is it understood, when we speak of acts of *emotional* and *volitive* reason, that they have a special relation to apperceptions we call value-apperceptions and the like? Has theoretical reason a special affinity to the primary apperceptions — the sensible ones in the narrower sense — as if these were "theoretical apperceptions"? And has every other reason-act likewise a special affinity to apperceptions of a special group? *We constantly stumble upon the problem of sensible feeling and feeling-act in a special sense.*

In what follows it is only made clear that, parallel to thing-apperceptions, there are peculiar value-apperceptions running parallel (as receptive value-appearances), and *this, of course, is very important*.

The Beilage thus crystallizes the open question of Part I: *whether there is a genuine spontaneity of feeling/willing (an own emotional/volitional reason) parallel to the theoretical*, or whether all spontaneity reduces to *theoretical* spontaneity bearing upon receptive value-apperceptions. Husserl leaves the question explicitly *unanswered* — but on one point he stands fast: *parallel to thing-apperceptions there exist peculiar receptive value-apperceptions*. This is the secured platform on which later sections will continue to build.

### **Appendix III. Sensible Feeling as Immanent Time-Unity Is Not an Act Referred to the Sensation-Content**

The third Beilage tackles the constitutive status of *sensible feeling* itself. Husserl had designated sensible feeling as an *act*, as a valuation referred to the sensation-content, in the simplest case to the primary content. But how, on this basis, does sensible feeling constitute itself? The primary content as *immanent time-unity* constitutes itself in *inner time-consciousness*; sensible feeling presumably constitutes itself as a *higher layer* over this consciousness that constitutes the primary content. It thereby presumably constitutes itself *as itself* a sensation-content — a *non-self-sufficient* one, that presupposes the primary content. Both layers together — the relatively self-sufficient founding (if it is really self-sufficient\!) and the founded — constitute the so-to-speak *feeling-laden* primary content. *Sensible sensing* and, in the same way, the sensible *feeling* built on it, is therefore *nothing other than inner consciousness* (constituting temporal contents) and has therein its intentionality (the inner one).

But does it then make sense to speak here of a *second* intentionality — namely, the one that the constituted feeling-content is supposed to have *with respect to the primary content*? Intentionality before grasping, meaning, turning-toward says nothing other than a "consciousness-of" that can give the possible underlay for a meaning. And so it manifestly makes *no sense* to speak of such a second intentionality. He can of course refer the two components to one another, but that is a matter of *predicative referring*. If an apperceiving founds itself on the sensibility, it brings of course a new intentionality with it — it constitutes a new object that on its part is to be set in relation to the primary contents and the like, which is again a matter of predicative acts.

Such *objective relations* are *not* to be confused with the *intentional* relations of "acts" on objects. Therefore *sensible feeling as constituted time-unity is no "act,"* just as little as the primary sensible content is an act. Quite differently the "feeling-apperceptions," which are constituted as time-unities and *at the same time* are *consciousness-of*. *Not the sensible feeling, but the sensible feeling-ing* (more generally: not sensation as content, but sensation as sensing) is an act.

A footnote, however, signals a real tension: "this is to be reconsidered again and again\! Compare the same position-taking in the next sheets \[where it is shown that *then the analogy with 'drives' is lost*\!\]." This is the sharpest of the structural tensions that drive the manuscript: if sensible *feeling* is not an act but only sensible *feeling-ing* is, then the schema cannot be projected onto drives (which are act-like in Husserl's sense). The Beilage therefore stands as a precise registration of an unresolved problem: *what is the exact ontological status of the lowest stratum of the feeling-life — feeling-content as a passive constituted unity, or feeling-sensing as an act?* Husserl decides for the second but flags that the decision has consequences he does not yet see how to integrate into the architectonic.

This closes Part I. The architectural yield of Chapter I is the secured doctrine that *value-apperception* exists as a distinct class of receptive apperceptions parallel to thing-apperceptions, that values constitute themselves through transient value-appearances analogously to how thing-realities constitute themselves through thing-appearances, and that on this base a tower can be erected: pleasing → wish → will, with joy as sated wish and will as *fiat*\-bearing wish-execution directed at the practical good. The unresolved problems are the precise status of feeling-sensings (act or not?), the question whether feeling/willing is genuinely spontaneous, and whether the parallel to belief-modi survives the asymmetry that belief is a "modus" while pleasing colors.

## **II. The Arousal of Feelings Issuing from Objects, in Contrast to Valuation Aiming at Objects. The Question of the Feeling-Character of Valuing**

### **§ 1\. The Differences of Intensity in the Aroused Feeling and in the Feeling-Light, in Contrast to the Differences of Value. The Arousal of Feeling-Acts by Value-Characterized Objects**

Husserl opens with a precise self-questioning. What he had used to call "the act of pleasing" (*Gefallen*) in his older and newer manuscripts — is *that* the "from-the-I-issuing" *spontaneous* holding-as-valuable, *Wertnehmen* or *Wertsetzen*? Set over against this is the "from-the-object-issuing," "by-the-object" being-affected: the *affect* of pleasure or joy, the proper *feeling* that is enjoyed or undergone. How both stand to one another — whether they are two "sides" of one and the same matter, or separable matters (perhaps factually or necessarily connected) that may even be detachable on either or one side — about this nothing has been said. The aroused feeling is *with me* and has a relation to the object: it is *aroused-by-it* feeling. What this "with me" means — whether the same or different in sensible and "spiritual" feelings — must first be considered; likewise what "aroused-by-the-object" and "issuing from the object" mean.

The object appears in a "feeling-character" — in *rosy light*, in *gloomy form*, *shadowed*. Husserl asks again whether this "light" or this darkness, this shadowing, *coincides* with the appearing or meant value-character or not. (Are "appearing" and "meant" the same here? The footnote: the appearing object of pleasing has the rosy shimmer; if the object — say the beloved — is *thought*, verbal, then the *word* has the rosy shimmer: "the name of the beloved.") Immediately a striking point becomes salient: the aroused feeling, and likewise the feeling-light on the object, can be more or less *lively* — more intense pleasure, more burning pain, rosier and more radiant light, less rosy, deeper and darker shadow, less dark. *But how is it with the differences of value as the correlate of value-holding?*

We speak of higher-valuable and less-valuable. That, one will say, runs *not at all* parallel to those intensity-differences in the feeling and in the feeling-light. Husserl draws the contrast sharply. *Value, disvalue belongs in a special way to the object*. Valuing refers to determinate moments of the object, to determinate value-grounds *that "belong" to the object*; but feeling does *not* so belong to the object — it belongs *to the great basin of experiences, to the "stream of consciousness," in which all feelings enter into a unity, into a feeling-stream, and in which every newly-aroused feeling alters the total niveau* and co-determines the manner of relation that emerging feelings have to their arousing-objects. The feeling is *not* determined by the fact that it is feeling "on" the object in such-and-such way (mediated through such-and-such motivation), aroused by it, etc.; and the feeling-light on the object is not determined by object and valuation of the object, but by the feeling-basin: a powerful joy, a strong positive affect raises all positive lights and depresses all shadows (i.e., illuminates them); *mutatis mutandis* a negative affect. So in the sphere of affection everything is in *constant flux*, everything is *relative*, a πρός τι.

The richness of the architecture is now visible. In joy, insofar as it is joy *at* something, we often have a taking-joy "for the sake of a value." Joy goes to the object that *stands there as value*. So with joy at overcoming a theoretical difficulty: I value the problem-solution, and once I have it, I take joy. Here it is a matter of the *fulfillment of a wish-intention*. I grieve over the death of a noble woman: her *not-being* is *disvalue*; she stands (apart from the thought of being) as "beautiful," as a value-object, and that she *is no more* saddens me. Here belongs the question: are joy and grief, in their *normal* concept, throughout founded *in this way* on valuing? The so-called feeling-acts refer to their objects in the manner just *of feelings*, as aroused by them — but aroused *for the sake of the object-values*, *motivated* and grounded thereby.

He sharpens. "Objects arouse joy" — but that is imprecise. *The being of objects*, that the object *is* or *is not*, can arouse joy and grief. He gives examples: I take joy in the beloved person — in her being-there, in her being-present-to-me-and-attesting-her-love-to-me, in the way she actually now gives herself. I take joy or grief over *facts*, over that this or that has set in, that such-and-such mathematical proposition really holds (so that my conjecture is confirmed which lies "first and foremost" close to my heart and now can give me joy). In the case of the existence of objects, the feeling is in a certain way still referred to the object that stands there as being, but "properly" it is *feeling of joy over the being*. Likewise with the doings of the beloved: I have joy *"in" the beloved*; the value-source is the beloved, her properties of "beauty," her value-properties. Transfers can then occur to outer doings; equally, value-properties of the person may give value and ground joy over them. A positive value-fact has its *termini*, its *objects-about-which*; insofar as they are value-sources or value-derivations, the joy-character carries over to them or joy-arousal goes from them.

If joy is joy *over the fact*, it can mean joy over the *Sachverhalt* or joy over the *Sachlage*. In a way both, but in a more proper sense the latter: the joy-arousal (like the valuation) goes *through* the state-of-affairs-positing, but the value is the same with immediately equivalent states-of-affairs, and likewise the "object" of joy is the same. In a certain way, joy refers and directs itself at the *Sachlage* and at its termini (objects and objective existences). In another way at the *value-Sachlage* and at the objects insofar as they are value-grounding. This gives the talk of "arousal" various senses. *The arousal goes from the objects, but from the objects insofar as they are value-characterized*, and possibly more properly from the (value-grounded, motivated) *Sachlagen* insofar as they are *value-Sachlagen*.

So we have, in joy and grief in the specific sense, *clearly distinguished and really separable*: a *valuation aimed at the "object,"* going *toward* the object (with all that it presupposes), and a *feeling-arousal* going *toward the subject*, namely the joy and grief themselves, which one the one hand characterize the object as delightful or grievous but at the same time *take hold of the subject*: I am sad, I am joyful. But nothing has been said about *separability or non-separability*: whether valuation is possible without arousal, and *especially* whether arousal is possible *without* valuation — the same joy, but not joy at valuing. In the latter respect: could not an object arouse a "feeling" *without* the feeling having its "ground," its "motive," in a *Wertnehmung*, in a value-positing? Could not the latter be one time, and another time the same object arouse the same feeling, but *without being a value-object*? In that respect the feeling would *not* be joy (despite the same *qualitative content*), because the *total phenomenon* would be different: the arousal would issue not merely from the object but *from the value-object*; not from the *Sachlage* but from the *value-Sachlage*. (Footnote: "this is unclear. The 'object' simply stands as valuable. The reflection finds the valuing.")

§ 1 thus opens Part II with a careful, almost surgical separation: there is *valuation* directed at the object (and its value-grounds), and there is *feeling-arousal* directed back upon the subject. The intensity-differences in feeling do not run parallel to the differences of value: feeling lives in the *stream*, in a basin where everything is relative; value is rooted in *objective grounds*. The question pressed in the rest of Part II is whether feeling-arousal *requires* valuation as its motive — which is the question whether the *feeling*\-aspect of the *Gemüt* is by essence value-correlated, or whether there is a stratum of feeling (arousal) that is in principle *valuation-free*.

### **§ 2\. Sensible Feelings as Feelings Whose Arousal Has No Valuing of the Object Underlying It. Is Valuing an Aroused Feeling?**

Husserl bracketed the question of *qualitative identity* of the aroused feeling and now raises the problem in completely general form: *Can a feeling — positive or negative, pleasure or displeasure — be aroused by an object without there underlying the arousal a valuing of the object?* And, even more generally: must feelings *in general* be *aroused* — i.e., does it belong to the essence of every feeling to have an *arousal-direction* and (insofar as relating-to-objects in feelings is supposed to have the character of *being-aroused-by-the-object*) hence an *object by which it is aroused*? Are there *non-aroused* feelings? One could even ask: do feelings, by their essence, have only the *one* kind of object-relation that we call "arousal-by-them"?

He answers the first question in the affirmative: *certainly* objects can arouse feelings without founding valuation. The field of *sensible feelings* offers examples in arbitrary number. Under this title several distinct things must be marked off: the *agreeable-feeling in the "enjoyment"* of a food, as sensible feeling at the taste, at the taste-sensation; the feeling at the *objective* food, that is bitten and chewed in the mouth — the feeling related to the bite and the feeling related to the rest of the food on the plate. Yet again: the pleasing in the sight of the cake placed on the table when one expects to get some; another again when one is full and does not want to eat. The latter is similar to: pleasing at the cake displayed in the bakery window. Likewise the pleasure in a beautiful visual form or beautiful color, but "as it just presents itself"; the pleasing at the color-property of the object, or the form-property, or at the object itself insofar as it has these properties. The agreeable-feeling in *handling* velvet and the pleasing at the velvet itself insofar as so handled it arouses this agreeable-feeling — and *essentially "the same"* pleasing that he has at the *seeing* of the velvet "with reference to it." The pleasing at the Amati violin for the sake of its beautiful tone (which he may not be able to hear — the violin is not being played); the pleasing at the beautiful tone *itself* and *in itself*.

A taxonomy follows. (I) Feelings (a) "*at* the sensation-content" — pleasure at the pleasant taste, "at," "in"; (b) feelings at the *apprehended object* (perceptual object) — pleasure at, pleasing at the cake — namely at the perceptual object itself, or at the object by virtue of certain consequences. (II) Feelings at fantasy- and memory-content, at the fantasy- or remembered (presentified) object, namely at it itself and at it by virtue of its relations to others. The presentifications can be full or empty. With (II) one must ask whether the feelings are "real" feelings or *presentifications* of feelings or both: now feeling oneself touched by a real pleasure, or remembering a sensible pleasure, or even now in memory taking joy on the basis of remembered pleasure. (III) Instead of apprehended objects we take objects in general — *thought* objects and objects posited in thinking, or merely thought-out objects, states-of-affairs, etc.

These are mere beginnings, and further problems belong here: whether *valuing* is *also* a feeling — only an apprehensively-mediated or a presentification-mediated one; whether, if the distinction between *valuing* and the (further) *aroused* feeling is made, what is in question is not just feelings of *higher level*, or whether (perhaps also) sensible feelings further attached, or attached feelings with dark thing-underlay.

§ 2 thus marks the field of sensible feelings as the test-case for the question: feelings *can* be aroused without underlying valuation. But this does not yet decide whether such feelings *are* the same in kind as valuing-feelings (the joy in beauty, the joy in research). The next sections probe.

### **§ 3\. The Fusion of the Sensation-Feeling with the Sensation-Content. The Feeling-Tone. The Distinction between the Taste-Pleasure and the Joy It Motivates over the Having of the Taste-Pleasure. The Transition of Joy into the Joyful Mood**

Husserl restricts himself to the most primitive case — *sensation-pleasure*, *sensation-feelings*. It is clear that the *sensation-content* itself is what *carries* the pleasure (or the pain), purely in itself and quite immediately. The feeling is *groundless*, insofar as it has no *proper* "motive" — unless one wished to call the sensation-content itself the motive. One could say: the sensation-content "arouses" pleasure not *for the sake of* anything (which would point back to something else) but "*through itself*." In it itself lies the "ground" of the pleasure-arousal; the pleasure does not merely issue from it but is rooted purely in *it* and has in it its one and only and ultimate *source*.

But — Husserl checks himself — is this *phenomenologically* spoken? Does the taste-pleasure (the pleasure *in* the pleasant taste, *at* the taste-sensation) really "issue" from this sensation as an "arousal" running out from it, with the sensation as a "source" out of which the feeling proceeds and as the "ground," "motive" of the pleasure? How does it come about that it sometimes seems to him that this is so, and sometimes the entirely opposite? With smells it occurs that they please at first and momentarily and then displease — quite decidedly — and *then* it does not seem that they alter in being "sweet," pleasure-toned, etc., as they were. Likewise "captivating" melodies. Does it not depend on whether we *see-into* the sensation the pleasant taste and lift out its feeling-sensation-toning, or whether we speak of the *pleasing* or *displeasing*, which can well have its source in this *sensation-feeling*? (The crucial editorial footnote: "There is no phenomenological 'arousal'-relation between sensation-content and sensation-pleasure.")

A distinction is offered: (1) a feeling is referred to an object, to something, and *issues from* the object, *issues from* something insofar as he is what *makes* pleasure. (2) A feeling has its *source* in something; it can have its source in one and its *object* in another. Source would be the *grounding* or also the *ground*; immediate grounds ("in itself") and mediate ones are to be distinguished.

But sensation-pleasure is "aroused" by the sensation-content, *whether or not* this is objectified and attended to. Whether the pleasure at the sensation-content disappears or modifies itself when this content figures as *displaying* for a perceptual object, is a problem. It does *not* belong to "aroused" feeling in the proper sense that its arouser be "*conscious as object*" — so also nothing of conscious motivation, of necessary grounding of the feeling in a *Wertnehmung*, a holding-as-valuable of an "in any sense presented" object. "Object" should not just mean *attended-to*, that on which a glance is directed (a meaning-toward and a grasping), but should also mean "*apprehended*."

He now opens an important distinction: apprehensions can be experienced *in the background* and carry their feelings (the *apprehended* objects "arouse" feelings), without rays of meaning-toward, of attending, passing through them. If it happens belatedly, then we say: feelings *were* experiences, and the feelings "were referred to" the apprehended objects, were "aroused" by them — but the relation of *arousing* is *potential* insofar as no own line or direction from the object to the feeling is "conscious." (What does "apprehended object" mean — *appearing*? *Intuitive*?)

But also *emptily-presented*, "as it were emptily apprehended" objects can arouse their feeling. The example is fine: my friend is in the room; I am speaking with my neighbor and not turned toward my friend; yet the *background-consciousness* of his presence does not leave me, and with it a steady feeling is aroused that determines the whole "feeling-stream."

Insofar as sensation-contents that are just experiences have their apprehending-apprehensions, the question arises whether sensation-feelings are *necessarily* the bases of feelings *at the apprehended objects*, or whether they necessarily, corresponding to the apprehension, "convert" themselves into such feelings. The word "convert" is meant to suggest that the sensation-feelings perhaps not merely awaken new feelings but *fuse* with such, or, through the feeling-tendencies belonging to the apprehended contents, undergo *alteration*, yielding something new that does not merely contain the sensation-feelings.

Husserl gives a beautiful example: he had been talking with the children during eating, attending thematically to their tales; he nonetheless enjoys the eating with pleasure, and the pleasure refers partly to the actual sense-sensations, partly to the objective — the bite in the mouth, the food on the plate, etc. Now he attends to the sense-contents, which continue to function apprehendingly, "referred to" the bite. He finds *the pleasure-feeling one with the content*. He can attend to the content specially, but the feeling is *one with it*. He may distinguish the feeling that hangs specially on the content and further feeling-flows that are aroused thereby (perhaps blissfulness of enjoying, perhaps joy at the pleasure itself, etc.).

If one holds to the former, one does *not* have the impression as if there were two things here: (1) the *arousing* — the sensation-content; (2) "with me" — the feeling aroused by it. This distinction one will make only with respect to those *further* feelings. *The direct feeling belonging to the sensation-content itself is fused with it*; it follows all turns and shifts of this content, lies *upon* it. Here one cannot find anything of an *arousing*, of a relation, of an opposition between *arousing* and *aroused*. One can speak of that when one passes from the sensation-content over to the (perhaps apprehended) object. The food, the bite, arouses by being chewed in the mouth the agreeable-feeling; it arouses the taste, and with the taste the feeling. *In* "me," in my bodily I, it arouses *taste and feeling*: both at the same "place." "Deeper in me" are the *mediately aroused* feelings; if I have my "joy" at the pleasant taste and at the eating with the whole rhythm of sensible sensations and with the inseparably one (or rather not-yet-separated) sensible feelings, then this joy is *joy of the central I*, even if it does not come from the "innermost." (The footnote gives the case of the sweet pastry: it pleases me at first, then displeases, "purely in eating and *without turning to the sensation*." Does the sensation-pleasure remain the same despite the conversion of pleasing into displeasing?)

The problem is now sharpened: *Is the sensation-pleasure and the proper aroused pleasure of one genus?* Every "aroused" sensible pleasure certainly — but again the question whether this "arousal" is the same as in *spiritual* pleasure (joy). "Arousal" can mean a *being-effected-in-the-I*, just as a light effects in me the sensation of dazzling and the unpleasant feeling of dazzling unitary with it, or as the food effects in me the taste-sensations and the taste-pleasure. The aroused — sensation, feeling — has its "cause" in the arousing; the arousing is a "stimulus" exercised on the I. By contrast, when the pleasant taste arouses joy in me, this joy has its "ground" in the pleasant taste — namely *in the layer of sensible sensation-"pleasure"* — and again as "grounding" or motivating comes into question the *now-having* or *enjoying* of the sensible sensation-pleasure and the *facta* it presupposes. *Sensation-pleasure has no "grounds," no motives. Sensation-pleasure has no "objects," no Sachlage, to which it refers.* (Footnote: "but this holds not only of sensation-pleasure but also of 'sensible pleasure at the object,' the pleasant taste.") *What we call joy has grounds, motives.* The pleasing, the joy, the "spiritual pleasure" has its objects, refers to states-of-affairs, etc. (Husserl notes the misleadingness: "the beautiful cake in the shop window pleases — this would also be 'spiritual pleasure'.")

Connected with this: with sensation-pleasure it makes no sense to speak of *reason and unreason*; here no peculiar axiological objectity constitutes itself. Every "spiritual" pleasure, every pleasing, by contrast, stands under reason-norms and constitutes peculiar value-objectities. (Footnote: the apperceived sensation-pleasure stands under *apperceptive* reason, not under an own kind of reason.)

*Sensation and sensation-feeling are one.* The feeling is no separable layer from the sensation-content; one cannot really speak of a "layer." The sensation-feeling is a *feeling-tone*, a "sensible character" that is generically the same in all pleasure-toned contents and again in all pain-toned. This character is differentiated — a taste-pleasure is different from an eye-pleasure, etc. He cannot really lift out the characters but only abstract the moment of feeling-toning by setting pleasure-toned and displeasure-toned, or pleasure-toned and indifferent, taste-contents or visual contents over against one another. (Long footnote: in the unity of a sensation-course feeling-tones can change, vary, gradate by intensity. How is it with the *unity of feeling* belonging to the *total rhythm*? It seems that this is not again a sensation-pleasure, as if it belonged to the sensation-content as feeling-tone, but is already a "*pleasing*" that belongs intentionally to the unity of the pleasure-toned or pleasure-rhythm-overlaid sensation. But this has its difficulties.)

A delicate question: does *indifference* correspond to its own toning, and so does it belong to the essence of every sensible content to have a feeling-tone? Certain is that, through such comparisons and analyses, the distinction of sensation-content and feeling-moment is possible in many cases, while both are completely one — analogously to the distinguishable moments in the sensation-content itself. (But not as if the "tone" did not have its own position in the complex: it has quality, intensity, etc.) The feeling-tone has a *changing intensity* — but an intensity that belongs to it and not to the sensation-content (intensity of tone-pleasure and intensity of tone in the ordinary sense). As to quality, the feeling-moment qualifies itself as *pleasure* and *pain* and possibly as *mixture*.

Aroused-by-an-object pleasing is the case where the object stands there *as pleasing*: it appears, surrounded by a "rosy light." The sensation-content has *no rosy light*, no rosy shimmer. While eating, the food still on the plate has the rosy light, *not* the bite in the mouth nor the taste-content. (Footnote: "always the same error, as if it depended on apprehension." A self-correction by Husserl.) The bite, presumably, because we have not really constituted it as object during the chewing-and-pleasant-taste-sensing; it is not as when we constitute the objectity of a button held in the mouth — wondering, e.g., whether we would recognize it as a button if we had not seen it, etc.

In acts of pleasing, in affects of "joy," it should further be noticed that the rosy shimmer either has *the character of object-belonging* — as the aureole grounded in the object by what it is or what belongs to it — or it has *the character of borrowed reflection*. Every kind of thing has its rosy shimmer when I am in joyful mood, but not as own shimmer. On the other hand, the *own* shimmer can have its special primary belonging to the content of the object, to such-and-such content-moments, or accrue to it for the sake of the relation to others. Here is the question: *how does the rosy shimmer stand to the appearing delightfulness?*

A fine illustration: a face *looks lovely*. Either the person is directly apprehended as loveable, good, noble, etc., and the face *expresses* this — then the face pleases as *expression*, indeed as actual indication of it. Or the face "*reminds*" of it, resembles such an expression but not certainly; he does not unhesitatingly apprehend it as such, not seriously — he comports himself similar to a sentence he hears and understands but without taking position. He *suspects* perhaps that the face expresses this. Naturally not verbally, but purely according to the apprehension (as in ordinary perception: *Wahr-nehmung*, *Anmutung*, *Zweifel*, etc.).

He continues: we *enjoy* eating — we sense the pleasant taste. In *that* sense we do not enjoy in pleasing, in joy, etc. We enjoy the sensible pleasure — that can simply mean that we sense the pleasant taste; but we can also have *joy at the having of the taste-pleasure*, at the sensing of the pleasant taste. Then we have a *joy*, a *pleasing*, that has its ground (its motive) in the pleasant taste, and more closely and properly in its pleasure-(feeling-)character. *Enjoying* (taking-joy) in this manner, we shall in general look toward the pleasant taste, "make it object," or — directing the food, the eating — toward this "enjoyment," this pleasant taste. Yet we may take joy even when we do not look attentively toward it: i.e., the joy-attention passes, after the turning-away, into *joyful mood* (frohe Stimmung). One can say first that the *objectivation* recedes into the background but is still there; equally the joy undergoes a *background-turn* which does not annul it. In the background he is "conscious" of his chewing-movements and of the taste-pleasure localized in the palate; he does not merely sense — he naturally has certain apprehensions, certain liftings-out (which yet are *not* attendings). The taste is intense, in a certain way distinguished, accentuated (over against the pressure and pull of his clothes); certain lines run from "eating" to this taste. And not only is the taste a pleasure-taste, but it makes one *jolly*; on it a *pleasing* is referred, although no turning-toward, no meaning-toward is directed; from it spreads a *cheerful mood*, a feeling-stream: the *whole feeling-stream of the background, of "inner life," receives a possibly mighty influx*. Not only is he reminded of joyful things — what is so aroused gets an *increase of joy*, and finally everything and anything receives a *rosy shimmer*, a joyfulness-character. (Of course we can also turn back to the joy-mood and take joy even in *it*.)

The problem still remaining: can it be that, despite the differences indicated and yet to be multiplied, *something* may yet be left of the claim that *feeling* as sensation-feeling and *feeling* as joy, as pleasing, as value-constituting act — *both* are "feelings," experiences of *one and the same essential genus*?

§ 3 has thus accomplished a delicate piece of microphenomenology: it dissolves the apparent "arousal" between sensation-content and sensation-feeling (they are *fused*, one with the other), preserves the genuine arousal between sensible pleasure and the *joy* over the having of the pleasure (which is grounded in the pleasure but is not it), and traces the migration of joy into *mood* — the irradiation of every content by a "rosy shimmer" that has no localized object. The unanswered question — whether sensation-feelings and *joy*\-feelings are of one genus — is held open, with an editorial footnote tipping toward the answer "no."

### **§ 4\. Wallowing in Fantasy — the Joy at Scientific Research: Experience-Pleasure as Presupposition of Joy as Valuing Pleasing. The Valuing Pleasing as Feeling-Apprehension**

Husserl opens with a methodological detour: just as sensations bear feelings (have feeling-toning), do not also other "experiences" or, in general, occurrences appearing in the time-stream of constituted psychic being, have feeling-toning? (Apart from the obvious that *phantasms* as presentification-modifications of sensations have their feeling-modifications as presentification-modifications.) Take *wallowing in fantasy*: pleasure-laden fantasies, joys *in* fantasy on the basis of fantasy-pleasure are "imagined." But it is "*sweet*" to be given over to such fantasy-joys. The fantasies, including the feeling-fantasies, are *experiences*: the fantasies themselves, not the fantasized. Do feeling-tonings attach to the fantasies — to the experiences of the pleasure-presentification with all its underlays?

Or are these *pleasing-acts*, joy-acts? Is this wallowing (an actual experience) analogous to *taste-wallowing* — the experiencing of the sensible pleasant taste? Naturally, if I attentively and objectivatingly (subjectivatingly) turn to the latter, I *take joy* over it. Likewise: I wallow fantasizing. If I turn to the *fantasizing as such* and to the *pleasure-feeling*, I may again have my "delight" in it — it can please me. It can also displease me: fantasy-wallowing paralyzes the will to deed, etc. Likewise in the pleasant taste, instead of taking joy (giving myself in joy to it), I can turn away in displeasing: it is perhaps *not the time* to enjoy sensible joy, etc. Do I have pleasing at fantasizing for the sake of the pleasure that hangs to it (by virtue of its content) as *experience-pleasure*, as *feeling-tone*? Likewise displeasing at fantasizing of the ugly for the sake of the *experience-displeasure* that adheres to it as such?

He continues: how is it with the joy at *scientific research*, at the progress of theoretical cognition? Living in scientific research, I am turned to its problems and matters. But my feeling is engaged. Do the experiences of theoretical research have an *experience-pleasure* that is *in principle of the same genus* as a sensation-pleasure? In reflection I can turn to these experiences. Can I not, in the turning to their feeling-toning, *enjoy* scientific research in similar way as I enjoy my eating and the pleasure of eating when hungry — i.e., take joy *at* this pleasure? Presumably yes.

But — Husserl presses — is *joy at experience-pleasure* the same as *joy at the scientific research itself*? In the former I am turned to the pleasure of the experience, of the researching; it is my joy-motive (joy-ground) and *object* at once. In the joy at scientific research: is the research my object, and is it object "for the sake of the pleasure attached to it"? Roughly in the sense that I take joy at the research because I really take joy at the *pleasure* of the research? Can I, in the end, primarily take joy *only* at the pleasure (enjoy it), and is everything else delightful only *for the sake of the pleasure*, *for the sake of the enjoyment* of the pleasure — of the *possible* enjoyment when I am not actually enjoying (turning toward it joyfully)?

He ventures: one is *sure*: *joy and pleasure inseparably belong together*. But what is to be distinguished is *experienced pleasure*, *sensed* (i.e., sensation-pleasure and experience-pleasure of any kind) on the one hand, and *joy as pleasing* on the other, which *presupposes* pleasure but *is not pleasure*. Analogously to how a perception presupposes sensation but is not sensation. Every pleasing at an object presupposes that the experience in which the object is conscious *bears an experience-pleasure*. Pleasing at a fantasy presupposes experience-pleasure of the fantasizing; pleasing at an *appearance* presupposes experience-pleasure of the appearing; pleasing at scientific research presupposes experience-pleasure at scientific research; pleasing at a food presupposes experience-pleasure of the food-perception or other "presentation." (Footnote: "actually 'appearance' — turning is not necessary.") And the food-perception has experience-pleasure according to the law that, when a "well-known" pleasure is attached to a perceptual object under certain circumstances, then *its perceiving carries experience-pleasure*.

But how then with the *perceiving of the pleasant taste itself* — the attending and objectivating turning to this taste-pleasure? We would have to say: this perceiving-look itself bears, like every objectivating of a feeling, *itself a feeling*. More precisely: every objectivation directed at a complex whole in which a feeling-factor appears that is drawn into the objectivation is itself the bearer of a feeling. So, as soon as I look toward a sensible pleasure, this looking-toward has a *pleasure-character*. And even when I look toward a *joy* (which has a pleasure-component), this looking-toward has pleasure-character.

And further: this experience-pleasure is just experience-pleasure and is not yet a *pleasing*. But it is, so to speak, *feeling-representative* for the pleasing referred to the objective — or rather for the value-character that constitutes itself in it. But where are the phenomenological evidences for this construction? It is, as it stands, surely incorrect. (The editorial footnote: Husserl bracketed the foregoing and noted in the margin: "all this is construction and false.")

One thing is beyond doubt: wherever there is pleasing, holding-as-valuable, taking-joy, etc., there are also *feelings* — and indeed feelings that as such belong to the same genus as sensation-feelings. And it is doubtless that *bare feelings* do not have "intentionality" in any further or better or other sense than *sensation*. They are experience-unities, lasting-experiencewise, but not "intentional experiences" — i.e., experiences that have "object-directedness" in any sense. *Feelings* therefore *belong essentially to the inventory of all valuing acts*, first to those in which value-objects "appear" as such, are given. Just as sensations must be there for *being-objects* to be given, to first appear. The experienced sensations enter into apprehensions; therein lies the "intentional" in the peculiar sense.

*Somehow* the experienced feelings — for there to be a pleasing, a valuing (as Wertnehmen) — likewise enter into something like *apprehensions*. And these apprehensions are *as little themselves feelings* as the objectivating apprehensions, the *being-apprehensions* in the narrower sense, are sensations. So one cannot simply say that the being-objectivations that form the "underlay" carry along feeling-characters. With the sensible taste we have as a rule a complex of moments, of which certain in their sequence and Gestalt carry a sequence and rhythm of feelings. That already points to the fact that the total objectivation will have its components, and that *especially upon them* the feeling will be *laid*. But that does not explain the "relation to" the corresponding objectivity-moments. It must just be answered (and nothing else, in line with the matter, can be demanded) by saying: a peculiar apprehension, a peculiar valuing, pleasing-"*consciousness*," a peculiar "intentionality" *enlivens* the "feeling-sensation."

Are *sensations* states? One will not say so. Sensations are *objects* in an unusual sensation-reflection, and when the gaze directs itself to them, it does *not* take them as I-states. Of common-feelings, however, one may grow doubtful. How is it with feelings? *Sensation-feelings* we do not properly find "outwardly" as states either, namely as I-states.

The architectural decision § 4 reaches is the central one of Part II: feelings are not themselves intentional. *Feelings as such* (taken purely) are *not* intentional experiences but only *experience-unities*, as little as bare sensations are intentional. What gives them intentional reach is *apprehension*: just as the sensible apprehension takes up the sensation and constitutes the being-object, so a *peculiar feeling-apprehension* takes up the feeling-sensation and constitutes the *value-object*. The *valuing pleasing is feeling-apprehension*. With this Husserl secures the doctrine he had been groping toward in §§ 5–6 of Part I: feelings are objectivating *not by themselves* but by virtue of an apprehension that *enlivens* them — and this apprehension stands in fully analogous position to thing-apprehension at the level of *being*.

### **Appendix IV. Sensation-Feeling and Object-Feeling**

The Beilage (likely September 1911, the editors note) crystallizes a distinction that has emerged in §§ 3–4: between *sensation-feeling* — the "feeling-tone" lying *in and on* the sensation itself — and *object-feeling*, which is *pleasing* (Gefallen). The "sensation-content," the sensation as a *unity*, is itself an "object": one can look toward it and grasp it as object in its unity. Then one finds *on it itself* the feeling-tone, and this is the "*motive*" of pleasing or displeasing — *for the sake of which* it pleases. *No transient apprehension is required*, and so no transient object. If such are present, to them too belongs a pleasing or displeasing, possibly *of quite different sign*. The sensation-feeling, the feeling-tone, can preserve itself in its character (perhaps not in its time-stretch, perhaps as to intensity and total-intensity-character), while the *pleasing*, the *feeling Stellungnahme*, can change, can *reverse* its sign.

The example: I eat a sweet pastry; it tastes excellent; after a while I grow weary of it, it begins to disgust me, becomes repulsive. The taste has *not* essentially changed, *but the feeling-tone in and on the "pleasant taste"* has *not* turned around either; it is perhaps no longer so intense, but according to its feeling-tone *positive*. Above all, the *eternal* sweet-eating becomes "boring"; *it no longer pleases me* despite its positive feeling-tone. The dulling here does not consist in the taste itself losing the positive feeling-tone. Naturally one will likewise distinguish, in a sweetish melody that pleases me very much at first — while afterward its triviality comes to consciousness and it begins to displease me — the *steadily sweet character* of the melody and the *pleasing grounded therein*, on the one hand, and afterward the *displeasing grounded in the triviality*. (But of course the melody is *not* a mere sensation-object.)

When we turn to the *sensation-unity*, it has *in itself* that feeling-tone, and *for the sake of this* it pleases or displeases "*immediately*." This is the *simplest, most immediate kind of grounding* of feeling-acts. The sensation pleases or displeases "*for the sake of itself*" — and that means, the ground of the feeling-acts lies *purely in the object, immanent*.

The sensation-unity "constitutes" itself, and *in and with* it the unity of the feeling-rhythm. It constitutes itself, i.e., a unity-consciousness is *experience*, regardless of whether a ray of attention and object-positing passes through or not. This "unity-consciousness" ("*apprehension*" of unity) is now itself the *bearer* of a feeling that has "relation" to *the identical, the unity*, that is apprehended. (A footnote pushes back against an obvious objection: one must *not* think that in the course of eating "Gestalt-qualities of pleasure and displeasure" form themselves, such that strongly-emerging pleasure-moments would still ground a strong displeasure-moment with respect to the *whole*. The displeasure at the whole is *displeasing of the whole* and in no way a sensation-feeling that belongs to it as Gestalt-unity of the partial feelings. We can turn to this Gestalt-unity — it is a *rhythm of feelings*, unitary, but not a pleasure or a displeasure. By contrast, *this unity* can please or displease.)

The Beilage thus makes explicit and clean the distinction §§ 3 and 4 had reached: sensation-feeling (= feeling-tone of the sensation, fused with the sensation) is *not* itself an act of pleasing; it is the *most immediate motivational ground* of pleasing as object-feeling, where the apprehended sensation-unity grounds, by virtue of its feeling-tone, an act of feeling-apprehension. Crucially, the *sign* (positive or negative) of the object-feeling (pleasing or displeasing) need *not* coincide with the *sign* of the underlying sensation-feeling: one can have *displeasure at* a sensation that still has positive sensation-feeling-tone (the sweet pastry that has become boring). This breaks the simple parallelism between sensation-feeling and object-feeling and prepares Part III, where the *spontaneity* of the act-side will be addressed in its analogy with the spontaneity of thinking.

## **Part III. The Analogy between Thought-Acts and Axiological Acts. Receptivity and Spontaneity in the Constitution of Being- and Value-Objectivities**

### **§ 1\. Affection, Apprehension, Turning-Toward, and Creative Act of the Understanding**

Husserl opens by recalling a contrast that has been recurrent in Parts I and II: *being-motivation* (Seinsmotivation) over against *value-motivation* (Wertmotivation). Being-motivation he identifies with *empirical motivation* — sensible, sensation-motivation — and stresses its *universality*: from the standpoint of the *general consciousness*, every experience also functions as "*sensation*" and is *constitutive* for empirical "being," for *nature*. But what then is the *contrary* term? Not, surely, valuing as related to nature in any given case. Or valuing as such, regardless of whether it is nature-related? Whatever else there may be alongside it, this much is clear: when valuing is set in *relation to feeling*, we may indeed say — analogously to the way in which, through (primary) sensations, *spatio-temporal* being constitutes itself, together with the apprehension-modes connected with such being — that through the feelings *interwoven with*, *founded in* the sensations, *something* constitutes itself, *namely values*.

Yet — Husserl immediately corrects himself — even *this* schema does not embrace everything. It does not embrace *thinking*, which is constitutive *as "creative" consciousness* for whole *classes of objectivities*; it does not embrace the *spontaneous valuing*, the *creative* consciousness in which *"synthetic" objectivities* arise — the "*for the sake of*," the "*in consequence of*," and so on; and it does not embrace the *desire- and will-objectities* (Begehrungs- und Willensobjektitäten). The picture of "feelings constituting values just as sensations constitute being" is too narrow: it covers only the *receptive* level on the value-side, leaving aside the whole spontaneous side that has its analogue in *thinking*. The architectural task of Part III is therefore announced: to articulate, on the value-side, the same fundamental distinction between *receptivity* and *spontaneity* that on the being-side runs between sensible apprehension and the creative acts of the understanding (judgment).

He sets up the schema explicitly. *We have*: **1\) Receptivity – Sensibility:** **a)** the *field of sensations* (which already hints at a function), of associations, of *blind motivation*, which is "*constitutive*" for *nature*, physical and psychophysical; **b)** the *field of original feeling- and drive-passivities*; and **c)** the *blind motivations belonging to them*, which are constitutive for *objectivities of higher level* — for which we have no comprehensive names: *objects of love*, *value-objects*, *deeds*, *creations* — all *blind*: e.g., the instinctive love of children and parents, nest-building, etc.

**2\) Spontaneity – Understanding:** **a)** the *creative function of thinking* and the understanding-motivations of thinking; **b)** the *creative function of valuing*; **c)** further the *creative function of wishing and willing*, with all their spontaneous motivations. The *spontaneous* lies in *I-activity* in a special sense. *Spontaneity presupposes receptivity*. (Footnote: The word "spontaneity" justifies itself by relation to the I; it means therefore *I-spontaneity*; it concerns the *acts properly so called* welling up from the I. *But the affections?*)

But, he continues, distinctions must still be drawn within the spontaneous. We must distinguish: **I.** the *primally welling spontaneity* (urquellende Spontaneität) — the acts themselves, the act-positings, the acts of understanding; **II.** the *streaming-out, after-living spontaneity* — the *transitions into passivity*, into *condition-states*; **III.** the *deposit* (Niederschlag) of the acts of understanding *in the form of secondary sensibility* — the darkened that was once bright, the passive that was once active. *Toward this latter we are receptive*: there is thus an *original receptivity* and a *spontaneity that has passed over into receptivity*.

But are not serious difficulties still left? In thinking, in valuing, willing — in short in *every spontaneous consciousness* — we have a "*seeing*" consciousness-of, *not* a *blind* one. But does *spontaneity* make for the *seeing* and *receptivity* for the *blindness*? Does the *blind* consciousness which we call "*having an appearance*," a presentation, a re-presentation, etc., *not become a seeing one through simple turning-toward* (Zuwendung)? And what *is* this simple turning-toward? Is it one of the *spontaneous acts*, is it *thinking* turning-toward, is it *being-positing*? But how, when I turn toward a *fantasy-object*? I would then have to say it is a "*quasi*\-turning"; and if I do *not* execute it as such, i.e., if in doing so I *live in the fantasy-world*, then it is an *actual* turning-toward, and thus a *positing of the fantasized as such* — an objectivating positing.

Yet much here must be clarified and divided. (A long footnote here is the editors' annotation to the manuscript: "In what follows I tried to bring the apprehension-functions as close as possible to the thinking-functions of the understanding, and tried at first to see the 'positing,' grasping, attending, traversing, explicating *apprehending* as 'execution' of the apprehension over against background-apprehending. But it turns out that there is a *cardinal* difference between the *execution* of the acts of understanding and *actual grasping*, between *grasping-functions* and *synthetic (creative) understanding-functions*. Even so, reading further is instructive.") In Husserl's *doctrine of attentional modifications*, it has emerged that *turning-toward* the object of a sensible appearance — which beforehand was conscious *without* turning — does *not* mean the *sending-into* of a ray of attention into an "appearance" that, exactly *as it was*, has now received a ray *into itself*. We must indeed speak of a *ray of turning-toward* and of a *turning-toward of the I*; but we must also say: the turning-toward is *not* (according to the simile) *mere* turning-toward; the *whole phenomenon* — and above all, no doubt, the *apprehension itself* — has undergone "*activation*"; it is *executed* apprehension (vollzogene Auffassung), whereas, with respect to *sensation*, we must continue to say that it constitutes itself *passively*: time-consciousness is not "executed." In any case, the *animating activity* in this direction is *not in the same way* of the character of *spontaneous formation*. But is it *much better* with the *thing-appearance*?

Is then this *executed* apprehension, over against the *passively* had — so to speak *suffered* — apprehension, not a *spontaneity*, indeed a *spontaneity of positing* of certain "*matter*"? Of course, an "*imperfect*" spontaneity, one that *presses toward* series of newly executed apprehensions and series of explications and relating spontaneous acts. A vast difference is already there between the layer of the *phantom-belonging apparition* and the layer of the *substantial-causal thing*, which can become conscious in really executing fashion only in *relating acts* — in which, to be sure, the real thing is *all along already apprehended as thing*, but is "*seen*" *as that* in executing fashion only when an "if-and-so" or a "because-and-so" in the *dependence* of causal "appearances" is grasped.

Are we not, then, led back to the recognition that *what we initially set down as receptivity* requires a *necessary division*? More precisely, that we must distinguish:

**1\)** *the layer of mere affections* (which contain *nothing of function*) — the layer within which the *primary contents* (as immanent temporalities, of course) constitute themselves. Here we have an *original and a priori* form, the form of all immanent experiences: *immanent time*. (Footnote: But the *constituting* takes place *through intentionality* — yet *not* through *acts proper*.)

**2\)** Over against the affections stand the *functions*: to these belong all *sensible apprehension-functions*, through which the consciousness of a *thing* arises, but also all *sensible feeling-functions* (Gefühlsauffassung), through which the consciousness of an *empirical value* arises, and what may further belong on the same level.

Now every function — and likewise what is *formed by* function — admits a *twofold experience-mode*. (Premised here, Husserl notes parenthetically, must be that every function is *essentially founded in affection* and forms with it the *unity of a functional concrete whole of the experience*. We now have such *concreta* in view, possibly under bare regard of the *moments* of affection.) A twofold mode, he says: a function can be *executed*, *enlivened by spontaneity*, "*living*" function; and it can be *non-executed*, *non-spontaneous*, *un-living* — petrified function. *In this sense* we divide *receptivity* of un-living, in-spontaneous functions from the *spontaneity* of executed functions; and *in this sense* we do *not* mix together (must keep carefully separate) *receptivity* and *affectivity* (Reception and Affection).

But this is *not yet enough*. For now a *fundamental distinction* is required *within the functions*, which we can pursue, in the parallelism between receptive and spontaneous functions, *within one sphere*, e.g., that of spontaneity alone.

"*Turning-toward*" — let us connect to this. The expressions "reception" and "receptivity" are *ambiguous*. The un-living function (function in the status of petrification, *of un-livingness*) can pass over into livingness, and living function into petrified. But not every living function is *re-animation* of a petrified one, not every petrified one has *become* petrified, not every one is *petrification of a previously living one*. These relations must be *studied*.

The *becoming-living* can be a *mere settling-into-it*, a *mere turning-toward*. So in the *whole sphere of affection*. Indeed, in the extended sense one is to speak of an affection *in the total framework of time-consciousness*: every immanent-unitary experience "*affects*" insofar as it *can be object of possible mere turning-toward and positing apprehension*. To say it "affects the inner sense" means nothing other than: a *gaze of "inner perception*," a simple looking-toward that *grasps* and *posits* what is *otherwise constituted*, occurs or can occur. The sphere of *mere affection*, the *sphere of mere sensibility*, signifies: there is a sphere of experiences, of immanent temporal contents, that *can undergo no other animation* than that as *temporal-immanent contents*, no other than the *simple turning-toward* in "*inner perception*," i.e., in *simple immanent reflection*.

Note here: it is called *perception* because immanent time-consciousness is *self-presentational*. *Simple turning-toward, simply positing, thetic grasping of what is conscious as self-present actuality*, characterizes *perception*; and if the presentation is that of the *time-constituting consciousness*, then the perception is "*inner* perception." It has its parallel immanent reflection-modes in *inner remembrance, inner fantasy*, etc.

This turning-toward of inner reflection is indeed *already* an *actus* of freedom, a *spontaneity* — but one *bound throughout* to the "*pre-givennesses*" (Vorgegebenheiten) of the inner consciousness, to what is *otherwise constituted* and which receives, by the most original law of consciousness, its *necessary a priori form of immanent time*. Behind this group of inner graspings there lies, by the way, *another* class of simple turnings-toward: the *reflections on the consciousness itself*, which constitutes the immanent contents.

Remain within the framework of immanent time-consciousness\! In the mere *turning-toward* we have a *grasping*, a *taking-out*, a simple looking-toward that is at the same time a *seeing-out*, a *grasping-out*. For *whatever is grasped there has its horizon, its environment*.

**3\)** Similarly, when we pass from sensible *sensations* to *apprehensions* — i.e., to *appearances*. The *outer appearance* is content of inner consciousness and *as such* admits an *inner turning-toward*; and insofar as it, *as that unitary immanent content*, has *layers, parts*, it admits various "*inner* perceptions." On the other hand, insofar as it is a *unity of function*, it has the character of an *intentional experience* and admits — when it was *un-living* functional unity — an *animating ray of simple turning-toward*, a *positing, grasping turning-toward*, whereby *active perception*, *outer perception* arises (similarly the parallels: remembrance, etc.). *Seeing* is nothing but the *livingness of the apprehension-function as perceptive* (likewise of the remembrance-function: *quasi-positing*, livingness of the modified function).

To the mere turning-toward, while it always remains living activity, living function, can *append itself* the *traversing in free flow of motivation-connections of various layers*, which we *ontically* designate as *looking over the object*, *traversing it with the gaze*, *looking at it from all sides*, etc. In this the object *explicates itself*. The *total function* "implies" *part-functions*, "*intentional rays*," which in turn "*point to*" series of new intentional complexes, or to series of new intentional rays in these, etc. The intentions pass over *into one another in unity* in the flow of the functional complexes; and what in the *preceding* series is "unfulfilled" intention is in the *following* fulfilled, etc. There comes also into play here the *play of "closer determinations*," namely of what was *previously only more indeterminately* intended; but also of "*other-determinations*," always within the framework of the consciousness-unity in which one and the same object continually stands. Further, the difference between the *free traversing* of a group of co-belonging perceptions (e.g., in free eye-movements), wherein the object, always presenting *new sides* perceptually, *shows itself*, and the *quite differently-natured traversing of explication*, the *individual turning to what appears in one and the same appearance*, the *individual animation of part-intentions*, which here can simultaneously undergo *closer determination* — insofar as the intention can become *richer, more determinate*, even *without new addition of perceptions* (of appearance-material).

Further: even with unaltered preservation of the appearance-stock, a *re-presenting* procedure is possible, by which the actual appearances are translated into *series of appearance-re-presentations*, and we can experience in them, even if imperfect, *fulfillment by remembering re-presentation*.

Further still: the difference between the intentional connections that belong to the constitution of the *space-phantom* and those that belong to the constitution of *the real predicates of the real subject*, in general of the *real thing*. As to the simple explication of what is given through phantoms, the *categorical judgment*, the *categorical identifying synthesis* stands; so to the explicative *unfolding* of the *reality-intentions* belonging to the *confused* apprehension — to which unfolding belongs the *modification of belief* into "*supposition*" (Annehmen) and *taking-thereto* — stands the *hypothetical* and *causal* judgment. (Footnote: "this sentence was later put in square brackets, with a question mark in the margin." — Editors' note.) That is to say: *explication is a sub-stage to judgment-synthesis* — namely, to the judgment-synthesis that "*analytically*" grasps *what "lies" in the appearing thing*, what is intended.

So in the sphere of *thing-constituting functions* we have a *quite different situation* from the sphere of *affection*, of *inner consciousness*. There, livingness or coming-to-livingness consists merely in this: that as a first "turning-toward" *an enlivening ray of inner perception is directed onto the immanent experience*, and the *animation* consists in *nothing but the looking-toward the constituted or the further-constituting itself*. What is further possible there is *explication*, an *attending to parts and moments*. Beyond that, what is *inwardly perceived* is "*there in advance*" (im Voraus da) — there *in* the perception just as before it. By contrast, in the sphere of thing-constituting functions there is, to be sure, also a certain pre-givenness — *constantly and from the start the thing stands there, and I can simply turn toward it* — but it is "there" *through the medium of the infinitely manifold play of "intentions," of actual and indicated ones*. *It appears*; and *the bare turning-toward and bare explication does not here accomplish everything*, is *not all "enlivening*." The animation makes *active meaning*, transforms the *latent into the patent, into actual meaning*; and the unfolding of the meaning can mean: on the one hand, the unfolding of *what is reell contained in it*, on the other, the *explication of the meant as such*, *as it is just meant*. It can also mean: the unfolding of *the being as it is in the sense of the meaning*, as it is *determined* (so far as the meaning is determined), eventually *as it possibly is*, and *as it would, in the further course of new and new appearances, "turn out" to be*, and eventually *really turns out*. *Meaning points to new meanings*, and in the running-off of the actual appearances the meaning *confirms itself, determines itself further, refutes itself*, etc. — all of which is to be understood appropriately, since *no predication* is yet to be executed. Thus: within these functions there occur *completely new* events.

But what kind of *spontaneity of execution* do we have here? *No longer mere turning-toward and holding-fast*, but a *manifold* of turnings-toward, *traversing and holding-through* of the unity *in dependence*: holding-through not only of the unity of the *phantom-appearing* through the flow of phantom-appearances, but of the unity of the *real*, which demands the regard to environment-changes and dependences; or *traversing of hypothetical appearance-series in re-presentation* under regard of the *hypothetically dependent* series, under maintenance of the unity of the appearing real *through* these hypothetical series.

But here too the *object is pre-given*. It is what continuously appears unitarily; it is what, in continuous holding-through, *explicates itself according to its properties*, etc. Through the spontaneity of *traversing* — the traversing of the appearance-series constitutive of the *space-shape*, of the *dependence-appearances* constitutive of *reality*, etc. — *no new objectivity is constituted*. *Everything is bound by the actual appearances* and their stock of apprehension-functions. We now contrast: *such* apprehension-functions and *those* functions as *understanding-functions* which constitute *states of affairs*, *subjects, predicates*, etc. The "*turning-toward*" *grasps* the *otherwise already constituted*. In general: "*intuition*," empirical presentation, is either *simple grasping* or *transition from first turning-toward to ever-continued grasping of what is steadily, continuously given as appearing*. The spontaneities of *self-turning, traversing* etc., *enable* the grasping of the *one*, but they *do not constitute a new* one; they only *traverse the one and the same object that was always already pre-given*. The properly *creative* acts, the *acts of understanding*, *constitute on the ground of pre-givennesses new objectivities*; in *predicative synthesis* the living meaning directed upon them *generates itself*, and generates itself *spontaneously, creatively*. *We are receptive with respect to the foundations of these acts*, but *active with respect to the constitution of the understanding-objects*. There is no "apprehending" here; rather, *apprehension* is something *passively coming to us*, something *befalling* us. We just *have* the appearance, and if the apprehension-mode changes, *appearance jumps into appearance*. *Spontaneity*, however, *does not mean willfulness*. We can, of course, willfully *fashion* things; we can also at times willfully execute ambiguous appearances in this way or that, etc. But it is a *free fashioning*, a constituting of *new-kinded* objectivities, in which the *givenness of "perception" can subsist only in this livingness of the activity* and *cannot subsist beforehand* in an *un-living* form of givenness.

Frankly, Husserl admits, he is *not yet at all satisfied* with all these descriptions. *Only one thing is clear*: the *sphere of acts of understanding* is an *essentially new* one over against the sphere of the functions of mere appearing, just as this latter is an essentially new one over against the sphere of *merely primary contents*.

It is also clear that mere outer and inner perceiving and the equally-standing *acts of clear and dark "presentation" (intuition, etc.)* already have the *various modes of belief* and (as we now see) also the *modes of supposition and taking-thereto*; and that *the bare turning-toward*, which here appears as *attention*, indeed "*animates*," *but is not yet a mode of the higher understanding-sphere*. *Mere grasping is not yet synthesis*. It is *simple thesis*. The general feature of all turning-toward, or being-turned-toward, is the mode of "*living*," of the *animation of the function* — i.e., of the *apprehension-function inspirited by the ray of activity* — whether or not, by the way, *grasping be executed as being-positing* or as hypothetical positing, etc. *I live in the simple perception, I see the thing*; *I live in the explication of the "heavy*," in which I, say, "think" the thing as *lifted* and *pressing weightily on the hand*, etc. *Toward the thing I am turned* in these *hypothetical change-modes*, etc.

But in *judgment-synthesis* I execute *forming syntheses*: *subject-formation, relation of the predicate to the subject*, etc. *Insofar as I live in all that, I am "occupied" with the state of affairs*. It is *my object*, but *not in the manner of simple turning-toward* or of the continuous unity of an apprehension. I can also say I am *turned toward the state of affairs* — but it is *not as if I were turned toward something pre-given that could already "appear" to me before the turning*. Of course, I *can subsequently turn toward* an *emerging thought, judgment-thought*. But that is *no active, executing thinking*.

All this is *not yet sufficiently worked out* — these various turnings-toward, the various ways in which objects are *properly* and *non-properly* constituted, given and not given, etc. One must, no doubt, say: *the judgment is "passed*," *executed*. Is it correct *also* to speak of an *executing* with respect to *apprehension*? *No*: it is *simply there*; *I have* the appearance. *I do not have the judgment, I judge* — I *execute* a *subjicieren, prädicieren*. In the executing of judgment I am *conscious of the state of affairs*, I am *occupied* with it — but *not* as if it *were constituted in advance and I merely turned toward it*. Of course, *after* I have judged, the judgment-experience *sinks back*; I can now turn toward the *judged*, the *already constituted, having-been-constituted*. But this is *no executing judging*. The *original consciousness of the state of affairs is the active*: the *passing-of-judgment*. The *turning-back* to the judged is already a *secondary modification*. I *need* it, of course, for new *judgment-formations* — e.g., "from this it follows" — but then the new judging, as executing, in its execution is *founded in the modified earlier judgment*, in its *having-been-executed*, no longer in *being-executed*. First I must have *established* a judgment, then I can *look thereon* and on that basis *execute new* judgments. But the *original consciousness of the thing* does *not* lose its originarity when I turn toward another, *and it does not owe its originarity to the attending*, etc.

From the foregoing we are to learn that we must *fundamentally-essentially* distinguish the *"functions" that constitute appearances* from the *properly active (spontaneous) understanding-functions*: the former are the functions of the *lower "synthesis*," the latter of the *higher, of logical synthesis*. Kant, where he speaks of *synthesis*, has indeed perhaps just the *lower* functions in view (synthesis of the imagination); on the higher level he speaks of *analytic unity*. The latter presupposes the former. The former appears to him as *the properly creative*: precisely because the latter only *unfolds* what the former has "*unconsciously*" *created*.

But — when *we* speak of "creative," we mean the *creative constituting that lies in spontaneity as such*, while the *lower synthesis* is *not creative*: insofar as *the appearance is simply something had*, and we have *no questions* as to how the imagination "*makes*" appearance out of sensation. *It makes nothing at all* insofar as it *is receptivity*. *Psychologically* and *phenomenologically-genetically* one may speak of *producing*; *phenomenologically-statically* one can only analyze the *components of the appearance and the appearance itself in its essence*. Phenomenologically-statically, however, we do find the essential difference between *apprehension* and *logically combining function*, of *logically variously forming* function.

We must, therefore, *not place on the same level* — as the foregoing makes clear — the *animation* that takes place when the *ray of attention lives in a sensing or apprehending or lives itself into one*, when before it was not in it, and *the life of the forming, self-constituting spontaneity of the logical understanding*. (Footnote: "*That is essentially a genesis*.") *The activity of spontaneous thinking can sink back into un-livingness*: that is to say, the *thought-act that was executed* has *transformed itself into an un-living, deceased thought-act*, which is no longer an *executing* thinking, but is to be *re-transformed* into an *re-executing* one. *This difference between execution and the deceased activity of non-execution that is reactivatable is essentially other than the difference between attending, actively positing apprehension and non-positing apprehension*.

To be sure, it must be considered again and again whether something common does not remain over: whether, just as we call the executing thinking, in a certain sense, *more original* than the non-executing, we must also see the *positing* apprehension as the more original over against the *non-positing*. *Positing* would be the *activation* of the belief-moment and similar moments — which seems to start out well. Or even better: the whole apprehension *gains life and loses life*, and that affects, of course, above all the *mode of consciousness* (the modalities). At any rate, what Husserl tried to work out in other manuscripts is also right: that the *synthetic act*, the logically *executed* one, stands over against the *non-executed*, which by no means becomes the executed by the ray of turning-toward that it now admits. *Every act of understanding admits a modification into a mere "presentation*," in the sense that *presentation* is an act of *simple turning-toward, grasping*. The *judgment* is *synthetic grasping*; the *nominal* positing posits *simply*, exactly as an *intuition-positing* posits simply (cf. *Logical Investigations*). In the synthetic "*grasping*" I do not, however, *properly* grasp the *state of affairs*; that I do in the *subsequent one-rayed* grasping.

The architectural yield of § 1 is decisive for Part III. Husserl has *quadrupled* the schema of receptivity vs. spontaneity inherited from Part II. *Receptivity* is itself *split*: between (a) *mere affection* — the immanent-temporal layer of primary contents that is *not yet function* at all — and (b) *function* properly so called, i.e., *apprehension* (sensible thing-apprehension, sensible feeling-apprehension), which is *founded* in affection but introduces an essentially new dimension. *Within function*, in turn, there is a *further* division between *un-living* (petrified, non-executed) and *living* (executed, animated by attention) function. And this last division — between *animated* and *un-animated* function — must *not be confused* with the division between *receptive* and *spontaneous* in the highest sense, where *spontaneity* means the *creative act of the understanding*: the *judgment*, which *constitutes* its *new objectivity* (the state of affairs) and is not merely an animation of a pre-given object. The *thing* is *pre-given* and is at most *traversed and unfolded* by the spontaneity of attention; the *state of affairs* is *not* pre-given but *generated* by the synthetic spontaneity of judging. The whole schema is therefore set up so that the *parallel* on the value-side, which the next sections will develop, can be seen with proper sharpness: there too there will be a layer of *mere affection* (sensation-feeling), a layer of *passive function* (feeling-apprehension constitutive of empirical value), and finally — and this is the philosophical novum of Part III — a layer of *creative spontaneity in the value-sphere*, analogous to judgment, in which *new value-objectivities* arise.

### **§ 2\. Theoretical Turning-Toward and Feeling-Turning-Toward**

Husserl now turns to the *parallel* sphere on the value-side. We have so far spoken — he reminds us — of the *theoretical understanding-sphere*, the *logical*, and of the *lower understanding-functions*, the *functions of empirical apprehension*. The "*turning-toward*," *simple grasping, positing*, of which we have so far spoken — whether referred to the sensibly-appearing or to *presentation in the categorial domain* (nominal presentation of states of affairs, etc.) — was *being-grasping, being-positing*. *Attention* here means essentially the same (with possible inclusion of the modifications).

*We now try to view the construction of the valuing and practical reason exactly analogously*. To the *sensed primary content* corresponds, then, the "*sensible*" *feeling*; to the *sensibly primary sensing* the *feeling-sensing* (Gefühlsempfinden); to the *apprehending as a thing* (the field of sensation-functions) corresponds the *apprehending of empirical values*, the field of the lower, the "*empirical*" feeling-functions (Gemütsfunktionen). *Does there now correspond to the simple thesis of theoretical turning-toward a simple thesis of feeling-turning-toward — to the simple looking-toward, attending-toward, a simple feeling-toward (Hinfühlen), a simple desiring-toward and willing-toward?*

The *feeling-sensing* is *founded in the primary sensing*, in the sensing of sensible contents; *empirical valuing* in an *empirical apprehending*. *Is the turning-toward-in-feeling, the "grasping" in feeling, founded in an "understanding-like" (in an extended sense understanding-like) grasping* (a *being-positing* or its modifications)? And is, then, further, *the higher reason-function of feeling founded in the higher reason-function of the understanding* (the logical)? These are *self-imposing* questions.

Just as the *non-positing* and the *positing* empirical apprehending are of *one genus*, and *both again* are of one genus with the *logical judgment-combining and \-forming*, so the *feeling-self-turning* would be a *feeling-life* that did *not* bring a *new-kinded act* to feeling — that *contains no turning-toward* — and *again* the *feeling at the understanding-level* would be of the same genus. We would then have, *everywhere*, the *lower stage* (receptivity) and the *higher* (spontaneity), only that in the new domains everything would be *founded in the primary domain*, the being-constituting one.

So I have, e.g., a sensation, and I turn toward the sensation-content, a primary content like, e.g., a tone. *It is pleasant, sensibly pleasant; it pleases me; I live in the pleasing, am turned to the tone in the pleasing*. I see a beautiful old violin, I execute the looking-at, *live in the looking-at*; I look at it. But the empirical *thing-apprehending* founds a *value-apprehending*, an *empirical valuing*. *In this I live, I have pleasure (Wohlgefallen) at the thing, am turned to it in pleasure*. The turning-toward is an *intuiting* turning-toward, but by virtue of the *founding* it is *at the same time* a *pleasing* turning-toward. But now a *being-positing* can direct itself not only to the *violin*, but also to the *pleasingness*, to the *value-predicate*. *And here is the rub*. Here, indeed, the *insufficiency of the attempted theory* shows itself.

*Either*, Husserl now says, we *separate the theoretical self-turning, the grasping, thought-positing, from the empirical thing-apprehending* — do *not* take it as a *mere mode* of the latter, but as *its own ray of activity*, which has *no special affinity to thing-apprehension*, but can also *associate itself* with feeling-apprehension, *shine into* it, *take from* it, *and that in the same way*. *Every act, one would then say, executes a spontaneous or non-spontaneous "apprehending," and into every apprehension a grasping can enter, a ray of turning-toward or taking-out can associate itself*. And if it is a *synthetic* act, then (provided the act is not from the start a theoretical act, which in every step is theoretical, just positing, grasping) *parallel to the act-steps* a *grasping* can take place, a *looking-toward what comes synthetically into "apperception," into constitution* in this act. So, e.g., when I interpret the conscious *expression* of a *questioning* or *wishing* thus: "*S may be p*" — I look toward the *wish-form*, look toward the "*may*," and *express* it; this expressing demands the *looking-toward*, which is a *grasping*. And as I can theoretically look-toward, I can then execute *further functions of the understanding*: "*it is pleasing that S is p*," "*it is to be wished*," "it may be," predicatively, etc.

*Or* we *give up the parallelism* between feeling-turning and theoretical turning, and similarly, further on, *between theoretical understanding and emotional understanding and practical reason*.

The architectural yield of § 2 is a *fork*, deliberately presented as a fork. Husserl has distilled the issue of the parallelism into its sharpest form. *On the one hand*, the analogy is highly attractive: feeling-sensation is to sensation as feeling-apprehension is to thing-apprehension; one would expect a feeling-thesis (mere turning-toward in feeling) parallel to the simple thesis of perception, and beyond it a *higher* feeling-spontaneity parallel to judgment. *On the other hand*, the very sentence "I posit the *pleasingness* (the value-predicate)" turns out to be a *theoretical* positing — a being-positing about a value — and so the "theoretical ray" *cuts across* the value-side rather than living within it as a parallel mode. Two responses are then on the table: *either* one *separates* the "ray of grasping/positing" from any specific apprehension, treating it as a *neutral* activity that can shine into either thing-apprehension or feeling-apprehension; *or* one *abandons* the parallelism altogether and concedes that thinking, valuing, willing are *not* simply three rungs of one structure. The remainder of Part III will work out the consequences of the *first* horn, but the second horn is left explicitly open.

### **§ 3\. Turning-Toward as Mode of Liveliness, Grasping, and Thought-Positing. The Constitution of Empirical and Axiological Dependencies**

The section opens with a programmatic *Inhaltsangabe* supplied by the editors: "*The fundamental difficulty. A feeling-apperception: feeling-acts objectivate, function as 'appearances,' and these have, like all appearances, modes of 'positing,' modal differences*." The whole section is organized around this rubric — Husserl is going to test whether *Gemütsapperzeption* really has the formal-modal articulation that *theoretical* apperception exhibits.

He begins with a careful terminological partition. *Two distinct senses* of "thought-positing" (Denksetzung) must be kept apart: (1) *belief* as thought-positing — *Glaube als Denksetzung* — and (2) a *hidden* "belief" (thought-positing) that is *no belief at all* but only the *mode of original actuality*. The footnote to this passage records the editors' note that Husserl later changed the "as" to "and" and remarked: "I said *belief as thought-positing*: usually belief and thinking are identified — but that is very dangerous." The point is that the original, *living*, attentive consciousness of an object does have a kind of "actuality" or "originarity" that is sometimes called belief, but this is *not* yet the predicative thesis of belief proper that figures in judgment. *A priori* to every original actuality there corresponds a *possibility* of thought-positing — of *taking-out* (Entnehmen), of *subject-positing*, etc. *Thought-positing*, in this strict sense, is *always bound to pre-givenness*, to an underlying "apprehension," to underlying acts from which it *takes out* its theme. And Husserl adds the consequence: *every act* (and "act" already says this) can in principle be the *substrate of thought-positing*, including the thought-act itself in its full concretion, since whenever it is a *thought-synthesis* it produces a *new consciousness-of* on which a further thinking can ground itself. So thinking can build on thinking indefinitely.

Husserl now puts the question that will guide the rest of § 3: is it *thereby excluded* that the *empirical thing-apprehending*, the *having of a thing-appearance*, is itself *a thinking* — a *confused* thinking that, in turning-toward, *passes over* into the *mode of clarity*? At least *a lower mode*, *a lower stage* of thinking, something *generically related* to thinking? An editor's footnote to this question records simply: "It turns out that this question is to be *affirmed*." That terse note signals where Husserl will land — but the path there is essential.

What is *first* excluded is the *crassest* version of such an identification: that the *being-grasping turning-toward* should be nothing other than the *re-actualization* of a *dark* thinking, in the manner in which a judgment, a conviction, *emerges* from the dark and is *re-executed*. In re-executing a judgment we are turned step by step to the state of affairs *as it builds itself up*; in the *stirring* (*regende*) thought, by contrast, *the turning-toward to the things is missing*. Already in this case one must distinguish *simple turning-toward to the emergent thought (the thought-content)* from *the renewal of execution*. *Reactivation of a feeling-act*, similarly, is *not* the *turning-toward to the object constituted in it*, *not* its grasping — *if* we identify grasping with *being-positing* together with *turning-toward*. The empirical thing-apprehending is therefore *not* simply the surfacing of a buried judgment.

But — and here Husserl makes the decisive move that justifies the editor's affirmation — must we *not* say: every act has its mode of *liveliness* (= turning-toward) and its mode of *un-liveliness* (background)? *In the lively-executed act we are turned toward the objective that it constitutes*: we live, simply, in the consciousness, which is in its way *consciousness-"of"*; *every* lively consciousness *is, in its mode of being-conscious, a turning-toward*. *Lively intuiting* (intuiting in which I live) is *intuiting turning-toward*; *lively feeling, valuing* is *feeling-, valuing turning-toward*; *lively desiring and willing* is *desiring turning-toward*, and so on. And *every* lively consciousness can be *substrate* of a thought-positing. Then sometimes *the intuited thing* is the thought-subject; sometimes *the pleasing* is thought-subject; and *if it is a thing-pleasing*, then *pleasingness* is among its predicates and thinking can predicate this property *of the thing*: the predicate can be *found and posited in the subject*. The *ideal possibility of predicative thought-positing* is the very thing that *makes* every act *count as* "consciousness of an object" — for the object is *properly* "object" *first in thinking*: only there is it *Seiendes* that *is* and *is so and so determined*.

The *other side* of the same coin: every act, as consciousness-of, is *appearance* in the broadest sense — *the act gives the object*; the act is the *pre-givenness* of the object that *becomes givenness in the proper sense* in thinking. *Intuitions* in particular are called *giving acts*, but this raises the question of how to *extend* the concept of intuition beyond the sensible sphere, beyond primary contents and appearing thingnesses. And *every* act, taken as a *concrete whole*, is a possible substrate: the thought-act in which thought-subject and thought-objects are objectivated *itself* constitutes, *as a whole*, a *new* object that can become the *Worüber* of a *new* thinking. Thinking thus *steps out of the row of acts* through its special function while, *as act*, it nevertheless *belongs* in that row — and this double belonging is the source of the *self-referentiality* of thinking.

Husserl now states the result with formal sharpness: *turning-toward*, as the *liveliness* in the sphere of *every* act, of *every* act-kind, has been *clearly distinguished* from *thought-positing*. But *does anything still remain of the analogy* between thought-acts and feeling-acts, will-acts? — that is, after we have *moved* the burden of "thought-positing" *off* the parallel scheme and made it instead a *secondary*, *predicative* operation that can be performed *on any* act?

Husserl introduces a further refinement: *turning-toward as the more general* must be distinguished *from the simple thesis of grasping* as it shows up, e.g., in *selective collecting* (*Kolligieren*). Within a *plurality* of lively intuitions whose objects we attentively heed, we can *in one grip* seize *two* objects (or one object as *this*); likewise, where several things please us, we can direct *one* pleasure on A *and* B; likewise *one* will-positing, one *positing-of-the-ought*, can posit A *and* B *as one* "to-be." This is *something other than* collectively grasping A and B and *while doing so* having pleasure at A *and* pleasure at B. I can also *assume* A — assume-positingly posit A — and *thereupon* B *stands as consequence*; and *now B pleases*, but *under assumption*, *in the manner of joy*. The pleasing is *not* "I rejoice" but "*I would rejoice*." Likewise: *that* A is — *that would be joyful* on account of the *pleasing thing tied to it* (which would be joyful). The example is offered as something to be *studied*: it shows that *axiological pleasing* has its own *modal* texture (positing, assuming, hypothetical) that does *not* simply derive from the grasping-thesis.

Husserl then reaches the section's empirical illustration, which prepares the parallel he is constructing. *I see a thing*; the intuition is such that the thing *looks "heavy."* I can *attach* to this intuition a *positing-presenting*, an *assuming carrying-over* of the intuition into *phantasy modifications* of content: *if I were to lift* this thing, I would feel a great pressure; *if* the thing were thrown, it would smash *these and those* things, etc. These are *empirical dependencies* coming intuitively to consciousness — dependencies in the *thing-being-sphere*: between *cause* and *effect*, between *occurrence at the thing* and its *consequences as occurrence at this very real thing as so constituted*, or between *occurrence in the assumption* and *occurrence as consequence under assumption*.

But *we have also dependencies with respect to value-determinations* (Wertbestimmtheiten), with respect to *axiological predicates of things, persons, states of affairs*. *Suppose* the thing were colored *thus and so* — *then it would be beautiful*. Suppose the English were to go to war — *that would be sad*. Suppose *this and this* came to pass — *then this would have to be done*, *then this would have to happen*, *then one of the two would be joyful*, etc. — *the one if this were so, the other if that were so*.

*We thus find analogies*, Husserl insists — *but are they mere analogies?* Hypothetical *being-connections*, connections of real *change*, *how do they constitute themselves*? In the *thing-apprehensions and their connections*: the apprehension of A and that of B and their union are so constituted that the apprehension of *change in A* shows a *unity of consciousness with change in B*, *characterized* by the fact that the change in B *appears as "necessary consequence."* And this is *already prior to predication*: just as in the positing of *togetherness*, A and B are *consciously co-existent* (e.g., really simultaneous) *before* a predication of simultaneity takes place — though that predication has its *underlay* in this unity-consciousness ("*A is simultaneous with B*") — so too *mere succession* is such a unity, and so too the *consequent-upon* (*Eintreten infolge davon*). *This* constitutes itself *through its own unity of consciousness*, i.e., through *its own form of higher-stage apprehension*. *Only afterwards* may explication and predication come in — and I form: "if A changes thus and so, B changes necessarily, *consequently*, thus and so." This holds for *causal* connections, but *also* for *spatial* ones: the apprehension of a thing is one that *lets the thing appear in spatial connection*. To its essence belongs that, when spatial form changes, these changes are *bound by the laws of geometry* — but this is a *logical expression*. *To the essence of the spatially appearing as such belong certain necessities of co-being and the dependencies thereby determined, necessities of possible change*; and insofar as everything thingly-real is *first of all* spatially formed, these necessities belong to it. — Husserl supplements: *I have the "apprehension" of simultaneity, the intuition of it, when I collectively grasp the simultaneous in its co-enduring* — *prior* to explication and predication. The latter *presupposes* such a "together." Likewise, *I have the "intuition" of causal dependence* when I grasp A and grasp B and *see* the latter run off *in the character of "consequent."* In the transition from A to B I *find* this character at B without *yet* forming a relation-judgment. *To every intuitive relation belongs such a transition-consciousness* as foundation for a relational predication.

*Now we go over to the axiological apprehensions*. (Husserl's footnote here: "*Here, every act is called 'apprehension.'*" — the term is being used in its broadest, generic sense covering both theoretical and feeling-acts.) Here too we find relatively "simple" axiological apprehensions and unities of such apprehensions in connection with, and founded in, simple sensible (thingly) apprehensions. I consider A, B, C — but *one unity of consideration* spans all of this in one and excludes other things. Likewise, I rejoice at A, B, C — *one unity of joy* goes to the whole (e.g., when I follow an interesting disputation). I rejoice at A *for the sake of B*. I rejoice at the *beautiful thought* and *go over* to the *person* who utters it, and she has *the character of beauty, of the valuable* — and of the *valuable consequent on, with reference to* … *That* then comes to predicative synthesis and to assertion in a relational judgment.

*But axiological acts also build themselves upon thought-acts.* I know that A will be B — I hear it. And now I think that *if A is B, C must be D*. *C D\!* *That* is joyful. *Now I rejoice that A will be B*. In general, to the *un-predicatively founded axiological acts there correspond predicatively founded ones*. I express, I predicate what I had before: "*if A were B, that would be beautiful*"; the *"A is B\!"* stands as *beautiful* when "A is B" has been apprehended and stands as *pleasing*, etc. The *joy*, the *valuing*, goes to the *joyful state of affairs*, and that is posited *through the medium of the proposition*; not on the *proposition* does it depend — that is *not* the object of joy — but *on the state of affairs itself*, and there again on the *joyful state of affairs as such*: it depends only on the determination that is *bearer of the pleasing*.

The section closes with the question that has been driving the whole inquiry, now sharpened: *Wherein, then, shall the analogy between thought-acts and axiological acts consist? An analogy that is presumably at the same time the analogy between true (Seiend), beautiful, good?*

The architectural yield of § 3 is profound. Husserl has carried out three coordinated moves. *First*, he has established that the *turning-toward* (Zuwendung) is the *most general* mode of *liveliness* of *any* act-kind whatsoever — a thinking, valuing, willing, intuiting all admit the same modal alternation of lively/un-lively, of being-attended and being-in-the-background. *Second*, he has *separated* this mode of liveliness from *thought-positing* in the strict sense, where thought-positing is now a *new* function that takes any lively act as its *substrate* and *generates* a *thought-objectivity* on it. The *predicative consciousness of objectivity in the strict sense* belongs to *thinking*; every *act* is an "object-consciousness" only by virtue of its *capacity to become substrate for predicative objectivation*. *Third*, on this basis, Husserl has uncovered the *parallelism of structure* he was seeking: the *empirical thingly sphere* exhibits *necessary dependencies* (causal, spatial) that *constitute themselves in the apprehensions and their unities* prior to predication, and these are subsequently *expressed* in relational judgments; *exactly so*, the *axiological sphere* exhibits its own *value-dependencies* (this is beautiful, that is sad, *if* this were so *then* that would be valuable) that *constitute themselves in feeling-apprehensions and their unities* prior to predication and are afterwards *expressed* in axiological judgments. The *founding* relation (axiological on theoretical) holds throughout, but the *higher-stage forms* of value-constitution are *not derivable from* theoretical syntheses — they are *parallel*, with their own *modal structure* (assumption, conditional valuing, "would-be-joyful"). The closing question — wherein the analogy *finally* consists, and how it links up with the classical triad of *true*, *beautiful*, *good* — is what § 4 will not exhaustively answer (the manuscript *opens* into a continuation), but will at least take a first step toward by going *beneath* the apprehensions to the *intentionality of feeling-sensibility* itself.

### **§ 4\. Feeling-Sensibility and Intentionality**

The footnote to the section heading dates the manuscript: "*Anfang November 1911*" — early November 1911, contemporary with the late lectures on inner time-consciousness and the first attempts to articulate the *Repräsentanten*\-doctrine in its full breadth.

Husserl begins from the bottom. *The deepest-lying intentionality* lies in the *inner consciousness* that *constitutes the unity in the immanent time-flux*. In it constitutes itself *the unitary, enduring, arising and passing-away lived experience*, e.g., the experience of *sensible sensation in the narrower sense* (the unity of the primary content). This is the layer of *Urbewusstsein* in its constitutive function — *not yet* an "act" in the act-theoretic sense, but the *condition* under which any act-content can be *one* enduring content at all.

*How then stands the matter with sensible feeling?* A feeling can attach itself, *as pure state*, to an enduring tone — and indeed *purely according to its content*, *independently* of the *eventual apprehension* into which the tone is woven; likewise to a color, *not* as *thing-color* but as *pure content* (color *just as it now appears*). *The immanent temporal unity of this feeling constitutes itself in inner consciousness*. It is *evident* that the *relatedness* of the feeling to the immanent primary content *at the same time means* a *unity of feeling with content in inner consciousness*: *together they form the unity of an immanent lived experience*, which constitutes itself in inner consciousness *in such a way* that the *inner constituents* of the primary content and of the feeling *cover* (*decken sich*) in inner time-consciousness — namely, that the experience-unity constitutes itself through the *steady unity of the constituents on both sides* (each constituting feeling-component is feeling *at* a determinate content-component).

Husserl now extends this picture upward. Suppose the feeling is directed *to a thing* that is conscious in an *appearance-continuity* — suppose, for simplicity, an *unchanged appearance* underlies as state-experience. Then *every feeling-component* (every immanent constituent of the feeling in inner consciousness) *unites itself with a determinate constituent of the unitary appearance*. *Every moment* (every moment constituted in inner consciousness) is *appearance-moment and feeling-moment at once*. And the *constituents of the moment* belonging to feeling-moment and appearance-moment *must cover*. *But the feeling directs itself to the appearing thing*, and perhaps not at all in respect of its color, etc. The feeling can be *feeling at the appearance itself*, in opposition to its other possibility, *feeling at the appearing thing*. In the *former* case it is *exactly as when* the feeling is feeling *at a primary content*. (Husserl notes parenthetically: *in the broader sense*, primary contents are *sensednesses*, but *so are all experiences as unities of inner consciousness*.)

*How does this difference clarify itself? — that is naturally a matter of fundamental significance.* The primary content, the sensible sensation, has *unity in inner consciousness*, and this latter has *intentionality*; *the sensible sensation as constituted unity does not*. The *primary feeling* is the feeling *immediately belonging to the primary content*, and it likewise has *no intentionality*. The *non-intentional primary content undergoes an apprehension*, becomes intentional appearance, a *state-of-being* (Zuständlichkeit), but a state of *theoretical-intentional* sphere. *Likewise the primary feeling undergoes a "feeling-apprehension," it becomes intentional feeling, which is founded in the sensible-theoretical appearance and makes possible a higher-stage appearance: so to speak, the feeling-appearance.* The apprehension consists, on the *lower* stage, in *theoretical "intentional rays,"* in what stands in the *play of theoretical motivations*, what *unites with others of its kind into agreement*, what can undergo *agreeing fulfillment* or *disagreeing cancellation*, etc.

*Feeling-apprehension, likewise, consists of emotional "intentional rays" and ray-complexes, founded by the underlying ray-complexes that form a unity of theoretical appearance.* Intentionalities have their *own ways* of uniting, of *covering*, of unitarily fusing; and at the same time *a part of the theoretical rays may remain uncovered*. (The point is significant: not every theoretical intention has its emotional counterpart — there are theoretical features of the appearance "lit up" by the appearance that do *not* find a feeling-correlate; the feeling-apprehension is "selective" in its founding.)

Husserl now poses what in turn has to be considered: *whether already in the state-sphere we have to take account of feeling-transference, whether already there, alongside the determinately founded feeling-intentionality (specifically directed to coloration, shape, and the like), one must distinguish a transferred one*: the *whole* appearance, *according to its whole content*, *bears feeling and intentional feeling*, but only *certain* of its constituting intentions have the *distinction* that the feeling-intentionality *goes "specifically"* upon their objectivity. But *here one comes into difficulty* with *cases* in which, *with respect to certain components* of the appearing, a *positive*, with respect to others a *negative* feeling is *originally* state-present. *Here much is to be studied.* This open remark notes that the lived ambivalence of feeling toward complex objects (something attractive in one feature, repellent in another) cannot be reduced to a single global feeling-tone of the appearance.

Now the decisive, programmatic question rises: *Just as the theoretical appearance is intentional apprehension of a primary content, is the feeling-appearance also, in respect of its feeling-side, intentional "apprehension" of a primary feeling?* We are moving here *entirely in the originally-sensible sphere, in the sphere of original states*, and *if we keep this in view*, then it will probably have to be said that *primary feelings must necessarily underlie feeling-intentionality* — namely, such primary feelings as are, *in the primary sphere, unitary with the primary contents*. Insofar as the *complex of primary contents*, according to its *double "Repräsentanten*," bears a *double intentionality*, this is so unitary that the *feeling-objectivity* unitarily *appears*; and now the *gaze of turning-toward* can either *go through* the founding *layer* and be directed to the *appearing theoretical object*, or *go to* the founded, the new, and, *directed to the feeling-character*, also *take the whole in one*, or *one upon the other*, *referring* the feeling-predicate *to the thing*. *Perhaps, however, one must say that it lies in the essence of theoretical turning-toward that in one ray it can properly only go through one layer*. *That is to be thoroughly thought through.*

The doctrine here — terse but exact — is the *Repräsentanten*\-doctrine *generalized to feeling*. In the standard theoretical apprehension, primary contents *function* as *Repräsentanten* of an objective property: the apprehensional moment *animates* the sensation so that *through* it the appearing thing-property is consciously given. Husserl now claims: in the feeling-apprehension, the same complex of primary contents bears *a second* function, namely, as *Repräsentanten* of a *value-property* — and this is grounded in the *primary feeling* that is *immanently one with* the primary content. The *primary feeling*, like the primary content itself, is *not yet intentional*; it acquires intentionality only by a *feeling-apprehension* that is *founded* on the theoretical apprehension of the primary content, animating *the same* immanent material *toward* a value-determination. Thus the *complex of primary contents* bears a *double intentionality* through *double Repräsentanten*. This is the point at which Part III of Hua XLIII/2 reaches the deepest formal claim: *the parallel between theoretical and feeling-apprehension is grounded not in a parallel of separate sense-fields, but in a doubling of the apprehensional function on a single immanent sense-content*. The "lower sensibility" *carries* both the appearance of the thing and the appearance of its value because the *same* primary materials are simultaneously *sensible content* and *primary feeling*, *animatable* in two essentially different but materially coincident ways.

Husserl closes the section by extending the picture, very briefly, to a *third* layer: *just as with the emotional primary contents and apprehensions, so it must stand with the primary drive-contents and their apprehensions*. The example: *the drive directed to the continuation of an agreeable tone, of an agreeable taste*, etc., and *resistance to the unpleasant*. But — and the manuscript signals here, with the editorial footnote referring to a continuation in *Ax*, "*Feeling-Consciousness — Consciousness of Feelings. Feeling as Act and as State*" (which corresponds to a later text, page 143 of the volume) — *here at once the objection arises that this is already intentionality*: there is *already a directedness-toward*, the *striving intention fulfills itself*, *finds satisfaction or dissatisfaction*. The objection is decisive: the drive-life seems *not* to admit a layer of *primary, non-intentional drive-content* analogous to primary feeling, for the very *characterization* of drive — its *directedness* and its capacity for *fulfillment/non-fulfillment* — is the mark of intentionality. The text *breaks off* on this aporia, with a reference to its continuation elsewhere in the corpus.

The architectural yield of § 4 — and with it of Part III as a whole — is this. Husserl has *not* in the end given the parallelism of theoretical and axiological reason a fully symmetrical structure: the question whether the analogy is *more than mere analogy* is left open at the end of § 3 and is approached in § 4 only by going *beneath* the apprehensions, into the layer of *immanent constitution* of feeling and primary content together. What § 4 *establishes* is that the *parallelism is grounded at the level of the Repräsentanten*: a *single immanent material* (primary content \+ primary feeling, fused in inner consciousness) bears *two* apprehensional intentionalities, whose respective objectivities (thing and value) are *constituted in coordinate parallel*, with the value-intentionality *founded* in but not *reducible to* the theoretical. The *modes of turning-toward* differ — one through the layer to the underlying object, one to the founded character — and there is even a tentative suggestion that the *theoretical* turning-toward can in *one ray* go through *only one layer*, which would mean that the *unitary grasping* of value-on-thing requires either a *synthetic act* or a *new kind of feeling-turning* not reducible to theoretical attention. And finally, the *third* parallel layer — drive-sensibility — is shown to *resist* the same articulation, since drive *seems already* intentional from its primary level upward; this is left as an open problem with an explicit cross-reference forward.

In retrospect, the three parts of Hua XLIII/2 (so far as covered here) have built a stratified phenomenology of the affective sphere. *Part I* established the *founding* relation of value-acts on intuiting thing-acts and worked out the basic distinction between *Sachbestimmtheit* and *Wertbestimmtheit*, *Gemütsapperzeption* and *Wahrnehmung*, with careful attention to what willing, joy, wish, and valuing each are *as acts* and how each relates to the thing as bearer of value. *Part II* descended *one layer further* into *sensibility*: the *intensity-differences in affected feeling and feeling-light*, the *sensible feelings* whose excitation does *not* presuppose a valuing, the *fusion of sensation-feeling with sensation-content* in the *Gefühlston*, and the *limit-cases* of phantasy-indulgence and the joy of scientific research. *Part III* has now lifted the inquiry to the highest level: the *analogy* between thought-acts and axiological acts, between true, beautiful, and good — and has carried this out by *quadrupling* the receptivity/spontaneity scheme (mere affection, animated apprehension, simple grasping-thesis, creative thought-positing), by *separating* the *liveliness*\-mode of any act from its *thought-positing*, by demonstrating that *axiological dependencies* (this would be beautiful if …, that would be sad if …) constitute themselves at *their own apprehensional level* and only afterwards become *predicative* in axiological judgment, and finally by going *beneath* both layers to the *immanent constitution* in which *primary content and primary feeling* are *unitary*, bearing *double Repräsentanten* and thereby a *double intentionality* through which the *parallel* between thing-appearance and feeling-appearance is *materially* grounded. The texts breaks off, characteristically, on the open question of whether *drive*\-intentionality fits this picture or requires its own essentially different articulation — a question that the later Husserliana texts (and Husserl's reflections on instinct, drive, Trieb-Intentionalität) will continue to pursue.
