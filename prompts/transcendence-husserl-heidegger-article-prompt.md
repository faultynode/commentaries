# Task: article on transcendence in Husserl and Heidegger

Written for: the Fable pass that drafts the article. Work in the
`commentaries` repo root. The primary texts are in the sibling `sources`
repo at `../sources/sources/` (the index's `sources/heidegger/...` and
`sources/husserl/...` pointers resolve there).

## What you are writing

A scholarly article for specialists in phenomenology on how **Husserl**
and **Heidegger** each understand *transcendence*, how each author's
understanding **develops over time** across the works the index cites,
and where the two **converge and diverge**.

Publish under `articles/`, not `commentaries/`.

### What the index is, and is not

- It is a **pointer index with the note-writer's own analysis**. The
  passages it points to are your evidence. Its theses, verdicts and
  Synthesis (A-F) are **commentary tier**: claims to test against the
  paragraphs, never authority for them. If a paragraph narrows, widens
  or embarrasses a thesis of the note, say so in the article and log it
  in the Register.
- **Its translations are the note-writer's working renderings, not an
  edition.** Never quote them as Husserl or Heidegger, never cite them
  as an English edition.
- **Its line pointers drift.** Open each, read around it, confirm the
  passage says what the note says. If not, search the file for the
  sentence and record the corrected location. If you cannot find it,
  treat it as `not-found` and do not quote.

### Hazards the index flags; honour them

1. **Husserl's *transzendent* names a class of objects** (what is not
   really contained in the experience that intends it); **Heidegger's
   names a way of being of the one who intends.** A shared word is not a
   shared concept (guide T4: treat the same word in two authors as two
   terms). The few places where Husserl gives transcendence to the
   subject side (*Ideas I* § 57, Hua 11, Cartesian Meditations § 48, as
   the index lists them) are the hinge of an honest comparison.
2. **Chronology limits "anticipation".** A text can anticipate only what
   it predates; Husserl texts of 1931 and 1936 can only *converge*. Use
   the index's three bands (before 1927; during 1927-29, including Hua
   10, which Heidegger edited; after). **Infer no influence from
   resemblance.**
3. **The Heidegger concept is a product of 1927-29.** The index treats
   GA 20 (1925) as target-not-concept and GA 17 as silent. Present the
   arc that way; do not backdate the concept.
4. **GA 9 has two layers**: the 1929 text, and later marginal notes in
   which Heidegger disowns the 1928 concept. Never present a marginal
   note as the 1929 text. The later history, "the abandonment of
   transcendence", is outside the index except through those notes;
   beyond them it is **[UNSUPPORTED - needs GA 65 ff.]**.
5. **File conditions:** GA 26 has headings promoted from line breaks and
   scrambled list numerals (do not read fake `###` lines as section
   titles); *Ideas I* German is only the OCR-noisy `recheck`
   conversion, so verify wording against the CW2 English; GA 27 is
   English only; Hua 11 `:2768` rests on an unverified date.
6. **What the index leaves unmined** (say so, per guide A3; do not fill
   from memory): the *Logical Investigations* and Husserl before 1907,
   *Ideas II*, Hua 8, 13, 14, 15, GA 61/18/19, GA 65 and later,
   Husserl's marginalia on Heidegger. "Not indexed" is not "absent".
   Never write that an author "never" says something on the index's
   silence.
7. **Third positions (Sartre, Marion, Henry) are named but unindexed.**
   Use only as a labelled foil, or not at all.

## From pointer to quotation

The article argues from the primary texts, quoted as the guide directs
(W1-W4): terms and short phrases woven into your own sentences; a full
sentence or passage only when its wording is itself the evidence, led
into and followed by what it shows (W3).

1. Pick the passages that carry the argument (the spine) from the
   Husserl and Heidegger sections. Open the file at the pointer.
2. Copy quotations out of the file. **Never retype from memory** (repo
   rule). Quote complete sentences where you quote sentences; do not
   ellipsize the part you rely on.
3. Record file, line span and section number for every quotation.
   Afterwards confirm each by searching the file for the quoted words.
4. Quote in English, and handle any original as the guide's T2, W8, C4
   and section 13 direct.

**Which English.** Use a published English translation held in
`../sources/sources/` where one exists, and name it. The corpus holds,
for instance: the CW2/CW3 *Ideas*, CW4 (time-consciousness), *Being and
Time*, GA 20, GA 24 (revised Hofstadter), GA 3 (Taft) and GA 27. Check
what is actually in the folder; do not assume. Where only the German is
held (GA 26 and GA 9 *Vom Wesen des Grundes*, Hua 2, Hua 11, Hua 1,
Hua 6 are the likely cases), do not pretend to an edition: translate the
**whole paragraph yourself** and label the citation *(my translation)*,
and log it in the Register. If a published English exists outside the
corpus, name it as **[English edition not in corpus]** and give no page.

**Cite both editions for every quotation of a primary text** (guide W7): (a) the English
edition (translator, title, year, page) and (b) the German edition
(Husserliana or Gesamtausgabe volume, page; for *Sein und Zeit* both
German and translation pagination as the files carry it; for *Ideas I*
the German page markers). Take pages only from page markers in the
files; if the English file carries German margin numbers, use them;
otherwise locate the passage in the German file. If a page cannot be
determined write `[page not located]`. **Never guess a page.** Always
give the § number. State the convention once in the first note (guide
W7), and give archive line pointers (`GA 26:1653`) in that note, saying
they are not pagination and will drift.

## What the article must deliver

Argue a thesis the index makes possible but does not state, per
article-prompt.md, and reconstruct the strongest opposing reading before
rejecting it (guide A10, M4-M8). Within that, in whatever order the
argument needs:

1. **Husserl's concept and its development**, work by work through the
   Husserl sections in date order, giving the senses it shows (the
   transcendent as not really contained; the thing's transcendence; the
   closure of consciousness; transcendence in immanence and the ego's
   own; the stream as the first transcendent; "no mystical outside";
   the other, the world) and **when each enters or shifts**. Date
   doctrinal shifts (guide T5); leave open the tensions the texts leave
   open (guide M9).
2. **Heidegger's concept and its development**, work by work through
   the Heidegger sections: GA 20's exposition and critique of immanence
   and transcendence; transcendence announced and deferred in *Sein und
   Zeit*; the two concepts under one word in GA 24; the full doctrine in
   GA 26, including its relation to intentionality; the published
   statement in GA 9 and what it drops; GA 27; GA 3 and Davos. Say
   exactly where the evidence stops.
3. **Similarities and divergences**, staged as the guide stages a
   confrontation: around one question both authors ask (A7); parallels
   first, then differences, then the root of the differences (A8); each
   author explained in terms the other could have understood (A9);
   criticism held until the reconstruction is complete (A10). Treat
   Bernet as a witness compared with the texts, not as a conclusion to
   adopt.

The arc of each author is carried by the sections that treat that
author in date order; the article does not recapitulate them. It closes
as the guide directs (A12, A13).

## Form

- Open, thesis, section heads and close per guide A1-A3, A6, A11-A13:
  numbered section heads with noun phrases, no "Introduction" or
  "Conclusion" heads, no "I argue" (X1), the textual basis and the
  omissions declared early with reasons (A3).
- Prose per guide sections 4-7: one step per paragraph (P1), the relation
  of each sentence to the last marked (P3), terms fixed at first use
  (T1-T4, T6), authors as subjects of active verbs (P8), "we" for the
  course of the argument and "I" for aims, limits and judgements (V1),
  credit before criticism (V3), what you do not know said plainly (V6).
- **Three tiers visible in the sentence:** primary (quotation with
  both-edition citation); commentary (the index's analysis, named as
  that: "the index reads him as..."); yours (borrows the authority of
  neither). The guide's W5 keeps these voices apart.
- Notes carry what the guide's N1-N4 assign them. Check how existing
  files in `articles/` format notes, for format only, never for content.
- If the Heidegger or the Husserl evidence is thinner, say so (guide V6).
- Length: **7,000-10,000 words of your own prose**, excluding
  quotations, citations and the Register.
- Dates come from the index (its bands and section headings); a date
  from anywhere else must say where.
