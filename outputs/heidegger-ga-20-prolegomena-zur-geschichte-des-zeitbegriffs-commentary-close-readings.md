# Heidegger, *Prolegomena zur Geschichte des Zeitbegriffs*: close readings of key passages

These readings take up the passages approved in the [key-passage list](../runs/heidegger-ga-20-prolegomena-zur-geschichte-des-zeitbegriffs-commentary-key-passages.md) and read them for their wording, where the [main commentary](../commentaries/heidegger/heidegger-ga-20-prolegomena-zur-geschichte-des-zeitbegriffs-commentary.md) maps the argument at equal depth. The text is the German of GA 20, the Marburg lecture course of summer 1925, edited by Petra Jaeger (Klostermann). Passages are cited by § and by the printed page, "GA 20 n". Unit references such as "§2.3" name the third paragraph of the main commentary under § 2. All English renderings are my working translations, made close enough to show the structure under discussion. They are not Theodore Kisiel's published translation (*History of the Concept of Time*), which is cited by name where it differs in a way that matters.

The German comes from a converted file that is not wholly reliable. Page numbers are taken from running heads and can be one page out. Ten pages (27, 46, 47, 85, 87, 130, 137, 301, 417, 419) were recovered from the PDF's text layer and have lost their italics, so no reading argues from the absence of emphasis on them. Line-end hyphenation in the file is joined silently. Any other damage is noted where it occurs, and none of it is repaired.

## §1, GA 20 2–3: productive logic as a leap ahead of the sciences

Reads [§1.3](../commentaries/heidegger/heidegger-ga-20-prolegomena-zur-geschichte-des-zeitbegriffs-commentary.md), third paragraph under § 1.

> Because reality, nature as well as history, can be reached only, as it were, by leaping over the sciences, this prescientific, properly philosophical disclosing of these realities becomes what I call *productive logic*: an anticipatory disclosure and conceptual penetration of possible object-domains for the sciences. Unlike traditional theory of science, it does not run along behind a determinate fact, a contingent, historically given science, and examine that science for its structure. It is a logic that leaps ahead into the primary subject-field of a possible science and, by disclosing the constitution of being of this subject-field, first makes available the basic structure of the possible object of this science. That is the process of *original* logic, as Plato and Aristotle brought it out, though only within very narrow limits. Since then the concept of logic has been buried and no longer understood.

German:

> Weil zur Wirklichkeit — Natur sowohl wie Geschichte — nur vorgedrungen werden kann gewissermaßen im Überspringen der Wissenschaften, wird dieses vorwissenschaftliche, eigentliche philosophische Erschließen dieser Wirklichkeiten zu dem, was ich als *produktive Logik* bezeichne, vorausgehende Erschließung und begriffliche Durchdringung möglicher Gegenstandsgebiete für die Wissenschaften, die nicht, wie traditionelle Wissenschaftstheorie, hinter einem bestimmten Faktum einer zufälligen, historisch vorgegebenen Wissenschaft herläuft und sie auf ihre Struktur untersucht, sondern eine Logik, die vorausspringt in das primäre Sachfeld einer möglichen Wissenschaft und durch Erschließung der Seinsverfassung dieses Sachfeldes die Grundstruktur des möglichen Gegenstandes dieser Wissenschaft erst bereitstellt. Das ist der Prozeß der *ursprünglichen* Logik, wie sie von *Plato* und *Aristoteles* herausgestellt wurde — allerdings nur in ganz engen Grenzen. Seither ist der Begriff der Logik verschüttet und nicht mehr verstanden worden.

Verbs of motion carry the passage, and they set philosophy and the theory of science on one track facing opposite ways. Reality is reached by pressing forward (*vordringen*); theory of science runs along behind (*hinter … herlaufen*); productive logic leaps ahead (*vorausspringen*); and the leap that gets philosophy there is a leaping-over (*Überspringen*) of the sciences. Behind and ahead are relative to one thing, the science as it actually exists. Theory of science comes after it; productive logic comes before it, in the subject-field from which a science is first carved. What it trails is "a determinate fact" (*Faktum*). The word is the Marburg School's own. In §4 Heidegger defines late-nineteenth-century "scientific philosophy" as philosophy that "has for its proper object the fact of science" (*das Faktum der Wissenschaft*, GA 20 21, :608). Here he attaches two adjectives to it: the fact is "contingent" and "historically given". The neo-Kantian starting point is demoted from a ground to an accident, and that demotion, not an argument, is what tells against it.

The grammar of the definition says more than its content. The prescientific disclosing "becomes what I call productive logic" (*wird … zu dem, was ich … bezeichne*). Heidegger does not claim that logic already is such a disclosing. He renames: whatever phenomenological disclosure turns into will be called logic, and the appositive then defines the new usage. The final sentences protect the renaming with a history: this was the "original" logic of Plato and Aristotle, since "buried" (*verschüttet*). In §9 the same word names the most dangerous kind of covering-up, a discovery made and then lost so that it survives only as semblance (§9.6, GA 20 119). The history of logic is thus offered as the first case of what §9 will theorize, and the decline thesis of §2 (next reading) is already at work in it.

The inference is weaker than the wording. Two paragraphs earlier, the gap between a science's object-domain and its primary subject-matter was an open suspicion; in the commentary's paraphrase, "it is not settled" that the two coincide (§1.1). Here it supports an "only": reality "can be reached only" by leaping over the sciences. The qualifier "as it were" (*gewissermaßen*) concedes that the leap is a figure. The claim about Plato and Aristotle is asserted, and the part of the course meant to bear it out, the Aristotle station of the history of the concept of time, was never delivered (§3.3). *Sein und Zeit* keeps the doctrine and hardens the history. At SZ 10, research into basic concepts "must run ahead of the positive sciences, and it *can*"; "the work of Plato and Aristotle is proof of it". It is "productive logic" in that it "as it were leaps forward" (*gleichsam vorspringt*) into a region of being. The 1925 concession "only within very narrow limits" is gone.

*Überspringen* returns later in the course with its value reversed. The tradition "overleaps" the worldhood of the world (§22, heading: *Das traditionelle Überspringen der Frage nach der Weltlichkeit der Welt*), and natural Dasein overleaps its environment (§24.5). Philosophy must leap over the sciences to reach the matters; the tradition leapt over the matters to reach a theory of them. One verb names the method's virtue in §1 and the tradition's fault in §22, and only the direction of the leap tells them apart.

## §2, GA 20 8: the history of the concept of time as a history of decline

Reads [§2.3](../commentaries/heidegger/heidegger-ga-20-prolegomena-zur-geschichte-des-zeitbegriffs-commentary.md), third paragraph under § 2.

> But then the history of the concept of time is the history of the discovery of time and the history of its conceptual interpretation; that is, this history is the history of the question of the being of beings, the history of the attempts to uncover beings in their being, carried by the understanding of time prevailing at each stage, by the stage reached in the conceptual working-out of the phenomenon of time. So the history of the concept of time is, more exactly, in the end *the history of the decline and the history of the mutilation of the ground-question* of scientific research into the being of beings: a history of the incapacity to pose the question of being radically anew and to work it out anew in its first foundations, an incapacity grounded in the being of Dasein.

German:

> Die Geschichte des Zeitbegriffs ist dann aber die Geschichte der Entdeckung der Zeit und die Geschichte ihrer begrifflichen Interpretation; d. h. diese Geschichte ist die Geschichte der Frage nach dem Sein des Seienden, die Geschichte der Versuche, das Seiende in seinem Sein zu entdecken, getragen von dem jeweiligen Verständnis der Zeit, von der jeweiligen Stufe der begrifflichen Ausarbeitung des Zeitphänomens. So ist die Geschichte des Zeitbegriffs genauer am Ende *die Geschichte des Verfalls und die Geschichte der Verstümmelung der Grundfrage* wissenschaftlicher Forschung nach dem Sein des Seienden: Geschichte des Unvermögens, die Seinsfrage radikal neu zu stellen und in ihren ersten Fundamenten neu auszuarbeiten — ein Unvermögen, das im Sein des Daseins gegründet ist.

The passage proceeds by identifications strung on "is" and "that is" (*d. h.*). Each one shifts the subject. The history of a concept is the history of a discovery. The history of the discovery of time is the history of the question of being. That history is a history of decline. And the decline is an incapacity grounded in Dasein. Nothing is argued between the links; "that is" does the work of an inference without making one. Heidegger knows this. The next sentence calls the whole account a "wholly external characterization" (*ganz äußerliche Kennzeichnung*) of time's role, to be replaced in the course by the question of why time has the function at all.

Two nouns carry the thesis. *Verfall* (decline) is the noun that becomes, as *Verfallen* (falling), a basic movedness of Dasein in §13f and §29. Here it still names the decay of a historical question, but the final clause already pins that decay to "the being of Dasein", fifteen sections before Dasein is introduced as the questioning being (§17.1). The commentary rightly calls this the first appearance of the thesis that the forgetting of being is an ontological fact about the questioner. The wording shows how the thesis enters: by a noun that will change its subject from history to Dasein while keeping its form. *Verstümmelung* (mutilation) is a bodily image. A mutilated body survives in damaged form, and so the question of being persists, maimed, inside the very concepts that block it. §13f says the same in other words: since Aristotle the question "has fallen silent, and so fallen silent that one no longer knows that it has fallen silent" (*verstummt, und zwar so verstummt, daß man nicht mehr darum weiß, daß sie verstummt ist*, GA 20 179, :2700). Kisiel's "distortion" loses this. A distortion misrepresents; a mutilation leaves the thing present but incapable, which is exactly the "incapacity" (*Unvermögen*) the colon then names.

Two qualifiers limit the claim. "More exactly" (*genauer*) concedes that the earlier identifications were loose. "In the end" (*am Ende*) places the decline at the far end of the history, read back from where it has arrived. The judgment is retrospective, made from a present that claims to see what the past could not. When Heidegger recalls the thesis at the opening of the Main Part, he weakens it. The history of the attempts to determine beings in their being "would perhaps be" (*sei vielleicht*) the history of decline (§14, GA 20 191, :2815). Reported speech and "perhaps" replace the indicative of §2. The commentary's §14.1 keeps the "perhaps"; the hedge belongs to Heidegger, not to the paraphrase. What the course offers in place of the historical survey is the critique of Husserl (§§10–13), whose result (§13f, read below at row 18) discharges the promise of the final clause: the two neglects are "not accidental negligences of the philosophers" (*nicht zufällige Nachlässigkeiten der Philosophen*, :2704); in them the history of Dasein itself reveals itself, a Dasein that falls.

## §2, GA 20 9–10: neither historical nor systematic, but phenomenological

Reads [§2.4](../commentaries/heidegger/heidegger-ga-20-prolegomena-zur-geschichte-des-zeitbegriffs-commentary.md), fourth paragraph under § 2.

> This opinion remains plausible as long as one believes that a systematic philosophical discussion is possible in a radical sense without being, in its innermost ground, historical. But should that be the case, should it turn out that precisely the ground-question of philosophical research, the question of the being of beings, compels us to press forward into an original sphere of research that lies *before* the traditional division of philosophical work into historical and systematic knowing, then the *prolegomena* to the investigation of beings in their being are to be won only by way of *history*; that is, the manner of research is *neither historical nor systematic, but phenomenological*.

German:

> Diese Meinung bleibt so lange einleuchtend, als man glaubt, eine systematische philosophische Erörterung sei in einem radikalen Sinne möglich, ohne daß sie im innersten Grunde historisch sei. Sollte das aber der Fall sein, sollte es sich ergeben, daß gerade die Grundfrage der philosophischen Forschung, die Frage nach dem Sein des Seienden, dazu zwingt, in eine ursprüngliche Forschungsphäre vorzudringen, die *vor* der traditionellen Aufteilung der philosophischen Arbeit in historisches und systematisches Erkennen liegt, dann sind die *Prolegomena* zur Erforschung des Seienden in seinem Sein nur auf dem Wege über die *Geschichte* zu gewinnen, d. h. die Forschungsart ist *weder historisch noch systematisch, sondern phänomenologisch*.

*Note on the text.* The file reads "zu." at the foot of p. 9 and resumes "gewinnen" on p. 10 (:458). The conditional "Sollte … dann sind … zu gewinnen" needs the continuation, so the full stop is a conversion artifact; it is left out of the quotation above. The italics fall on p. 9 and p. 10, neither of them a text-layer page.

On its face the passage contradicts itself. Systematic philosophy must be "in its innermost ground historical" (*historisch*). The prolegomena are to be won "only by way of history". And yet the manner of research is "*neither historical* nor systematic". The "that is" (*d. h.*) presents the last clause as a restatement of the one before it, so the way through history is said to be not historical. Two words are doing different jobs. The way runs through *Geschichte*, history as what happens, here the history of the question of being itself, and Heidegger italicizes it. What the research is not is *historisch* in the sense the paragraph before gave the word: "Historie" (historiography) as the doxographical collection of opinions about time. Those collections "remain blind" (GA 20 9). The sphere the question forces us into lies "*before*" (*vor*, italicized) the division of labour into historiographical and systematic work. "Phenomenological" names that prior sphere, not a third method beside the other two.

The first sentence, though, uses *historisch* for what systematic philosophy must be at its innermost ground, and there the word cannot mean historiography. In 1925 *historisch* still does double duty. *Sein und Zeit* separates the two senses in its terms: historicity (*Geschichtlichkeit*) for the being of what is historical, historiography (*Historie*) for the discipline. At SZ 10, the philosophically primary is "the interpretation of what is properly historical (*geschichtlich*) in its historicity", not a theory of historiographical knowledge. GA 20 has the distinction in substance but not yet in vocabulary, and the contradiction on the surface of this passage marks the place where the vocabulary is missing. Kisiel renders *historisch* throughout as "historiological". That is right for the second and third occurrences and too narrow for the first, where Heidegger means that philosophy is historical in its being, not historiographical in its method.

The syntax is conditional, and the commentary rightly keeps it so. "Should that be the case, should it turn out that…" (*Sollte … sollte es sich ergeben*) governs everything up to the colon. The phenomenological character of the research is not asserted here. It is made to depend on what the ground-question will "compel". The next paragraph sets the proof as an aim of the course, to be shown "from the thematic content of the matters" and not from an "idea of philosophy" or a "standpoint". The conditional is therefore a promissory note on the immanent critique (§§10–13) and the exposition of the question of being (§§14–17). They show that phenomenology, kept faithful to its maxim, cannot reach its own field without asking about being, and cannot ask about being without the history in which the question was mutilated (previous reading). A short paragraph closes § 2 (:462), and it is in the German edition: the historical clarification is "only didactically separated" (*nur didaktisch getrennt*) from the analysis of the phenomenon of time, which in turn prepares "historical understanding". Kisiel's translation adds one sentence to the summary paragraph that opens § 3, taken from Simon Moser's transcript: the discussion of time is "neither systematic in the traditional sense nor historiological, but phenomenological". The German edition's paragraph (:466) lacks it, so Kisiel's text states the formula twice where Jaeger's states it once.

## §4, GA 20 26: Brentano's intentional in-existence

Reads [§4.11](../commentaries/heidegger/heidegger-ga-20-prolegomena-zur-geschichte-des-zeitbegriffs-commentary.md), eleventh paragraph under § 4.

> So the question arises: what is the nature of psychic phenomena as against physical ones? Brentano poses this question in the first book of the *Psychology*. He says that psychic phenomena are distinguished from all physical ones by nothing so much as by this: that something objective dwells within them. Should there, therefore, be differences within the field of psychic phenomena, they must be differences with respect to this basic structure of dwelling-within, differences in the way in which something is objective to the experiences. These differences, in which something is objective to the various experiences (in representing the represented, in judging the judged, in willing the willed), form, according to these differences, the chief class-differences within psychic phenomena. This basic structure of the psychic, that something objective dwells within every experience, Brentano designates as *intentional in-existence*.
>
> *Intentio* is an expression of scholasticism and means as much as: *directing oneself toward*.

German:

> So entsteht die Frage: Welches ist die Natur der psychischen Phänomene gegenüber den physischen? Diese Frage stellt *Brentano* im ersten Buch der Psychologie. Er sagt, die psychischen Phänomene unterscheiden sich von allen physischen durch nichts so sehr als dadurch, daß ihnen etwas Gegenständliches innewohnt. Sollte es deshalb Unterschiede innerhalb des Feldes der psychischen Phänomene geben, so müssen es Unterschiede sein hinsichtlich dieser ihrer Grundstruktur des Innewohnens, Unterschiede in der Weise, in welcher den Erlebnissen etwas gegenständlich ist. Diese Unterschiede, in denen den verschiedenen Erlebnissen etwas gegenständlich ist, im Vorstellen das Vorgestellte, im Urteilen das Geurteilte, im Wollen das Gewoll, je nach diesen Unterschieden bilden sich die vorzüglichsten Klassenunterschiede innerhalb der psychischen Phänome. Diese Grundstruktur des Psychischen, daß jedem Erleben etwas Gegenständliches innewohnt, bezeichnet *Brentano* als *intentionale Inexistenz*.
>
> *Intentio* ist ein Ausdruck der Scholastik und besagt soviel wie: *sich richten auf*.

*Note on the text.* This is p. 26, which audits as partial, and the file drops letters here: "Gewoll" for *Gewollte* and "Phänome" for *Phänomene*, and in the preceding sentences "z-uordnenden" and "heranbring". They are quoted as the file has them. None bears on the reading. The next page, p. 27, comes from the text layer; the italics above fall on p. 26 and are not affected.

Heidegger's paraphrase turns Brentano's Latinate term into a German verb of dwelling. "In-existence" becomes "dwelling-within" (*innewohnen*, *Innewohnen*) three times before the technical name arrives. Whether *innewohnen* is Brentano's own word cannot be checked here, because the *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint* is not in the corpus. As Heidegger gives it, though, the paraphrase sets up a reversal he completes in §19. There "in" is derived from *innan*, "to dwell", and in-being means "dwelling alongside" the world (§19.3, GA 20 213). In Brentano the object dwells in the experience. In the analytic, Dasein dwells in the world. The figure of dwelling survives, and its direction turns round. What was a relation inside the psychic becomes the way the one who has a world is.

What dwells within is "something objective" (*etwas Gegenständliches*), a nominalized adjective, not "an object" (*ein Gegenstand*). The phrasing leaves open whether what is in the experience is the thing itself or the thing as it is meant. §5 makes this Brentano's failing: he "wavered between the being itself and the how of its being-grasped" (§5.10). Heidegger's wording here already holds the place of that ambiguity. Brentano's word "nature" is glossed in the same paragraph as "what a thing is, seen from itself" (*was von ihm selbst her gesehen ist*, GA 20 26). That is the formula of §9 for the phenomenon: what shows itself "from itself" (§9.5). Brentano's maxim of a "natural" classification is thus read as phenomenological before the word "phenomenology" has been explained.

The argument turns on a "therefore" (*deshalb*) that claims more than it shows. Psychic phenomena differ from physical ones by the in-dwelling of something objective. *Therefore* any differences within the psychic "must" be differences in the mode of that in-dwelling. Nothing makes it necessary that the mark which divides one class from another also articulates the class inside. The step is plausible only on the maxim just stated, that the principle of order comes from the nature of the matter, and on the assumption that this nature is a single structure. The three classes Brentano derives, representation, judgment and interest, inherit that assumption, and so does the founding thesis that every psychic phenomenon is or rests on a representation (§4.11). That thesis is what Heidegger will find in Husserl too, and will meet with the thesis that knowing is a founded mode of being-in-the-world (§20).

The gloss on *intentio*, "an expression of scholasticism", answers in advance the charge §5 rejects: that Husserl took over "a dark, metaphysical, scholastic concept" from Brentano (§5.1). Heidegger grants the provenance and reduces it to a word-meaning, "directing oneself toward". The same gloss returns in §5 with "literally" (*dem Wortsinne nach*, GA 20 37). There the plainness of the word is the point: the task is not to find something behind it but to see what so trivial-looking a meaning contains.

## §4, GA 20 31: "phenomenology is descriptive psychology"

Reads [§4.13](../commentaries/heidegger/heidegger-ga-20-prolegomena-zur-geschichte-des-zeitbegriffs-commentary.md), thirteenth paragraph under § 4.

> To blame for this misunderstanding is, in a certain way, the self-interpretation that Husserl for his part gave of his work in the introduction to this volume: "Phenomenology is descriptive psychology." This self-explication of his own work is wholly inappropriate to what is worked out in it. In other words, when Husserl wrote the introduction to these investigations, he was not able properly to survey what he had in fact laid down in this volume. Only two years later did he himself set this misinterpretation right, in the *Archiv für systematische Philosophie*, 1903.

German:

> Schuld an diesem Mißverständnis ist in gewisser Weise die Selbstinterpretation, die *Husserl* seinerseits in der Einleitung zu diesem Bande von seiner Arbeit gegeben hat: »Phänomenologie ist deskriptive Psychologie«. Diese Selbstauslegung des eigenen Werks ist dem, was darin erarbeitet wird, ganz unangemessen. Mit anderen Worten, *Husserl* war, als er die Einleitung zu diesen Untersuchungen schrieb, nicht imstande, eigentlich das zu übersehen, was er faktisch in diesem Bande vorgelegt hatte. Erst zwei Jahre später hat er diese Fehlinterpretation selbst richtig gestellt, im »Archiv für systematische Philosophie«, 1903.

The paragraph sets an author's account of his work against the work, and it sets them apart in words. What Husserl *said* is a "self-interpretation" (*Selbstinterpretation*), then a "self-explication" (*Selbstauslegung*), then a "misinterpretation" (*Fehlinterpretation*). What Husserl *did* is what he "in fact" (*faktisch*) laid down. The word *Auslegung* becomes in §28c the course's technical term for interpretation as the forming-out of understanding. Here it already names the possibility that a researcher's interpretation of himself falls short of his own understanding. This is the hermeneutic rule of the whole immanent critique, stated as a judgment on one sentence. §8b states it as method: phenomenology is to be understood "from what lies in it as task … not from what is said about it" (§8.3). Heidegger can then read the *Investigations* against their introduction, as he will read *Ideas I* against its maxim (§§10–13).

The pivot is *übersehen*. In the sentence it means "to survey": Husserl "was not able properly to survey" what he had done. The word also means "to overlook", and the course exploits that sense later. In §17 the questioning being "is first and above all overlooked" (*zuerst und vor allem übersehen*) in the very course of questioning, and the analytic resolves "not to overlook" it (GA 20 199–200, :2979). The structures match. A researcher who cannot survey what his own research contains has, in that respect, overlooked it. The passage does not make the pun, but the later course depends on the structure it describes: the questioner fails to see what lies nearest in its own activity.

Two qualifiers keep the charge in proportion. The self-interpretation is to blame "in a certain way" (*in gewisser Weise*). The other cause is the readers: the Munich circle took the work for "improved descriptive psychology", and Natorp for a relapse into psychologism. And Husserl "himself" corrected it "only two years later". The correction cuts against the critique that follows. If the formula was set right in 1903, it is hard to see why §8b has to reject the reading of phenomenology as "descriptive psychology" again (GA 20 109, :1679), or why §§10–13 find the psychic still unexamined at the base of *Ideas I*. Heidegger's answer, given only later, is that the correction changed the label and left the ground in place: the being of the intentional went on being taken from the tradition. The 1903 correction removed the name and kept the presupposition, and the immanent critique is aimed at the presupposition.

Heidegger gives the formula in guillemets as a quotation but cites no page, and he names the 1903 correction only by journal and year. The corpus holds the *Logical Investigations* in German only for volume I (Hua XVIII). The English volumes translate the second edition of 1913, in which Husserl revised the passages on the nature of phenomenology, so they do not show the first-edition wording. So the quotation and the correction cannot be checked against Husserl here. The reading rests on Heidegger's report, and the report's accuracy is not part of what it claims.
