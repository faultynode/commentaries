# Husserl, *Vorlesungen zur Phänomenologie des inneren Zeitbewußtseins*: close readings of key passages

These readings take up the passages approved in the [key-passage list](../runs/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary-key-passages.md). The [main commentary](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md) works through Part One of the lectures at equal depth. Each reading here follows one understanding that a passage carries: what Husserl means by a term or a claim, how his wording builds it, what the passage does between its neighbours, and where the understanding goes in the rest of the lectures and in the manuscripts printed with them.

The text is the German of Husserliana X, edited by Rudolf Boehm (1966): Edith Stein's redaction of the lectures, first published by Heidegger in 1928, followed by the supplements and by Part B, the supplementary texts that print Husserl's manuscripts of 1893–1917. Both parts are quoted from one converted file in `faultynode/sources` (`sources/husserl/husserl-hua-10-vorlesungen-zur-phanomenologie-des-inneren-zeitbewusstseins.md`). Passages are cited by § and by Hua X page; Part B texts by their number ("Nr. 54") and Hua X page. In Part One the file's bracketed numbers are the pages of the 1928 first edition; in Part B they are the Hua X pages, and the plain number beside them is the page of Bernet's Meiner edition. The main commentary cites § numbers only, and unit references such as "§6.7" name the seventh paragraph of the commentary under § 6. Hua X's editorial apparatus is quoted from the split-off file `husserl-texte-zur-phanomenologie-des-inneren-zeitbewusstseins.md` (Bernet's Meiner edition), and John Barnett Brough's English translation (*On the Phenomenology of the Consciousness of Internal Time*, Collected Works IV) from `husserl-cw4-on-the-phenomenlogy-of-the-consciousness-of-internal-time.md`; Brough is named wherever he is cited. All other English renderings are my working translations, kept close to the German so that its structure shows.

The file has three limits that bear on more than one reading. Its page numbers come from running heads, which sometimes sit at the foot of the page they close, so a page can be one out. It has lost Husserl's emphasis entirely: Hua X sets emphasis in spaced type, the file has none, and the apparatus records emphases restored from the manuscripts that the file does not show. No reading argues from emphasis or its absence. And §31 is missing about a page (Hua X 67–68) after the sentence on the word "now"; the reading of that passage stops where the file stops. Other damage is noted where it occurs, and none of it is repaired.

## Introduction, Hua X 4: temporal objectivity as individual objectivity

Reads [Introduction, second paragraph](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#introduction).

The lectures announce a theory of individuation under the name of a theory of time. To show how "temporal objectivity" is constituted in consciousness is, for Husserl, to show how any individual object is constituted, because what makes something this one thing, and not merely a thing of this kind, is its place in time. The Introduction does not argue for this. It states it in an apposition, and the rest of the lectures have to earn it.

> Of course, we all know what time is; it is the most familiar thing of all. But as soon as we try to give an account of time-consciousness, to set objective time and subjective time-consciousness in their right relation, and to bring ourselves to understand how temporal objectivity, individual objectivity in general, can be constituted in subjective time-consciousness; indeed, as soon as we even try to subject purely subjective time-consciousness, the phenomenological content of the experiences of time, to an analysis, we become entangled in the strangest difficulties, contradictions and confusions.

German:

> Natürlich, was Zeit ist, wissen wir alle; sie ist das Allerbekannteste. Sobald wir aber den Versuch machen, uns über das Zeitbewußtsein Rechenschaft zu geben, objektive Zeit und subjektives Zeitbewußtsein in das rechte Verhältnis zu setzen und uns zum Verständnis zu bringen, wie sich zeitliche Objektivität, individuelle Objektivität überhaupt, im subjektiven Zeitbewußtsein konstituieren kann, ja sowie wir auch nur den Versuch machen, das rein subjektive Zeitbewußtsein, den phänomenologischen Gehalt der Zeiterlebnisse einer Analyse zu unterziehen, verwickeln wir uns in die sonderbarsten Schwierigkeiten, Widersprüche, Verworrenheiten.

The equation sits between two commas, with no connective. "Temporal objectivity, individual objectivity in general" (*zeitliche Objektivität, individuelle Objektivität überhaupt*) sets the second phrase beside the first as another name for it. "In general" (*überhaupt*) widens the scope from temporal objects in the narrow sense, tones and melodies, to everything individual. Brough renders the clause "how temporal objectivity—and therefore any individual objectivity whatever—can become constituted" (CW4 L4329–4331). His "and therefore" supplies an inference the German does not draw. The German identifies, and leaves the ground of the identity unstated. That ground, that individuality is fixed by temporal position, is the thesis §31 has to prove, and Brough's connective makes it look already proved.

The sentence's syntax also ranks the tasks. Its first "as soon as" (*sobald*) governs three infinitives in a row: to give an account of time-consciousness, to relate objective to subjective time, and to understand how objectivity "can be constituted" (*konstituieren kann*). The modal verb matters. The question is not whether objects are constituted but how that is possible, which makes it a question about conditions and not about causes. Then the sentence restarts with a stronger phrase, "indeed, as soon as we even" (*ja sowie wir auch nur*). The confusion begins before the question of objectivity is reached, in "purely subjective time-consciousness" itself. The order of difficulty is the reverse of the order of the programme. Husserl names objectivity first as the goal and then admits that the ground beneath it, the analysis of time-experiences, is where the contradictions lie.

The paragraph sits between Augustine and Brentano. Augustine's confession that he knows what time is until asked supplies the opening "of course, we all know" (*natürlich … wissen wir alle*), which turns the Latin into a German commonplace. Brentano, named in the next sentence, supplies the theory that Section One takes apart. §1 then does something the Introduction's first task seems to forbid: it excludes objective time, the very term the second infinitive set in relation to consciousness. The exclusion does not drop the programme. It moves "objectivity" inside consciousness. When the problem returns at §31, the tone has its fixed place in "'objective' (be it the immanent) time" (*„objektiven“ (sei es auch die immanente) Zeit*, Hua X 64). The quotation marks and the parenthesis record what §1 did to the Introduction's word.

§31 is where the apposition is redeemed, and it is redeemed in the same structure. "The question of the constitution of the objectivity of individual temporal objects and processes" is "most closely connected" with the question of identical temporal positions: "all objectification takes place in time-consciousness; without clarification of the identity of temporal position, no clarification of the identity of an object in time can be given" (*im Zeitbewußtsein vollzieht sich alle Objektivierung; ohne Aufklärung der Identität der Zeitstelle ist auch keine Aufklärung der Identität eines Objektes in der Zeit zu geben*, Hua X 64). The Introduction's comma has become a necessary condition, and a few pages later a definition: the tone-point "in its absolute individuality" is held fast "by matter and temporal position, the latter of which first constitutes individuality" (*nach Materie und Zeitstelle, welch letztere erst Individualität konstituiert*, Hua X 66–67). Matter gives the kind; temporal position gives the individual. Section Three extends the claim downward. At §40 the immanent contents themselves "endure, they have their time, they are individual objectivities" (*sie sind individuelle Objektivitäten*, Hua X 84). By then the "individual objectivity in general" of the Introduction includes the sensations and acts that the Introduction still called "purely subjective".

Part B shows that in 1905 the equation was also a question. The convolute that holds Nr. 35, which Husserl labelled "Seefeld manuscripts and older ones on individuation" and dated to the summer holidays of 1905 (Hua X 237, note), ends by asking whether "temporality and spatiality, provided they are understood phenomenologically and not empirically-transcendently, are really full principles of individuation" (*Sind Zeitlichkeit und Räumlichkeit … wirklich volle Prinzipien der Individuation?*, Nr. 35, Hua X 253). There space stands beside time, and the "really" doubts what the lectures of the same year assert. The Introduction keeps time alone and drops the question. It stakes the lectures on a claim that the working notes held open, and §31's antinomy, "time is rigid, and yet time flows", is where that claim is tested.

## §1, Hua X 7 and note: "sensed" as a relational concept

Reads [§1.6](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#the-exclusion-of-objective-time), sixth paragraph under § 1.

"Sensed" names a function before it names a kind of content. In the 1905 text a sensed temporal datum is the stuff an apprehension animates, as sensed red is the stuff of a perceived red. The note added later keeps the definition by function and draws the consequence the 1905 text did not: if "sensed" says only that something presents, it no longer says what the presenting thing is, whether a sensuous content or something already constituted in a quite different way. And then the note drops the schema of content and apprehension altogether. The passage is the first place in the lectures where the doctrine they start from is withdrawn, and it is withdrawn in a footnote.

> If we call "sensed" a phenomenological datum that, through apprehension, makes us conscious of something objective as bodily given, which is then called objectively perceived, then in the same sense we must distinguish a "sensed" temporal item and a perceived temporal item. The latter means objective time. The former, however, is not itself objective time (or a position in objective time), but the phenomenological datum through whose empirical apperception the relation to objective time is constituted. Temporal data, or, if you will, temporal signs, are not *tempora* themselves.
>
> [Note:] "Sensed" would then be the indication of a relational concept, which in itself would say nothing about whether what is sensed is sensuous, indeed whether it is immanent at all in the sense of the sensuous; in other words, it would remain open whether what is sensed is itself already constituted, and perhaps quite otherwise than the sensuous. — But this whole distinction is best left aside; not every constitution has the schema content of apprehension — apprehension.

German:

> Nennen wir empfunden ein phänomenologisches Datum, das durch Auffassung als leibhaft gegeben ein Objektives bewußt macht, das dann objektiv wahrgenommen heißt, so haben wir in gleichem Sinne auch ein „empfundenes“ Zeitliches und ein wahrgenommenes Zeitliches zu unterscheiden. Das letztere meint die objektive Zeit. Das erstere aber ist nicht selbst objektive Zeit (oder Stelle in der objektiven Zeit), sondern das phänomenologische Datum, durch dessen empirische Apperzeption die Beziehung auf objektive Zeit sich konstituiert. Temporaldaten, wenn man will: Temporalzeichen, sind nicht *tempora* selbst.
>
> „Empfunden“ wäre dann also Anzeige eines Relationsbegriffes, der in sich nichts darüber besagen würde, ob das Empfundene sensuell, ja ob es überhaupt immanent ist im Sinne von Sensuellem, m.a.W. es bliebe offen, ob das Empfundene selbst schon konstituiert ist, und vielleicht ganz anders als das Sensuelle. — Aber dieser ganze Unterschied bleibt am besten beiseite; nicht jede Konstitution hat das Schema Auffassungsinhalt — Auffassung.

The main text defines "sensed" by a conditional: "if we call 'sensed'" (*nennen wir empfunden*) whatever makes an object conscious through apprehension. The definition is functional from the start. A datum is sensed because of what an apprehension does with it, not because of anything in the datum. The note takes the conditional at its word. Its "then" (*dann also*) answers the main text's "if": given that definition, "sensed" is "the indication of a relational concept" (*Anzeige eines Relationsbegriffes*), and a relation says nothing about the nature of its first term. The note's grammar keeps that consequence hypothetical. "Would be", "would say", "would remain open" (*wäre*, *würde*, *bliebe*) all stand in the subjunctive. The last sentence switches to the indicative and to a different kind of claim: "this whole distinction is best left aside" (*bleibt am besten beiseite*), because "not every constitution" has the schema. The hypothesis about one word turns into a judgement on the whole framework, and the dash between them carries the step. The note does not argue that the schema fails. It names the case it cannot cover, a sensed content that is "itself already constituted", and leaves the reader to see that time-consciousness is that case.

The main text's own word for temporal data shows how far the 1905 doctrine went. "Temporal signs" (*Temporalzeichen*) is coined on the model of Lotze's "local signs", which the paragraph before introduced with an ironic symmetry: "if one speaks there of local signs, one would have to speak here of quality signs". "If you will" (*wenn man will*) takes the coinage under protest. §6 says what is wrong with it: to call the data temporal signs is to have "at first only a new word" (*zunächst nur ein neues Wort*, Hua X 18), with nothing yet said about how a present sign yields a consciousness of what is no longer now. The Latin *tempora* gives the times the dignity of a technical term, and the sentence denies that the sensed data are such times. On the 1905 view the denial needs no further support, because the data are contents and time comes from the apprehension. The note's question, whether such data are "already constituted", removes that support. If the temporal "signs" are themselves temporal unities, the apprehension that was supposed to make them temporal comes too late.

The apparatus confirms that the hedge is a later stratum and shows that it is larger than the note. The note is not in the 1905 manuscript ("7,35–40 Fußnote fehlt im Ms.", Texte L885), and the quotation marks around "sensed" in the note are the editor's. But the conditional the note hangs on is also absent. For the lines that begin "If we call 'sensed'…", the manuscript reads only "In the same sense we must now also distinguish a sensed [temporal item]" (*Im gleichen Sinne haben wir nun auch zu unterscheiden ein empfundenes usw.*, 7,8–10). The quotation marks around "sensed" in "the 'sensed' temporal data", two sentences later, are not in the manuscript either (7,17). So the 1905 lecture asserted sensed temporal data outright. The later redaction made the term conditional in the text, put it in quotation marks, and withdrew the frame in the note. The commentary's "a note added later" is right but too small. The revision runs through the paragraph, and the note is its most explicit part.

The paragraph sits inside the exclusion of objective time. Before it, the parallel with space established that the visual field is "quasi-spatial" and that sensed red is called red "only equivocally". After it, sensed simultaneity is "not objective simultaneity" (*empfundenes „Zugleich“ nicht objektive Gleichzeitigkeit*), and the chalk example puts change in the phenomenon and duration in the object. The 1905 argument needs the sensed–perceived distinction to keep the two orders apart: objectivity lies "not in the 'primary' contents, but in the apprehension-characters" (Hua X 8). The note strikes at exactly this sentence. Once primary contents can be "already constituted", the line between the order of experience and the order of objects can no longer be drawn by assigning contents to one side and apprehensions to the other.

Part B shows where the doubt came from. The note on "representation" (Nr. 48, Hua X 319) calls the view that sensuous contents are simply "there" and then "apprehended in this way or that" "my original view, my theory of representation", and judges that "such an interpretation is likely to be quite untenable" (*eine solche Interpretation dürfte ganz unhaltbar sein*). The next text, which Bernet dates no earlier than September 1909 (Nr. 49, Hua X 321), uses the note's own formula and suspends it in the same way: what it says holds "whether or not we interpret the intuition of time according to the schema 'content of apprehension — apprehension'" (*ob wir die Zeitanschauung nach dem Schema „Auffassungsinhalt — Auffassung“ interpretieren oder nicht*). What constitutes the difference in time, it adds, is an "apprehension" "in a fundamentally different sense". The §1 note compresses this to a clause, and Section Three works it out. §43 keeps the word "apprehension" for the phases of the flow, as "primal apprehensions" (*Urauffassungen*, Hua X 92) borne by primal contents that are no longer constituted. The schema the note set aside returns at the bottom of the lectures, emptied of its content–apprehension structure. The note's last clause is therefore not a dismissal but a programme: the lectures go on to show which constitution lacks the schema, and what "apprehension" means there instead.

## §3, Hua X 12: duration of sensation and sensation of duration

Reads [§3.3](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#the-original-associations), third paragraph under § 3.

The maxim separates two things that every theory of time-consciousness is tempted to merge: the time an experience takes, and the time it is conscious of. That a sensation lasts is a fact about the sensation. That something is sensed as lasting is a fact about what the sensation presents. The first does not yield the second. The maxim is negative. It shows a gap, and it leaves open what fills it. Brentano fills it with a moment added by phantasy; Husserl, in the course of the lectures, fills it with the form of consciousness itself. The passage matters because both answers start from it, and because Husserl uses it against the answer it was first used to support.

> So phantasy proves itself here to be productive in a peculiar way. This is the only case in which it truly creates a new moment of representations, namely the temporal moment. So we have discovered the origin of temporal representations in the domain of phantasy. […] Many think that the question of the origin of the concept of time is to be answered no differently from that of the origin of our concepts of colours, tones and so on. Just as we sense a colour, we also sense the duration of the colour; like quality and intensity, temporal duration too is an immanent moment of sensation. […] But that is a palpable error. That the stimulus lasts does not yet mean that the sensation is sensed as lasting, but only that the sensation lasts too. Duration of sensation and sensation of duration are two different things. And it is the same with succession. Succession of sensations and sensation of succession are not the same thing.

German:

> So erweist sich die Phantasie hier in eigentümlicher Weise als produktiv. Es liegt hier der einzige Fall vor, wo sie in Wahrheit neues Moment der Vorstellungen schafft, nämlich das Zeitmoment. So haben wir auf dem Gebiet der Phantasie den Ursprung der Zeitvorstellungen entdeckt. […] Viele meinen, die Frage nach dem Ursprung des Zeitbegriffs sei nicht anders zu beantworten als die nach dem Ursprung unserer Begriffe von Farben, Tönen usw. So wie wir eine Farbe empfinden, so empfinden wir auch die Dauer der Farbe; wie Qualität und Intensität, so sei auch zeitliche Dauer ein immanentes Moment der Empfindung. […] Aber das ist ein handgreiflicher Irrtum. Damit, daß der Reiz dauert, ist noch nicht gesagt, daß die Empfindung als dauernd empfunden wird, sondern nur, daß auch die Empfindung dauert. Dauer der Empfindung und Empfindung der Dauer ist zweierlei. Und ebenso ist es bei der Sukzession. Sukzession von Empfindungen und Empfindung der Sukzession ist nicht dasselbe.

The maxim works by inverting a genitive. In "duration of sensation" (*Dauer der Empfindung*) the sensation is what lasts; in "sensation of duration" (*Empfindung der Dauer*) duration is what is sensed. The same two nouns change places, and with them the sensation passes from bearer of a property to consciousness of an object. The singular verb, "is two things" (*ist zweierlei*), sets the pair up as one formula whose halves come apart. The sentence before shows how the error arises. "Lasts" and "sensed as lasting" share a verb, and the error lets the verb slide from the sensation's existence to its content: "the sensation lasts too" (*auch die Empfindung dauert*) is all that the stimulus guarantees. The theory under attack, which puts duration beside quality and intensity as "an immanent moment of sensation", is in the subjunctive (*sei*) as reported opinion. The rebuttal is in the indicative, and the maxim states it as a distinction to be seen, not proved.

The maxim is Brentano's, in Husserl's report, and it is the premise of Brentano's theory. Because lasting does not yield the sense of lasting, something must be added, and phantasy adds it: it "truly creates a new moment", the temporal moment, "the only case" in which it is productive. The paragraph that follows the passage gives the reason for the second half of the maxim. If earlier tones simply persisted while new ones sounded, "we would have a simultaneous sum of tones, but no succession of tones in our representation" (*eine gleichzeitige Summe von Tönen, aber keine Sukzession*, Hua X 12–13). Persistence produces togetherness, not succession. The extension to acts in the next paragraph, "exactly the same objection" against those who derive the representation of duration from the duration of psychic acts, plants the problem Section Three will face: acts also last, and their lasting does not make itself conscious.

§6 turns the second half against Brentano's own answer. Original association keeps the content A in consciousness and adds a moment "past". But then A "is not past at all but has remained present"; the graded moments of original association "are all now there … and are therefore simultaneous. And yet the succession of time excludes the together" (*sind also gleichzeitig. Und doch schließt das Nacheinander der Zeit das Zugleich aus*, Hua X 18). That is the "simultaneous sum of tones" again. Brentano's added moments are present contents, and a succession of present contents, however graded, is not a consciousness of succession. The maxim that required an addition rules out an addition of contents. Its conclusion, "the form of time is neither itself temporal content nor a complex of new contents" (Hua X 19), is the maxim restated as a thesis about form.

The maxim is also older than Brentano, and the 1905 manuscript said so. Where the printed §7 introduces Herbart and Lotze, the manuscript (Texte L1000, on Hua X 19,30f.) states the maxim again in its own words, "enduring representations are not yet representations of something enduring, successive representations not yet a representation of succession" (*Dauernde Vorstellungen sind noch nicht Vorstellungen von Dauernd, sukzessive Vorstellungen noch nicht Vorstellung von Sukzession*), and quotes Lotze, *Metaphysik* §154: if b followed a there would be "a change of representations, but not yet a representation of the change". A bracket inside the quotation corrects Lotze's "semblance" (*Schein*): "better: appearance!" (*besser: Erscheinung!*). The redaction cut all of this. The printed text leaves the maxim with Brentano and gives Herbart and Lotze only the opposite doctrine, that a succession must be grasped in one indivisible act. In the manuscript the maxim and that doctrine come from the same authors. The maxim does not decide between the momentary act and the enduring one; Herbart and Lotze drew from it the first conclusion, Husserl the second.

Husserl then gives the pair a positive relation. At §7 it is "evident" that "perception of duration itself presupposes duration of perception" (*daß Wahrnehmung der Dauer selbst Dauer der Wahrnehmung voraussetzt*, Hua X 22). The genitives are inverted again, but the relation has changed from "two different things" to "presupposes". Brentano separated the halves; Husserl keeps them separate and binds them. Consciousness of duration does not come from the duration of consciousness, yet it cannot occur without it. §18 applies the maxim to memory, in notation: a succession of memories, A′ and "afterwards" B′, would give "a 'perception' of the succession of these memories, and no memory-consciousness of it" (Hua X 43). It has to be written (A–B)′. Section Three draws the last consequence. If perceiving a duration presupposes that the perceiving lasts, the question becomes how the lasting of the perceiving is itself conscious without a further consciousness that lasts. §§36–39 answer that the constituting flow is not in time in the sense of its objects, so that "we cannot say: was" of it, and that it appears to itself without a second flow. The maxim began as a refutation of sensualism. By §39 it has become the reason the lectures need a consciousness whose own duration is no longer a duration of sensations at all.

## §6, Hua X 16: the phenomenological core of original association

Reads [§6.2](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#critique), second paragraph under § 6.

Husserl takes from Brentano a datum, not a law. The law of original association says how new psychic experiences arise from given ones, and it belongs to psychology. Its "phenomenological core" is what any such law presupposes: a single consciousness that holds what is now and what is just past together, so that both appear in one. Husserl reduces the dispute with Brentano to a question about that datum: in what manner does the past appear in it? The rest of Section Two is the answer, and the way the question is posed already fixes what kind of answer will count.

> Brentano speaks of a law of original association, according to which representations of a momentary memory attach themselves to the perceptions of each moment. What is meant by this is evidently a psychological law of the new formation of psychic experiences on the basis of given psychic experiences. These experiences are psychic, they are objectified, they have their own time, and their becoming and being produced is what is spoken of. Such things belong to the domain of psychology and do not interest us here. But there is a phenomenological core in these reflections, and the following discussion will hold to that alone. Duration, succession, changes appear. What lies in this appearing? In a succession, for example, a "now" appears, and in unity with it a "past". The unity of the consciousness that intentionally spans what is present and what is past is a phenomenological datum. The question now is whether, as Brentano asserts, the past really appears in this consciousness in the manner of phantasy.

German:

> Brentano spricht von einem Gesetz ursprünglicher Assoziation, wonach sich an jeweilige Wahrnehmungen Vorstellungen eines momentanen Gedächtnisses anschließen. Gemeint ist damit offenbar ein psychologisches Gesetz der Neubildung von psychischen Erlebnissen auf Grund gegebener psychischer Erlebnisse. Diese Erlebnisse sind psychische, sie sind objektiviert, sie haben selbst ihre Zeit, und von ihrem Werden und Hervorgebrachtwerden ist die Rede. Dergleichen gehört ins Gebiet der Psychologie und interessiert uns hier nicht. Jedoch steckt ein phänomenologischer Kern in diesen Betrachtungen, und an den allein wollen sich die folgenden Ausführungen halten. Dauer, Sukzession, Veränderungen erscheinen. Was liegt in diesem Erscheinen? In einer Sukzession z.B. erscheint ein ,,Jetzt'', und in Einheit damit ein ,,Vergangen''. Die Einheit des Gegenwärtiges und Vergangenes intentional umspannenden Bewußtseins ist ein phänomenologisches Datum. Es ist nun die Frage, ob wirklich, wie Brentano es behauptet, das Vergangene in diesem Bewußtsein in der Weise der Phantasie erscheint.

The reason Husserl gives for setting the law aside is a list of three predicates: the experiences it concerns "are psychic, they are objectified, they have their own time". The third is the decisive one. An experience that has its own time is an event in the world, with a before and after measurable like any other, and a law about how such events give rise to one another explains nothing about how time appears. "Becoming and being produced" (*Werden und Hervorgebrachtwerden*) names the causal register being excluded. The exclusion has a cost the lectures will meet later. Section Three has to ask what time the time-constituting consciousness has, and it cannot answer that the flow "has its own time" in this sense without making it psychic again. The production vocabulary returns too: at §11 the primal impression is the "source-point" of the "production" of the enduring object, with both words in quotation marks.

The positive half of the passage moves from law to appearing in four short sentences. "Duration, succession, changes appear" states a fact without a theory. "What lies in this appearing?" turns it into a question of description. The answer uses the two time-words as nouns in quotation marks: a "now" (*„Jetzt“*) and a "past" (*„Vergangen“*) appear "in unity". The quotation marks make the words names of characters with which something appears, not dates. The datum is then stated as a single noun phrase built around a participle: "the unity of the consciousness that intentionally spans what is present and what is past" (*Die Einheit des Gegenwärtiges und Vergangenes intentional umspannenden Bewußtseins*). The adverb "intentionally" carries the whole correction of Brentano. The past is not present in consciousness as an extra content; it is reached by an intention that spans it. "Spans" (*umspannen*) recurs where the datum recurs. At §18 the consciousness of succession has "a temporal stretch that spans the one-after-another" (Hua X 43), and in Nr. 39 the past phases of the sounding tone are "intentionally spanned" (*intentional umspannt*, Hua X 282) together with the present point.

The final sentence keeps Brentano's terms in order to refute them. It asks whether the past appears "in the manner of phantasy" (*in der Weise der Phantasie*). A note at once widens the word: "phantasy" here covers "all presentifying acts" (Hua X 17, note). So the question is not whether the just-past is imagined but whether it is re-presented. Put so, it has a structural answer. The next paragraph shows what follows if it is: the recollection of yesterday's succession becomes "phantasies of phantasies", and the difference between the just-past and the long past disappears. §19 settles it in the vocabulary the question used: "our position on Brentano's doctrine that the origin of the apprehension of time lies in the domain of phantasy is now finally decided". Phantasy is presentification, and presentified time "necessarily refers back to originally given time, not phantasized but presented" (*nicht phantasierte, sondern präsentierte*, Hua X 45). Husserl even turns Brentano's word "origin" against him: no representation can "spring" (*„entspringen“*) from presentification, and "even the concept of phantasy does not spring from phantasy".

The commentary is right that the rest of the lectures answer this question. The close reading shows that the question already contains the answer's form. By naming the datum as a unity that "intentionally spans" two modes of appearing, Husserl has ruled out any content-based solution, Brentano's included, before the critique begins. What §§12, 17 and 19 add is the name and character of the spanning intention: retention, an "original consciousness" (§12) that is "presentative, not representative" (§17).

## §6, Hua X 19 (with §5, Hua X 15): non-real, *reell*, and where the past is not

Reads [§6.7](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#critique), seventh paragraph under § 6.

The passage turns on the difference between two German words that English renders alike. *Real* means existent, actual, belonging to reality. *Reell* (really inherent) means belonging to the experience as one of its components, as against what the experience intends. Brentano called the past "irreal": a past red is no red, as an imagined thaler is no thaler. Husserl answers that Brentano's mechanism makes the past a present and *reell* component of consciousness, and that no added moment can make such a component irreal. The reply is sound against the mechanism. But it is phrased in a word Brentano did not use, and Husserl's own theory will end up saying that the past is not *reell* in consciousness either. The close reading follows the word.

> Equally dubious is the attempt to present the past as something non-*reell*, non-existent. An added psychic moment surely cannot make irreality, cannot remove present existence. In fact the whole domain of the original associations is a present and *reell* experience. To this domain belongs the whole series of original temporal moments produced by original association, together with the other moments that belong to the temporal object.

German:

> Sehr bedenklich ist auch der Versuch, das Vergangene als ein Nichtreelles, Nichtexistierendes hinzustellen. Ein hinzutretendes psychisches Moment kann doch nicht Irrealität machen, nicht gegenwärtige Existenz fortschaffen. In der Tat ist der ganze Bereich der ursprünglichen Assoziationen ein gegenwärtiges und reelles Erlebnis. Zu diesem Bereich gehört die ganze Reihe der durch ursprüngliche Assoziation erzeugten originären Zeitmomente mitsamt den übrigen Momenten, die dem zeitlichen Gegenstand zugehören.

Brentano's words in §5 are *real* and *irreal*: "The modifying temporal predicates are, according to Brentano, irreal; only the determination of the now is real" (*Die modifizierenden Zeitprädikate sind nach Brentano irreale, real ist nur die Bestimmung des Jetzt*, Hua X 15). That is a claim about predicates. "Past" alters what it attaches to, as "imagined" does. Husserl's paraphrase changes the noun and the adjective. The predicate becomes "the past" as a thing, and "irreal" becomes "non-*reell*, non-existent" (*Nichtreelles, Nichtexistierendes*). The two negatives are set side by side as if they said the same. The next sentence uses Brentano's own word, "irreality" (*Irrealität*), and the third turns to Husserl's: the domain of original association is "a present and *reell* experience" (*ein gegenwärtiges und reelles Erlebnis*). The rebuttal works by putting both senses on one side of the ledger. What original association produces is present and *reell*, so it cannot be irreal. Against the mechanism this holds. Moments produced now and present now are now, whatever they are called. Against Brentano's thesis about predicates, that a past red is not a red, it says nothing.

The apparatus shows that the slide was in the manuscript and that the redaction smoothed it. The 1905 text of the paragraph, which the first edition moved to this place (19,1–9, Texte L1000), began differently: "Brentano does say, to be sure: past is an altering predicate, the past as such is something not *reell*, not existent. By this he surely does not mean that the past is only something present furnished with a new moment, and so real. That would be a contradiction" (*Brentano sagt allerdings: Vergangen sei ein alterierendes Prädikat, das Vergangene als solches sei ein nicht Reelles, nicht Existierendes … also Reales*). In 1905 Husserl put "not *reell*" into Brentano's mouth as the gloss on "altering predicate", and two lines later used "real" (*Reales*) for the present content. The two words were not yet held apart. The printed text drops the attribution and the "real", so that only "*reell*" is left on Husserl's side. The commentary's gloss, that *reell* "in this context means really inherent in the experience, not existent in the world", is the right sense for the printed sentence. It is not the sense the 1905 lecture consistently had.

The neighbours show why the argument needs the word anyway. The paragraph before ends the reductio: the moments of original association, if they are times, "are all now there … and are therefore simultaneous". The paragraph after draws Section One's result: temporal form is neither temporal content nor a complex of contents added to it. *Reell* is the word that makes the result general. Whatever is *reell* in consciousness is present, and so any theory that explains the past by a *reell* component, a phantasm, an after-image, a mark, will have explained a present. The past must be found elsewhere than among the experience's *reell* parts.

§12 locates it there and takes back Brentano's thesis with it. The retained tone "is not *reell* present in the retentional consciousness" (*er ist im retentionalen Bewußtsein nicht reell vorhanden*, Hua X 31). What is *reell* is the retention: "The retentional consciousness contains *reell* a consciousness of the past of the tone" (*enthält reell Vergangenheitsbewußtsein vom Ton*, Hua X 32). The analogy Husserl reaches for is Brentano's. "Just as a phantasy-tone is no tone but a phantasy of a tone … so a primarily remembered tone is in principle something other than a perceived one." That is §5's "a past tone c is no tone c", with the alteration now located in the mode of consciousness instead of in a predicate of the content. So Husserl does not refute Brentano's thesis that the past is not *reell*. He refutes the claim that original association could secure it, and then secures it himself by moving the *reell* from what is intended to the intending. The past is non-*reell*, as Brentano said, because the consciousness of it is *reell* and present.

The word carries into Section Three, where the question becomes what is *reell* in the retention itself. When the flow is said to be "not in time" (§36), and primal consciousness not "content" (§40), the *reell*–intentional distinction is pushed one level down. At §6 Husserl needed the word to stop the past being a component. By the end he needs a consciousness whose phases are *reell* and present and still intend what is no longer present. That is the double intentionality of §39.

## §6, Hua X 19–20: the form of time is not a content

Reads [§6.8](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#critique), eighth paragraph under § 6.

Section One ends with a negative definition. The form of time, the pastness, duration and succession with which something appears, is neither a temporal content nor a set of new contents attached to the old ones. The formula excludes every explanation that adds something to consciousness, and in 1905 it points to the one alternative Husserl then had: the act-characters, the ways in which consciousness apprehends its contents. The negative half survives the whole work. The positive pointer does not, and the word "form" changes its address on the way.

> We see, then, that an analysis of time-consciousness is useless that tries to make the intuitive stretch of time intelligible merely through continuously graded new moments that are somehow patched onto, or fused into, those content-moments that constitute the temporally localized objective item. In short: the form of time is neither itself temporal content, nor is it a complex of new contents somehow attaching themselves to the temporal content. Now even if Brentano did not fall into the error of reducing, in the manner of sensualism, all experiences to mere primary contents, even if he was indeed the first to recognize the radical separation into primary contents and act-characters, his theory of time shows that he nonetheless failed to take into account precisely the act-characters that are decisive for it. The question how time-consciousness is possible and to be understood remains unsolved.

German:

> Wir sehen also, daß eine Analyse des Zeitbewußtseins unbrauchbar ist, welche die intuitive Zeitstrecke bloß verständlich machen will durch kontinuierlich abgestufte neue Momente, die sich irgendwie denjenigen Inhaltsmomenten anstücken oder einschmelzen, die das zeitlich lokalisierte Gegenständliche konstituieren. Kurz gesagt: Die Zeitform ist weder selbst Zeitinhalt, noch ist sie ein Komplex neuer, an den Zeitinhalt sich irgendwie anschließender Inhalte. Wenn nun Brentano auch nicht in den Irrtum verfallen ist, in der Weise des Sensualismus alle Erlebnisse auf bloße primäre Inhalte zu reduzieren, wenn er sogar als der Erste die radikale Scheidung in primäre Inhalte und Aktcharaktere erkannt hat, so zeigt seine Zeittheorie, daß er doch gerade auf die für sie entscheidenden Aktcharaktere nicht Rücksicht genommen hat. Die Frage, wie Zeitbewußtsein möglich und zu verstehen ist, bleibt ungelöst.

The formula is built from a pair, form and content, and from two negations that close two exits. "Neither itself temporal content" (*weder selbst Zeitinhalt*) rules out that pastness is a moment beside quality and intensity, the view §3 called "a palpable error". "Nor a complex of new contents" rules out Brentano's view, that pastness is a series of phantasms added to the content. The verbs of the first sentence describe the two ways a content could be added: "patched onto" (*anstücken*) and "fused into" (*einschmelzen*). The apparatus shows that the manuscript had "attach" (*anschließen*) where the print has "patch onto" (19,13, Texte L1000). The second sentence keeps the old verb: "contents somehow attaching themselves" (*sich irgendwie anschließender Inhalte*). That is the verb of Brentano's law as §6 stated it, by which representations of momentary memory "attach themselves" (*sich … anschließen*) to each perception (Hua X 16). The formula thus names Brentano's law in its own wording and denies that what it describes could be the form of time. "Somehow" (*irgendwie*), used twice, keeps open how the adding is pictured and closes the question: however it is pictured, it adds contents.

What the formula does not say is what the form is. "Form of time" (*Zeitform*) occurs here for the first time in the lectures, and it is defined only by what it is not. The positive pointer comes in the next sentence, by way of praise and blame. Brentano was "the first" to separate primary contents from act-characters, and he failed to take into account "precisely the act-characters that are decisive" for time. The concessive syntax, "even if … even if … nonetheless" (*wenn … auch … wenn … sogar … so … doch*), makes the failure sharper by setting it against his own discovery. In 1905 the form of time therefore belongs to the act: pastness is a way of apprehending, not a thing apprehended. §§11–19 develop this in the language of apprehension and act-character. The section ends on an open question, "how time-consciousness is possible", in the modal language of the Introduction.

The negative result governs every later step. §12 applies it to after-images: a fading tone is a weak present tone, and "the intuition of the past itself cannot be picturing" (Hua X 31). §18 uses "form of time" of a succession, "a temporal form, a temporal stretch that spans the one-after-another" (Hua X 43), and keeps it on the side of what is given. But the positive pointer to act-characters is what the later strata abandon. The §1 note sets aside the schema of apprehension-content and apprehension (Hua X 7, note). Section Three replaces the act with the flow, and §37 says that what the *Logical Investigations* called an "act" is itself "a flow in which an immanent temporal unity is constituted" (Hua X 76). If acts are constituted unities in time, the form of time cannot be explained by them either. The 1905 formula excluded contents. The later strata extend the exclusion to acts.

A manuscript of 1907 to 1909 in Part B draws the consequence for the word "form" itself (Nr. 44, Hua X 296–297, a text Bernet assigns to his third group, 1907–1909). "Time is the form of all individual objectivity." But "time is not the form of the experiences, of the 'contents', that make up consciousness itself"; what belongs to absolute phenomenality is a "becoming" (*Werden*), which "is the foundation for all apprehension of time, but not that apprehension itself". Then: "The form of time is not a phansiological form in the final sense, not a form of absolute being, but only a form of 'appearances', that is, only a form of individual objects. We must say: it is not an absolute but only a categorial form" (*Sie ist keine absolute, sondern nur eine kategoriale Form*). The form that §6 refused to identify with a content is here refused to the constituting consciousness as well. It belongs to what is constituted, and the constituting has only a becoming. The word "becoming" is the one §6.2 set aside with the psychological law ("their becoming and being produced", Hua X 16). It returns at the bottom of the analysis, no longer a causal process among psychic events, and §36 will say that we lack names for it.

So the formula of §6 holds to the end, and its scope widens. In 1905 it says the form of time is not a content and points to acts. By Nr. 44 and Section Three, the form of time is not a content, not an act, and not a form of the absolute flow at all. It is the form of the objects the flow constitutes, the "individual objectivity in general" of the Introduction.

## §7, Hua X 22–23: the temporal object in the special sense

Reads [§7.2](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#interpretation-of-the-grasping-of-temporal-objects-as-momentary-grasping-and-as-enduring-act), second paragraph under § 7.

A temporal object "in the special sense" is an object that has its duration inside it: not a thing that happens to last, but a tone-in-its-duration, a melody, a change, a persisting taken as persisting. Such an object cannot be given in a point, and the passage draws two consequences from that. The perceiving of it must itself last. And no division of the object into parts saves the momentary view, because every part, down to the single tone, is again a temporal object. Section Two begins from this object. The passage defines it and then shows that the problem it poses reappears at every scale.

> It is indeed evident that the perception of a temporal object itself has temporality, that perception of duration itself presupposes duration of perception, that the perception of any temporal shape itself has its temporal shape. And if we abstract from all transcendences, perception retains, in all its phenomenological constituents, its phenomenological temporality, which belongs to its essence and cannot be removed. Since objective temporality is in each case constituted phenomenologically, and stands there appearingly for us as objectivity or as a moment of an objectivity only through this constitution, a phenomenological analysis of time cannot clarify the constitution of time without regard to the constitution of temporal objects.
>
> By temporal objects in the special sense we understand objects that are not only unities in time but also contain temporal extension in themselves. When a tone sounds, my objectivating apprehension can make the tone, which lasts there and dies away, into its object, and yet not the duration of the tone or the tone in its duration. The latter as such is a temporal object. […] Each tone itself has a temporal extension: when it is struck I hear it as now, but as it sounds on it has an ever new now, and each preceding now changes into a past. So at any time I hear only the actual phase of the tone, and the objectivity of the whole enduring tone is constituted in an act-continuum that is in part memory, in a smallest, punctual part perception, and in a further part expectation.

German:

> Es ist ja evident, daß die Wahrnehmung eines zeitlichen Objektes selbst Zeitlichkeit hat, daß Wahrnehmung der Dauer selbst Dauer der Wahrnehmung voraussetzt, daß die Wahrnehmung einer beliebigen Zeitgestalt selbst ihre Zeitgestalt hat. Und sehen wir von allen Transzendenzen ab, so verbleibt der Wahrnehmung nach allen ihren phänomenologischen Konstituenten ihre phänomenologische Zeitlichkeit, die zu ihrem unaufhebbaren Wesen gehört. Da sich objektive Zeitlichkeit jeweils phänomenologisch konstituiert und nur durch diese Konstitution für uns als Objektivität oder Moment einer Objektivität erscheinungsmäßig dasteht, so kann eine phänomenologische Zeitanalyse die Konstitution der Zeit nicht ohne Rücksicht auf die Konstitution der Zeitobjekte aufklären.
>
> Unter Zeitobjekten im speziellen Sinn verstehen wir Objekte, die nicht nur Einheiten in der Zeit sind, sondern die Zeitextension auch in sich enthalten. Wenn ein Ton erklingt, so kann meine objektivierende Auffassung sich den Ton, welcher da dauert und verklingt, zum Gegenstand machen, und doch nicht die Dauer des Tones oder den Ton in seiner Dauer. Dieser als solcher ist ein Zeitobjekt. […] Jeder Ton hat selbst eine zeitliche Extension, beim Anschlagen höre ich ihn als jetzt, beim Forttönen hat er aber ein immer neues Jetzt, und das jeweilig vorangehende wandelt sich in ein Vergangen. Also höre ich jeweils nur die aktuelle Phase des Tones, und die Objektivität des ganzen dauernden Tones konstituiert sich in einem Aktkontinuum, das zu einem Teil Erinnerung, zu einem kleinsten, punktuellen Teil Wahrnehmung und zu einem weiteren Teil Erwartung ist.

*Note on the text.* In the file the first sentence of the passage does not begin a line. The paragraph starts in the middle of src L248, after the text of a long footnote quoting Stern, which breaks off mid-word ("eines einheitlichen Be-") and resumes at L252 ("wußtseinsaktes sind"). The main text runs on from L248 to L256 ("einer Objektivität erscheinungsmäßig dasteht"). The quotation above gives the main text only, in its own order. No wording is changed.

The definition rests on a contrast of prepositions: objects that are "not only unities in time" (*Einheiten in der Zeit*) but "contain temporal extension in themselves" (*die Zeitextension auch in sich enthalten*). Any object is in time. A temporal object in the special sense has time in it, as a property of what it is. The tone example makes the distinction a matter of where attention goes, not of which objects exist. The same sounding tone can be my object without its duration, and then it is a unity in time; or "the tone in its duration" can be my object, and "this as such" (*dieser als solcher*) is the temporal object. The definition picks out an aspect under which anything enduring can be taken. That matters for what follows. The problem Section Two studies is not confined to music. It is the problem of any duration taken as duration.

The first paragraph argues from this object to the perceiving. Its three clauses repeat one structure: the perception "of" a temporal item "itself" has the corresponding temporal character. The middle one is the inversion of §3's maxim: "perception of duration itself presupposes duration of perception". Brentano's pair, which separated the two, is here bound by "presupposes" (*voraussetzt*). And "evident" carries the claim. The apparatus shows that the evidence was once argued for. From this sentence on, the printed text rests on two sheets that the editor judges were written somewhat before the lectures of February 1905, and there the sentence ran: "It is evident, for this obviously belongs to the essence of the situation" (*Es ist evident, denn das gehört offenbar zum Wesen der Sachlage*, 22,21, Texte L1085). The manuscript sheet begins mid-sentence, with a colour that "stands there as enduringly the same colour", and adds that "the same holds of the apprehensions and of the moment of attention". So in the sheet the presupposition was the last step of a list that already put apprehensions and attention in time. The redaction cut the list and the "for", and the sentences before it, from "What blocks clarification" to "cannot be solved without the others" (22,1–21), are not in the manuscript at all. The editor suggests Edith Stein wrote them.

The second paragraph runs the melody argument and turns it against itself. "Hearing is perceiving", yet when the second tone sounds the first is no longer heard. The natural reply, that memory supplies the past part and expectation the rest, is set out in the voice of a hearer "inclined to say" so. Then comes the decisive "but": "all that has been said transfers to the single tone" (*alles Gesagte überträgt sich auch auf den einzelnen Ton*). The melody's problem is not that it has several tones. It is that each tone, too, is a temporal object, so that the split into memory, perception and expectation recurs inside every tone, and perception shrinks to "a smallest, punctual part" (*einem kleinsten, punktuellen Teil*). The superlative shows where the regress ends: at a point, which cannot be heard. The printed text then adds "That seems to go back to Brentano's doctrine. Here a deeper analysis must begin". The apparatus records that these sentences are not in the manuscript (23,33–35). In the sheet, the act-continuum of memory, perception and expectation was Husserl's own account, and it went on to distinguish "a productive and a reproductive phantasy", the first "producing the temporal spread" (Texte L1085–L1086). Only then did the sheet say that this view "is not workable". The redaction turned an early stage of Husserl's own thinking into a doctrine of Brentano's.

The passage sets up Section Two by fixing its object and its difficulty. §8 begins by excluding transcendence and taking "the tone purely as hyletic datum" (Hua X 24–25): the immanent temporal object, the case where the perceiving and the perceived are both in the same immanent time. The temporal object in the special sense now has two levels, transcendent and immanent, and the second is chosen because there the question of the first paragraph cannot be avoided. If perception of duration presupposes duration of perception, then the immanent tone and its perceiving both last, and the question is how the lasting of the perceiving is itself conscious. §§34–39 take that question up when they distinguish the immanent temporal object from the flow that constitutes it. At §45 the definition is used to mark a limit: there are "non-temporal transcendencies", such as the mathematical state of affairs a judgement is about, which "is nothing temporal" (*ist kein Zeitliches*, Hua X 96). The phrase "in the special sense" was the first sign of that limit. Temporal extension belongs to some objects and not to others, and the theory of time-consciousness is the theory of those that have it.

## §8, Hua X 24–25: "retention" among the quoted words

Reads [§8.1](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#immanent-temporal-objects-and-their-modes-of-appearance), first paragraph under § 8.

Retention is the holding-fast of what has just been, inside the same consciousness that is now aware of something new. It is not a memory that comes back, and not an image left behind. The tone that has ended is "still" conscious, "for a while", as having been. §8 introduces the term by deriving it from everyday words for holding and lasting, each set in quotation marks. The quotation marks do two jobs. They mark ordinary words used in a strict sense, and they mark the place where a technical term is being made. The manuscript behind §8 shows that the second job was done by the redaction.

> We now exclude all transcendent apprehension and positing and take the tone purely as hyletic datum. It begins and ends, and its whole unity of duration, the unity of the whole process in which it begins and ends, "moves back" after the ending into an ever more distant past. In this sinking back I "hold" it still fast, have it in a "retention", and as long as the retention lasts, the tone has its own temporality, it is the same, its duration is the same. […] "During" this whole flow of consciousness the one and same tone is conscious as enduring, as enduring now. "Before" (if it was not, say, expected) it is not conscious. "Afterwards" it is "for a while" "still" conscious in the "retention" as having been; it can be held fast and stand, or remain, in the fixing regard. The whole stretch of the tone's duration, or "the" tone in its extension, then stands there as something dead, so to speak, no longer producing itself in a living way, a formation animated by no producing point of the now, but which continuously modifies itself and sinks back into "emptiness".

German:

> Wir schalten jetzt alle transzendente Auffassung und Setzung aus und nehmen den Ton rein als hyletisches Datum. Er fängt an und hört auf, und seine ganze Dauereinheit, die Einheit des ganzen Vorgangs, in dem er anfängt und endet, „rückt“ nach dem Enden in die immer fernere Vergangenheit. In diesem Zurücksinken „halte“ ich ihn noch fest, habe ihn in einer „Retention“, und solange sie anhält, hat er seine eigene Zeitlichkeit, ist er derselbe, seine Dauer ist dieselbe. […] „Während“ dieses ganzen Bewußtseinsflusses ist der eine und selbe Ton als dauernder bewußt, als jetzt dauernder. „Vorher“ (falls er nicht etwa erwarteter war) ist er nicht bewußt. „Nachher“ ist er „eine Zeitlang“ in der „Retention“ als gewesener „noch“ bewußt, er kann festgehalten und im fixierenden Blick stehend bzw. bleibend sein. Die ganze Dauerstrecke des Tones oder „der“ Ton in seiner Erstreckung steht dann als ein sozusagen Totes, sich nicht mehr lebendig Erzeugendes da, ein von keinem Erzeugungspunkt des Jetzt beseeltes Gebilde, das aber stetig sich modifiziert und ins „Leere“ zurücksinkt.

The definition runs through a chain of words for holding. "In this sinking back I 'hold' it still fast" (*„halte“ ich ihn noch fest*): the quotation marks fall on the verb, not on *fest* (fast) or *noch* (still), so that the ordinary phrase *festhalten* (hold fast) is split and its core marked. "Have it in a 'retention'" (*habe ihn in einer „Retention“*) then gives the Latin for the same act: *retinere* is to hold back. The term is the German verb translated into Latin, and the sentence lays the two side by side. The second set of quotation marks extends the chain into time: "for a while" (*„eine Zeitlang“*), "still" (*„noch“*), "afterwards" (*„Nachher“*), "during" (*„Während“*), "before" (*„Vorher“*). These are the particles of everyday temporal speech, each marked as now meaning a mode of consciousness rather than a date. "Still" carries the most. A tone "still" conscious is not present, not yet gone, and not brought back. Retention is the "still".

One clause makes retention itself a temporal process: "as long as it lasts" (*solange sie anhält*). The tone "has its own temporality" only within that lasting. So retention does not preserve the past forever. It holds it "for a while" and lets it sink back "into 'emptiness'" (*ins „Leere“*). The end of the paragraph sharpens this with the image of death. The elapsed stretch stands there "as something dead, so to speak" (*ein sozusagen Totes*), "no longer producing itself in a living way" (*sich nicht mehr lebendig Erzeugendes*), "animated by no producing point of the now". Production belongs to the now. What is retained no longer produces itself, but it still "continuously modifies itself". Retention is therefore not a static holding. It is the continuous change of what is held, and the paragraph's last sentence says that this change is "essentially identical" to the one the elapsed part undergoes while the tone still sounds. The same modification runs before and after the end of the tone.

The editor's note rests §§8–10 on a manuscript of 10–13 November 1911, which Part B prints in full as Nr. 53 (Hua X 359 ff.). Set beside it, Stein's redaction shows its hand at the term. The manuscript has no quoted "hold" and no quoted "retention" at this point. It says plainly: "it keeps its own temporality as long as I can follow it in retention" (*Seine eigene Zeitlichkeit behält er, solange ich ihn in der Retention verfolgen kann*, Nr. 53, Hua X 359). In 1911 "retention" was an established word that needed no introduction. The staged derivation, from "hold" to "retention", is the redaction's, written so that the term appears to be coined where the printed lectures first use it. The later string of quoted particles is Husserl's own: the manuscript has "'for a while' in the 'retention' as having been 'still' conscious" exactly. The redaction also changed the vocabulary of time. Where the manuscript says that a "temporal phase, an objective time-point of the tone's duration" is the actual now, and that "a continuity of time-points" is conscious as "before", Stein writes "temporal phase (corresponding to a time-point of the tone's duration)" and "a continuity of phases". The redaction separates the phase of the flow from the point of the object's time that it corresponds to, a distinction the manuscript of 1911 had not yet drawn in its wording. And where the manuscript's next paragraph speaks of how "the objectively temporal 'appears'" (*das objektiv Zeitliche „erscheint“*), §8 has "the immanent-temporal object" (Hua X 25). "Objective", for the immanent tone, was Husserl's word in 1911. The redaction replaced it.

The history of the term is longer than either text. An editor's note in Part B records that "retention" first occurs on an undated sheet probably from 1904, that it "is missing from the original draft of the lectures of 1905", and that it was introduced afresh "around 1908/9" (*um 1908/9*). The 1905 stratum of the lectures says "primary memory" (*primäre Erinnerung*). The printed text joins the two vocabularies without marking the seam: §14 opens by recalling that "we called primary memory or retention a comet's tail" (*Wir bezeichneten die primäre Erinnerung oder Retention als einen Kometenschweif*, Hua X 35). The quotation marks of §8 make the reader meet the word as new at the point where, in Husserl's work, it was already old.

What §8 fixes, §§11–13 develop. Retention becomes the continuum in which each retention is retention of the preceding one (§11). It is a "peculiar intentionality", not an echo (§12). And it must be preceded by an impression (§13). The "still" of §8 stays the core. Section Three asks the question the clause "as long as it lasts" raised. If retention lasts and is modified, it is itself a temporal process, and the consciousness of its lasting must be accounted for. §39 answers with the double intentionality of retention, in which the same retentions that hold the tone fast also make the flow conscious of itself.

## §9, Hua X 26–27: the object in its how, and the two senses of intentionality

Reads [§9.3](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#the-consciousness-of-the-appearances-of-immanent-objects), third paragraph under § 9.

"The object in its how" (*Objekt im Wie*) is the object as it is given at a moment of the flow: the tone as now, as just past, as further past. It is not the tone simply, which stays the same, and it is not the consciousness of the tone, which is a lived experience. It stands between them, and its place there forces a distinction in the word "intentionality". There is the relation of the appearance to what appears in it, and there is the relation of consciousness to the appearing-in-its-how and, through that, to what appears simply. The passage separates these two relations. Without the separation the lectures could not later ask what the flow itself is, since the flow would be either the object's modes or the object's consciousness and nothing else.

> It is now a matter of examining more closely what we can find and describe here as the phenomenon of time-constituting consciousness, the consciousness in which temporal objects with their temporal determinations are constituted. We distinguish the enduring immanent object and the object in its how, the one conscious as actually present or as past. Each temporal being "appears" in some running-off mode or other, one that continually changes, and the "object in the running-off mode" is in this change always another again, while we nevertheless say that the object and each point of its time and this time itself are one and the same. This appearance, "object in the running-off mode", we shall not be able to call consciousness (no more than we shall call the spatial phenomenon, the body in the how of its appearing from this side or that, from near or far, a consciousness). "Consciousness", "lived experience", relates to its object by means of an appearance in which precisely the "object in its how" stands there. Evidently we must recognize the talk of "intentionality" as ambiguous, according as we have in view the relation of the appearance to what appears, or the relation of consciousness on the one hand to the "appearing in its how" and on the other to the appearing simply.

German:

> Es gilt nun, näher zu untersuchen, was wir hier als Phänomen des zeitkonstituierenden Bewußtseins, desjenigen, in dem sich die zeitlichen Gegenstände mit ihren zeitlichen Bestimmtheiten konstituieren, vorfinden und beschreiben können. Wir unterscheiden das dauernde, immanente Objekt und das Objekt im Wie, das als aktuell gegenwärtig oder als vergangen bewußte. Jedes zeitliche Sein „erscheint“ in irgendeinem und einem kontinuierlich sich wandelnden Ablaufsmodus, und das „Objekt im Ablaufsmodus“ ist in dieser Wandlung immer wieder ein anderes, während wir doch sagen, das Objekt und jeder Punkt seiner Zeit und diese Zeit selbst seien ein und dieselben. Diese Erscheinung „Objekt im Ablaufsmodus“ werden wir nicht Bewußtsein nennen können (so wenig wir das Raumphänomen, den Körper im Wie der Erscheinung von der oder jener Seite, von nah oder ferne, ein Bewußtsein nennen werden). Das „Bewußtsein“, das „Erlebnis“ bezieht sich auf sein Objekt vermittelst einer Erscheinung, in der eben das „Objekt im Wie“ dasteht. Offenbar müssen wir die Rede von der „Intentionalität“ als doppelsinnig erkennen, je nachdem wir die Beziehung der Erscheinung auf das Erscheinende im Auge haben oder die Beziehung des Bewußtseins einerseits auf das „Erscheinende im Wie“, andererseits auf das Erscheinende schlechthin.

*Note on the text.* The paragraph straddles a page break (Hua X 26–27) and is hard-wrapped with end-of-line hyphenation in the file (src L306–L328). The hyphens are joined silently here.

The phrase is built on an interrogative made into a noun. "How" (*Wie*) is a question word, and "the object in its how" turns the answer to "how is it given?" into a part of the object's description. The paragraph uses the phrase in three forms and lets each specify the others. First, plainly: "the object in its how, the one conscious as actually present or as past". Then, in quotation marks and with the technical term of the section: "the 'object in the running-off mode' is in this change always another again". Then, in quotation marks again, as what an appearance presents: the appearance "in which precisely the 'object in its how' stands there". The quotation marks turn each phrase into a name for a single thing, with a grammar of its own. The object in its how "is always another again", while "the object and each point of its time and this time itself" are "one and the same". The same object is many objects-in-their-how, and the sentence holds the two predicates together with "while … nevertheless" (*während … doch*).

The negative step is the decisive one. This appearance "we shall not be able to call consciousness". The spatial parenthesis gives the reason: no one calls a body seen "from this side or that, from near or far" a consciousness. The aspect is on the object's side, even though it exists only for someone. With that, the paragraph has three terms and two relations. Consciousness relates "by means of" (*vermittelst*) the appearance, and in the appearance the object-in-its-how "stands there" (*dasteht*). So "intentionality" is "ambiguous" (*doppelsinnig*). It may mean the relation of an appearance to what appears in it, which is a relation between two terms on the object side. Or it may mean the relation of consciousness to both of them, "on the one hand" to the appearing-in-its-how and "on the other" to the appearing simply. The word "evidently" (*offenbar*) introduces this as a consequence. It is one only because the appearance has been denied the title of consciousness one sentence earlier. Everything rests on that denial.

The 1911 manuscript printed as Nr. 53 shows what the redaction left out (Hua X 364). There Husserl declared two phrases ambiguous, "relatedness to the object" (*Bezogenheit auf den Gegenstand*) and "intentionality". He added that consciousness relates to the identical object "necessarily through the appearance", which is "*a priori* clear", and he glossed the "appearing in its how" with a parenthesis: "(that is, the appearance in the ontic sense)" (*also die Erscheinung im ontischen Sinn*). The gloss names the side on which the object-in-its-how belongs. It is an appearance in the ontic sense, on the object's side, and not an appearance in the sense of a lived experience. Stein's text keeps the distinction and drops the label. It also drops the manuscript's next step, which comes straight after: "Definition", calling the phenomena that constitute immanent temporal objects "running-off phenomena", since "immanent objects are themselves again … appearances, say of external objects". §10 opens with the same decision in milder words: we will "rather avoid" the talk of "appearances" for these phenomena (Hua X 27).

The passage sets up the next step of Section Two. §10 builds the diagram of time on the "running-off modes" this paragraph has just put on the object's side, and §11 names the consciousness that is not an appearance: primal impression and retention. But the triad of §9 is unstable, because its first term, "consciousness" or "lived experience", is still pictured as one more thing that lasts. Section Three shows the cost. §37 distinguishes "consciousness (flow), appearance (immanent object), transcendent object" (Hua X 76). The appearance of §9 has become an immanent object, and consciousness has become a flow that is not an object at all. The ambiguity of §9 is between the appearance's relation to what appears and consciousness's relation to both. It is not the "double intentionality" of §39, which lies inside the flow itself, between retention's relation to the tone and its relation to the flow's own past phases. The commentary keeps the two apart, and the wording supports it: §9 says "ambiguous" (*doppelsinnig*), §39 says "two inseparably unified intentionalities". The first is a double sense of a word, the second a double structure of one consciousness.

*Ideas I* takes over the phrase and moves it into a new framework. In §131 the noema contains two concepts of the object: "the noematic 'object simply'" and "the 'object in the how of its determinations'" (*„Gegenstand im Wie seiner Bestimmtheiten“*), and the "sense" is "this noematic 'object in its how'" (*dieser noematische „Gegenstand im Wie“*, Hua III/1 303, first edition 272). The temporal "how" of the lectures, now, just past, further past, becomes the "how of its determinations", the way the object is meant. The word comes from the analysis of time, and so does its place. In the lectures as in *Ideas I*, the object in its how is neither the object simply nor the consciousness of it.

## §11, Hua X 29–30: primal impression, "source-point" and "production"

Reads [§11.1](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#primal-impression-and-retentional-modification), first paragraph under § 11.

The primal impression (*Urimpression*) is the consciousness in which the now of the object is given. It is the start of every retentional series, and it is defined by its place in that series: it is what passes into retention and is never itself the retention of anything. §11 introduces it by two images, the "source-point" and the "production" of the enduring object, and keeps both in quotation marks. The images say that everything in time-consciousness flows from the impression. The quotation marks say that this is a way of speaking. The lectures and their supplements keep pulling at that tension, until the "production" image is withdrawn.

> The "source-point" with which the "production" of the enduring object begins is a primal impression. This consciousness is caught up in constant change: the bodily tone-now changes continually (*scil.* in the manner of consciousness, "in" consciousness) into a having-been, and an ever new tone-now continually relieves the one that has passed into modification. But when the consciousness of the tone-now, the primal impression, passes into retention, this retention is itself again a now, something actually existing. While it is itself actual (but not actual tone), it is retention of tone that has been. […] Every actual now of consciousness, however, is subject to the law of modification. It changes into retention of retention, and does so continually. There results accordingly a continuous continuum of retention, such that each later point is retention for each earlier one. And each retention is already a continuum. […] This leads to no simple infinite regress, because each retention is in itself continuous modification, which carries in itself, so to speak, the heritage of the past in the form of a series of shadings.

> Der ,,Quellpunkt'', mit dem die ,,Erzeugung'' des dauernden Objektes einsetzt, ist eine Urimpression. Dies Bewußtsein ist in beständiger Wandlung begriffen: stetig wandelt sich das leibhafte Ton-Jetzt (*scil.* bewußtseinsmäßig, ,,im'' Bewußtsein) in ein Gewesen, stetig löst ein immer neues Ton-Jetzt das in die Modifikation übergegangene ab. Während sie selbst aktuell ist (aber nicht aktueller Ton), ist sie Retention von gewesenem Ton. […] Jedes aktuelle Jetzt des Bewußtseins unterliegt aber dem Gesetz der Modifikation. […] Das führt auf keinen einfachen unendlichen Regreß, weil jede Retention in sich selbst kontinuierliche Modifikation ist, die sozusagen in Form einer Abschattungsreihe das Erbe der Vergangenheit in sich trägt.

*Note on the text.* From the heading of §11 the file is hard-wrapped at print width, with end-of-line hyphenation ("Reten-/tion", "Re-/tention", src L425–L426, L438–L439). The hyphens are joined silently here.

The definition is a copular sentence whose subject is an image: the "source-point" (*„Quellpunkt“*) is a primal impression. The thing defined stands in the predicate, and the image in quotation marks does the work of introducing it. "Source-point" has already appeared, one page earlier, without quotation marks and with a hedge: the running-off modes of an immanent object "have a beginning, so to speak a source-point" (*sozusagen einen Quellpunkt*, §10, Hua X 28). §11 replaces the hedge with quotation marks and adds a second image: the source-point is where "the 'production' (*„Erzeugung“*) of the enduring object begins". "Production" belongs to the same family as the "producing point of the now" of §8, which was unquoted there. In §11 both words are held at a distance in the sentence that names the impression for the first time.

The rest of the paragraph turns the image into a structure without using it again. The primal impression "passes into retention", and the retention "is itself again a now, something actually existing". The parenthesis, "but not actual tone", carries the distinction the whole passage depends on: the retention is actual as consciousness and past in what it intends. Then "every actual now of consciousness" falls under "the law of modification" and becomes "retention of retention". The phrase is the formal core of the theory, and it recurs at §39 for the flow's consciousness of itself. Its regress is stopped by a claim about continuity, not about a source: each retention "is already a continuum" and "carries the heritage of the past" (*das Erbe der Vergangenheit*) in a series of shadings. What blocks the regress is that the series is continuous and each member contains all the others in modified form. The "source-point" appears nowhere in that argument. It is the limit at which the series begins, and the rest of the paragraph never needs it as a producer.

The image does not go away. The impression is where something new comes in, and the image of a source is how Husserl first says so. At §31 the primal impression is "the primal source (*Urquelle*) for all further consciousness and being" (Hua X 67), and in Section Three the actuality-point becomes the "primal source-point" (*Urquellpunkt*, §36, Hua X 75). Beilage I, from the same manuscript strata, takes the image as far as it will go and then calls it an image. "The primal impression is the absolute beginning of this production, the primal source, that from which everything else continually produces itself. But it itself is not produced, it does not arise as something produced but through *genesis spontanea*; it is primal generation (*Urzeugung*)." Then: "If it is said … a source suddenly springs up, those are images (*so sind das Bilder*). It can only be said: consciousness is nothing without impression" (Hua X 100). The quotation marks of §11 are the short form of that sentence. "Source" and "production" are images, and the literal content they cover is only that consciousness needs impression.

§42 reverses the direction of the image. Discussing reproduction, Husserl says that at the head of a remembered content stand "primal memories", not "something set in 'from outside', 'alien to consciousness', primally-generated-sprung-forth (*Ur-gezeugt-Entsprungenes*), but something that has surfaced, resurfaced". This moment, "like the impression, is no product of spontaneity, but in a certain way something receptive. One could also speak here of passive reception" (Hua X 88). The hyphenated coinage collects the vocabulary of §11 and Beilage I, generation and springing forth, and says it of what impression receives from outside. The impression is not the producer of the flow but the place where the flow is given something it did not make. Beilage I said the impression arises by *genesis spontanea*; §42 says it is "no product of spontaneity". The clash is one of words. Beilage I meant that nothing in consciousness generates the impression, §42 that no act of the ego does. But the clash shows why §11 needed the quotation marks. "Production" had to name both the impression's novelty and the dependence of everything else on it, and no single image could do both.

## §12, Hua X 31: retention is no image

Reads [§12.2](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#retention-as-a-peculiar-intentionality), second paragraph under § 12.

Retention is an original consciousness of the past. It does not present the past by means of something present, an echo, an after-image, a weakened copy of the tone. It is itself the way the past is given. The argument for this is a regress. A present content can stand for a past one only if the past is already given in some other way, so standing-for presupposes the very consciousness it was meant to explain. The passage rules out every image theory of retention by that one step, and the step is carried by a preposition in quotation marks.

> One speaks of fading, paling and so on of the contents of sensation when perception proper passes over into retention. But it is already clear from the preceding discussion that the [retentional] "contents" are not contents in the original sense at all. When a tone fades, it is itself first sensed with particular fullness (intensity), and to this is joined a rapid slackening of intensity. The tone is still there, still sensed, but in mere echo. This genuine tone-sensation is to be distinguished from the tonal moment in retention. The retentional tone is not a present one but precisely one "primarily remembered" in the now: it is not *reell* present in the retentional consciousness. But the tonal moment that belongs to it cannot be another tone *reell* present either, not even a very weak one of the same quality (as echo). A present tone can indeed remind us "of" a past one, present it, picture it; but that already presupposes another representation of the past. The intuition of the past itself cannot be picturing. It is an original consciousness.

German:

> Man spricht von Abklingen, Verblassen usw. der Empfindungsinhalte, wenn eigentliche Wahrnehmung in Retention übergeht. Nun ist es aber schon nach den bisherigen Ausführungen klar, daß die relationalen ,,Inhalte'' gar keine Inhalte im ursprünglichen Sinne sind. Wenn ein Ton abklingt, so ist er selbst zunächst mit besonderer Fülle (Intensität) empfunden, und daran schließt sich ein rasches Nachlassen der Intensität. Der Ton ist noch da, ist noch empfunden, aber im bloßen Nachhall. Diese echte Ton-Empfindung ist zu unterscheiden von dem tonalen Moment in der Retention. Der retentionale Ton ist kein gegenwärtiger, sondern eben im Jetzt ,,primär erinnerter'': er ist im retentionalen Bewußtsein nicht reell vorhanden. Das tonale Moment, das zu diesem gehört, kann aber auch nicht ein reell vorhandener anderer Ton sein, auch nicht ein sehr schwacher qualitätsgleicher (als Nachhall). Ein gegenwärtiger Ton kann zwar ,,an'' einen vergangenen erinnern, ihn darstellen, verbildlichen; das aber setzt schon eine andere Vergangenheitsvorstellung voraus. Die Vergangenheitsanschauung selbst kann nicht Verbildlichung sein. Es ist ein originäres Bewußtsein.

The argument requires "retentional", and Brough's translation, made from the printed Hua X, has "the retentional 'contents'" (CW4 L6433). The apparatus records no variant here. The file's word is most likely a conversion error. The English above gives "[retentional]" in brackets, and nothing in the reading depends on the adjective.

The regress sits in one sentence and turns on "of". "A present tone can indeed remind us 'of' a past one" (*Ein gegenwärtiger Ton kann zwar „an“ einen vergangenen erinnern*). German *sich erinnern an* is "to remember", and *erinnern an* without the reflexive is "to remind of". The second is a relation between two things, one of which recalls the other. Husserl's quotation marks fall on the preposition *an* (of), the word that carries the relation. A present tone can remind of a past one, as a portrait reminds of a face, and the verbs that follow say what kind of relation that is: "present it, picture it" (*ihn darstellen, verbildlichen*). Then comes the objection: "but that already presupposes another representation of the past". To be reminded of something as past, I must already be conscious of it as past. The image-relation needs a term on its far side, and the past has to be given there first. "Already" (*schon*) does the arguing. Every theory that puts an image in the present has assumed the consciousness of the past it set out to explain.

The conclusion comes in two short sentences. "The intuition of the past itself cannot be picturing (*Verbildlichung*). It is an original consciousness" (*ein originäres Bewußtsein*). "Original" means that it gives its object itself, as perception does. §17 draws the consequence that primary memory is perception in the wide sense. The neighbouring sentences hold the argument in place with the word *reell* (really inherent), as §6 did. The retained tone "is not *reell* present", and the tonal moment of retention cannot be "another tone *reell* present". The quotation marks around "contents" at the start of the paragraph say the same thing from the side of the theory being refuted. What fades in retention is not a content, because a content is something *reell* present, and what is *reell* present is now.

Part B prints the manuscript of 1908 on which §§12–13 rest (Nr. 47, Hua X 311–312). Its wording shows what the redaction changed. Where §12 says "retention", the manuscript says "fresh memory" (*frische Erinnerung*). Where §12 has "the [retentional] 'contents'", the manuscript has "the contents that have grown pale and faded away", which "are no longer contents of sensation at all, and not 'contents' in the original sense at all". It goes on: "It is admittedly hard to find one's way here. What is the talk of 'contents' supposed to mean?" (*Es ist hier freilich schwer, sich auszukennen. Was soll die Rede von ,,Inhalten''?*). The redaction cut the admission. It also changed the pronoun of the conclusion. The manuscript says "*It* [the intuition of the past] is an original consciousness" with a feminine pronoun (*Sie ist ein originäres Bewußtsein*) whose referent is unmistakable. §12 has the neuter "It is" (*Es ist*), which in German reads as "this is" and loosens the tie to "the intuition of the past". The regress sentence, with its quoted "of", is the manuscript's word for word.

Part B also shows what the argument was aimed at. In a text of 1904–05, Husserl explained the evidence of memory by the claim that "appearance and temporal positing are essentially one, an appearance functioning as image is thus also an image-like temporal positing" (*bildlich fungierende Erscheinung also auch bildliche Zeitsetzung*), with the present appearance as "representative" of the remembered one (Nr. 18, Hua X 181). That is an image theory of memory, Husserl's own. The 1908 argument of Nr. 47 does not reject images for memory in general. §28 still asks how remembering differs from image-consciousness, and answers that memory is a "self-presentification", not a picture. It rejects them for the consciousness of the just-past, and it does so by a regress that any image theory of retention must meet.

The understanding returns at each step that follows. §13 says that comparing what is retained with "something outside it has no sense at all" (Hua X 34): since retention is not an image, there is no original to check it against, and its evidence cannot be the evidence of a good likeness. §17 calls primary memory "presentative, not representative". In Section Three the question changes level. If retention is original consciousness and not an image, what is the retention itself? §39 answers that each retention is an intention both on the retained tone and on the earlier phases of the flow. The "of" in quotation marks at §12 marked a relation that retention does not have, the relation of an image to what it pictures. The rest of the lectures describe the relation it has instead.

## §17, Hua X 41–42: "presentatively, not representatively"

Reads [§17.1](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#perception-as-self-giving-act-in-contrast-to-reproduction), the paragraph under § 17.

Primary memory is perception, if perception means the act that gives its object itself. The claim redraws the line that matters in time-consciousness. Brentano drew it between the now and everything else: only the now is perceived, the past is supplied. Husserl draws it between what is given and what is re-presented: the just-past is given in retention as the now is given in impression, and only recollection and phantasy present their objects again. The prefix pair "presentative" and "representative" carries the new line, and a definition stated as a conditional decides which side retention falls on.

> Perception here is the act that places something before our eyes as itself, the act that originally constitutes the object. Its opposite is presentification, re-presentation, as the act that does not place an object before our eyes itself, but just presentifies it, places it before our eyes as it were in an image, though not exactly in the manner of a genuine image-consciousness. Here there is no question at all of a continuous mediation of perception with its opposite. Earlier, consciousness of the past, namely the primary one, was not perception, because perception was taken as the act originally constituting the now. But consciousness of the past does not constitute a now; it constitutes rather a "just been", something that intuitively preceded the now. If, however, we call perception the act in which all "origin" lies, which constitutes originally, then primary memory is perception. For only in primary memory do we see what is past, only in it is the past constituted, and indeed not representatively but presentatively.

German:

> Wahrnehmung ist hier der Akt, der etwas als es selbst vor Augen stellt, der Akt, der das Objekt ursprünglich konstituiert. Das Gegenteil ist Vergegenwärtigung, Re-Präsentation, als der Akt, der ein Objekt nicht selbst vor Augen stellt, sondern eben vergegenwärtigt, gleichsam im Bilde vor Augen stellt, wenn auch nicht gerade in der Weise eines eigentlichen Bildbewußtseins. Hier ist von einer kontinuierlichen Vermittlung der Wahrnehmung mit ihrem Gegenteil gar keine Rede. Das Vergangenheitsbewußtsein konstituiert aber nicht ein Jetzt, vielmehr ein ,,soeben gewesen'', ein dem Jetzt intuitiv Vorangegangenes. Nennen wir aber Wahrnehmung den Akt, in dem aller ,,Ursprung'' liegt, der originär konstituiert, so ist die primäre Erinnerung Wahrnehmung. Denn nur in der primären Erinnerung sehen wir Vergangenes, nur in ihr konstituiert sich Vergangenheit, und zwar nicht repräsentativ, sondern präsentativ.

The result rests on a conditional. "If, however, we call perception the act in which all 'origin' lies" (*Nennen wir aber Wahrnehmung den Akt, in dem aller „Ursprung“ liegt*), "then primary memory is perception". The form is the one §1 used for "sensed": a word is given a definition by stipulation, and the consequence follows from the definition. Here the stipulation is explicit about what it replaces. "Earlier" (*vorhin*), perception was "the act originally constituting the now", and by that definition retention was not perception. The paragraph does not say the earlier definition was wrong. It sets a second concept beside it, "a quite different concept of perception", as the sentence before the passage puts it. Both stand, and retention falls on opposite sides of the two.

The quotation marks around "origin" (*„Ursprung“*) tie the stipulation to the question of the whole lecture. §2 asked about the "origin of time" in the same quotation marks, and distinguished the epistemological origin, where something is originally given, from Brentano's psychological origin, where a representation arises. The perception "in which all 'origin' lies" is perception in the first sense. Brentano located the origin of time in phantasy. Husserl now locates it in perception widened to include primary memory, and §19 will say that "no representation can 'spring' (*„entspringen“*) from" presentification (Hua X 45).

The prefix pair does the rest. "Not representatively but presentatively" (*nicht repräsentativ, sondern präsentativ*) appears to mirror the hyphenated noun a few lines earlier, "presentification, re-presentation" (*Vergegenwärtigung, Re-Präsentation*). The hyphen splits *Repräsentation* into re- and presentation, so that the word means "presenting again", which is what recollection does. But the adjective *repräsentativ* is unhyphenated, and it has a second sense. It is the word of the theory of representation that Husserl held in 1905, in which a present content "represents" an object by standing for it. Nr. 48 in Part B looks back on that view as "my theory of representation" (*meine Repräsentationstheorie*, Hua X 319). Retention is "not representative" in both senses. It does not present the past again, as recollection does, and it does not present it through a stand-in, as an image would. The pair covers both refusals at once, and that is why it works as the summary of §§12–17. The hedge on the image is careful too: presentification places something before our eyes "as it were in an image" (*gleichsam im Bilde*), "though not exactly in the manner of a genuine image-consciousness". The image is a comparison, not a theory of recollection.

The apparatus shows that this paragraph comes from the 1905 lecture sheets (sheets 38–40, Texte L762) and that Husserl hesitated over it there. The manuscript has "Repräsentation" without the hyphen (41,14). The hyphen, which makes "re-presentation" mean "presenting again" and keeps it apart from the theory of representation, came in the redaction. The sentence "Here there is no question at all of a continuous mediation of perception with its opposite" is an insertion Husserl made in the manuscript (41,17–19). It states the point §19 develops: the step from perception to retention is continuous, the step to recollection discrete. And beside the sentence that follows the passage, that the just-been can be "seen directly only in primary memory", Husserl wrote in the margin: "That is a little incorrect" (*Das ist ein wenig inkorrekt*, 41,29ff.). The manuscript's own wording of that sentence was more guarded: what the just-been "'means'" (*„bedeutet“*) can only be seen directly in primary memory (41,29–31). The marginal doubt does not touch the presentative–representative pair. It touches the claim that retention sees the past as directly as perception sees the now. That is the point Section Three reopens.

What follows uses the new line at once. §18 needs it to say how a succession is "originally given": retention and recollection both belong to it, but only recollection can identify a past duration "as an identical object of many acts" (Hua X 43). §19 states the difference between retention and reproduction as the difference between a continuous and a discrete modification. In Section Three the pair is no longer enough. If perception is the act "in which all origin lies", the question is what consciousness gives the acts themselves. §§40–42 answer with primal consciousness, impression and reproduction at the level of the flow, where §42 again sets "impression" against "reproduction" as presentation against re-presentation. The 1905 pair survives that move. The concept of perception it was built on, an act, does not.

## §18, Hua X 43–44: the prime on the dash

Reads [§18.1](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#the-significance-of-recollection-for-the-constitution-of-the-consciousness-of-duration-and-succession), the first paragraph under § 18.

Recollection modifies a succession as a whole, its form included. To remember that B followed A is not to have a memory of A and then a memory of B. It is to have the following itself as remembered. The point sounds like row 3's maxim carried over from sensation to memory, and it is. What §18 adds is a notation that shows where the modification falls: the mark for memory goes on the sign for the relation as well as on the signs for its terms. The notation then lets Husserl say what makes a past succession an identical object, one that can be returned to again and again.

> But something new enters here: B follows A; a succession of two enduring data is given, with a determinate temporal form, a stretch of time that spans the one-after-the-other. The consciousness of succession is an originally giving consciousness; it is "perception" of this one-after-the-other. We now consider the reproductive modification of this perception, namely recollection. I "repeat" the consciousness of this succession, I presentify it to myself in remembering. This I "can" do, and indeed "as often as I like". A priori, the presentification of an experience lies within the domain of my "freedom". (The "I can" is a practical "I can", and not a "mere representation".) What, then, does the presentification of the succession of experiences look like, and what belongs to its essence? One will say at first: I presentify to myself first A and then B; if I originally had A—B, I now have (if the index signifies memory) A'—B'. But that is insufficient, for it would mean that I now have a memory A' and "afterwards" a memory B', and indeed in the consciousness of a succession of these memories. But then I would have a "perception" of the succession of these memories, and no memory-consciousness of it. I must therefore represent it by (A—B)'. This consciousness does indeed include an A', B', but also a —'. Of course the succession is not a third piece, as if the way the signs are written one after the other designated the succession. Still, I can write down the law:
>
> (A—B)' = A'—'B'
>
> in the sense: there is present a consciousness of the memory of A and of B, but also a modified consciousness of the "B follows upon A".

*Note on the text.* The file sets most of the symbols in LaTeX markup (`$A'$`, `$(A—B)'$`, the law as a display formula, src L584–L588); they are given here as plain characters, with no change of sign. In the iterated formulas on p. 44 the file switches from the dash to a hyphen ("[(A - B) - (A - B)']'", src L604); the reading takes the two as the same sign, as Brough's translation does (CW4 L7353). No manuscript survives for §18 (see below), so the apparatus has no variants to check.

The error the passage corrects is to modify the terms and leave the relation unmodified. "A'—B'" primes A and B and keeps the plain dash between them. That dash is then a present dash. It means that the memory A' is followed, now, by the memory B', and that this following is lived through as it happens. Husserl's word for that is "perception" in quotation marks, the same quoted word he used two sentences earlier for the original consciousness of the succession, and the same widened concept of perception that §17 introduced: the act that gives something itself. A'—B' gives a succession itself, but the wrong one. It gives the succession of two rememberings, which is a present event, where recollection was supposed to give the past succession of A and B. The two quoted "perceptions" in the paragraph mark the two successions the notation must keep apart: the one originally given in 1905's terms, and the one that a bad analysis of memory would give in its place.

The correction is a bracket and a prime. "(A—B)'" puts the whole succession under the index of memory, and the law spells out what that includes: "an A', B', but also a —'". The dash takes its own prime. The modification that turns A into a remembered A turns the following into a remembered following, and Husserl's gloss names it in quotation marks as a modified consciousness of "B follows upon A" (*„es folgt auf A das B“*). A clause, not a noun: what is remembered is a state of affairs about A and B, and the prime falls on it as a whole. The law looks like a rule of distribution, the prime multiplied through the bracket. But the bracket is not removed. The left side is the definition, and the right side only lists what the remembering includes.

Husserl then warns his reader against his own notation. "Of course the succession is not a third piece, as if the way the signs are written one after the other designated the succession." The signs on the page stand in a left-to-right order, and that order is itself a succession. Read naively, the notation would commit the very error it was introduced to correct, letting a succession of signs stand for the sign of a succession. The dash is there to prevent this. It is a sign for the relation, so that the order of writing does not have to carry it. And the dash is "not a third piece": —' is not a third memory placed between A' and B', but the form that holds them together. "Freilich … Immerhin" (of course … still) is the concessive shape of the whole step. The notation is admitted against its risk, and the law is stated "in the sense" that follows, as an abbreviation of a description. A page later Husserl treats it as more than that. He "derives" (*ableiten*) "by the earlier law" that the first recollection of a repeated succession, "[(A - B) - (A - B)']'", contains "(A - B)' and [(A - B)']', therefore a memory of the second level, and indeed in succession", "and naturally also the memory of the succession (-)'" (Hua X 44). The derivation can only return what the notation was given. It does no harm, because each step can be checked against the description, but the inference is weaker than "derive" makes it sound.

The words in quotation marks around "repeat" carry the rest of the paragraph forward. "I 'can' do this, and indeed 'as often as I like'", and the "I can" is "practical", not "a mere representation": a capacity I have, not a possibility I think of. The iterated formulas depend on it. The paragraph that follows uses the law to say what identity in time is. "Only past durations can I 'originally' intuit in repeating acts, really intuit, identify and have objectively as an identical object of many acts. The present I can live through again, but it cannot be re-given" (*Nur vergangene Dauern kann ich in wiederholenden Akten „originär“ anschauen*, Hua X 43). "Originally" is in quotation marks here, and for a reason: §17 had made recollection the opposite of the originally giving act. Recollection is "original" only in this one respect, that it alone gives the same past succession again and again. The law is what makes this possible. A series of rememberings, "(A—B) — (A—B)' — (A—B)'' …", is a present series, "originally given as presence" (*originär gegeben als Präsenz*, Hua X 44), whose members are each the memory of one and the same succession. Without the bracket there would be no way to write that difference. A'—B', A''—B'' would be a series of new successions of images, with nothing to make them the same.

The apparatus shows that the passage does not belong to the 1905 lectures, under whose running head it stands. No manuscript for §18 was found. Husserl noted on the lecture sheets that Stein had used here "the sheets on the a priori of memory or of the consciousness of succession from 1917" (*die Blätter über Apriori der Erinnerung bzw. des Bewußtseins der Sukzession aus 1917*, Texte L1170, L1212). The notation and the "I can" are a 1917 reading of the 1905 maxim. Part B shows what the maxim looked like before the notation. A sheet from the vacation of 1904 begins from a principle about relations, "the perceiving of a relation presupposes the perceiving of the foundations" (*das Wahrnehmen einer Relation setzt voraus das Wahrnehmen der Fundamente*), and draws from it a chiasmus the editor took for its title: "the perception of succession presupposes succession of perceptions" (*Die Wahrnehmung der Sukzession setzt Sukzession der Wahrnehmungen voraus*, Nr. 20, Hua X 189–191). In 1904 the succession of acts is a necessary condition that is not sufficient. In §18 it has become a component: (A—B)' "includes" A' and B', and adds the modified relation, which the components do not supply. The next sheet of 1904 states the method that §18 turns into a thesis: "we recognize this on the basis of the repeated presentification of one and the same succession" (*aufgrund der wiederholten Vergegenwärtigung einer selben Sukzession*, Nr. 21, Hua X 194). There repetition was a way of noticing the acts. In §18 it is what constitutes the past as an identical object, and Husserl closes the section's iteration by saying that it is "very common, especially in the phenomenological method of working" (Hua X 44).

The understanding returns twice in Section Two. §20 describes the freedom the "I can" announced: presentification can run "faster" or "slower", in steps or "in a flash", while "the relative modes of running-off" of the presentified points stay unchanged (Hua X 48). That is the law's bracket stated as a fact about tempo: the reproducing act has its own time, and the reproduced succession keeps its form inside it. Beilage IV, written to §32, states the result that the bracket served: "The perception of a temporal object I can 'repeat', but in the succession of these perceptions the consciousness of the succession of two like temporal objects is constituted. Only in recollection can I have an identical temporal object repeatedly" (*Nur in der Wiedererinnerung kann ich einen identischen Zeitgegenstand wiederholt haben*, Hua X 108). The prime on the dash is where that claim begins. It makes the relation, and not only its terms, something memory gives, and so it makes the past something that can be given more than once.

## §25, Hua X 54: "implicitly" up to the living present

Reads [§25.2](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#the-double-intentionality-of-recollection), the second paragraph under § 25.

Every recollection reaches the present. It reproduces a past stretch of experience intuitively, and with it, without intuition, the whole stream that has run from that stretch to now. That second reproduction is "implicit": it is one empty intention directed at the series of events that would fill the gap, and it ends in the "living present", the actual now in which the remembering is going on. Because the intention runs through to the now, the present acts back on the remembered past. Each new event fulfils and determines the forward-reaching intentions of what came before, and so changes how the old can be reproduced. The phrase "living present" makes its first appearance in the lectures here, as the end-point of this implicit reproduction, not yet as a term.

> Is the latter, then, also reproduction? That is an easily misleading question. Of course the whole is reproduced, not only the conscious present of that time with its flow, but "implicitly" the whole stream of consciousness up to the living present. That says, as a basic piece of a priori phenomenological genesis: memory is in a constant flux because the life of consciousness is in constant flux, and does not merely fit itself link to link in the chain. Rather, every new thing acts back on the old; its forward-going intention is fulfilled and thereby determined, and that gives the reproduction a definite colouring. Here, then, an *a priori* necessary retroaction shows itself. The new points again to something new, which is determined as it enters and modifies the reproductive possibilities for the old, and so on. And the retroactive force goes back along the chain, for the reproduced past bears the character *past* and an indeterminate intention toward a certain temporal situation relative to the now. So it is not that we have a mere chain of "associated" intentions, one recalling the other, this one the next (streaming); we have one intention, which is in itself an intention toward the series of possible fulfilments.

German:

> Ist nun auch das Letztere Reproduktion? Das ist eine leicht irreführende Frage. Natürlich, das Ganze wird reproduziert, nicht nur die damalige Bewußtseinsgegenwart mit ihrem Fluß, sondern „implicite“ der ganze Strom des Bewußtseins bis zur lebendigen Gegenwart. Das sagt, als ein Grundstück apriorisch-phänomenologischer Genese: Die Erinnerung ist in einem beständigen Fluß, weil das Bewußtseinsleben in beständigem Fluß ist, und nicht nur Glied an Glied in der Kette sich fügt. Vielmehr wirkt jedes Neue zurück auf das Alte, seine vorwärtsgehende Intention erfüllt sich und bestimmt sich dabei, und das gibt der Reproduktion eine bestimmte Färbung. Hier zeigt sich also eine *a priori* notwendige Rückwirkung. Das Neue weist wieder auf Neues, das eintretend sich bestimmt und für das Alte die reproduktiven Möglichkeiten modifiziert usw. Dabei geht die rückwirkende Kraft der Kette nach zurück, denn das reproduzierte Vergangen trägt den Charakter *Vergangen* und eine unbestimmte Intention auf eine gewisse Zeitlage zum Jetzt. Es ist also nicht so, daß wir eine bloße Kette „assoziierter“ Intentionen hätten, eins an das andere, dies an das nächste (Strömende) erinnernd, sondern wir haben eine Intention, die in sich Intention auf die Reihe von möglichen Erfüllungen ist.

"The latter" is what the paragraph before set apart. Every presentification contains two things: the reproduction of the consciousness in which the past object was given, and "what attaches to this reproduction as constitutive for the consciousness 'past' or 'present' … or 'future'", the intentions that place the object in time. Those intentions are fulfilled, Husserl had said, "by establishing the filled connections up to the actual present" (*bis zur aktuellen Gegenwart*). The question is whether they too are reproduction. It is "easily misleading" because they reach into the present, and the present is not reproduced; it is lived. The manuscript on which §25 rests, Nr. 45 of 1907–09, gives the reason in a parenthesis the redaction dropped: the question is misleading "given the temptation to confuse actuality and impression" (*bei der Versuchung, Aktualität und Impression zu verwechseln*, Hua X 304). The intentions of placement end in the actual now, but that does not make them impressions. The answer is therefore yes, with a qualification in quotation marks: the whole is reproduced, the intervening stream "implicitly".

"Implicitly" (*implicite*) carries the claim. The past stretch is reproduced as an intuition, phase by phase. The stream from there to now is not run through at all. It is reproduced as something folded into the recollection, an intention on a series that the recollection does not unfold. The paragraph after the passage names it: "this intention is a non-intuitive, an 'empty' intention, and its object is the objective temporal series of events", "the dark surroundings of what is actually recollected" (Hua X 55). The last sentence of the passage gives its form: "not a mere chain of 'associated' intentions", each recalling the next, but "one intention which is in itself an intention toward the series of possible fulfilments". The quotation marks around "associated" (*„assoziierter“*) distance the empiricist account, in which memory is held together by links between ideas. Husserl's unit is the single intention whose object is the whole series, and the series is fulfilled only if I actually go through it, event by event, up to now.

"Living present" (*lebendige Gegenwart*) is where that series ends. In this paragraph the phrase does not yet name a structure. It renames the "actual present" of the paragraph before, and at the end of the next paragraph the same point is called "the living now" (*dem lebendigen Jetzt*, Hua X 55), as §26 calls it again: future-intentions concern "the temporal surroundings that terminate in the living now" (*die im lebendigen Jetzt terminiert*, Hua X 56). §18, from the 1917 stratum, speaks of "the living horizon of the now" (*den lebendigen Horizont des Jetzt*, Hua X 43) and means retention by it. The adjective is unsettled in Hua X. Here it marks the present as the place where the life of consciousness is going on, and therefore the place where the empty intention of every recollection is anchored. A recollection is an act that happens now, and the intention that places its object in time runs from the object to that now.

The anchoring explains the retroaction. If the empty intention runs to the present, the present moves, and what is remembered is placed anew with each step. The wording is exact about what changes. Each new event fulfils "its forward-going intention", the expectation the old had of what would come, "and determines itself thereby" (*erfüllt sich und bestimmt sich dabei*). The result is "a definite colouring" (*eine bestimmte Färbung*) of the reproduction, and a modification of "the reproductive possibilities for the old". The retroactive force runs back "because the reproduced past bears the character *past* and an indeterminate intention toward a certain temporal situation relative to the now". What is redetermined is the old's expectations, now fulfilled, and its place relative to a now that keeps moving. The paragraph does not say that what was experienced changes in content. The main commentary reads it that way ("the past is not fixed in its sense once and for all but is redetermined by what follows"), and that goes beyond the wording.

The status of the claim changed in the redaction. In Nr. 45 the sentence reads: "'Implicitly': that says psychologically: memory is in a constant flux" (*„Implicite“: das sagt psychologisch*, Hua X 304). §25 has "That says, as a basic piece of a priori phenomenological genesis". Where Nr. 45 has "There, then, we have a retroaction" (*Da haben wir also eine Rückwirkung*), §25 has "Here, then, an *a priori* necessary retroaction shows itself". The argument between the two sentences is the same in both texts, word for word, including its "because": memory flows because the life of consciousness flows. So the redaction raised a psychological observation to an a priori law without adding a step. The apparatus for §§25–27 records only the differences from the 1928 first edition (Texte L1277), so it does not say whether Stein or Husserl made the change. The redaction also cut the manuscript's caution about the spatial analogy that follows: "these analogies may not be taken quite literally and simply accepted. How far they really reach as strict analogies must be shown by exact investigation" (Hua X 305).

The retroaction returns in a later work, and the comparison shows what §25 does not yet claim. In the lectures on passive synthesis, a perceived ball turns out green and dented where it had been anticipated as red and round. The change "radiates back, in the form of a retroactive cancellation, into the retentional sphere", and the earlier apprehension "is *implicitly* 'reinterpreted'" (*wird* implicite *„umgedeutet“*, Hua XI 30–31). There the past's sense is changed, and the old sense stays conscious beneath the new one, crossed out. Hua XI has the same word, *implicite*, and the same direction of force, from the new back along the chain. But there the force acts on the sense of what was perceived, and through retention, whereas §25 speaks only of the colouring of reproduction and its possibilities. In Hua X the living present is where every recollection's empty intention ends, and the past it acts back on is a past whose place, not whose content, is in question.

## §31, Hua X 64: "Time is rigid, and yet time flows"

Reads [§31.1–31.2](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#primal-impression-and-the-objective-individual-point-of-time), the first two paragraphs under § 31.

The problem of objective time is how something that does not move is constituted in something that never stops moving. A tone sinks into the past, phase by phase, and yet each phase keeps its place in time, so that it can be found again and identified in later acts. The past changes its distance from the now but not its position. Husserl first calls this an antinomy, dissolves it in a sentence, and then restates it as a problem in a paradox of five words. The paradox has a single grammatical subject, "time", for two opposite predicates, and the sentence after it already splits that subject in two. The reading follows that split. It is the step on which §31's account of individuation depends, and the one that Section Three will have to take back.

> Apparently we are led here to an antinomy: in sinking back the object constantly changes its temporal position, and yet in sinking back it is supposed to keep its temporal position. In truth the object of the steadily receding primary memory does not change its temporal position at all, but only its distance from the actual now, and this because the actual now counts as an ever new objective point of time, while the past temporal thing remains what it is. But how, against the phenomenon of the constant change of time-consciousness, consciousness of objective time comes about, and first of identical temporal positions: that is now the question. Most closely connected with this is the question of the constitution of the objectivity of individual temporal objects and processes: all objectivation takes place in time-consciousness; without clarification of the identity of temporal position, no clarification of the identity of an object in time can be given.
>
> Set out more closely, the problem is the following. The now-phases of perception continually undergo a modification; they do not simply preserve themselves as they are; they flow. In this is constituted what we call sinking back into time. The tone sounds now, and at once it sinks into the past, it, the same tone. This concerns the tone in each of its phases, and therefore the whole tone as well. Now the sinking seems fairly intelligible through our considerations so far. But how does it come about that, as against the sinking of the tone, we nevertheless speak of a fixed position in time belonging to it, that points and durations of time can be identified in repeated acts, as our analysis of reproductive consciousness showed? The tone, and every point of time in the unity of the enduring tone, has after all its absolutely fixed position in "objective" (be it the immanent) time. Time is rigid, and yet time flows. In the flow of time, in the continual sinking into the past, a non-flowing, absolutely fixed, identical, objective time is constituted. That is the problem.

German:

> Scheinbar werden wir hier auf eine Antinomie geführt: das Objekt ändert im Zurücksinken ständig seine Zeitstelle, und sollte doch im Zurücksinken seine Zeitstelle bewahren. In Wahrheit ändert das Objekt der sich stetig zurückschiebenden primären Erinnerung gar nicht seine Zeitstelle, sondern nur seinen Abstand vom aktuellen Jetzt, und zwar darum, weil das aktuelle Jetzt als ein immer neuer objektiver Zeitpunkt gilt, während das vergangene Zeitliche bleibt, was es ist. Wie aber entgegen dem Phänomen der ständigen Änderung des Zeitbewußtseins Bewußtsein von der objektiven Zeit und zunächst von identischen Zeitstellen zustandekommt, das ist nun die Frage. Damit hängt aufs engste die Frage nach der Konstitution der Objektivität individueller zeitlicher Gegenstände und Vorgänge zusammen: im Zeitbewußtsein vollzieht sich alle Objektivierung; ohne Aufklärung der Identität der Zeitstelle ist auch keine Aufklärung der Identität eines Objektes in der Zeit zu geben.
>
> Näher ausgeführt ist das Problem das folgende: Die Jetztphasen der Wahrnehmung erfahren stetig eine Modifikation, sie erhalten sich nicht einfach wie sie sind, sie fließen. Darin konstituiert sich, was wir als Zurücksinken in die Zeit bezeichnen. Der Ton erklingt jetzt, und alsbald sinkt er in die Vergangenheit, er, derselbe Ton. Das betrifft den Ton nach jeder seiner Phasen, und darum auch den ganzen. Nun scheint das Herabsinken durch unsere bisherigen Betrachtungen einigermaßen verständlich. Aber wie kommt es, daß wir gegenüber dem Herabsinken des Tones doch davon sprechen, daß ihm eine feste Stellung in der Zeit zukommt, daß sich Zeitpunkte und Zeitdauern in wiederholten Akten identifizieren lassen, wie es unsere Analyse des reproduktiven Bewußtseins aufwies? Der Ton und jeder Zeitpunkt in der Einheit des dauernden Tones hat ja seine absolut feste Stelle in der „objektiven“ (sei es auch die immanente) Zeit. Die Zeit ist starr, und doch fließt die Zeit. Im Zeitfluß, im stetigen Herabsinken in die Vergangenheit konstituiert sich eine nicht fließende, absolut feste, identische, objektive Zeit. Das ist das Problem.

*Note on the text.* The file sets the running head for p. 64 before the § heading (src L928), and the apparatus keys the whole passage to Hua X 64 (64,2–35, Texte L1330, L1355). The first paragraph joins two places in the manuscript. Up to "das ist nun die Frage" it rests on sheets "44"–"45b" of the 1905 lecture manuscript; from "Damit" on, on sheets "58"–"61"; sheets "54"–"57" are lost (src L1001; Texte L1347).

The antinomy lasts one sentence. It is announced as apparent (*scheinbar*), and "in truth" (*in Wahrheit*) removes it at once by separating two things the first sentence ran together, the position of what is past and its distance from the now. The position stays, the distance grows. The removal rests on a clause that assumes what is in question: the distance changes "because the actual now counts as an ever new objective point of time" (*weil das aktuelle Jetzt als ein immer neuer objektiver Zeitpunkt gilt*). "Counts as" (*gilt*) presupposes an objective order of points in which the now can count as one. So the dissolution holds for the description and not for the constitution, and the next sentence says so: "But how … consciousness of objective time … comes about: that is now the question." The antinomy falls away as a contradiction and comes back as a question of origin. The manuscript had no antinomy. Where the print has "Apparently we are led here to an antinomy", it has "Accordingly it seems as if the object constantly changed its temporal position" (*Danach scheint es, als ob das Objekt seine Zeitstelle immerfort änderte*, 64,2–7, Texte L1330). The Kantian word, with its promise of a contradiction reason cannot escape, is the redaction's, and the paragraph does not keep that promise.

The second paragraph restates the problem and ends in the paradox. "Time is rigid, and yet time flows" (*Die Zeit ist starr, und doch fließt die Zeit*). The two clauses mirror each other. The first puts "time" before its predicate, the second after it, so that the sentence begins and ends on the same word and lays two opposed predicates on one subject. "And yet" (*und doch*) admits the clash without resolving it. The next sentence resolves it by grammar: "In the flow of time … a non-flowing, absolutely fixed, identical, objective time is constituted." "And yet" has become "in". The flowing and the rigid are no longer one subject with two predicates but two terms, one constituted in the other. The flow is now "the flow of time" (*Zeitfluß*), and what it constitutes is "objective time". Both are still called time. That is the point the paradox leaves open, and the point §36 closes: the flow "is something we name after what is constituted, but it is nothing temporally 'objective'" (Hua X 75). Once Section Three has said this, "time flows" is no longer a statement about time. It is a borrowed name for what constitutes time.

The manuscript reached the paradox from the tone. Before "Time is rigid" it has: "'The tone sinks into the past', and yet it does not change its time" (*„Der Ton sinkt in die Vergangenheit“, und doch ändert er nicht seine Zeit*, 64,33, Texte L1355). The print cuts the tone and keeps the general form, which makes time itself the subject of both clauses. The parenthesis "(be it the immanent)" is Husserl's own insertion in the manuscript (64,32): the fixed time in question is not the world's time, which §1 excluded, but the order of immanent data, and the problem arises already there. The two adjectives also differ. "Rigid" (*starr*) belongs to the paradox; "fixed" (*fest*) to the description of position before it and the resolution after it. A rigid thing does not bend or move; a fixed position is one that does not change. Brough renders both as "fixed" ("Time is fixed, and yet time flows", CW4 L9049–L9051), which turns the paradox into a claim about positions and loses its image, the stiff against the flowing. The manuscript shows that the image was meant. On the sheets behind the start of §31 Husserl wrote that the objects move in the time-field, "where they change their place, while a piece of objective time is immovable and rigid" (*unbeweglich und starr*), and that "now is no objective determination of time, any more than past or future" (Texte L1333).

The redaction also changed the rank of the question the paradox leads into. In the print, the constitution of the objectivity of individual objects is "most closely connected" (*aufs engste … zusammen*) with the question of identical positions. In the manuscript the same thesis appears twice, once on the earlier sheets in square brackets and once on sheet "58", and both times it is "a main theme" or "the main theme of the theory of time-consciousness" (*das Hauptthema der Theorie des Zeitbewußtseins*), which "is not yet clarified": how the objectivity of the time-point is constituted in the constant flow, "and likewise the objectivity of the stretch of time and finally of the individual temporal object" (Texte L1323, L1355). The print makes the individuation thesis a neighbour of the paradox; the manuscript made the paradox a statement of the main theme.

The manuscript drew one more consequence, which Stein did not print. After answering the question with the identity of the object held fast against the flow of pushing back, Husserl wrote: "From this we understand how seductive it is to call time a form of consciousness, and even a form of intuition", and added in pencil: "only, of course, it must be said: not time, but original time-consciousness is the necessary form of every original intuition of an objectivity" (*nicht die Zeit, sondern das originäre Zeitbewußtsein ist die notwendige Form jeder originären Anschauung einer Objektivität*, Texte L1335). That is the paradox read in the Kantian vocabulary, and corrected. Rigid time is what is constituted; the flowing consciousness of time is the form. The rest of §31 carries the problem out on the side of sensation: each now brings "an original difference … belonging to a new dimension" even where content is unchanged (Hua X 65), and temporal position is what "first constitutes individuality" (Hua X 66–67). §§35–36 then deny the flow the predicates the paradox gave it: it changes, but its "change" can be neither "faster" nor "slower", and "there is nothing there that changes" (Hua X 74). The paradox of §31 survives, but only as the statement of a difference in level between the flow and the time it constitutes. Its second clause, taken literally, is what Section Three gives up.

## §31, Hua X 67: what the word "now" says

Reads [§31.7](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#primal-impression-and-the-objective-individual-point-of-time), the seventh paragraph under § 31, as far as the file runs.

The now cannot be defined before primal impression, and primal impression cannot be defined without the now. Husserl accepts the circle and decides its direction. The now-point is "to be defined through original sensation"; every sentence that runs the other way, defining individuality or originality by the now, is only "a pointer to what is meant". The word "now", taken in its strictest sense, says what primal impression has as its content. The word is a pointer, the impression is the definition. The passage turns the theory of individuation, which §31 has built on temporal position, back onto a consciousness that cannot itself be placed in a prior order of positions.

> What "individual" means here is the original temporal form of sensation, or, as I can also say, the temporal form of original sensation, here of the sensation of the respective now-point and only of it. But strictly, the now-point itself is to be defined through original sensation, so that the statement made is to count only as a pointer to what is meant. Impression is distinguished from phantasm by the character of originality. Now within impression we have to single out the primal impression, over against which stands the continuum of modifications in the consciousness of primary memory. Primal impression is the absolutely unmodified, the primal source for all further consciousness and being. Primal impression has as its content what the word now says, provided it is taken in the strictest sense. Each new now is the content of a new primal impression. Continually a new and ever new impression lights up, with ever new matter, now the same, now changing. What distinguishes primal impression from primal impression is the individualizing moment of the original.

German:

> Was hier ,,individuell'' heißt, das ist die ursprüngliche Temporalform der Empfindung, oder, wie ich auch sagen kann, die Temporalform der ursprünglichen Empfindung, hier der Empfindung des jeweiligen Jetztpunktes und nur dieses. Aber eigentlich ist der Jetztpunkt selbst durch die ursprüngliche Empfindung zu definieren, so daß der ausgesprochene Satz nur als Hinweis auf das, was gemeint sein soll, zu gelten hat. Impression gegenüber Phantasma unterscheidet sich durch den Charakter der Originarität. Nun haben wir innerhalb der Impression die Urimpression hervorzuheben, der gegenüber das Kontinuum von Modifikationen im primären Erinnerungsbewußtsein dasteht. Die Urimpression ist das absolut Unmodifizierte, die Urquelle für alles weitere Bewußtsein und Sein. Urimpression hat zum Inhalt das, was das Wort Jetzt besagt, wofern es im strengsten Sinne genommen wird. Jedes neue Jetzt ist Inhalt einer neuen Urimpression. Stetig leuchtet eine neue und immer neue Impression auf, mit immer neuer, bald gleicher, bald wechselnder Materie. Was Urimpression von Urimpression scheidet, das ist das individualisierende Moment der ursprünglichen.

*Note on the text.* The file breaks off here, at "the individualizing moment of the original" (src L993). The next line is the running head for p. 69, followed by §31's last paragraph. About a page of Hua X is missing (67,35 to 68,36; the apparatus annotates lines the file does not contain, Texte L1357), and no other copy is in the corpus. Brough's translation, made from the printed text, gives the missing part in English: the individualizing moment is that "of the impression of the original temporal position", which "is naturally nothing by itself"; the whole now-point undergoes past-modification, "and only by means of this modification have we exhausted the complete concept of the now, since it is a relative concept and refers to a 'past'" (CW4 L9305–L9320). The reading stops where the file stops. It does not argue from the missing German. The apparatus is incomplete as well: the Texte file passes from its note on 65,3 straight into a note on 67,25, so the manuscript wording of the passage's first two sentences cannot be checked (Texte L1355–L1356). The file also has no emphasis, and Brough's quotation marks around "now" may stand for spaced type in the print.

The passage first defines "individual" (*„individuell“*) by a phrase it then turns round. "The original temporal form of sensation, or, as I can also say, the temporal form of original sensation." The adjective "original" moves from the form to the sensation, and "as I can also say" (*wie ich auch sagen kann*) treats the two phrases as one. It is the same inversion of a genitive pair that §3 used against Brentano ("duration of sensation and sensation of duration"), used here in the other direction: there the inversion marked a difference, here it is offered as an identity. The identity is not argued. It holds only if the temporal form of a sensation is original just because the sensation is, and the next sentence makes that so. "But strictly" (*aber eigentlich*), "the now-point itself is to be defined through original sensation." The first sentence had used the now-point to define "individual"; the second makes the now-point depend on what the first sentence defined by it. Husserl does not repair the circle. He demotes the first sentence: it is "to count only as a pointer to what is meant" (*nur als Hinweis auf das, was gemeint sein soll*). A definition becomes a pointing.

The same order holds for the word. "Primal impression has as its content what the word now says, provided it is taken in the strictest sense" (*hat zum Inhalt das, was das Wort Jetzt besagt, wofern es im strengsten Sinne genommen wird*). The meaning of the word "now" (what it "says", *besagt*) is used to point to the content, and the condition "in the strictest sense" admits that the word ordinarily says more: an extended now, a present of several seconds, the now of a whole melody. Only the narrowest sense of the word fits, and that sense is the one the impression fixes. Brough's English gives the rest of the thought from the missing page: the strictest sense is not the full concept, which is reached only with the modification into the past, because "now" is relative and points to "past" as "past" points to "now". The word, taken strictly, singles out a limit that has no content of its own apart from the impression.

The manuscript shows Husserl working toward this wording. The apparatus notes, for the lines it keeps, that "has as its content" (*hat zum Inhalt*) is a correction in the manuscript for "is" (*ist*), and that "content of a new" (*Inhalt einer*) is a correction for "a" (*eine*) (67,32 and 67,34, Texte L1357). Husserl first wrote that primal impression is what the word now says, and that each new now is a new primal impression. He corrected both to a relation of consciousness and content. The correction keeps the now from collapsing into the act that gives it, and it keeps the impression on the side of consciousness, as something that has a content. A second correction concerns the definition of impression itself. "By the character of originality" (*durch den Charakter der Originarität*) replaces, in the manuscript, the deleted "the absence of phantasy-modification" (*die Abwesenheit der Phantasiemodifikation*), and the next sentence first began "If we exclude phantasy-modification, then…" (67,26f. and 67,27, Texte L1356). Impression was first defined by what it lacks, the mark of phantasy. Husserl changed this to a positive character. Yet the following sentence keeps a negative description: primal impression is "the absolutely unmodified" (*das absolut Unmodifizierte*). Originality is a positive character, but the only way the passage can describe it is as the point before every modification. In the margin Husserl glossed the primal impression as "the pure one, which is free of all modification and is the foundation of the objectivation of the now" (67,28, Texte L1357).

The passage sits at the turn of §31 from the objectivation of position to its source. The paragraphs before it took temporal position as one side of the apprehension of a sensation, with "representatives of temporal position" to be apprehended (Hua X 66). A page earlier the phenomenological difference between the same sensation now and at another now was "the primal source of the individuality of the 'this'" (*Urquell der Individualität des „dies“*, Hua X 66). Here the source is widened to "all further consciousness and being". The step from individuality to being rests on the definition just given. If the now is defined through primal impression, and every temporal position is a former now held fast in modification, then every position, and every individual placed by it, derives from a primal impression. "Further" (*weitere*) says that the derivation runs through modification: everything after the primal impression is a modification of it. §31's last paragraph applies the result to transcendent perception, where the bird's flight appears "as primal givenness in the now-point" and new "primal being" (*Ursein*) wells up "in the living source-point of being, the now" (Hua X 69).

Section Three keeps the order and drops the vocabulary of content. §36 describes the flow as something "springing forth in an actuality-point, primal source-point, 'now'", and adds: "For all this we lack names" (Hua X 75). The now is in quotation marks there, as a name borrowed from what is constituted. The passage at §31 already pointed that way. It made the word "now" a pointer to something the word can only indicate, and made the now-point depend on the impression rather than the reverse. What Section Three revises is the other half of the sentence, "has as its content". Once the §1 note withdraws the schema of apprehension-content and apprehension (Hua X 7), the primal impression can no longer be a consciousness that has a now as its content. It becomes the actuality-point of the flow itself, and the definition through the word, which here still separated the impression from what it gives, is what remains of §31's attempt to say what the now is.

## §34, Hua X 73: three levels, counted from the top

Reads [§34.1](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#distinction-of-the-levels-of-constitution), the paragraph under § 34.

Time is constituted on three levels: things in objective time; immanent unities in a time before experience, among them the appearances that constitute things; and a flow that constitutes time and is not itself in it. Each level is constituted in the one below, and only the lowest constitutes without being constituted. Section Three is organized by this list. The list's wording, though, does two things that its manuscript source did not. It counts the levels from the top, from the things down to the flow, and it presents them as a finding of the lectures so far. The manuscript counted from the bottom and kept its terms in quotation marks.

> Having studied time-consciousness, starting from the most conspicuous phenomena, in a few main directions and in various layers, it will be good to establish the various levels of constitution for once in their essential structure and to go through them systematically.
>
> We found:
>
> 1. the things of experience in objective time (where various levels of empirical being would still have to be distinguished, which have not been considered so far: the experiential thing of the single subject, the intersubjectively identical thing, the thing of physics);
>
> 2. the constituting manifolds of appearance of various levels, the immanent unities in pre-empirical time;
>
> 3. the absolute time-constituting flow of consciousness.

German:

> Nachdem wir, von den augenfälligsten Phänomenen ausgehend, das Zeitbewußtsein nach einigen Hauptrichtungen und in verschiedenen Schichten studiert haben, wird es gut sein, die verschiedenen Konstitutionsstufen einmal in ihrem wesensmäßigen Aufbau festzustellen und systematisch durchzugehen.
>
> Wir fanden:
>
> 1. die Dinge der Erfahrung in der objektiven Zeit (wobei noch verschiedene Stufen des empirischen Seins zu scheiden wären, die bisher nicht berücksichtigt wurden: das Erfahrungsding des einzelnen Subjekts, das intersubjektiv identische Ding, das Ding der Physik);
>
> 2. die konstituierenden Erscheinungsmannigfaltigkeiten verschiedener Stufe, die immanenten Einheiten in der präempirischen Zeit;
>
> 3. den absoluten zeitkonstituierenden Bewußtseinsfluß.

The second level carries the weight, and its two appositive phrases name it from opposite sides. "The constituting manifolds of appearance" looks up: these appearances constitute the things of level 1. "The immanent unities in pre-empirical time" looks down: the same appearances are themselves unities, constituted and placed in a time. The present participle "constituting" (*konstituierenden*) is used for level 2 and again, compounded, for level 3, "time-constituting" (*zeitkonstituierenden*). Both levels constitute. What sets the third apart is what it constitutes, time itself, and the adjective "absolute", which says that it is not in turn constituted by anything. Level 2 is constituting and constituted at once, and that double position is what the earlier lectures lacked. In Section Two the appearance of a tone was either the sensed content or the apprehension of it. Here the appearances, acts included, are unities in their own time. "Pre-empirical" (*präempirisch*) says which time that is: not the time of experience (*Erfahrung*), in which the things of level 1 stand, but a time before experience in that sense, the immanent time of §1 and §31.

The wording of the frame claims more than the lectures had shown. "We found" (*wir fanden*) presents the list as a summary. But the 1905 lectures did not find the third level. They found retention, primal impression and the continuum of modifications, and treated them as the consciousness of immanent objects, with apprehension-contents and apprehensions of temporal position as late as §31. The absolute flow, as distinct from the immanent unities, is a result of the manuscripts of 1907–09 and 1911 on which §§35–39 rest. "For once" (*einmal*) and "systematically" (*systematisch*) also sit oddly with "a few main directions" (*einigen Hauptrichtungen*): the list is offered as an ordering of what was done, and it is the introduction of something that was not.

The apparatus confirms that the frame is not Husserl's. No manuscript was found for §34. The editor's table says it was "presumably edited by Edith Stein" (*vermutlich redigiert von Edith Stein*), and the text "roughly follows" a single sheet of 1907–09 printed in Part B as Nr. 40, on which Stein noted that she had "used" it for this place (Texte L793, L1384; src L4875). Nr. 40 is headed "Levels of objectivity" (*Stufen der Objektivität*), and it lists them in the opposite order: "1. The flow of 'consciousness'. 2. The pre-empirical 'time' with past, 'now', after; and the pre-empirically 'existent', what endures and changes (tone as 'content of consciousness'). 3. The levels of empirical being" (Hua X 286–287). Three differences follow from the comparison. First, Nr. 40 begins with the flow, so that each level is built on the one before. §34 begins with the things and descends, which suits a lecture course that started from the "most conspicuous phenomena", and makes the flow the last thing found rather than the first thing presupposed. Second, Nr. 40 puts its key words in quotation marks: "consciousness", "time", "now", "existent", "content of consciousness". The flow is a flow of "consciousness", and the second level has a "time" and a "now" that are not yet time and now in the ordinary sense. §34 drops all of them and adds the adjectives "absolute" and "time-constituting". The manuscript held its vocabulary at a distance, and the redaction states it as settled. Third, Nr. 40's second level is the immanent content, "tone as 'content of consciousness'", while §34's is "manifolds of appearance". The redaction has already moved acts and appearances onto the second level, which §37 will argue for.

The note printed just before Nr. 40 in Part B, the end of Nr. 39, gives the third level the sense §34 only names. "In a certain way before all positing of unity, that is, all objectivation, lies the absolute consciousness. Unity is unity of objectivation, and objectivation is objectivating, but not objectivated. All non-objectivated objectivation belongs to the sphere of absolute consciousness" (*Objektivation ist eben objektivierend, aber nicht objektiviert*, Hua X 286). The participle pair is the same one §34 hides in its adjectives. Levels 1 and 2 are objectivated; level 3 is objectivating and not objectivated. "In a certain way" (*gewissermaßen*) hedges the "before": the flow does not precede the unities in time, since it is not in time at all.

Section Three then works through the list from the bottom. §35 opens on "this absolute consciousness, which lies before all constitution" (*dieses absolute, aller Konstitution vorausliegende Bewußtsein*, Hua X 73), and contrasts it with "the constituted unities of the most varied level". §36 calls it "absolute subjectivity", for which "we lack names". §37 restates the three levels from the side of the act as "consciousness (flow), appearance (immanent object), transcendent object", and with that revises the *Logical Investigations*. §§40–44 treat the immanent contents and the thing-appearances, and §45 adds what the list left out, objects that are not in time at all. The parenthesis on the levels of empirical being, the single subject's thing, the intersubjective thing, the thing of physics, is the one point of the list Section Three does not take up. Nr. 40 had already marked it as another inquiry, into empathy, "body and soul", and "the identical one world" of communication (Hua X 287). The list is the plan of Section Three, then, not its result, and the descending order it was given by the redaction is the order of the lecture course, not the order of constitution.

## §35, Hua X 74: neither change nor rest

Reads [§35.3](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#differences-between-the-constituted-unities-and-the-constituting-flow), the third paragraph under § 35.

Whatever is in time either changes or rests, and whatever changes does so at some rate. The constituting flow does neither. It cannot rest, because none of its phases can be held and stretched out; it cannot change, because there is nothing in it that changes; and it has no rate, because a rate is measured against a duration, and the flow has none. §35 sets this out as the point-by-point negation of two laws it has just stated for everything constituted in time. The passage says what the flow is not, in the words of what it constitutes, and calls the result absurd.

> If we now consider, by comparison, the constituting phenomena, we find a flow, and every phase of this flow is a continuity of shadings. But in principle no phase of this flow can be spread out into a continuous succession, that is, the flow cannot be thought so transformed that this phase extended itself in identity with itself. Quite the contrary, we find in principle and necessarily a flow of continual "change", and this change has the absurd feature that it runs exactly as it runs, and can run neither "faster" nor "slower". Further, every object that changes is missing here; and insofar as in every process "something" proceeds, what is in question here is no process. There is nothing there that changes, and therefore there can be no meaningful talk of something that endures either. It is therefore senseless to want to find here something that, in a duration, for once does not change.

German:

> Betrachten wir nun im Vergleich dazu die konstituierenden Phänomene, so finden wir einen Fluß, und jede Phase dieses Flusses ist eine Abschattungskontinuität. Aber prinzipiell ist keine Phase dieses Flusses auszubreiten in eine kontinuierliche Folge, also der Fluß so umgewandelt zu denken, daß diese Phase sich ausdehnte in Identität mit sich selbst. Ganz im Gegenteil finden wir prinzipiell notwendig einen Fluß stetiger ,,Veränderung'', und diese Veränderung hat das Absurde, daß sie genau so läuft, wie sie läuft, und weder ,,schneller'' noch ,,langsamer'' laufen kann. Sodann fehlt hier jedes Objekt, das sich verändert; und sofern in jedem Vorgang ,,etwas'' vorgeht, handelt es sich hier um keinen Vorgang. Es ist nichts da, das sich verändert, und darum kann auch von etwas, das dauert, sinnvoll keine Rede sein. Es ist also sinnlos, hier etwas finden zu wollen, was in einer Dauer sich einmal nicht verändert.

*Note on the text.* The file is hard-wrapped here with end-of-line hyphenation (src L1108–L1119); the quotation joins the lines. The apparatus for §§35–39 records only departures from the 1928 first edition (Texte L1390), so the differences from the manuscript discussed below come from comparing the passage with Nr. 54 in Part B, which prints the manuscript (Hua X 370, src L6020). Brough's translation agrees with the file's text of §35, including the final negation (CW4 L9805–L9830).

The passage is built as a reply to the second of two laws stated just before it. Law 2 reads: "In principle every phase of a change can be spread out into a rest, every phase of a rest led over into a change" (*Prinzipiell ist jede Phase einer Veränderung in eine Ruhe auszubreiten*, Hua X 74). The reply repeats the law's words with the quantifier reversed: "in principle no phase of this flow can be spread out" (*prinzipiell ist keine Phase dieses Flusses auszubreiten*). "In principle" (*prinzipiell*) stands in both, so the denial has the same rank as the law. A phase of a constituted process can be held and extended, so that what was momentary becomes a stretch of rest. A phase of the flow cannot "extend itself in identity with itself". The manuscript was blunter about the relation. It says the flow behaves so "in contradiction to 2)" (*im Widerspruch zu 2)*), and where §35 has "extended itself" it has "perpetuated itself" (*sich perpetuierte*). The redaction dropped the cross-reference and kept the verbal mirror, which still shows the point: the flow is defined by violating a law that holds for everything in time.

The sentence on change is where the passage turns. The flow is a flow of continual "change", and this change "has the absurd feature" (*hat das Absurde*) "that it runs exactly as it runs, and can run neither 'faster' nor 'slower'". The noun is a property: absurdity is something the change has, not something said about it. What makes it absurd is the tautology "it runs exactly as it runs". Law 2 had given every change its speed "in a figure" (*im Gleichnis*) "with reference to the same duration". Even for objects, then, a rate was a figure of speech measured against a duration. The flow has no duration against which it could be measured, so it has no rate even in the figure. "Faster" and "slower" are in quotation marks because they have no application. The phrase recurs with a different sense in Section Two: there presentification can be run through "'faster' or 'slower'" at will (§20, Hua X 48). Reproduction is free in its tempo because it is an immanent process. The flow that constitutes it is not.

The next sentences deny the remaining term. "Every object that changes is missing here", and since in every process "something" (*„etwas“*) proceeds, "what is in question here is no process". Then: "There is nothing there that changes, and therefore there can be no meaningful talk of something that endures either." The last sentence closes the other side of law 2's disjunction: "It is therefore senseless to want to find here something that, in a duration, for once does not change." The flow is not change, since nothing changes. Nor is it rest, since nothing endures that could stay unchanged. Both alternatives of law 2 are refused. That double refusal is what makes the quotation marks around "change" necessary: the word is the only one available for something that is not still, and it says more than the flow allows.

The manuscript reached the same place by a different route, and the redaction smoothed it. In Nr. 54 the word "change" is not in quotation marks; the file prints it with emphasis, as *Veränderung* (Hua X 370). Husserl stressed the word and then took it back outright: "But further: where is the object that changes in this flow? In every process, after all, something proceeds *a priori*? Here, however, nothing proceeds. The change is no change" (*Hier geht aber nichts vor. Die Veränderung ist keine Veränderung*). The quotation marks around "change" and "something", the hedges on which a reading of §35 as cautious vocabulary rests, are the redaction's. Husserl's own text used the word with full weight and contradicted it. The manuscript's last sentence also differs. It says it is "senseless to want to find here something that once changes in a duration" (*was in einer Dauer einmal sich verändert*). §35 has "for once does not change". So the manuscript denied change twice, and the redaction turned the second denial into a denial of rest. The redaction's version is the more complete argument, since it answers both terms of law 2. It is not the manuscript's.

The passage gives Section Three its method. What the flow is can be said only by negation, in the vocabulary of the constituted, and with that vocabulary held at a distance. §36 states the consequence: these phenomena are "objectivities of a fundamentally different kind" from what is constituted in time, and the flow is "something we name after what is constituted" (Hua X 75). The same Nr. 54 continues with the contrast that makes the point concrete. Every act is "a flow in which an immanent temporal unity is constituted", and the unity, "the judgment, the wish", has "its immanent duration" and may proceed "faster and less 'fast'" (Hua X 372). Acts have rates; the flow in which they are constituted does not. §39 asks how such a flow can be conscious of itself at all, and answers that it appears to itself without a second flow. That answer presupposes §35. A flow with a rate would need a time in which to have it, and a second flow would be needed to constitute that time. The refusal of "faster" and "slower" is the first step out of that regress.

## §36, Hua X 75: named after what is constituted

Reads [§36.1](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#the-time-constituting-flow-as-absolute-subjectivity), the paragraph under § 36.

The flow has no name of its own. Every word for it, "flow", "now", "before", "succession", is taken from what it constitutes and applied to it in a sense that cannot be the same. Husserl does not conclude that the flow cannot be described. He concludes that its description must say "belongs to" where the constituted would say "is", and must mark its images as images. §35 had shown what the flow is not. §36 tries to say what it is, and the sentence on which everything turns is one whose verb Husserl cannot supply.

> The time-constituting phenomena are therefore evidently objectivities of a fundamentally different kind from those constituted in time. They are not individual objects or individual processes, and the predicates of such cannot meaningfully be ascribed to them. So it can also make no sense to say of them (and to say in the same meaning) that they are in the now and were before, that they succeed one another in time or are simultaneous with one another, and so on. But one can and must say: a certain continuity of appearance, namely one that is a phase of the time-constituting flow, belongs to a now, namely the one it constitutes, and belongs to a before, namely as the one that is constitutive (we cannot say: was) for the before. But is the flow not a succession, does it not after all have a now, an actual phase, and a continuity of pasts, conscious now in retentions? We cannot say otherwise than: this flow is something that we name so after what is constituted, but it is nothing temporally "objective". It is absolute subjectivity, and has the absolute properties of something to be designated in the image as "flow", springing forth in a point of actuality, a primal source-point, "now", and so on. In the experience of actuality we have the primal source-point and a continuity of moments of echo. For all this we lack the names.

> Die zeitkonstituierenden Phänomene sind also evidentermaßen prinzipiell andere Gegenständlichkeiten als die in der Zeit konstituierten. Sie sind keine individuellen Objekte bzw. keine individuellen Vorgänge, und die Prädikate solcher können ihnen sinnvoll nicht zugeschrieben werden. Also kann es auch keinen Sinn haben, von ihnen zu sagen (und in gleicher Bedeutung zu sagen), sie seien im Jetzt und seien vorher gewesen, sie folgten einander zeitlich nach oder seien miteinander gleichzeitig usw. "Nämlich das, sie konstituiert" (namely the one it constitutes) lacks a word in the file, most likely the relative pronoun ("das, das sie konstituiert"); Nr. 54 reads "nämlich zu dem, das sie konstituiert" (src L6026). The English follows the sense. The apparatus restores emphases from the manuscript at 75,9, 10, 15f., 17 and 18 (Texte L1393). The file shows none of them, and the passage cannot be read for emphasis.

The paragraph moves from "is" to "belongs to". The first sentences deny the flow every predicate of the constituted: it is not in the now, was not before, does not follow or coincide. Then comes a permission stated as an obligation, "one can and must say" (*kann und muß man sagen*): a phase of the flow "belongs to a now" (*gehöre zu einem Jetzt*), the one it constitutes, and "belongs to a before". The relation of belonging replaces the copula. A phase is not now; it belongs to the now it constitutes. The parenthesis three lines earlier prepared this: one cannot say these things "in the same meaning" (*in gleicher Bedeutung*). The words are not forbidden, only their sameness of meaning. "Now" may be said of the flow, as a now to which a phase belongs, not as a now in which it is.

The second "belongs" is where the language gives out. A phase belongs to a before "as the one that is constitutive (we cannot say: was) for the before". The parenthesis stands where the past tense would go and refuses it. But it leaves a present tense standing, "is constitutive" (*konstitutiv ist*), and "is" is no less temporal than "was". The redaction's sentence denies one tense and uses another. The manuscript has no such verb. Nr. 54 reads "namely to the one which constitutive (we cannot say: 'was') for the before" (*nämlich zu dem, das konstitutiv (wir können nicht sagen: „war“) für das Vorher*, Hua X 371). The relative clause has no finite verb at all; the parenthesis occupies its place. Whether Husserl left the verb out on purpose or by a slip of the pen, the gap says exactly what the parenthesis says: there is no tense in which the constituting phase can be said to stand to the before it constitutes. The redaction filled the gap with "is", and with it the problem it was meant to show. (The quotation marks around "war" in Nr. 54 are the editor's, 371,5, Texte L2197.)

The objection that follows is stated in the flow's own favour: is it not a succession, with a now, an actual phase, and pasts held in retentions? The answer concedes the vocabulary and denies its reference. "This flow is something that we name so after what is constituted" (*das wir nach dem Konstituierten so nennen*), "but it is nothing temporally 'objective'". The quotation marks around "objective" hold the word at a distance, and the adverb "temporally" (*zeitlich*) says in which sense. The apparatus to Nr. 54 shows that Husserl added that adverb in the manuscript (371,11), and that at 371,10, the line of the same sentence, he struck "immanent" before "flow" (Texte L2197). He had first written of an "immanent" flow and "nothing 'objective'". Both were too broad or too narrow. The flow is not immanent, since immanence belongs to the unities of the second level of §34. And it is not nothing objective in every sense, since it can be described. It is nothing objective in time.

What it is, positively, is "absolute subjectivity" (*die absolute Subjektivität*), the first use of the phrase in the lectures. Its properties are "absolute" too, but they are those "of something to be designated in the image as 'flow'" (*eines im Bilde als „Fluß“ zu Bezeichnenden*). "Flow" is in quotation marks and declared an image. So is "now", in the apposition "a point of actuality, a primal source-point, 'now'", where three names are tried for one thing and only the last, the borrowed one, is marked. The print adds a participle that the manuscript lacks: the flow is something "springing forth in" (*Entspringenden*) the point of actuality. Nr. 54 has "the absolute properties of something to be designated in the image as 'flow', of a point of actuality, primal source-point 'now' etc." (Hua X 371). There the properties are those of a point; in §36 they are those of something that springs forth from one. The redaction extended the image of the source that row 10 traced through Section Two into the description of the flow itself. Then the last sentence: "For all this we lack the names" (*Für all das fehlen uns die Namen*). The manuscript says, more plainly, "for all this we have no names" (*haben wir keine Namen*). The print's "lack" makes the absence a deficiency in a language that ought to have them.

The paragraph is the end of a path that began at §31 and finishes in §39. At §31 "time flows" was a statement about time, and the now was defined through primal impression and pointed to by the word. §35 refused the flow the predicates of change, rest and rate. §36 draws the consequence for language: the flow can only be named "after what is constituted", in images and with belonging in place of being. §39 then has to explain how such a flow appears to itself without being in time, and answers with the double intentionality of retention, the transverse intentionality toward the constituted and the longitudinal one toward the flow. The "and so on" (*usw.*) after the list of names, and the "lack" that closes it, are the place in Hua X where the language of time-consciousness admits it is borrowed. The rest of Section Three goes on using it, under that admission.

## §37, Hua X 75–76: what was called an "act"

Reads [§37.1](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#appearances-of-transcendent-objects-as-constituted-unities), the paragraph under § 37.

An act is not the last term of an analysis of consciousness. A judgment or a wish is an immanent unity with its own duration, constituted, like a sensed tone, in the flow; and the consciousness that constitutes it is not the act but the flow. The triad on which this rests, "consciousness (flow), appearance (immanent object), transcendent object", replaces the triad of act, content and object. Each term moves down a level: what had been the act becomes an appearance, an object in immanent time, and only the flow is left as consciousness. The printed text presents this as a correction of the *Logical Investigations*. The manuscript stated it without naming the earlier book.

> It is still to be remarked that when we speak of the "act of perception" and say that it is the point of perceiving proper, to which a continuous series of "retentions" is attached, we have thereby described no temporal immanent unities, but precisely moments of the flow. For the appearance, say that of a house, is a temporal being, an enduring, changing one, and so on, just as the immanent tone is, which is no appearance. But the house-appearance is not the perceptual consciousness and retentional consciousness. This can be understood only as the time-constituting, as moment of the flow. In the same way the memory-appearance (or the remembered immanent, perhaps the remembered immanent primary content) is to be distinguished from the memory-consciousness with its memory-retentions. We have to distinguish everywhere: consciousness (flow), appearance (immanent object), transcendent object (if a primary content is not the immanent object). Not all consciousness has relation to "objectively" (namely transcendently) temporal things, to objective individuality, as, for example, that of external perception does. In every consciousness we find an "immanent content"; in the case of the contents that are called "appearances", this is either appearance of something individual (of an external temporal thing) or appearance of something non-temporal. In judging, for example, I have the appearance "judgment", namely an immanent temporal unity, and in it the judgment in the logical sense "appears". Judging always has the character of the flow. Everywhere, accordingly, what in the *Logical Investigations* we called "act" or "intentional experience" is a flow in which an immanent temporal unity is constituted (the judgment, the wish and so on), which has its immanent duration and perhaps proceeds faster or less fast. These unities, which are constituted in the absolute stream, are in the immanent time, which is one, and in it there is a simultaneous and an equally long duration (or perhaps the same duration, namely for two immanent objects enduring simultaneously), and further a certain determinability by before and after.

Aber die Haus-Erscheinung ist nicht das Wahrnehmungsbewußtsein und retentionale Bewußtsein. Dieses kann nur verstanden werden als das zeitkonstituierende, als Moment des Flusses. Ebenso ist die Erinnerungserscheinung (oder das erinnerte Immanente, ev. der erinnerte immanente primäre Inhalt) zu unterscheiden vom Erinnerungsbewußtsein mit seinen Erinnerungsretentionen. Im Urteilen z.B. habe ich die Erscheinung „Urteil“, nämlich immanente zeitliche Einheit, und darin „erscheint“ das Urteil im logischen Sinne. Das Urteilen hat immer den Charakter des Flusses. Überall ist sonach das, was wir in den *Logischen Untersuchungen* „Akt“ oder „intentionales Erlebnis“ nannten, ein Fluß, in dem eine immanente Zeiteinheit sich konstituiert (das Urteil, der Wunsch usw.), die ihre immanente Dauer hat und ev. schneller oder weniger schnell vonstatten geht. Diese Einheiten, die sich im absoluten Strom konstituieren, sind in der immanenten Zeit, die eine ist, und in ihr gibt es ein Gleichzeitig und gleichlange Dauer (oder ev. dieselbe Dauer, nämlich für zwei immanente gleichzeitig dauernde Objekte), ferner eine gewisse Bestimmbarkeit nach Vorher und Nachher.

A note to "appears" says that "appearance" is used here "in an extended sense" (src L1296). The apparatus restores an emphasis at 76,12 that the file does not show (Texte L1393).

The paragraph opens by reclassifying a phrase used all through Section Two. "When we speak of the 'act of perception' and say that it is the point of perceiving proper, to which a continuous series of 'retentions' is attached", we have described "no temporal immanent unities, but precisely moments of the flow". The quotation marks around "act of perception" (*„Wahrnehmungsakt“*) and "retentions" take back the words of the earlier analysis without discarding the analysis. What §§11–17 described as a now-apprehension with its tail of retentions was not an act enduring in time. It was a cross-section of the flow. The point is made by contrast with a thing that is in time: the appearance of a house "is a temporal being, an enduring, changing one", "just as the immanent tone is". Appearance and tone stand together on the second level of §34. The house-appearance can endure and change, as §35 said everything constituted must. The "perceptual consciousness and retentional consciousness" cannot, and "can be understood only as the time-constituting".

The triad follows as a rule for every case: "consciousness (flow), appearance (immanent object), transcendent object". Each pair is an apposition with the explanatory term in parentheses. Consciousness is glossed as "flow", appearance as "immanent object", so that the middle term is defined as an object, and the parenthesis after the third term, "if a primary content is not the immanent object", allows for the case where there is no transcendent object, the sensed tone. The quotation marks then go on to the word "objectively", "'objectively' (namely transcendently) temporal": the parenthesis fixes the sense, as §31's "(be it the immanent)" fixed the opposite one. A judgment shows why the extension is needed. In judging "I have the appearance 'judgment', namely an immanent temporal unity, and in it the judgment in the logical sense 'appears'". The verb is in quotation marks because what appears is not temporal at all; the note says "appearance" is used "in an extended sense". The triad thus holds even where the object is ideal. The flow constitutes the judging, the judging as immanent unity has a duration, and the judgment in the logical sense is what appears in it.

The sentence that names the earlier work is the redaction's. In Nr. 54, the manuscript of late 1911 on which §37 rests, Husserl wrote: "Judging is always the flow, and so everywhere what we call act is a flow in which an immanent temporal unity is constituted" (*Das Urteilen ist immer der Fluß, und so überall ist das, was wir Akt nennen, ein Fluß*, Hua X 372). §37 has "Judging always has the character of the flow. Everywhere, accordingly, what in the *Logical Investigations* we called 'act' or 'intentional experience' is a flow". Three things changed. "Is the flow" became "has the character of the flow", which is weaker: the manuscript identified judging with the flow, the print attributes a character to it. "What we call act", in the present tense, a statement about usage now, became "what in the *Logical Investigations* we called", in the past tense, a statement about a book. And the pair "'act' or 'intentional experience'" was added. That pair is the *Logical Investigations*' own: the Fifth Investigation fixes the term "intentional experiences" and adds that "as the shorter expression we shall use the word *act*" (*das Wort* Akt *gebrauchen*, LU V §13, Hua XIX/1 392). The redaction quotes the earlier terminology correctly. But in Husserl's sentence the target was not his book. It was the word "act" wherever it is used, his own present usage included. Two smaller changes go the same way. The manuscript's triad is "consciousness (flow), appearance (immanent object) and object" (*und Gegenstand*); §37 adds "transcendent". And the manuscript's "objectively temporal" has neither quotation marks nor the parenthesis "(namely transcendently)" (Hua X 371–372). The redaction made the triad more explicit and turned a statement of method into a self-correction.

The passage is the hinge of Section Three. Before it, §§35–36 said what the flow is by negation. After it, the analysis can proceed by levels. §38 asks how one flow constitutes the one immanent time in which these unities are simultaneous and successive, the time the last sentence here announces: "immanent time, which is one". §39 asks how the flow, which is not in that time, is conscious of itself. §§40–42 return to the immanent contents and to impression and reproduction, now as unities on the second level. §43 turns to the thing-appearances and the "primal apprehensions" in which they are constituted, which is where the *Logical Investigations*' schema of content and apprehension is put in its place. The parenthesis "faster or less fast" connects back to §35: the act, as immanent unity, has a tempo; the flow does not. The understanding the paragraph establishes is a single displacement. The act leaves the side of consciousness for the side of the constituted, and consciousness, in the strict sense of Section Three, is what remains: the flow, which is not an act. The redaction's reference to the earlier book makes this look like the correction of a book. In the manuscript it is a change in what the word "act" can name.

## §38, Hua X 78: "together" is not "simultaneous"

Reads [§38.2](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#unity-of-the-flow-of-consciousness-and-constitution-of-simultaneity-and-succession), the second paragraph under § 38.

The flow has two kinds of togetherness, and neither is simultaneity. Several primal sensations, a tone and a colour, occur together in one phase; that is the basis on which the simultaneity of the tone and the colour is constituted. A primal sensation also occurs together with the continuous series of retentions of earlier ones; that is the basis on which succession is constituted. Both are "together" (*Zugleich*), a word that says at once without saying at the same time. "Simultaneous" (*gleichzeitig*) says at the same time, and time is what these togethers constitute. So the passage keeps the two words apart: the togethers of the flow may be named, but neither may be called simultaneous. The terms it coins for them are Stein's, and the manuscript shows what they replace.

> But "together" with the consciousness of primal sensation are continuous series of modes of running-off of "earlier" primal sensations, of earlier now-consciousness. This together is a together of modes of consciousness continuously modified in their form, whereas the together of the primal sensations is a together of purely form-identical modes. In the continuity of the modes of running-off we can take out a point; then we find in it, too, a together of form-alike modes of running-off, or rather an identical mode of running-off. These two togethers must be distinguished in essence. The one is a basic piece for the constitution of simultaneity, the other a basic piece for the constitution of temporal succession, although on the other hand simultaneity is nothing without temporal succession and temporal succession nothing without simultaneity, so that simultaneity and temporal succession must be constituted correlatively and inseparably. Terminologically we can distinguish between the fluxional pre-together and the impressional together of fluxions. We cannot call either the one or the other together a simultaneous. Of a time of the last constituting consciousness there can no longer be any talk. With the primal sensations that initiate the retentional process, the simultaneity of, say, a colour and a tone is originally constituted, their being in one "actual now"; but the primal sensations themselves are not simultaneous, and still less do we call the phases of the fluxional pre-together simultaneous phases of consciousness, any more than we can call the succession of consciousness a temporal succession.

The apparatus restores emphases at 78,14–17 that the file does not show (Texte L1393), so the passage cannot be read for emphasis. "Pre-together" renders *Vor-Zugleich* literally; "together" is kept throughout for *Zugleich* and *Zusammen*, and "simultaneous" for *gleichzeitig* only.

The two togethers are told apart by form. The primal sensations of one phase, a tone-now and a colour-now, are "a together of purely form-identical modes": each is primal sensation, they differ only in content, "only the now is the same" (Hua X 78). The primal sensation and the retentions that accompany it form "a together of modes of consciousness continuously modified in their form": the impression and its retentions of the just-past, and of the past before that, each a different mode of running-off. The first together grounds simultaneity, the second succession. The word that names both is put in quotation marks at its first appearance, "'together' with the consciousness of primal sensation", and the passage uses *Zusammen* and *Zugleich* side by side. Neither carries a reference to time. *Gleichzeitig* does: it is built on *Zeit*. The rule "we cannot call either the one or the other together a simultaneous" (*ein oder das andere Zugleich ein Gleichzeitig nennen*) is a rule about that one syllable. The togethers of the flow may be called togethers. Calling them simultaneous would put them in the time they constitute, and "of a time of the last constituting consciousness there can no longer be any talk".

The terms then have to say which together is which, and they do it by morphology. "The impressional together of fluxions" (*impressionales Zugleich von Fluxionen*) is the together of primal impressions, each the head of its own flux; the adjective "impressional" names what they have in common, the form of impression. "The fluxional pre-together" (*fluxionales Vor-Zugleich*) is the together of a primal sensation with its retentional continuum. The last sentence fixes the assignment. Its "phases" are what "we still less call simultaneous phases of consciousness", and §39 opens by saying what it is: "the continuum of phases that attach to a primal sensation, each of which is retentional consciousness of the earlier now" (Hua X 79). The prefix *Vor-* can be read two ways. It may mean a together "before" simultaneity, prior to it in constitution; or the together of the "before", the earlier nows held in retention with the present one. The manuscript supports the second reading more than the first.

The manuscript is Nr. 54, of late 1911, and its terms are different. "We distinguish terminologically, say: the phansic momentary-together, the phansic time-stretch-together" (*Wir unterscheiden terminologisch etwa: das phansische Momentan-Zugleich, das phansische Zeitstrecken-Zugleich*, Hua X 375). The final sentence of the paragraph is otherwise nearly the same, with one substitution: "still less do we call the phases of the time-stretch-together simultaneous phases of consciousness" (*die Phasen des Zeitstrecken-Zugleich*, Hua X 375–376). So Stein's "fluxional pre-together" renders Husserl's "time-stretch-together", and her "impressional together of fluxions" his "momentary-together". The manuscript's terms are plainer about structure: one together is at a moment, the other spans a stretch of modes, "as the word stretch indicates", Nr. 54 adds a page later, "a continuous one-dimensional series" (Hua X 376). Both are qualified as "phansic" (*phansisch*), the adjective Nr. 54 uses for the level of the flow, as in "the phansic succession of primal sensations". The redaction dropped it. It also dropped the hedges. Husserl distinguished "say" (*etwa*), and added a warning "with regard to the clumsy mode of expression" (*hinsichtlich der schwerfälligen Ausdrucksweise*): "we do not, for instance, call one or the other together a simultaneous". Stein's "we cannot call" (*wir können nicht … nennen*) turns his remark on usage, "we do not call", into a statement of impossibility. And where §38 says "of a time of the last constituting consciousness there can no longer be any talk", Husserl wrote: "we do not, for very serious reasons, speak of a time of consciousness" (*Wir sprechen aus sehr ernsten Gründen nicht von einer Zeit des Bewußtseins*, Hua X 375). The reasons are §§35–36. The manuscript also says what the primal sensations are positively: we "take the primal sensations as consciousness of a simultaneity … but do not call them simultaneous themselves". §38's "the primal sensations themselves are not simultaneous" keeps the denial and drops the positive half.

The main commentary renders the two terms as "the fluxional pre-simultaneity" and "the impressional simultaneity of fluxions", and adds "Neither may be called simultaneity proper". That gives "simultaneity" to the very word Husserl withholds it from, and makes a refusal a matter of degree (see Corrections in the list). The whole argument of the passage is that *Zugleich* is not *Gleichzeitigkeit*. The two togethers are the material out of which simultaneity and succession are constituted, and the constituting may not be described in the vocabulary of what it constitutes. This is §36's rule, "we name it after what is constituted", applied with a coined word instead of a borrowed one.

§39 builds on the second together. The continuum of retentions attached to a primal sensation is what the double intentionality of retention runs through: each retention is retention of the tone and retention of the earlier retention. The "pre-together" is that continuum seen at one phase. In the part of Nr. 54 that §39 rests on, Husserl had written "momentary-together" for it, and later noted in the margin: "Everywhere I have unfortunately used the expression *momentary-together* precisely for the *stretch-together*. So to be corrected everywhere" (src L6285, to Hua X 381). Stein's "pre-together" in §39 carries out that correction, and confirms the assignment in §38. She did not carry it out everywhere: §39 still has "the momentary-together, which sinks down" (Hua X 82), where the note asks for the stretch-together. The two togethers, momentary and stretched, are the last things Hua X can say of the flow's structure before it turns to how the flow appears to itself.

## §39, Hua X 81–82: longitudinal and transverse intentionality

Reads [§39.2–39.3](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#the-double-intentionality-of-retention-and-the-constitution-of-the-flow-of-consciousness), the second and third paragraphs under § 39.

Every retention intends two things at once. It retains the tone that was just sounding, and it retains the phase of the flow that held that tone a moment ago. Through the first intentionality the tone is constituted as an enduring object in immanent time. Through the second, which runs "lengthwise" along the flow, each phase holds the phase before it, and the flow is constituted as a unity of its own, without a second flow to constitute it. This is Husserl's answer to the regress that opens §39: if I can look back on the flow as a unity, must it not be a constituted temporal series, and need a further consciousness to constitute it? The answer is that one flow constitutes both, by the doubling of retention. The manuscript of 1911 states the same answer in a different word. Where §39 says "retention", Husserl wrote "reproduction".

> In accordance with the last discussions we can give the following answer: it is the one, single flow of consciousness in which the immanent temporal unity of the tone is constituted and at the same time the unity of the flow of consciousness itself. However offensive (if not at first even absurd) it appears that the flow of consciousness constitutes its own unity, it is nevertheless so. And it can be made intelligible from its essential constitution. The regard can be directed, on the one hand, through the phases that *coincide* in the continual progress of the flow, as intentionalities of the tone. But the regard can also go onto the flow, onto a stretch of the flow, onto the transition of the flowing consciousness from the onset of the tone to its end. Every shading of consciousness of the kind *retention* has a double intentionality: one serving the constitution of the immanent object, the tone, which is the one we call *primary memory* of the (just sensed) tone, or more clearly, precisely retention of the tone. The other is constitutive for the unity of this primary memory in the flow; namely, the retention, at one with being still-consciousness, holding back, precisely retention, is retention of the elapsed tone-retention: in its continual shading-off in the flow it is continual retention of the continually preceding phases. […] So there runs through the flow a longitudinal intentionality, which in the course of the flow is in continuous unity of coincidence with itself.
>
> […] Accordingly, we hold, the unity of the flow itself is constituted in the flow of consciousness, as a one-dimensional *quasi*-temporal order, by virtue of the continuity of the retentional modifications and of the fact that they are continually retentions of the continually preceding ones. If I take the direction toward the tone, if I live attentively into the "transverse intentionality" (into the primal sensation as sensation of the respective tone-now, into the retentional modifications as primary memories of the series of elapsed tone-points, and, in the flow of the retentional modifications of the primal sensations and of the retentions already present, into the unity continually experiencing), then the enduring tone stands there, continually extending itself in its duration. If I adjust myself to the "longitudinal intentionality" and to what is constituted in it, I turn the reflecting regard from the tone (which has lasted so and so long) to what is new in the pre-together at one point, the primal sensation, and to what is retained "together" with it in a continuous series.

German:

> Nach den letzten Ausführungen können wir folgende Antwort geben: Es ist der eine, einzige Bewußtseinsfluß, in dem sich die immanente zeitliche Einheit des Tons konstituiert und zugleich die Einheit des Bewußtseinsflusses selbst. So anstößig (wo nicht anfangs sogar widersinnig) es erscheint, daß der Bewußtseinsfluß seine eigene Einheit konstituiert, so ist es doch so. Und es läßt sich aus seiner Wesenskonstitution verständlich machen. Der Blick kann sich einmal durch die im stetigen Fortgang des Flusses sich *deckenden* Phasen als Intentionalitäten vom Ton richten. Der Blick kann aber auch auf auch auf den Fluß, auf eine Strecke des Flusses, auf den Übergang des fließenden Bewußtseins vom Ton-Einsatz zum Ton-Ende gehen. Jede Bewußtseinsabschattung der Art *Retention* hat eine doppelte Intentionalität: einmal die für die Konstitution des immanenten Objekts, des Tones dienende, das ist diejenige, die wir *primäre Erinnerung* an den (soeben empfundenen) Ton nennen, oder deutlicher eben Retention des Tones. Die andere ist die für die Einheit dieser primären Erinnerung im Fluß konstitutive; nämlich die Retention ist in eins damit, daß sie Noch-Bewußtsein, zurückhaltendes, eben Retention ist, Retention der verflossenen Ton-Retention: sie ist in ihrem stetigen Sich-abschatten im Fluß stetige Retention von den stetig vorangegangenen Phasen. […] So geht also durch den Fluß eine Längsintentionalität, die im Lauf des Flusses in stetiger Deckungseinheit mit sich selbst ist.
>
> […] Demnach, meinen wir, konstituiere sich im Flusse des Bewußtseins vermöge der Stetigkeit der retentionalen Abwandlungen und des Umstandes, daß sie stetig Retentionen von den stetig vorangegangenen sind, die Einheit des Flusses selbst als eine eindimensionale *quasi*-zeitliche Ordnung. Nehme ich die Richtung auf den Ton, lebe ich mich aufmerkend in die „Querintentionalität“ ein (in die Urempfindung als Empfindung vom jeweiligen Ton-Jetzt, in die retentionalen Abwandlungen als primäre Erinnerungen der Reihe der abgelaufenen Ton-Punkte und im Fluß der retentionalen Abwandlungen der Urempfindungen und der schon vorhandenen Retentionen die Einheit immerfort erfahrende), so steht der dauernde Ton da, sich in seiner Dauer immerfort erweiternd. Stelle ich mich auf die „Längsintentionalität“ ein und auf das in ihr sich Konstituierende, so werfe ich den reflektierenden Blick vom Ton (der so und so lange gedauert hat) auf das im Vor-Zugleich nach einem Punkt Neue der Urempfindung und das nach einer stetigen Reihe „zugleich“ damit Retinierte.

*Note on the text.* The passage runs from src L1308 to L1361 (Hua X 81–82); the two omissions, marked […], are the step-by-step iteration of retentions (src L1325–L1336) and its comparison with the presentification of a thing-appearance (src L1336–L1346), which the reading discusses but does not need in full. The file is hard-wrapped from L1325 on, with end-of-line hyphenation ("Quer-/intentionalität", L1353–L1354); the quotation joins the lines. The file doubles two words, "auf auch auf den Fluß" (L1316); Nr. 54 has "dem Fluß entlang gehen" [go along the flow] (src L6154). It is quoted as the file has it. The file's italics here (*deckenden*, *Retention*, *primäre Erinnerung*, *quasi*) cannot be checked against the print; the apparatus records no emphasis restored in these lines (Texte L1393).

The solution is a doubling of one word. "Retention of the elapsed tone-retention" (*Retention der verflossenen Ton-Retention*) puts the noun in both places: a retention whose object is a retention. The first intentionality has the tone as object; the second has the earlier retention. The genitive is iterated, "retention of the retention of the primal sensation before that", and the iteration is what makes the flow a unity: each phase holds its predecessor, which held its predecessor, so that the whole elapsed stretch is retained in every new phase. The phrase "longitudinal intentionality" (*Längsintentionalität*) names this direction along the flow, and it is introduced as a result, "so there runs through the flow", with no quotation marks, after the mechanism has been described. "Transverse intentionality" (*Querintentionalität*) enters later and differently, in quotation marks and as a direction of attention: "if I live attentively into the 'transverse intentionality'". Both names are spatial images. The flow is pictured as a band, along which one intentionality runs, and across which, at each phase, the other cuts through retentions of the tone. §36 has already warned that such words are named after what is constituted.

The wording concedes the difficulty while removing it. "However offensive (if not at first even absurd) it appears … it is nevertheless so" (*so ist es doch so*). The concession is strong, and "nevertheless" does not answer it. The answer is the description that follows, and it depends on a single qualification: the unity the flow constitutes of itself is "a one-dimensional *quasi*-temporal order". The Latin adverb carries the weight. If the flow's self-constituted order were temporal, the regress would return, since a temporal order needs a time-constituting consciousness. It is "quasi"-temporal: it has a before and after, a now-phase and a series of earlier ones, but not in time. The weakness of the inference sits here. The order is described with the same structure as the time it is said not to be, and "quasi" marks the difference without saying in what it consists. §39's last paragraph names the result "this pre-phenomenal, pre-immanent temporality" (Hua X 83), and row 23 reads that sentence.

The manuscript is Nr. 54, of late 1911, and it uses another word for the second intentionality and for its iteration. Husserl introduces the kind under the name "retention", in quotation marks ("every shading of consciousness of the kind 'retention'"), and calls the first intentionality "'memory' of the (just sensed) tone". But the second is "reproduction": "the retention, at one with being memory of the tone, is reproduction of the elapsed tone-sensation, more precisely of the primal sensation. And more precisely still: in its continual shading-off in the flow it is continual reproduction of the continually preceding phases" (*stetige Reproduktion von den stetig vorangegangenen Phasen*, Hua X 380). The iteration follows: "reproduction of the reproduction", "the first primal sensation changes into reproduction of it, this reproduction into reproduction of this reproduction" (Hua X 380). The unity of the flow is constituted "by virtue of the continuity of the reproductive modifications". In the 1905 lectures, §19 had set retention and reproduction on different axes: retention is continuous modification, reproduction a discrete one, presentification and not perception. In 1911 Husserl described the flow's self-relation in the vocabulary §19 had reserved for recollection and phantasy. Stein's text replaces every occurrence with "retention", and adds the phrase "or more clearly, precisely retention of the tone". The apparatus, which records only departures from the 1928 edition, says nothing of the change. Whether Husserl asked for it cannot be settled from the corpus. What the comparison shows is that the solution was first stated in words that, read against §19, would have made the flow's self-awareness a presentification.

The manuscript is also more tentative. Stein's "In accordance with the last discussions we can give the following answer" is, in Nr. 54, "For this difficulty I have attempted the following solution" (*habe ich folgende Lösung versucht*), and the one flow is qualified in a parenthesis the print dropped: "the one, single flow of consciousness (possibly within a 'last' consciousness)" (*evtl. innerhalb eines „letzten“ Bewußtseins*, Hua X 378–379). Husserl left open in 1911 that the flow might itself stand within a further consciousness. That is the regress the passage claims to stop, admitted as a possibility. Stein's text closes it. The print also adds a clause to the iteration that Nr. 54 lacks: a retention of a retention intends the retained at the second level "and finally the primal datum, which here is objectivated throughout" (Hua X 81). And the term "pre-together" in the last sentence renders what Nr. 54 calls "momentary-together", a term Husserl later said in the margin he had used "unfortunately" for the "stretch-together", "so to be corrected everywhere" (src L6285); the print follows his correction.

The passage is the solution on which the rest of Section Three depends. §40 can call the flow "primal consciousness" because §39 has shown how it is conscious of itself without being an object in time. §§42–44 take the "inner perception" of acts to be this self-awareness, not a second act. Row 23 reads the paragraph that states the result in its strongest form: "the self-appearance of the flow does not require a second flow". Against the manuscript, the reading here adds three qualifications: that the self-retention of the flow was first conceived as reproduction, that the end of the regress was first proposed as an attempt, and that a "last" consciousness was first left open. The print's answer is cleaner than the one Husserl wrote.

## §39, Hua X 83: the flow appears to itself, "and yet"

Reads [§39.5](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#the-double-intentionality-of-retention-and-the-constitution-of-the-flow-of-consciousness), the fifth paragraph under § 39.

The flow does not need a second flow in order to appear. It appears to itself, in itself, because each of its phases retains the earlier phases: the longitudinal intentionality of row 22 is the flow's self-appearance. But what appears in a phase is never that phase. The actual phase makes past phases appear; it does not appear to itself as actual. So constituting and constituted coincide, in that the flow constitutes the flow, and they do not coincide, in that the constituting phase is never the constituted one. The passage puts both in one sentence joined by "and yet". The manuscript went on from there to the question the print cuts: whether a "last" consciousness must after all be assumed above the flow.

> Accordingly, in the one, single flow of consciousness two intentionalities, inseparably unified and requiring one another like two sides of one and the same thing, are interwoven with each other. By virtue of the one, immanent time is constituted, an objective time, a genuine one, in which there is duration and change of what endures; in the other, the quasi-temporal arrangement of the phases of the flow, which always and necessarily has the flowing "now"-point, the phase of actuality, and the series of pre-actual and post-actual (not yet actual) phases. This pre-phenomenal, pre-immanent temporality is constituted intentionally as form of the time-constituting consciousness and in it itself. The flow of the immanent time-constituting consciousness not only is, but is of so remarkable and yet intelligible a kind that in it there must necessarily be a self-appearance of the flow, and hence the flow itself must necessarily be graspable in flowing. The self-appearance of the flow does not require a second flow; rather, as phenomenon it constitutes itself in itself. The constituting and the constituted coincide, and yet they cannot, of course, coincide in every respect. The phases of the flow of consciousness [in] which phases of the same flow of consciousness are phenomenally constituted cannot be identical with these constituted phases, and are not so either. What is brought to appearance in the momentary-actual of the flow of consciousness are, in the series of its retentional moments, past phases of the flow of consciousness.

German:

> Demnach sind in dem einen, einzigen Bewußtseinsfluß zwei untrennbar einheitliche, wie zwei Seiten einer und derselben Sache einander fordernde Intentionalitäten miteinander verflochten. Vermöge der einen konstituiert sich die immanente Zeit, eine objektive Zeit, eine echte, in der es Dauer und Veränderung von Dauerndem gibt; in der anderen die quasi-zeitliche Einordnung der Phasen des Flusses, der immer und notwendig den fließenden ,,Jetzt''-Punkt, die Phase der Aktualität hat und die Serien der voraktuellen und nachaktuellen (der noch nicht aktuellen) Phasen. Diese präphänomenale, präimmanente Zeitlichkeit konstituiert sich intentional als Form des zeitkonstituierenden Bewußtseins und in ihm selbst. Der Fluß des immanenten zeitkonstituierenden Bewußtseins ist nicht nur, sondern so merkwürdig und doch verständlich geartet ist er, daß in ihm notwendig eine Selbsterscheinung des Flusses bestehen und daher der Fluß selbst notwendig im Fließen erfaßbar sein muß. Die Selbsterscheinung des Flusses fordert nicht einen zweiten Fluß, sondern als Phänomen konstituiert er sich in sich selbst. Das Konstituierende und das Konstituierte decken sich, und doch können sie sich natürlich nicht in jeder Hinsicht decken. Die Phasen des Bewußtseinsflusses, denen Phasen desselben Bewußtseinsflusses sich phänomenal konstituieren, können nicht mit diesen konstituierten Phasen identisch sein, und sind es auch nicht. Was im Momentan-Aktuellen des Bewußtseinsflusses zur Erscheinung gebracht wird, das sind in der Reihe der retentionalen Momente desselben vergangene Phasen des Bewußtseinsflusses.

*Note on the text.* The file is hard-wrapped with end-of-line hyphenation (src L1390–L1415); the quotation joins the lines. "Denen Phasen" (src L1411) lacks a preposition; Nr. 54 reads "in denen Phasen" (src L6203), and the English supplies "[in]". A note to "in itself" refers to Beilage VIII (src L1420). §39 ends here, and with it the stretch of Nr. 54 that the redaction used ("up to p. 382, line 7", src L6284).

The sentence on self-appearance argues by necessity and then by denial. The flow "not only is" but is "of so remarkable and yet intelligible a kind" that a self-appearance "must necessarily" exist in it, and so it "must necessarily be graspable in flowing". The doubled "necessarily" claims an essential law; the ground offered is the description of row 22, the retention of retentions. Then the denial: "does not require a second flow; rather, as phenomenon it constitutes itself in itself". The pronoun "itself" (*sich selbst*) is reflexive twice over: the flow constitutes itself, and does so in itself. In Nr. 54 the reflexive is stressed, "in *sich* selbst" (Hua X 382). The point of the stress is that no other locus is needed.

The "and yet" (*und doch*) then restricts the identity just claimed. "The constituting and the constituted coincide, and yet they cannot, of course, coincide in every respect." "Coincide" (*sich decken*) is the word used throughout Section Two for the identity-synthesis of phases, the coincidence in which the same tone-point is held through its modifications. Here it names the relation of the flow to itself. "Of course" (*natürlich*) presents the restriction as obvious, and the next sentence gives its content: the phases in which phases of the flow are constituted "cannot be identical with these constituted phases, and are not so either". The final sentence says which phases: what the "momentary-actual" brings to appearance are "past phases", in the series of its retentional moments. The constituting phase is always the actual one, the constituted are always earlier ones. The flow coincides with itself as a whole and fails to coincide with itself at every phase. The passage does not say how the actual phase is itself conscious. It says only that it is not conscious as constituted.

The preceding sentences frame this as the result of the two intentionalities. One constitutes "immanent time, an objective time, a genuine one"; the other, "the quasi-temporal arrangement of the phases of the flow". The order the second gives is called "this pre-phenomenal, pre-immanent temporality". The prefixes keep it below the level of what appears and of what is immanent, the second level of §34. The redaction then contradicts that placement in the next sentence: "the flow of the immanent time-constituting consciousness". Nr. 54 has "the flow of the consciousness constituting immanent time" (*Der Fluß des immanente Zeit konstituierenden Bewußtseins*, Hua X 382). In the manuscript "immanent" qualifies the time the flow constitutes; in the print it qualifies the consciousness itself. At §36 Husserl had struck "immanent" before "flow" in the same manuscript (371,10, Texte L2197). The print gives the flow back the adjective he took away, a sentence after calling its temporality "pre-immanent". Two smaller changes go the same way as in row 22. Nr. 54's last sentence has "in the series of the reproductive moments" where §39 has "retentional", and "are so, naturally, not" where §39 has "are not so either".

What the print omits is the next paragraph of Nr. 54. It begins: "But the question now arises whether we must not say that over all consciousness in the flow there still holds sway the last consciousness" (*es walte über allen Bewußtsein im Fluß noch das letzte Bewußtsein*). The actual phase of inner consciousness would then be conscious through the last consciousness, and that last consciousness would be what passes into "the reproductive (retentional) modification". Husserl weighs whether this last intentionality could be attended to, and ends: "it must be seriously considered whether one must assume such a last consciousness, which would be a necessarily 'unconscious' consciousness" (*ein notwendig „unbewußtes“ Bewußtsein*), since, if attending always presupposes an intentionality already there, it could never be attended to (Hua X 382). The question arises from the passage's last sentence. If the actual phase makes past phases appear but does not appear to itself, then either it is not conscious at all, or something else makes it conscious. The print's "and yet" holds the two halves together, and stops. The manuscript states the alternative it leaves, and does not decide it. This is the same "last" consciousness that Nr. 54's parenthesis "(possibly within a 'last' consciousness)" kept open when the solution was first proposed (row 22). Bernet's introduction to the Meiner edition reads the page as Husserl's "desperate attempt" to return the phenomena to a metaphysical path: he "dreams" of a last consciousness and, on waking, has to call it "unconscious" (Texte L541). The manuscript's own wording is more neutral than that. It asks a question, marks the answer as needing serious consideration, and names the price, an unconscious consciousness. Bernet is right that the page leaves the flow's present unaccounted for. Whether Husserl was trying to escape that or only stating it, the text does not settle.

Section Three takes the cut version as its basis. §40 calls the flow "primal consciousness" and says that it is not itself in turn content or object (row 24). Beilage VIII, to which the note on "in itself" refers, restates the double intentionality, and Beilage IX, "Primal consciousness and possibility of reflection", answers the manuscript's question directly. The actual phase is conscious "in the peculiar form of the 'now', without being objectual", and "precisely this primal consciousness is what passes into the retentional modification"; "retention of an unconscious content is impossible" (*Retention eines unbewußten Inhalts ist unmöglich*, Hua X 118–119). That is the print's answer to the question the manuscript asked here. The actual phase is conscious, but in a mode that is not an appearance: primal consciousness, given only to retention and reflection after it has passed. The "and yet" of §39 is where that answer is needed, and the manuscript shows that Husserl first met the need with a question.
