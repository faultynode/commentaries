# Husserl, *Vorlesungen zur Phänomenologie des inneren Zeitbewußtseins*: close readings of key passages

These readings take up the passages approved in the [key-passage list](../runs/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary-key-passages.md). The [main commentary](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md) works through Part One of the lectures at equal depth. Each reading here follows one understanding that a passage carries: what Husserl means by a term or a claim, how his wording builds it, what the passage does between its neighbours, and where the understanding goes in the rest of the lectures and in the manuscripts printed with them.

The text is the German of Husserliana X, edited by Rudolf Boehm (1966): Edith Stein's redaction of the lectures, first published by Heidegger in 1928, followed by the supplements and by Part B, the supplementary texts that print Husserl's manuscripts of 1893–1917. Both parts are quoted from one converted file in `faultynode/sources` (`sources/husserl/husserl-hua-10-vorlesungen-zur-phanomenologie-des-inneren-zeitbewusstseins.md`). Passages are cited by § and by Hua X page; Part B texts by their number ("Nr. 54") and Hua X page. In Part One the file's bracketed numbers are the pages of the 1928 first edition; in Part B they are the Hua X pages, and the plain number beside them is the page of Bernet's Meiner edition. The main commentary cites § numbers only, and unit references such as "§6.7" name the seventh paragraph of the commentary under § 6. Hua X's editorial apparatus is quoted from the split-off file `husserl-texte-zur-phanomenologie-des-inneren-zeitbewusstseins.md` (Bernet's Meiner edition), and John Barnett Brough's English translation (*On the Phenomenology of the Consciousness of Internal Time*, Collected Works IV) from `husserl-cw4-on-the-phenomenlogy-of-the-consciousness-of-internal-time.md`; Brough is named wherever he is cited. All other English renderings are my working translations, kept close to the German so that its structure shows.

The file has three limits that bear on more than one reading. Its page numbers come from running heads, which sometimes sit at the foot of the page they close, so a page can be one out. It has lost Husserl's emphasis entirely: Hua X sets emphasis in spaced type, the file has none, and the apparatus records emphases restored from the manuscripts that the file does not show. No reading argues from emphasis or its absence. And §31 is missing about a page (Hua X 67–68) after the sentence on the word "now"; the reading of that passage stops where the file stops. Other damage is noted where it occurs, and none of it is repaired.

## Introduction, Hua X 4: temporal objectivity as individual objectivity

Reads [Introduction, second paragraph](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#introduction).

The lectures announce a theory of individuation under the name of a theory of time. To show how "temporal objectivity" is constituted in consciousness is, for Husserl, to show how any individual object is constituted, because what makes something this one thing, and not merely a thing of this kind, is its place in time. The Introduction does not argue for this. It states it in an apposition, and the rest of the lectures have to earn it.

> Of course, we all know what time is; it is the most familiar thing of all. But as soon as we try to give an account of time-consciousness, to set objective time and subjective time-consciousness in their right relation, and to bring ourselves to understand how temporal objectivity, individual objectivity in general, can be constituted in subjective time-consciousness; indeed, as soon as we even try to subject purely subjective time-consciousness, the phenomenological content of the experiences of time, to an analysis, we become entangled in the strangest difficulties, contradictions and confusions.

German:

> Natürlich, was Zeit ist, wissen wir alle; sie ist das Allerbekannteste. Sobald wir aber den Versuch machen, uns über das Zeitbewußtsein Rechenschaft zu geben, objektive Zeit und subjektives Zeitbewußtsein in das rechte Verhältnis zu setzen und uns zum Verständnis zu bringen, wie sich zeitliche Objektivität, individuelle Objektivität überhaupt, im subjektiven Zeitbewußtsein konstituieren kann, ja sowie wir auch nur den Versuch machen, das rein subjektive Zeitbewußtsein, den phänomenologischen Gehalt der Zeiterlebnisse einer Analyse zu unterziehen, verwickeln wir uns in die sonderbarsten Schwierigkeiten, Widersprüche, Verworrenheiten.

The equation sits between two commas, with no connective. "Temporal objectivity, individual objectivity in general" (*zeitliche Objektivität, individuelle Objektivität überhaupt*) sets the second phrase beside the first as another name for it. "In general" (*überhaupt*) widens the scope from temporal objects in the narrow sense, tones and melodies, to everything individual. Brough renders the clause "how temporal objectivity—and therefore any individual objectivity whatever—can become constituted" (CW4 L4329–4331). His "and therefore" supplies an inference the German does not draw. The German identifies, and leaves the ground of the identity unstated. That ground, that individuality is fixed by temporal position, is the thesis §31 has to prove, and Brough's connective makes it look already proved.

The sentence's syntax also ranks the tasks. Its first "as soon as" (*sobald*) governs three infinitives in a row: to give an account of time-consciousness, to relate objective to subjective time, and to understand how objectivity "can be constituted" (*konstituieren kann*). The modal verb matters. The question is not whether objects are constituted but how that is possible, which makes it a question about conditions and not about causes. Then the sentence restarts with a stronger phrase, "indeed, as soon as we even" (*ja sowie wir auch nur*). The confusion begins before the question of objectivity is reached, in "purely subjective time-consciousness" itself. The order of difficulty is the reverse of the order of the programme. Husserl names objectivity first as the goal and then admits that the ground beneath it, the analysis of time-experiences, is where the contradictions lie.

The paragraph sits between Augustine and Brentano. Augustine's confession that he knows what time is until asked supplies the opening "of course, we all know" (*natürlich … wissen wir alle*), which turns the Latin into a German commonplace. Brentano, named in the next sentence, supplies the theory that Section One takes apart. §1 then does something the Introduction's first task seems to forbid: it excludes objective time, the very term the second infinitive set in relation to consciousness. The exclusion does not drop the programme. It moves "objectivity" inside consciousness. When the problem returns at §31, the tone has its fixed place in "'objective' (be it the immanent) time" (*„objektiven“ (sei es auch die immanente) Zeit*, Hua X 64). The quotation marks and the parenthesis record what §1 did to the Introduction's word.

§31 is where the apposition is redeemed, and it is redeemed in the same structure. "The question of the constitution of the objectivity of individual temporal objects and processes" is "most closely connected" with the question of identical temporal positions: "all objectification takes place in time-consciousness; without clarification of the identity of temporal position, no clarification of the identity of an object in time can be given" (*im Zeitbewußtsein vollzieht sich alle Objektivierung; ohne Aufklärung der Identität der Zeitstelle ist auch keine Aufklärung der Identität eines Objektes in der Zeit zu geben*, Hua X 63). The Introduction's comma has become a necessary condition, and a few pages later a definition: the tone-point "in its absolute individuality" is held fast "by matter and temporal position, the latter of which first constitutes individuality" (*nach Materie und Zeitstelle, welch letztere erst Individualität konstituiert*, Hua X 66–67). Matter gives the kind; temporal position gives the individual. Section Three extends the claim downward. At §40 the immanent contents themselves "endure, they have their time, they are individual objectivities" (*sie sind individuelle Objektivitäten*, Hua X 84). By then the "individual objectivity in general" of the Introduction includes the sensations and acts that the Introduction still called "purely subjective".

Part B shows that in 1905 the equation was also a question. The convolute that holds Nr. 35, which Husserl labelled "Seefeld manuscripts and older ones on individuation" and dated to the summer holidays of 1905 (Hua X 237, note), ends by asking whether "temporality and spatiality, provided they are understood phenomenologically and not empirically-transcendently, are really full principles of individuation" (*Sind Zeitlichkeit und Räumlichkeit … wirklich volle Prinzipien der Individuation?*, Nr. 35, Hua X 253). There space stands beside time, and the "really" doubts what the lectures of the same year assert. The Introduction keeps time alone and drops the question. It stakes the lectures on a claim that the working notes held open, and §31's antinomy, "time is rigid, and yet time flows", is where that claim is tested.

## §1, Hua X 7 and note: "sensed" as a relational concept

Reads [§1.6](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#the-exclusion-of-objective-time), sixth paragraph under § 1.

"Sensed" names a function before it names a kind of content. In the 1905 text a sensed temporal datum is the stuff an apprehension animates, as sensed red is the stuff of a perceived red. The note added later keeps the definition by function and draws the consequence the 1905 text did not: if "sensed" says only that something presents, it no longer says what the presenting thing is, whether a sensuous content or something already constituted in a quite different way. And then the note drops the schema of content and apprehension altogether. The passage is the first place in the lectures where the doctrine they start from is withdrawn, and it is withdrawn in a footnote.

> If we call "sensed" a phenomenological datum that, through apprehension, makes us conscious of something objective as bodily given, which is then called objectively perceived, then in the same sense we must distinguish a "sensed" temporal item and a perceived temporal item. The latter means objective time. The former, however, is not itself objective time (or a position in objective time), but the phenomenological datum through whose empirical apperception the relation to objective time is constituted. Temporal data, or, if you will, temporal signs, are not *tempora* themselves.
>
> [Note:] "Sensed" would then be the indication of a relational concept, which in itself would say nothing about whether what is sensed is sensuous, indeed whether it is immanent at all in the sense of the sensuous; in other words, it would remain open whether what is sensed is itself already constituted, and perhaps quite otherwise than the sensuous. — But this whole distinction is best left aside; not every constitution has the schema content of apprehension — apprehension.

German:

> Nennen wir empfunden ein phänomenologisches Datum, das durch Auffassung als leibhaft gegeben ein Objektives bewußt macht, das dann objektiv wahrgenommen heißt, so haben wir in gleichem Sinne auch ein „empfundenes“ Zeitliches und ein wahrgenommenes Zeitliches zu unterscheiden. Das letztere meint die objektive Zeit. Das erstere aber ist nicht selbst objektive Zeit (oder Stelle in der objektiven Zeit), sondern das phänomenologische Datum, durch dessen empirische Apperzeption die Beziehung auf objektive Zeit sich konstituiert. Temporaldaten, wenn man will: Temporalzeichen, sind nicht *tempora* selbst.
>
> „Empfunden“ wäre dann also Anzeige eines Relationsbegriffes, der in sich nichts darüber besagen würde, ob das Empfundene sensuell, ja ob es überhaupt immanent ist im Sinne von Sensuellem, m.a.W. es bliebe offen, ob das Empfundene selbst schon konstituiert ist, und vielleicht ganz anders als das Sensuelle. — Aber dieser ganze Unterschied bleibt am besten beiseite; nicht jede Konstitution hat das Schema Auffassungsinhalt — Auffassung.

The main text defines "sensed" by a conditional: "if we call 'sensed'" (*nennen wir empfunden*) whatever makes an object conscious through apprehension. The definition is functional from the start. A datum is sensed because of what an apprehension does with it, not because of anything in the datum. The note takes the conditional at its word. Its "then" (*dann also*) answers the main text's "if": given that definition, "sensed" is "the indication of a relational concept" (*Anzeige eines Relationsbegriffes*), and a relation says nothing about the nature of its first term. The note's grammar keeps that consequence hypothetical. "Would be", "would say", "would remain open" (*wäre*, *würde*, *bliebe*) all stand in the subjunctive. The last sentence switches to the indicative and to a different kind of claim: "this whole distinction is best left aside" (*bleibt am besten beiseite*), because "not every constitution" has the schema. The hypothesis about one word turns into a judgement on the whole framework, and the dash between them carries the step. The note does not argue that the schema fails. It names the case it cannot cover, a sensed content that is "itself already constituted", and leaves the reader to see that time-consciousness is that case.

The main text's own word for temporal data shows how far the 1905 doctrine went. "Temporal signs" (*Temporalzeichen*) is coined on the model of Lotze's "local signs", which the paragraph before introduced with an ironic symmetry: "if one speaks there of local signs, one would have to speak here of quality signs". "If you will" (*wenn man will*) takes the coinage under protest. §6 says what is wrong with it: to call the data temporal signs is to have "at first only a new word" (*zunächst nur ein neues Wort*, Hua X 18), with nothing yet said about how a present sign yields a consciousness of what is no longer now. The Latin *tempora* gives the times the dignity of a technical term, and the sentence denies that the sensed data are such times. On the 1905 view the denial needs no further support, because the data are contents and time comes from the apprehension. The note's question, whether such data are "already constituted", removes that support. If the temporal "signs" are themselves temporal unities, the apprehension that was supposed to make them temporal comes too late.

The apparatus confirms that the hedge is a later stratum and shows that it is larger than the note. The note is not in the 1905 manuscript ("7,35–40 Fußnote fehlt im Ms.", Texte L885), and the quotation marks around "sensed" in the note are the editor's. But the conditional the note hangs on is also absent. For the lines that begin "If we call 'sensed'…", the manuscript reads only "In the same sense we must now also distinguish a sensed [temporal item]" (*Im gleichen Sinne haben wir nun auch zu unterscheiden ein empfundenes usw.*, 7,8–10). The quotation marks around "sensed" in "the 'sensed' temporal data", two sentences later, are not in the manuscript either (7,17). So the 1905 lecture asserted sensed temporal data outright. The later redaction made the term conditional in the text, put it in quotation marks, and withdrew the frame in the note. The commentary's "a note added later" is right but too small. The revision runs through the paragraph, and the note is its most explicit part.

The paragraph sits inside the exclusion of objective time. Before it, the parallel with space established that the visual field is "quasi-spatial" and that sensed red is called red "only equivocally". After it, sensed simultaneity is "not objective simultaneity" (*empfundenes „Zugleich“ nicht objektive Gleichzeitigkeit*), and the chalk example puts change in the phenomenon and duration in the object. The 1905 argument needs the sensed–perceived distinction to keep the two orders apart: objectivity lies "not in the 'primary' contents, but in the apprehension-characters" (Hua X 8). The note strikes at exactly this sentence. Once primary contents can be "already constituted", the line between the order of experience and the order of objects can no longer be drawn by assigning contents to one side and apprehensions to the other.

Part B shows where the doubt came from. The note on "representation" (Nr. 48, Hua X 319) calls the view that sensuous contents are simply "there" and then "apprehended in this way or that" "my original view, my theory of representation", and judges that "such an interpretation is likely to be quite untenable" (*eine solche Interpretation dürfte ganz unhaltbar sein*). The next text, which Bernet dates no earlier than September 1909 (Nr. 49, Hua X 321), uses the note's own formula and suspends it in the same way: what it says holds "whether or not we interpret the intuition of time according to the schema 'content of apprehension — apprehension'" (*ob wir die Zeitanschauung nach dem Schema „Auffassungsinhalt — Auffassung“ interpretieren oder nicht*). What constitutes the difference in time, it adds, is an "apprehension" "in a fundamentally different sense". The §1 note compresses this to a clause, and Section Three works it out. §43 keeps the word "apprehension" for the phases of the flow, as "primal apprehensions" (*Urauffassungen*, Hua X 92) borne by primal contents that are no longer constituted. The schema the note set aside returns at the bottom of the lectures, emptied of its content–apprehension structure. The note's last clause is therefore not a dismissal but a programme: the lectures go on to show which constitution lacks the schema, and what "apprehension" means there instead.

## §3, Hua X 12: duration of sensation and sensation of duration

Reads [§3.3](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#the-original-associations), third paragraph under § 3.

The maxim separates two things that every theory of time-consciousness is tempted to merge: the time an experience takes, and the time it is conscious of. That a sensation lasts is a fact about the sensation. That something is sensed as lasting is a fact about what the sensation presents. The first does not yield the second. The maxim is negative. It shows a gap, and it leaves open what fills it. Brentano fills it with a moment added by phantasy; Husserl, in the course of the lectures, fills it with the form of consciousness itself. The passage matters because both answers start from it, and because Husserl uses it against the answer it was first used to support.

> So phantasy proves itself here to be productive in a peculiar way. This is the only case in which it truly creates a new moment of representations, namely the temporal moment. So we have discovered the origin of temporal representations in the domain of phantasy. […] Many think that the question of the origin of the concept of time is to be answered no differently from that of the origin of our concepts of colours, tones and so on. Just as we sense a colour, we also sense the duration of the colour; like quality and intensity, temporal duration too is an immanent moment of sensation. […] But that is a palpable error. That the stimulus lasts does not yet mean that the sensation is sensed as lasting, but only that the sensation lasts too. Duration of sensation and sensation of duration are two different things. And it is the same with succession. Succession of sensations and sensation of succession are not the same thing.

German:

> So erweist sich die Phantasie hier in eigentümlicher Weise als produktiv. Es liegt hier der einzige Fall vor, wo sie in Wahrheit neues Moment der Vorstellungen schafft, nämlich das Zeitmoment. So haben wir auf dem Gebiet der Phantasie den Ursprung der Zeitvorstellungen entdeckt. […] Viele meinen, die Frage nach dem Ursprung des Zeitbegriffs sei nicht anders zu beantworten als die nach dem Ursprung unserer Begriffe von Farben, Tönen usw. So wie wir eine Farbe empfinden, so empfinden wir auch die Dauer der Farbe; wie Qualität und Intensität, so sei auch zeitliche Dauer ein immanentes Moment der Empfindung. […] Aber das ist ein handgreiflicher Irrtum. Damit, daß der Reiz dauert, ist noch nicht gesagt, daß die Empfindung als dauernd empfunden wird, sondern nur, daß auch die Empfindung dauert. Dauer der Empfindung und Empfindung der Dauer ist zweierlei. Und ebenso ist es bei der Sukzession. Sukzession von Empfindungen und Empfindung der Sukzession ist nicht dasselbe.

The maxim works by inverting a genitive. In "duration of sensation" (*Dauer der Empfindung*) the sensation is what lasts; in "sensation of duration" (*Empfindung der Dauer*) duration is what is sensed. The same two nouns change places, and with them the sensation passes from bearer of a property to consciousness of an object. The singular verb, "is two things" (*ist zweierlei*), sets the pair up as one formula whose halves come apart. The sentence before shows how the error arises. "Lasts" and "sensed as lasting" share a verb, and the error lets the verb slide from the sensation's existence to its content: "the sensation lasts too" (*auch die Empfindung dauert*) is all that the stimulus guarantees. The theory under attack, which puts duration beside quality and intensity as "an immanent moment of sensation", is in the subjunctive (*sei*) as reported opinion. The rebuttal is in the indicative, and the maxim states it as a distinction to be seen, not proved.

The maxim is Brentano's, in Husserl's report, and it is the premise of Brentano's theory. Because lasting does not yield the sense of lasting, something must be added, and phantasy adds it: it "truly creates a new moment", the temporal moment, "the only case" in which it is productive. The paragraph that follows the passage gives the reason for the second half of the maxim. If earlier tones simply persisted while new ones sounded, "we would have a simultaneous sum of tones, but no succession of tones in our representation" (*eine gleichzeitige Summe von Tönen, aber keine Sukzession*, Hua X 12–13). Persistence produces togetherness, not succession. The extension to acts in the next paragraph, "exactly the same objection" against those who derive the representation of duration from the duration of psychic acts, plants the problem Section Three will face: acts also last, and their lasting does not make itself conscious.

§6 turns the second half against Brentano's own answer. Original association keeps the content A in consciousness and adds a moment "past". But then A "is not past at all but has remained present"; the graded moments of original association "are all now there … and are therefore simultaneous. And yet the succession of time excludes the together" (*sind also gleichzeitig. Und doch schließt das Nacheinander der Zeit das Zugleich aus*, Hua X 18). That is the "simultaneous sum of tones" again. Brentano's added moments are present contents, and a succession of present contents, however graded, is not a consciousness of succession. The maxim that required an addition rules out an addition of contents. Its conclusion, "the form of time is neither itself temporal content nor a complex of new contents" (Hua X 19), is the maxim restated as a thesis about form.

The maxim is also older than Brentano, and the 1905 manuscript said so. Where the printed §7 introduces Herbart and Lotze, the manuscript (Texte L1000, on Hua X 19,30f.) states the maxim again in its own words, "enduring representations are not yet representations of something enduring, successive representations not yet a representation of succession" (*Dauernde Vorstellungen sind noch nicht Vorstellungen von Dauernd, sukzessive Vorstellungen noch nicht Vorstellung von Sukzession*), and quotes Lotze, *Metaphysik* §154: if b followed a there would be "a change of representations, but not yet a representation of the change". A bracket inside the quotation corrects Lotze's "semblance" (*Schein*): "better: appearance!" (*besser: Erscheinung!*). The redaction cut all of this. The printed text leaves the maxim with Brentano and gives Herbart and Lotze only the opposite doctrine, that a succession must be grasped in one indivisible act. In the manuscript the maxim and that doctrine come from the same authors. The maxim does not decide between the momentary act and the enduring one; Herbart and Lotze drew from it the first conclusion, Husserl the second.

Husserl then gives the pair a positive relation. At §7 it is "evident" that "perception of duration itself presupposes duration of perception" (*daß Wahrnehmung der Dauer selbst Dauer der Wahrnehmung voraussetzt*, Hua X 22). The genitives are inverted again, but the relation has changed from "two different things" to "presupposes". Brentano separated the halves; Husserl keeps them separate and binds them. Consciousness of duration does not come from the duration of consciousness, yet it cannot occur without it. §18 applies the maxim to memory, in notation: a succession of memories, A′ and "afterwards" B′, would give "a 'perception' of the succession of these memories, and no memory-consciousness of it" (Hua X 43). It has to be written (A–B)′. Section Three draws the last consequence. If perceiving a duration presupposes that the perceiving lasts, the question becomes how the lasting of the perceiving is itself conscious without a further consciousness that lasts. §§36–39 answer that the constituting flow is not in time in the sense of its objects, so that "we cannot say: was" of it, and that it appears to itself without a second flow. The maxim began as a refutation of sensualism. By §39 it has become the reason the lectures need a consciousness whose own duration is no longer a duration of sensations at all.

## §6, Hua X 16: the phenomenological core of original association

Reads [§6.2](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#critique), second paragraph under § 6.

Husserl takes from Brentano a datum, not a law. The law of original association says how new psychic experiences arise from given ones, and it belongs to psychology. Its "phenomenological core" is what any such law presupposes: a single consciousness that holds what is now and what is just past together, so that both appear in one. Husserl reduces the dispute with Brentano to a question about that datum: in what manner does the past appear in it? The rest of Section Two is the answer, and the way the question is posed already fixes what kind of answer will count.

> Brentano speaks of a law of original association, according to which representations of a momentary memory attach themselves to the perceptions of each moment. What is meant by this is evidently a psychological law of the new formation of psychic experiences on the basis of given psychic experiences. These experiences are psychic, they are objectified, they have their own time, and their becoming and being produced is what is spoken of. Such things belong to the domain of psychology and do not interest us here. But there is a phenomenological core in these reflections, and the following discussion will hold to that alone. Duration, succession, changes appear. What lies in this appearing? In a succession, for example, a "now" appears, and in unity with it a "past". The unity of the consciousness that intentionally spans what is present and what is past is a phenomenological datum. The question now is whether, as Brentano asserts, the past really appears in this consciousness in the manner of phantasy.

German:

> Brentano spricht von einem Gesetz ursprünglicher Assoziation, wonach sich an jeweilige Wahrnehmungen Vorstellungen eines momentanen Gedächtnisses anschließen. Gemeint ist damit offenbar ein psychologisches Gesetz der Neubildung von psychischen Erlebnissen auf Grund gegebener psychischer Erlebnisse. Diese Erlebnisse sind psychische, sie sind objektiviert, sie haben selbst ihre Zeit, und von ihrem Werden und Hervorgebrachtwerden ist die Rede. Dergleichen gehört ins Gebiet der Psychologie und interessiert uns hier nicht. Jedoch steckt ein phänomenologischer Kern in diesen Betrachtungen, und an den allein wollen sich die folgenden Ausführungen halten. Dauer, Sukzession, Veränderungen erscheinen. Was liegt in diesem Erscheinen? In einer Sukzession z.B. erscheint ein ,,Jetzt'', und in Einheit damit ein ,,Vergangen''. Die Einheit des Gegenwärtiges und Vergangenes intentional umspannenden Bewußtseins ist ein phänomenologisches Datum. Es ist nun die Frage, ob wirklich, wie Brentano es behauptet, das Vergangene in diesem Bewußtsein in der Weise der Phantasie erscheint.

The reason Husserl gives for setting the law aside is a list of three predicates: the experiences it concerns "are psychic, they are objectified, they have their own time". The third is the decisive one. An experience that has its own time is an event in the world, with a before and after measurable like any other, and a law about how such events give rise to one another explains nothing about how time appears. "Becoming and being produced" (*Werden und Hervorgebrachtwerden*) names the causal register being excluded. The exclusion has a cost the lectures will meet later. Section Three has to ask what time the time-constituting consciousness has, and it cannot answer that the flow "has its own time" in this sense without making it psychic again. The production vocabulary returns too: at §11 the primal impression is the "source-point" of the "production" of the enduring object, with both words in quotation marks.

The positive half of the passage moves from law to appearing in four short sentences. "Duration, succession, changes appear" states a fact without a theory. "What lies in this appearing?" turns it into a question of description. The answer uses the two time-words as nouns in quotation marks: a "now" (*„Jetzt“*) and a "past" (*„Vergangen“*) appear "in unity". The quotation marks make the words names of characters with which something appears, not dates. The datum is then stated as a single noun phrase built around a participle: "the unity of the consciousness that intentionally spans what is present and what is past" (*Die Einheit des Gegenwärtiges und Vergangenes intentional umspannenden Bewußtseins*). The adverb "intentionally" carries the whole correction of Brentano. The past is not present in consciousness as an extra content; it is reached by an intention that spans it. "Spans" (*umspannen*) recurs where the datum recurs. At §18 the consciousness of succession has "a temporal stretch that spans the one-after-another" (Hua X 43), and in Nr. 39 the past phases of the sounding tone are "intentionally spanned" (*intentional umspannt*, Hua X 282) together with the present point.

The final sentence keeps Brentano's terms in order to refute them. It asks whether the past appears "in the manner of phantasy" (*in der Weise der Phantasie*). A note at once widens the word: "phantasy" here covers "all presentifying acts" (Hua X 17, note). So the question is not whether the just-past is imagined but whether it is re-presented. Put so, it has a structural answer. The next paragraph shows what follows if it is: the recollection of yesterday's succession becomes "phantasies of phantasies", and the difference between the just-past and the long past disappears. §19 settles it in the vocabulary the question used: "our position on Brentano's doctrine that the origin of the apprehension of time lies in the domain of phantasy is now finally decided". Phantasy is presentification, and presentified time "necessarily refers back to originally given time, not phantasized but presented" (*nicht phantasierte, sondern präsentierte*, Hua X 45). Husserl even turns Brentano's word "origin" against him: no representation can "spring" (*„entspringen“*) from presentification, and "even the concept of phantasy does not spring from phantasy".

The commentary is right that the rest of the lectures answer this question. The close reading shows that the question already contains the answer's form. By naming the datum as a unity that "intentionally spans" two modes of appearing, Husserl has ruled out any content-based solution, Brentano's included, before the critique begins. What §§12, 17 and 19 add is the name and character of the spanning intention: retention, an "original consciousness" (§12) that is "presentative, not representative" (§17).

## §6, Hua X 19 (with §5, Hua X 15): non-real, *reell*, and where the past is not

Reads [§6.7](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#critique), seventh paragraph under § 6.

The passage turns on the difference between two German words that English renders alike. *Real* means existent, actual, belonging to reality. *Reell* (really inherent) means belonging to the experience as one of its components, as against what the experience intends. Brentano called the past "irreal": a past red is no red, as an imagined thaler is no thaler. Husserl answers that Brentano's mechanism makes the past a present and *reell* component of consciousness, and that no added moment can make such a component irreal. The reply is sound against the mechanism. But it is phrased in a word Brentano did not use, and Husserl's own theory will end up saying that the past is not *reell* in consciousness either. The close reading follows the word.

> Equally dubious is the attempt to present the past as something non-*reell*, non-existent. An added psychic moment surely cannot make irreality, cannot remove present existence. In fact the whole domain of the original associations is a present and *reell* experience. To this domain belongs the whole series of original temporal moments produced by original association, together with the other moments that belong to the temporal object.

German:

> Sehr bedenklich ist auch der Versuch, das Vergangene als ein Nichtreelles, Nichtexistierendes hinzustellen. Ein hinzutretendes psychisches Moment kann doch nicht Irrealität machen, nicht gegenwärtige Existenz fortschaffen. In der Tat ist der ganze Bereich der ursprünglichen Assoziationen ein gegenwärtiges und reelles Erlebnis. Zu diesem Bereich gehört die ganze Reihe der durch ursprüngliche Assoziation erzeugten originären Zeitmomente mitsamt den übrigen Momenten, die dem zeitlichen Gegenstand zugehören.

Brentano's words in §5 are *real* and *irreal*: "The modifying temporal predicates are, according to Brentano, irreal; only the determination of the now is real" (*Die modifizierenden Zeitprädikate sind nach Brentano irreale, real ist nur die Bestimmung des Jetzt*, Hua X 15). That is a claim about predicates. "Past" alters what it attaches to, as "imagined" does. Husserl's paraphrase changes the noun and the adjective. The predicate becomes "the past" as a thing, and "irreal" becomes "non-*reell*, non-existent" (*Nichtreelles, Nichtexistierendes*). The two negatives are set side by side as if they said the same. The next sentence uses Brentano's own word, "irreality" (*Irrealität*), and the third turns to Husserl's: the domain of original association is "a present and *reell* experience" (*ein gegenwärtiges und reelles Erlebnis*). The rebuttal works by putting both senses on one side of the ledger. What original association produces is present and *reell*, so it cannot be irreal. Against the mechanism this holds. Moments produced now and present now are now, whatever they are called. Against Brentano's thesis about predicates, that a past red is not a red, it says nothing.

The apparatus shows that the slide was in the manuscript and that the redaction smoothed it. The 1905 text of the paragraph, which the first edition moved to this place (19,1–9, Texte L1000), began differently: "Brentano does say, to be sure: past is an altering predicate, the past as such is something not *reell*, not existent. By this he surely does not mean that the past is only something present furnished with a new moment, and so real. That would be a contradiction" (*Brentano sagt allerdings: Vergangen sei ein alterierendes Prädikat, das Vergangene als solches sei ein nicht Reelles, nicht Existierendes … also Reales*). In 1905 Husserl put "not *reell*" into Brentano's mouth as the gloss on "altering predicate", and two lines later used "real" (*Reales*) for the present content. The two words were not yet held apart. The printed text drops the attribution and the "real", so that only "*reell*" is left on Husserl's side. The commentary's gloss, that *reell* "in this context means really inherent in the experience, not existent in the world", is the right sense for the printed sentence. It is not the sense the 1905 lecture consistently had.

The neighbours show why the argument needs the word anyway. The paragraph before ends the reductio: the moments of original association, if they are times, "are all now there … and are therefore simultaneous". The paragraph after draws Section One's result: temporal form is neither temporal content nor a complex of contents added to it. *Reell* is the word that makes the result general. Whatever is *reell* in consciousness is present, and so any theory that explains the past by a *reell* component, a phantasm, an after-image, a mark, will have explained a present. The past must be found elsewhere than among the experience's *reell* parts.

§12 locates it there and takes back Brentano's thesis with it. The retained tone "is not *reell* present in the retentional consciousness" (*er ist im retentionalen Bewußtsein nicht reell vorhanden*, Hua X 31). What is *reell* is the retention: "The retentional consciousness contains *reell* a consciousness of the past of the tone" (*enthält reell Vergangenheitsbewußtsein vom Ton*, Hua X 32). The analogy Husserl reaches for is Brentano's. "Just as a phantasy-tone is no tone but a phantasy of a tone … so a primarily remembered tone is in principle something other than a perceived one." That is §5's "a past tone c is no tone c", with the alteration now located in the mode of consciousness instead of in a predicate of the content. So Husserl does not refute Brentano's thesis that the past is not *reell*. He refutes the claim that original association could secure it, and then secures it himself by moving the *reell* from what is intended to the intending. The past is non-*reell*, as Brentano said, because the consciousness of it is *reell* and present.

The word carries into Section Three, where the question becomes what is *reell* in the retention itself. When the flow is said to be "not in time" (§36), and primal consciousness not "content" (§40), the *reell*–intentional distinction is pushed one level down. At §6 Husserl needed the word to stop the past being a component. By the end he needs a consciousness whose phases are *reell* and present and still intend what is no longer present. That is the double intentionality of §39.

## §6, Hua X 19–20: the form of time is not a content

Reads [§6.8](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#critique), eighth paragraph under § 6.

Section One ends with a negative definition. The form of time, the pastness, duration and succession with which something appears, is neither a temporal content nor a set of new contents attached to the old ones. The formula excludes every explanation that adds something to consciousness, and in 1905 it points to the one alternative Husserl then had: the act-characters, the ways in which consciousness apprehends its contents. The negative half survives the whole work. The positive pointer does not, and the word "form" changes its address on the way.

> We see, then, that an analysis of time-consciousness is useless that tries to make the intuitive stretch of time intelligible merely through continuously graded new moments that are somehow patched onto, or fused into, those content-moments that constitute the temporally localized objective item. In short: the form of time is neither itself temporal content, nor is it a complex of new contents somehow attaching themselves to the temporal content. Now even if Brentano did not fall into the error of reducing, in the manner of sensualism, all experiences to mere primary contents, even if he was indeed the first to recognize the radical separation into primary contents and act-characters, his theory of time shows that he nonetheless failed to take into account precisely the act-characters that are decisive for it. The question how time-consciousness is possible and to be understood remains unsolved.

German:

> Wir sehen also, daß eine Analyse des Zeitbewußtseins unbrauchbar ist, welche die intuitive Zeitstrecke bloß verständlich machen will durch kontinuierlich abgestufte neue Momente, die sich irgendwie denjenigen Inhaltsmomenten anstücken oder einschmelzen, die das zeitlich lokalisierte Gegenständliche konstituieren. Kurz gesagt: Die Zeitform ist weder selbst Zeitinhalt, noch ist sie ein Komplex neuer, an den Zeitinhalt sich irgendwie anschließender Inhalte. Wenn nun Brentano auch nicht in den Irrtum verfallen ist, in der Weise des Sensualismus alle Erlebnisse auf bloße primäre Inhalte zu reduzieren, wenn er sogar als der Erste die radikale Scheidung in primäre Inhalte und Aktcharaktere erkannt hat, so zeigt seine Zeittheorie, daß er doch gerade auf die für sie entscheidenden Aktcharaktere nicht Rücksicht genommen hat. Die Frage, wie Zeitbewußtsein möglich und zu verstehen ist, bleibt ungelöst.

The formula is built from a pair, form and content, and from two negations that close two exits. "Neither itself temporal content" (*weder selbst Zeitinhalt*) rules out that pastness is a moment beside quality and intensity, the view §3 called "a palpable error". "Nor a complex of new contents" rules out Brentano's view, that pastness is a series of phantasms added to the content. The verbs of the first sentence describe the two ways a content could be added: "patched onto" (*anstücken*) and "fused into" (*einschmelzen*). The apparatus shows that the manuscript had "attach" (*anschließen*) where the print has "patch onto" (19,13, Texte L1000). The second sentence keeps the old verb: "contents somehow attaching themselves" (*sich irgendwie anschließender Inhalte*). That is the verb of Brentano's law as §6 stated it, by which representations of momentary memory "attach themselves" (*sich … anschließen*) to each perception (Hua X 16). The formula thus names Brentano's law in its own wording and denies that what it describes could be the form of time. "Somehow" (*irgendwie*), used twice, keeps open how the adding is pictured and closes the question: however it is pictured, it adds contents.

What the formula does not say is what the form is. "Form of time" (*Zeitform*) occurs here for the first time in the lectures, and it is defined only by what it is not. The positive pointer comes in the next sentence, by way of praise and blame. Brentano was "the first" to separate primary contents from act-characters, and he failed to take into account "precisely the act-characters that are decisive" for time. The concessive syntax, "even if … even if … nonetheless" (*wenn … auch … wenn … sogar … so … doch*), makes the failure sharper by setting it against his own discovery. In 1905 the form of time therefore belongs to the act: pastness is a way of apprehending, not a thing apprehended. §§11–19 develop this in the language of apprehension and act-character. The section ends on an open question, "how time-consciousness is possible", in the modal language of the Introduction.

The negative result governs every later step. §12 applies it to after-images: a fading tone is a weak present tone, and "the intuition of the past itself cannot be picturing" (Hua X 31). §18 uses "form of time" of a succession, "a temporal form, a temporal stretch that spans the one-after-another" (Hua X 43), and keeps it on the side of what is given. But the positive pointer to act-characters is what the later strata abandon. The §1 note sets aside the schema of apprehension-content and apprehension (Hua X 7, note). Section Three replaces the act with the flow, and §37 says that what the *Logical Investigations* called an "act" is itself "a flow in which an immanent temporal unity is constituted" (Hua X 76). If acts are constituted unities in time, the form of time cannot be explained by them either. The 1905 formula excluded contents. The later strata extend the exclusion to acts.

A manuscript of 1907 to 1909 in Part B draws the consequence for the word "form" itself (Nr. 44, Hua X 296–297, a text Bernet assigns to his third group, 1907–1909). "Time is the form of all individual objectivity." But "time is not the form of the experiences, of the 'contents', that make up consciousness itself"; what belongs to absolute phenomenality is a "becoming" (*Werden*), which "is the foundation for all apprehension of time, but not that apprehension itself". Then: "The form of time is not a phansiological form in the final sense, not a form of absolute being, but only a form of 'appearances', that is, only a form of individual objects. We must say: it is not an absolute but only a categorial form" (*Sie ist keine absolute, sondern nur eine kategoriale Form*). The form that §6 refused to identify with a content is here refused to the constituting consciousness as well. It belongs to what is constituted, and the constituting has only a becoming. The word "becoming" is the one §6.2 set aside with the psychological law ("their becoming and being produced", Hua X 16). It returns at the bottom of the analysis, no longer a causal process among psychic events, and §36 will say that we lack names for it.

So the formula of §6 holds to the end, and its scope widens. In 1905 it says the form of time is not a content and points to acts. By Nr. 44 and Section Three, the form of time is not a content, not an act, and not a form of the absolute flow at all. It is the form of the objects the flow constitutes, the "individual objectivity in general" of the Introduction.

## §7, Hua X 22–23: the temporal object in the special sense

Reads [§7.2](../commentaries/husserl/husserliana-10-inneres-zeitbewusstsein-commentary.md#interpretation-of-the-grasping-of-temporal-objects-as-momentary-grasping-and-as-enduring-act), second paragraph under § 7.

A temporal object "in the special sense" is an object that has its duration inside it: not a thing that happens to last, but a tone-in-its-duration, a melody, a change, a persisting taken as persisting. Such an object cannot be given in a point, and the passage draws two consequences from that. The perceiving of it must itself last. And no division of the object into parts saves the momentary view, because every part, down to the single tone, is again a temporal object. Section Two begins from this object. The passage defines it and then shows that the problem it poses reappears at every scale.

> It is indeed evident that the perception of a temporal object itself has temporality, that perception of duration itself presupposes duration of perception, that the perception of any temporal shape itself has its temporal shape. And if we abstract from all transcendences, perception retains, in all its phenomenological constituents, its phenomenological temporality, which belongs to its essence and cannot be removed. Since objective temporality is in each case constituted phenomenologically, and stands there appearingly for us as objectivity or as a moment of an objectivity only through this constitution, a phenomenological analysis of time cannot clarify the constitution of time without regard to the constitution of temporal objects.
>
> By temporal objects in the special sense we understand objects that are not only unities in time but also contain temporal extension in themselves. When a tone sounds, my objectivating apprehension can make the tone, which lasts there and dies away, into its object, and yet not the duration of the tone or the tone in its duration. The latter as such is a temporal object. […] Each tone itself has a temporal extension: when it is struck I hear it as now, but as it sounds on it has an ever new now, and each preceding now changes into a past. So at any time I hear only the actual phase of the tone, and the objectivity of the whole enduring tone is constituted in an act-continuum that is in part memory, in a smallest, punctual part perception, and in a further part expectation.

German:

> Es ist ja evident, daß die Wahrnehmung eines zeitlichen Objektes selbst Zeitlichkeit hat, daß Wahrnehmung der Dauer selbst Dauer der Wahrnehmung voraussetzt, daß die Wahrnehmung einer beliebigen Zeitgestalt selbst ihre Zeitgestalt hat. Und sehen wir von allen Transzendenzen ab, so verbleibt der Wahrnehmung nach allen ihren phänomenologischen Konstituenten ihre phänomenologische Zeitlichkeit, die zu ihrem unaufhebbaren Wesen gehört. Da sich objektive Zeitlichkeit jeweils phänomenologisch konstituiert und nur durch diese Konstitution für uns als Objektivität oder Moment einer Objektivität erscheinungsmäßig dasteht, so kann eine phänomenologische Zeitanalyse die Konstitution der Zeit nicht ohne Rücksicht auf die Konstitution der Zeitobjekte aufklären.
>
> Unter Zeitobjekten im speziellen Sinn verstehen wir Objekte, die nicht nur Einheiten in der Zeit sind, sondern die Zeitextension auch in sich enthalten. Wenn ein Ton erklingt, so kann meine objektivierende Auffassung sich den Ton, welcher da dauert und verklingt, zum Gegenstand machen, und doch nicht die Dauer des Tones oder den Ton in seiner Dauer. Dieser als solcher ist ein Zeitobjekt. […] Jeder Ton hat selbst eine zeitliche Extension, beim Anschlagen höre ich ihn als jetzt, beim Forttönen hat er aber ein immer neues Jetzt, und das jeweilig vorangehende wandelt sich in ein Vergangen. Also höre ich jeweils nur die aktuelle Phase des Tones, und die Objektivität des ganzen dauernden Tones konstituiert sich in einem Aktkontinuum, das zu einem Teil Erinnerung, zu einem kleinsten, punktuellen Teil Wahrnehmung und zu einem weiteren Teil Erwartung ist.

*Note on the text.* In the file the first sentence of the passage does not begin a line. The paragraph starts in the middle of src L248, after the text of a long footnote quoting Stern, which breaks off mid-word ("eines einheitlichen Be-") and resumes at L252 ("wußtseinsaktes sind"). The main text runs on from L248 to L256 ("einer Objektivität erscheinungsmäßig dasteht"). The quotation above gives the main text only, in its own order. No wording is changed.

The definition rests on a contrast of prepositions: objects that are "not only unities in time" (*Einheiten in der Zeit*) but "contain temporal extension in themselves" (*die Zeitextension auch in sich enthalten*). Any object is in time. A temporal object in the special sense has time in it, as a property of what it is. The tone example makes the distinction a matter of where attention goes, not of which objects exist. The same sounding tone can be my object without its duration, and then it is a unity in time; or "the tone in its duration" can be my object, and "this as such" (*dieser als solcher*) is the temporal object. The definition picks out an aspect under which anything enduring can be taken. That matters for what follows. The problem Section Two studies is not confined to music. It is the problem of any duration taken as duration.

The first paragraph argues from this object to the perceiving. Its three clauses repeat one structure: the perception "of" a temporal item "itself" has the corresponding temporal character. The middle one is the inversion of §3's maxim: "perception of duration itself presupposes duration of perception". Brentano's pair, which separated the two, is here bound by "presupposes" (*voraussetzt*). And "evident" carries the claim. The apparatus shows that the evidence was once argued for. From this sentence on, the printed text rests on two sheets that the editor judges were written somewhat before the lectures of February 1905, and there the sentence ran: "It is evident, for this obviously belongs to the essence of the situation" (*Es ist evident, denn das gehört offenbar zum Wesen der Sachlage*, 22,21, Texte L1085). The manuscript sheet begins mid-sentence, with a colour that "stands there as enduringly the same colour", and adds that "the same holds of the apprehensions and of the moment of attention". So in the sheet the presupposition was the last step of a list that already put apprehensions and attention in time. The redaction cut the list and the "for", and the sentences before it, from "What blocks clarification" to "cannot be solved without the others" (22,1–21), are not in the manuscript at all. The editor suggests Edith Stein wrote them.

The second paragraph runs the melody argument and turns it against itself. "Hearing is perceiving", yet when the second tone sounds the first is no longer heard. The natural reply, that memory supplies the past part and expectation the rest, is set out in the voice of a hearer "inclined to say" so. Then comes the decisive "but": "all that has been said transfers to the single tone" (*alles Gesagte überträgt sich auch auf den einzelnen Ton*). The melody's problem is not that it has several tones. It is that each tone, too, is a temporal object, so that the split into memory, perception and expectation recurs inside every tone, and perception shrinks to "a smallest, punctual part" (*einem kleinsten, punktuellen Teil*). The superlative shows where the regress ends: at a point, which cannot be heard. The printed text then adds "That seems to go back to Brentano's doctrine. Here a deeper analysis must begin". The apparatus records that these sentences are not in the manuscript (23,33–35). In the sheet, the act-continuum of memory, perception and expectation was Husserl's own account, and it went on to distinguish "a productive and a reproductive phantasy", the first "producing the temporal spread" (Texte L1085–L1086). Only then did the sheet say that this view "is not workable". The redaction turned an early stage of Husserl's own thinking into a doctrine of Brentano's.

The passage sets up Section Two by fixing its object and its difficulty. §8 begins by excluding transcendence and taking "the tone purely as hyletic datum" (Hua X 24–25): the immanent temporal object, the case where the perceiving and the perceived are both in the same immanent time. The temporal object in the special sense now has two levels, transcendent and immanent, and the second is chosen because there the question of the first paragraph cannot be avoided. If perception of duration presupposes duration of perception, then the immanent tone and its perceiving both last, and the question is how the lasting of the perceiving is itself conscious. §§34–39 take that question up when they distinguish the immanent temporal object from the flow that constitutes it. At §45 the definition is used to mark a limit: there are "non-temporal transcendencies", such as the mathematical state of affairs a judgement is about, which "is nothing temporal" (*ist kein Zeitliches*, Hua X 96). The phrase "in the special sense" was the first sign of that limit. Temporal extension belongs to some objects and not to others, and the theory of time-consciousness is the theory of those that have it.
