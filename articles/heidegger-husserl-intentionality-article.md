# Intentionality Objectivated

## Heidegger's Terminological Inheritance, 1921–1927

*A note on citation.* The corpus underlying this article is a working
archive of source texts and one topic index compiled against them.[^1]
Because I have no access to the printed editions behind those files, I
cite by the stable handles the texts themselves supply — Husserliana
section numbers, the section numbers of *Sein und Zeit* and of the
Marburg lecture courses — and, where a volume in the archive carries no
sections at the relevant place, by the archive's own line pointers, in
the form `GA 61:1160`. These pointers are not pagination and will drift;
they are given so that every claim below can be checked against the same
text I checked it against. Where the archive holds a translation rather
than an original, I say so at the point of use, because in an argument
about word-formation the difference is not a pedantry.

---

The charge is old enough to have become furniture. Heidegger, it is
said, took intentionality from Husserl and then buried the theft under a
tarpaulin of invented German: *Erschlossenheit*, *Entdecktheit*,
*In-der-Welt-sein*, *Zuhandenheit*, and behind them a Greek word,
ἀλήθεια, doing the work of a receipt from an older and less litigious
creditor. On this account the coinages are prophylactic. They put
distance between the doctrine and its source; they make the debt
unspeakable by making it unnameable; and they allow a philosophy that is
structurally Husserlian to present itself as a retrieval of the
pre-Socratics.

Stated as a claim about what Heidegger meant to do, the charge is
untestable and not very interesting. Nobody has access to the intention,
and the biographical record — which in any case lies outside the corpus
I am working from — could at best establish resentment, not concealment.
But the charge can be given a form that is testable, and the form is
this: it is a claim about the *distribution of avowal across textual
strata*. If the concealment story is right, then the places where
Heidegger acknowledges the inheritance should be few, thin, and
peripheral, and they should grow thinner as the doctrine matures; the
running exposition should be able to proceed without them; and the
coinages should function as replacements for Husserlian terms — that is,
they should be back-translatable into those terms without residue, since
a term coined to conceal a borrowing must denote what the borrowed term
denoted.

Tested that way, the charge fails, but it fails in an instructive
direction, and the instruction is not the one Heidegger's defenders
usually draw. It is not that Heidegger was scrupulous about his debts and
his readers careless. It is that the concealment story has correctly
identified a displacement and misidentified its object. What the
terminology conceals is not intentionality — which is avowed constantly,
emphatically, and in Heidegger's own hand — but the *order of
discovery*. The coinages that carry Heidegger's doctrine of truth were
minted in the confrontation with Husserl, and they were minted before
the Greek vocabulary arrived to receive them. By the time the doctrine is
presented as a retrieval of ἀλήθεια, in the Marburg *Sophist* course of
1924/25, the substantive position has already been in place for a year
in a lecture course where ἀλήθεια does not occur and where the Greek
ἀληθές is explicitly demoted to a failed attempt. The transfer of credit
runs not from Husserl to a neologism but from Husserl to antiquity; and
antiquity, unlike the man across the hall in Freiburg, does not read its
reviews.

That is my thesis, and I want to be clear at the outset about the sense
in which the archive supports it and the sense in which it does not. The
topic index I am working from was compiled on *truth*, not on
intentionality, and this is a real limitation rather than a formality.
Intentionality enters that index only where it happens to cross the theme
of truth; there is no systematic inventory of the passages where either
author treats directedness as such. What the index does supply, and what
makes the argument possible at all, is the place where intentionality is
load-bearing for both men. For Husserl, truth in the strict sense *is* an
intentional achievement — a synthesis of coincidence between an empty
intention and a fulfilling intuition of the same object. For Heidegger,
truth is the being-uncovering of a being that is already alongside what
it uncovers. Whatever else the two doctrines of truth are, they are two
answers to the question of what it is for something to be given *as what
it is meant to be*; and that is the question intentionality was invented
to hold open. A truth index is therefore not the wrong place to look. But
it is a place where the looking is oblique, and several steps below are
marked as needing an inventory that does not yet exist.

## "Intentionality objectivated"

Begin where the coining begins. In the Freiburg course of winter
1921–22, Heidegger introduces the vocabulary that will carry him for the
next decade — comportment, sense of relation, sense of actualization,
facticity, ruinance — and he introduces it by way of a philological
observation about Greek verbs. Certain Greek verbs formed on the stem of
their object are intransitive: one does not do wisdom, one σοφέω, acts
wisely. "The homonymous intransitive," he writes, "expresses an
essentially different basic mode of existentiell actualization: σοφέω [I
act wisely] has no transitive sense."[^2] A short table follows, in which
ἀληθεύω is glossed "I am truthful, sincere," and set beside σοφιστεύω,
"I act and teach like a sophist; I conceal…"[^3]

The philosophical use is immediate. What the intransitive expresses, he
says, is that "the object to which … is such that it determines, from its
own character, the comportment toward it. The object gives the comportment
a name."[^4] This is a formula that ought to be startling to anyone who
thinks the young Heidegger is escaping from Husserl. It says that the
comportment is individuated by what it is a comportment toward — which is
the thesis that acts are individuated by their objects, stated as a claim
about morphology rather than about consciousness. And Heidegger then makes
the identification himself. Distinguishing two senses of *comportment* —
"(1) To comport oneself, to behave" and "(2) To comport oneself to … , to
stand in relation to … , to have a relation" — he glosses the second in
four words: "The second is relation and indeed separated relation,
intentionality objectivated."[^5]

The gloss is worth dwelling on, because everything turns on the last
word. Heidegger is not saying that comportment-to is intentionality and
therefore good; he is saying it is intentionality *objectivated*, and in
his idiom objectivation is an accusation. The ranking that follows the
distinction confirms it: "The second meaning is more original as regards
the genesis of sense; the first is the surplus of sense over and against
possession."[^6] Relation-to is genetically prior; but what the
intransitive brings out, and what the philosophical use requires, is
"the independence of the comportment," so that "what is decisive in this
case is not the comportment to … but the comportment as such, the
being-in-this-comportment."[^7] The Greek intransitive is thus not a
device for deleting the object. It is a device for reaching the
directedness *before* it has hardened into a relation between two
standing terms.

Read as the concealment story would have us read it, this passage is
damning: at the founding moment of his own vocabulary, Heidegger demotes
the relation to the object and puts a Greek grammatical curiosity in its
place. Read with the second half of the sentence, it says something else
— that what is being demoted is not intentionality but a construal of
intentionality as a relation between separated relata, and that the new
vocabulary is being built to hold the phenomenon without that construal.
Which reading is right cannot be settled from this passage alone. It can
be settled from the marginalia.

For the archive also contains, bracketed off as a marginal note, the most
explicit statement of the debt anywhere in these four volumes:

> Prestruction and relucence as expressions of intentionality: formal,
> primal structure of facticity (of the ontological sense of life). What
> has always disturbed me: did intentionality come down from heaven? If it
> is something ultimate: in which ultimacy is it to be taken? … That I
> must live and "exist" intentionally — "explain"! That is, grasp the
> problematic as such! In regard to all categorial structures of
> facticity, intentionality is their basic formal structure.
>
> Basic sense of phenomenology. Carrying out a de-struction within the
> history of the spirit: how and to what extent intentionality has been
> seen; i.e., in what direction and with what means re-interpreted; what
> has hindered its appropriation. To what extent and how it was graspable
> by Husserl in relation to consciousness. Which motives operative, which
> sense of facticity (as existentiell).[^8]

Two of the strangest coinages in the course — prestruction and relucence,
the terms for how factical life builds its future in advance and how what
it cares about shines back onto it — are here declared to be *expressions
of intentionality*. And intentionality is declared to be the basic formal
structure of *all* the categorial structures of facticity, which is to
say of the entire apparatus the course exists to erect. The archive's
working notes file this passage under Heidegger's polemic against Husserl
and quote from it only the sentence about the *Destruktion*.[^9] That is
an under-reading with consequences, and I will come back to it. What the
passage establishes is that the coinages are not offered as replacements
for intentionality. They are offered as its articulation. The *Destruktion*
announced in the second paragraph is not a destruction of intentionality
but of the history in which it has been "re-interpreted" — and the target
within that history is named with precision: not Husserl, but Husserl "in
relation to consciousness."

The same course also supplies the term that has made the concealment
story so tempting, and supplies it as a charge against Husserl's own
school. In a late set of notes on the address and reception of the work,
Heidegger writes: "What is proper to man (intentionality) is renounced,"
and then, in the next paragraph: "All the talk about intentionality can
conceal the lack of direction toward the Object. But even where, instead
of talking, there is actual work based on intuition, it is still not more
certain that a correct direction is present."[^10] So *conceal* is
Heidegger's own predicate; and its subject is not his coinages but the
word "intentionality" itself. His complaint is that the term has become a
way of possessing directedness without exercising it — that one can say
"intentional" indefinitely and still be facing nowhere. Whatever one
thinks of the fairness of that, it establishes the motive under which the
renaming is undertaken. Heidegger coins not to hide a directedness he has
borrowed but because he judges the borrowed word to have become a hiding
place.

And the injunction that follows is the opposite of suppression. "It is at
hand: Husserl, *Logical Investigations*, *Ideas*. These books are often
quoted and emulated, but they are not understood. Otherwise, people would
stop quoting and would genuinely imitate them — by carrying on the actual
research opened up therein."[^11] A man concealing a debt does not tell
his readers to go and read the creditor.

## The paratextual ledger

So far the concealment story looks simply false. But notice where the
avowals are sitting. The declaration that prestruction and relucence are
expressions of intentionality is a bracketed marginal note. The complaint
that intentionality-talk conceals is in a sheaf of appended reflections on
how the text should be read. The identification of comportment-to as
"intentionality objectivated" is a four-word apposition inside a numbered
list, unremarked before or after. Not one of these is in the running
doctrinal exposition. The exposition proceeds, uninterrupted, in the new
words.

This pattern is not confined to 1921–22. It is the constant of the corpus,
and it holds most sharply at the most systematic moment. When, in 1927,
Heidegger rebuilds the concept of truth in §44 of *Sein und Zeit*, the body
of the text runs on Aristotle's *De interpretatione*, on Aquinas's
*Quaestiones disputatae de veritate*, on Kant, on Heraclitus — and the
acknowledgment of Husserl is a footnote:

> Zur Idee der Ausweisung als »Identifizierung« vgl. *Husserl*, Log.
> Unters. 2. A. Bd. II, 2. Teil, VI. Untersuchung. Über »Evidenz und
> Wahrheit« ebd. § 36–39, S. 115 ff. Die üblichen Darstellungen der
> *phänomenologischen* Wahrheitstheorie beschränken sich auf das, was in
> den *kritischen* Prolegomena (Bd. 1) gesagt ist und vermerken den
> Zusammenhang mit der Satzlehre *Bolzanos*. Die *positiven*
> phänomenologischen Interpretationen dagegen, die von *Bolzanos* Theorie
> grundverschieden sind, läßt man auf sich beruhen.[^12]
>
> [On the idea of exhibition as "identification" cf. *Husserl*, Logical
> Investigations, 2nd ed., vol. II, pt. 2, Sixth Investigation. On
> "Evidence and Truth" ibid. §§ 36–39, pp. 115 ff. The usual presentations
> of the *phenomenological* theory of truth confine themselves to what is
> said in the *critical* Prolegomena (vol. 1) and note the connection with
> *Bolzano's* doctrine of the proposition. The *positive* phenomenological
> interpretations, by contrast, which are fundamentally different from
> *Bolzano's* theory, are left to one side.]

The working notes call this "the single most important line in this file"
for the Husserl comparison, and they are right, but they quote only its
first clause and thereby miss what the note is doing.[^13] It is not a
bare acknowledgment. It is a reproach, and the reproach is that the
profession has been reading Husserl's theory of truth in the wrong volume
— taking the anti-psychologistic *Prolegomena* for the doctrine and
ignoring the positive analyses of the Sixth Investigation. Heidegger
appends a further sentence naming Emil Lask as the only reader outside
phenomenology to have taken those analyses up positively.[^14] At the exact
point where he introduces *Wahrsein als Entdeckend-sein*, that is,
Heidegger stops to complain that not enough people have read the Husserlian
text he is standing on.

The pattern, then, is not concealment but *stratification*: every avowal
that the new vocabulary is a vocabulary for intentionality sits in
paratext — marginalia, appendices, footnotes, appositional glosses —
while the body text proceeds in coinages presented as retrievals. And
this stratification, I want to argue, is not a habit of temperament but a
consequence of method, one the corpus states plainly.

The coinages are what Heidegger calls formal indications, and the whole
point of a formal indication is that it cannot be introduced by
definition. "The evidence with regard to the appropriateness of the
definition, i.e., its appropriateness to the object, is not authentic and
original; rather, this appropriateness is absolutely questionable, and the
definition must be understood precisely within this questionableness and
lack of evidence."[^15] A formally indicative term is confirmed not by
adequation but by repetition: "Its evidence matures precisely in its
genuine and ever more rigorous repeatability."[^16] It follows that a
formal indication cannot be launched in the running text by the sentence
"*Erschlossenheit* is what Husserl called intentionality." Such a sentence
would convert the indication back into a definition, would give the reader
a settled meaning in advance of the enactment, and would thereby destroy
precisely the questionableness within which the term is supposed to be
grasped. Heidegger's own reflection on terminological hygiene, a few lines
above the sentence on repeatability, is blunt about the stakes: "In
philosophy we have too facile an idea of what it actually means to abolish
a term."[^17]

The paratext is where the equations go that the body text is
methodologically forbidden to make. It is the ledger. And a ledger is
exactly what a reader trained to weight body over apparatus will not
read.

That is the honest form of the concealment intuition, and it should be
conceded rather than argued away. The method does make the debt
structurally deniable. A vocabulary that forbids itself to state its own
genealogy in the text where it operates is a vocabulary whose genealogy
will, in due course, become invisible; and the invisibility is not an
accident of reception but a predictable consequence of formal indication.
Heidegger is not hiding the debt. He is using a method that guarantees the
debt will be hard to see, and he does not appear to have minded.

## What Husserl was already doing to his own terms

There is a further reason to resist the picture of a Husserlian doctrine
smuggled out under an assumed name, and it is the strongest evidence in
the corpus, though it has to be reached by following Heidegger's own
footnote to its destination.

The footnote sends us to the Sixth Investigation, §§36–39. What is there?
Husserl arrives, by way of the analysis of fulfilment, at the point where
"a presentative intention has achieved its last fulfilment," where "the
genuine *adaequatio rei et intellectus* has been brought about" and "the
object is actually 'present' or 'given', and present as just what we have
intended it."[^18] In the epistemologically pregnant sense, self-evidence
is "the act of this most perfect synthesis of fulfilment," in which "the
object is not merely meant, but in the strictest sense *given*, and given
as it is meant, and made one with our meaning-reference."[^19] Then, in
§39, he does something that ought to have been more consequential for this
whole dispute than it has been. He observes that the phenomenological
situation just described will support not one concept of truth but four,
and he lays them out: truth as the objective correlate of the identifying
act, that is, the state of affairs, the full agreement of meant and given;
truth as the ideal relation among the epistemic essences of the coinciding
acts, the Idea of absolute adequation as such; truth as the object given
in the manner of the object meant, fulness itself; and truth as the
rightness of the intention, its adequacy to its true object.[^20]

And then he redistributes the words. "It seems therefore most suitable
that the concepts of truth and being should be so distinguished," he
writes, "that our concepts of truth … are applied *from the side of the
acts themselves* and their ideally graspable moments, whereas the concepts
of being (genuine being) are applied to the corresponding *objective
correlates*." Truth is to be reserved for senses (2) and (4), "as the Idea
of adequation, or as the rightness of objectifying assertion and meaning";
being for senses (1) and (3), "as the identity of the object at once meant
and given in adequation."[^21] Already at the head of the section he had
flagged the arbitrariness of the assignment: the objective correlate of
the identifying act, he says, is "called *being in the sense of truth*, or
simply *truth* — if one does not prefer to award this term to another
concept of the many that are rooted in the said phenomenological
situation."[^22]

This is not an incidental tidying. It is a deliberate act of terminological
reassignment, performed on a single phenomenological finding, and Husserl
performs it with full consciousness that the finding does not dictate the
names. The word *truth* is awarded; the word *being* is pinned down; a
sense that could have been called truth is called being instead. The
practice that the concealment story treats as Heidegger's dodge is thus
already, and explicitly, Husserl's own procedure — in the very section
Heidegger's footnote directs us to.

Once that is seen, the relation between the two doctrines of truth looks
quite different. Consider what Husserl's assignment leaves standing. He
has said that the object-side of the truth-situation should be called
*being in the sense of truth*. He has thereby put the word "being" into
the middle of the analysis of the intentional achievement — and left it
undischarged. What being *is*, such that the correlate of a fulfilled
intention can be called by that name, the Sixth Investigation does not
ask. Nor does the mature transcendental statement of the same doctrine.
When Husserl returns to it in *Ideen I*, the correlation is stated in a
form of exemplary clarity — "in the logical sphere, that of the assertion,
'being-truly' or 'being-actual' and 'being-rationally-exhibitable' stand
in correlation," and this "as an ideal, an essential possibility," not an
empirical one[^23] — and then, at the culmination of the phenomenology of
reason:

> Prinzipiell entspricht (im Apriori der unbedingten Wesensallgemeinheit)
> jedem "wahrhaft seienden" Gegenstand die Idee eines möglichen
> Bewußtseins, in welchem der Gegenstand selbst originär und dabei
> vollkommen adäquat erfaßbar ist. Umgekehrt, wenn diese Möglichkeit
> gewährleistet ist, ist *eo ipso* der Gegenstand wahrhaft seiend.[^24]
>
> [In principle (in the a priori of unconditioned essential universality)
> there corresponds to every "truly being" object the idea of a possible
> consciousness in which the object itself is graspable originarily and, in
> that grasping, completely adequately. Conversely, if this possibility is
> guaranteed, then *eo ipso* the object truly is.]

Set beside it the sentence in which Heidegger states his own position at
the corresponding point of §44:

> Das Wahrsein als Entdeckend-sein ist wiederum ontologisch nur möglich
> auf dem Grunde des In-der-Welt-seins.[^25]
>
> [Being-true as being-uncovering is in turn ontologically possible only
> on the ground of being-in-the-world.]

These are the same sentence with one term replaced. Both assert a
correlation between a being's showing itself as what it is and a condition
on the side of that for which it shows itself. Both make the correlation
a priori — Husserl's "im Apriori der unbedingten Wesensallgemeinheit,"
Heidegger's "ontologisch nur möglich auf dem Grunde." The difference is
the identity of the correlate: for Husserl, *die Idee eines möglichen
Bewußtseins*; for Heidegger, *In-der-Welt-sein*. The renaming operates on
the relatum, not on the relation. The correlational a priori — the form
"to every X there corresponds a possible Y in which X shows itself as it
is" — is taken over intact, and what is contested is what may be put in
for Y.

And the contest is exactly the one Heidegger's marginal note had announced
in 1921–22: the *Destruktion* concerns how intentionality "was graspable by
Husserl *in relation to consciousness*." Husserl, having said that the
object-correlate of the fulfilled intention should be called *being*, never
asks after the being of the term on the other side; he takes "possible
consciousness" as available. Heidegger's objection is not that the
correlation is wrong. It is that Husserl performed half of a terminological
operation — moved the word *being* into the analysis — and stopped before
the half that would have made it cost something. On this reading, §44 is
not a rejection of the Sixth Investigation. It is the completion of §39's
own gesture, carried past the point at which Husserl halted.

## The morphology of the transfer

If the coinages were meant to conceal, they are extraordinarily badly
designed for it, because they announce their content in their shape. This
is a claim about word-formation, and it can only be run on the German
volumes — *Sein und Zeit*, the 1923/24 and 1924/25 Marburg courses, and
Husserl's own German — because the 1921–22 course exists in the archive
only in English translation, and its most conspicuous coinages (ruinance,
relucence, prestruction, larvance) are a translator's Latinate renderings
of German forms I cannot inspect.[^26] Within the German, the pattern is
clean.

Husserl's operative terms for the truth-situation are, with near
uniformity, *nomina actionis* — nouns of process formed on verbs, in
*-ung*: *Erfüllung*, fulfilment; *Ausweisung*, exhibition; *Identifizierung*,
identification; *Bewährung*, verification; *Vernunftsetzung*, the positing
of reason. They name doings. This is not incidental to the doctrine; it is
the doctrine, since for Husserl truth in the strict sense is precisely an
act — "the act of this most perfect synthesis of fulfilment" — and its
ideality is the ideality of an act-form.

Heidegger's operative terms fall into two families, and neither is a
*nomen actionis*. The first is compounds of *sein*: *Wahrsein*,
*Entdeckend-sein*, *In-der-Welt-sein*, *In-der-Wahrheit-Sein*,
*Entdecktsein*; alongside them *Seinsbestimmung*, *Seinsweise*,
*Seinsart*, *Seinscharakter*. The second is abstract nominals in *-heit*
naming achieved or suffered states: *Erschlossenheit*, *Entdecktheit*,
*Unverborgenheit*, *Verdecktheit*, *Verschlossenheit*, *Verstelltheit*.
The two families divide the labour exactly as the doctrine requires. The
*-sein* compounds carry what for Husserl was an act and is now a way to
be; the *-heit* nominals carry what for Husserl was the objective
correlate and is now a condition in which beings stand.

The morphology, in other words, states the thesis. To rewrite *Erfüllung*
as *Entdeckend-sein* is to say, in the shape of the word before any
argument is offered, that what looked like a process performed by a
subject is a manner in which an entity is. A vocabulary designed to
conceal a borrowing would do the opposite: it would preserve the
grammatical category and vary the lexical root, producing synonyms.
Heidegger varies the category and, at the decisive places, preserves the
root — *entdecken* stands to *Entdecktheit* as *erfüllen* to *Erfüllung*,
and the difference between the pairs is the whole distance between an act
and a state.

The one place where he keeps a Husserlian *-ung* noun is instructive. It
is *Ausweisung*, and it appears in the footnote — "Zur Idee der Ausweisung
als »Identifizierung«" — and not in the body of §44, where the
corresponding work is done by *Entdecktheit* and *Erschlossenheit*. The
Husserlian morphology survives in the apparatus and is excluded from the
text. The stratification described above is thus visible at the level of
suffixes.

A further test suggests itself, and it is the test the concealment story
needs to pass: are the coinages back-translatable? Take the two central
ones. *Entdecktheit* very nearly is: the uncoveredness of an innerworldly
being, its standing available as what it is, is close enough to Husserl's
sense (1)/(3) — "the identity of the object at once meant and given in
adequation" — that one could argue about the residue. *Erschlossenheit* is
not, and cannot be, because there is no Husserlian term for it. Husserl's
correlate-pole is *die Idee eines möglichen Bewußtseins*: an idea, a
possibility, an essential structure quantified over. Heidegger's is the
disclosedness of a particular entity whose own being is at issue for it,
and which is *gleichursprünglich in der Wahrheit und Unwahrheit* — always
already both.[^27] One can translate an act into a state; one cannot
translate a possibility into a factical condition without losing the
modality that made the possibility do its work. Which is to say: the
coinage marks the place where the inheritance stops. It does not cover
the inheritance; it maps its edge.

## *Sachlichkeit*, or the word that hides by being ordinary

If there is a place in this corpus where the terminology genuinely
obscures a transfer, it is not among the neologisms at all. It is a word
that any German speaker in 1925 would have read without a flicker.

At the close of his first characterization of ἀλήθεια in the *Sophist*
course, having established that truth is "im eigentlichen Sinne doch eine
*Seinsbestimmung* des menschlichen Daseins selbst," and that
*In-der-Wahrheit-Sein* means "das jeweilige Seiende, mit dem das Dasein
Umgang pflegt, unverdeckt zur Verfügung haben," Heidegger adds a sentence
that reads like a summary and is in fact a definition:

> Wenn wir beim Sinn von Wahrheit als Unverdecktsein, Aufgedecktheit
> bleiben, wird deutlich, daß Wahrheit so viel besagt wie *Sachlichkeit*,
> verstanden als solches *Verhalten* des Daseins.[^28]
>
> [If we hold to the sense of truth as being-uncovered, uncoveredness, it
> becomes clear that truth means as much as *Sachlichkeit*, understood as
> such a *comportment* of Dasein.]

Two words in that sentence have histories in this corpus. *Verhalten* is
the word that the 1921–22 course glossed, in its second sense, as
"intentionality objectivated."[^29] [UNSUPPORTED — needs the German text
of GA 61: the archive holds only the English translation, so that
"comportment" there renders *Verhalten* is an inference from the
translator's lexical choice. The identification of GA 19's *Verhalten*
with GA 61's gloss is the load-bearing step of this section, and it
would be secured, or broken, by a single glance at the German.] And
*Sachlichkeit* — objectivity,
matter-of-factness, the disposition of one who sticks to the thing at hand
— is the word under which, one year earlier, Heidegger had described
Husserl's own founding achievement. In the 1923/24 course he pauses to
ask: "Was ist die Sorge der Sachlichkeit, die früher als das
charakteristische Moment des Durchbruchs der phänomenologischen Forschung
angenommen wurde?" — what is the care for *Sachlichkeit* that was earlier
taken as the characteristic moment of the breakthrough of phenomenological
research?[^30] And he answers, in the same section, that this care
miscarries: under the demand for *evidente Allverbindlichkeit* — evident
bindingness on everyone — "die Sachen selbst begegnen primär in dieser
Sorge als Probleme," the things themselves are encountered primarily as
problems, laid out in advance along determinate lines of inquiry.[^31]

So the term migrates. In 1923/24 *Sachlichkeit* names what phenomenology
was after and lost: the being-toward-the-matter that the care for
justified knowledge converts into a problem-field. In 1924/25 the same
word names what truth *is*. Heidegger has taken the watchword of the
phenomenological movement, diagnosed the movement as having betrayed it,
and then made it the definition of ἀλήθεια — and he has done so in the
body of the text, in ordinary German, with no mention of Husserl anywhere
near it.

This is the real concealment in the corpus, and its mechanism is the exact
inverse of the one the received charge describes. A neologism advertises
that something new is being said and invites the reader to ask where it
came from. An ordinary word does not. *Sachlichkeit* passes as an ethos —
sobriety, disinterestedness, a scholarly virtue — and reads as a piece of
edification rather than a technical identification. But in context it is a
technical identification, and a very strong one: truth is directedness
toward the matter, understood as a comportment of Dasein; and comportment,
by Heidegger's own gloss, is intentionality. The sentence is as close to
"truth is intentionality rightly enacted" as this corpus comes, and it is
unfootnoted.

I press this as far as the evidence goes and no further. I have inspected
one of the twenty-four occurrences of *Sachlichkeit* in the *Sophist*
course and one of the four in the 1923/24 course; the reading above stands
on those two and on the continuity of *Verhalten* between the courses.
Whether the term carries the same weight across its other occurrences is
not something I have checked, and the claim should be treated as
provisional until it is.[^32]

## The strongest case for suppression

The case I have been building can be resisted, and it is worth setting out
the resistance at full strength rather than in the weak form in which the
concealment story usually appears. The strong version does not need
Heidegger to have intended anything. It needs only that the vocabulary
functioned to suppress, and it has four supports, three of them
quantitative.

First, the frequency profile. Across the four volumes, as the doctrine
matures, both the creditor's name and the borrowed term thin out
dramatically. In the 1923/24 course, the volume of sustained engagement,
"Husserl" occurs some 225 times and *Intentionalität* 21 times. In the
1924/25 *Sophist* course, "Husserl" falls to 19 and *Intentionalität* to
11. In *Sein und Zeit*, across roughly 6,700 lines, "Husserl" occurs 14
times and *Intentionalität* exactly once — and that once is not in §44 but
in the analysis of ecstatic temporality.[^33] Meanwhile *Erschlossenheit*,
absent from the 1923/24 course and occurring once in the *Sophist*, appears
155 times in *Sein und Zeit*, and *Entdecktheit* 51 times. The curves cross.
As the coinages take over, the debt-words disappear; and by the systematic
statement, the word for the structure that the 1921–22 marginalia called
"the basic formal structure" of the entire apparatus has been reduced to a
single occurrence in another chapter.

Second, the placement. Every avowal I have adduced is paratextual — and
paratext is precisely where an author discharges an obligation at the
lowest available cost. A marginal note is not a claim the text has to
live with. A footnote can acknowledge a source while the argument above it
proceeds as though the source were an ornament. That the acknowledgments
are numerous does not tell against this; it tells for it, since cheap
acknowledgments can be numerous exactly because they are cheap.

Third, the order of operations. On the archive's own account of the
sequence, the polemic against Husserl is completed before the positive
vocabulary arrives: the 1923/24 course prosecutes the case — knowing as
the *Sorge um erkannte Erkenntnis*, the care for *evidente
Allverbindlichkeit*, traced back to Descartes' construal of *verum* as
*certum* with the scholastic ontology left standing underneath[^34] — and
contains, so the notes report, no ἀλήθεια, no *Unverborgenheit*, no
*Erschlossenheit*, all of which arrive the following year in the *Sophist*
course.[^35] That is exactly the order one would expect if the new
vocabulary were a way of proceeding without Husserl's: first clear the
ground of the predecessor, then build in terms he has no share in.

Fourth, and most seriously, the grammatical demotion with which the whole
apparatus begins. The intransitive verb table of 1921–22 does not merely
downgrade the relation to the object; it takes the *word for the relation*
and calls it intentionality *objectivated*. Whatever Heidegger meant by
that, the effect on the vocabulary is that comportment-to enters the system
already marked as derivative. And the table itself sits under a gloss that
should give any defender pause: directly above ἀληθεύω, "I am truthful,
sincere," stands σοφιστεύω, "I act and teach like a sophist; I conceal."
Heidegger has put the concealer in his own table of intransitives.

This is a serious case, and the first two supports I accept without
qualification. The frequency curves are real and the placement is real. The
reply to them is not that they are illusory but that they are consistent
with the stratification thesis rather than an alternative to it: a
formally indicative vocabulary becomes autonomous by design, and as it does
the paratext carries an ever larger share of a load that the body text is
methodologically barred from carrying. That the load is carried in an
ever-narrower place is a genuine cost of the method, and I do not think it
can be argued away. It can only be located.

The fourth support I take to be answered by the word *objectivated*
itself, on the reading given earlier — the demotion is of intentionality
construed as a relation between separated relata, not of intentionality —
and by the fact that the object-pole returns, explicitly and with a
qualifier, in the *Sophist* course: "Die ἀλήθεια ist ein eigentümlicher
Seinscharakter des Seienden, sofern das Seiende in einem *Verhältnis*
steht zu einem Hinsehen darauf, zu einem sich im Seienden umsehenden
Erschließen, zu einem Erkennen."[^36] The *Verhältnis* is back, and it is
back without idealism, since the same paragraph insists that "die Natur ist
vorhanden, auch bevor sie entdeckt wird" — nature is present at hand even
before it is discovered.[^37] But I do not think the tension between the
1921–22 intransitive and the 1924/25 *Verhältnis* is resolved by the texts,
and I decline to resolve it on their behalf. The *Sophist* course does not
mention the earlier claim; there is no retraction and no re-derivation; the
grammatical thesis is simply not operative any more. Whether it was
abandoned or absorbed is not recoverable from these four volumes. It is
worth adding that the thesis was philologically fragile to begin with:
Heidegger's own table glosses ἰατρεύω as "I doctor — someone, going over to
(transitive) someone," so the table does not in fact exhibit a uniform
intransitivity, and we have it only in translation.[^38]

The third support is a different matter. It is not merely answerable; it
is false, and its falsity is the hinge of this article.

## 1923/24: the date the direction of grounding reverses

The archive's working notes state, as a settled result, that the 1923/24
course "is the critique, not the doctrine," that "ἀλήθεια, Unverborgenheit
and Erschlossenheit are all at zero: the positive vocabulary has not
arrived yet, it comes the following year in GA 19," and — summarizing the
Heidegger side as a whole — that "the Heidegger side needed no retraction
at any stage; each volume tightened the previous one."[^39] The first two
of these are checkable, and they do not survive checking.

It is true that the strings ἀλήθεια, *Unverborgenheit* and *Erschlossenheit*
do not occur in the 1923/24 course. But *Entdecktheit* occurs ten times,
and *Entdecktsein* alongside it — and *Entdecktheit* is not an incidental
word. It is one of the two nouns that carry §44 of *Sein und Zeit* four
years later, where it appears fifty-one times. The notes' zero-count was a
search for the wrong strings, and the conclusion drawn from it — that the
positive vocabulary post-dates the critique — is the opposite of the truth.

What the 1923/24 course actually says, in the section on Dasein's flight
from itself, is this:

> Sein im Sinne des *Seins-in-einer-Welt* besagt *Entdecktsein*, in einer
> Welt *sichtig* stehen. Die *Entdecktheit* des Daseins ist es, vor der die
> Sorge auf der Flucht ist. Ein Versuch, der nicht zum Ausreifen gekommen
> ist, ist das ἀληθές der griechischen Ontologie. Dieses ἀληθές ist ein
> Blick der Griechen in die Entdecktheit, ist aber von den Griechen selbst
> wieder verdeckt worden.[^40]
>
> [Being in the sense of *being-in-a-world* means *being-uncovered*,
> standing sightedly in a world. It is the *uncoveredness* of Dasein from
> which care is in flight. An attempt that did not come to maturity is the
> ἀληθές of Greek ontology. This ἀληθές is a glimpse of the Greeks into
> uncoveredness, but it was covered over again by the Greeks themselves.]

Every substantive element of the 1927 doctrine is in that passage.
Uncoveredness is a determination of Dasein; it is equated with
being-in-a-world; untruth is not a lapse from it but the direction in which
care is already fleeing — which is the equiprimordiality thesis before it
has its name. And the Greeks appear, but in a subordinate role that is the
exact reverse of the one they will occupy a year later. Here ἀληθές is *an
attempt that did not come to maturity*, "ein Blick" — a glimpse — into a
phenomenon Heidegger has already named in his own terms, and one which the
Greeks themselves proceeded to cover over. *Entdecktheit* is the criterion;
ἀληθές is measured against it and found wanting.

In the *Sophist* course of the following winter, the relation is inverted.
There, ἀλήθεια is the phenomenon to be interpreted — approached through the
α-privative, "Das α ist ein α-privativum," so that the Greeks are credited
with an understanding "daß das Unverdecktsein der Welt erst *errungen*
werden muß," that the world's uncoveredness must first be wrested from
concealment[^41] — and Dasein is what the interpretation of ἀλήθεια
yields: "ἀληθεύειν meint: *aufdeckendsein*, die Welt aus der
Verschlossenheit und Verdecktheit herausnehmen. Und das ist eine Seinsweise
des menschlichen Daseins."[^42] The Greeks are no longer witnesses who
glimpsed and lost the phenomenon; they are the source from which it is
recovered.

That reversal is datable to within a year, and it is a doctrinal shift, not
a tightening. Something is retracted between winter 1923/24 and winter
1924/25 — not a thesis about truth, all of which survive, but the
*evidential standing of the Greek material*. In the earlier course
Heidegger holds the position on independent grounds and uses ἀληθές as
corroboration; in the later one he presents the position as the yield of
reading ἀληθές. The notes' claim that no retraction was needed at any stage
is true of the content and false of the warrant, and the distinction
matters more here than anywhere else in the comparison, because the warrant
is precisely what is at issue in a dispute about concealed sources.

And the terminological genealogy runs the same way. Consider
*Erschlossenheit*, the word that in 1927 names truth in the most original
sense. It has no occurrences in the 1923/24 course and one in the *Sophist*.
But the *verb* is everywhere in 1923/24 — some fifty-three occurrences of
*erschließ-* forms — and its central application is not to Dasein and not
to the Greeks. It is to Husserl. The section that carries the course's
sharpest thesis is titled "Das *Erschließen* des thematischen Feldes
»Bewußtsein« durch die Sorge um erkannte Erkenntnis," and its task is stated
as showing "daß diese *Sorge um erkannte Erkenntnis* es ist, die dieses
*thematische Feld »Bewußtsein«* erschließt" — that it is the care about
known knowledge which discloses the thematic field "consciousness."[^43]

The word that will name Heidegger's own concept of truth is, at this stage,
his word for the operation by which the Husserlian care produces the field
it then takes itself to have found. *Erschlossenheit* is not a Greek
retrieval and not a coinage from facticity. It is minted in the critique of
Husserl, as a description of what Husserl was doing, and subsequently
promoted to the name of the phenomenon Husserl was doing it to.

This is the strongest form in which the concealment intuition can be
vindicated, and it is worth stating precisely because it is so much
narrower than the received charge. Heidegger does not conceal that he uses
intentionality; he says so repeatedly. What goes unrecorded is that the
vocabulary in which he restates it was generated in the encounter with
Husserl rather than in the encounter with the Greeks — and the
presentation of 1924/25 and 1927, which derives the vocabulary from
ἀλήθεια and from Aristotle, systematically obscures that. The debt that is
displaced is not conceptual but genetic. And the displacement is
philosophically consequential rather than merely biographical, because a
doctrine that presents itself as the retrieval of an ancient phenomenon
claims a very different kind of warrant from one that presents itself as
the correction of a contemporary. The first cannot be refuted by showing
that the contemporary was misread.

## The remainder that cannot be renamed

There is one thing Husserl's vocabulary does that Heidegger's cannot, and
it marks the limit of the whole operation.

Return to the *Ideen I* formula. Its second sentence is the one that
matters: "Umgekehrt, wenn diese Möglichkeit gewährleistet ist, ist *eo
ipso* der Gegenstand wahrhaft seiend." The correlation is a biconditional.
From the guaranteed possibility of adequate originary givenness one may
infer the object's truly being. That converse is what makes the Husserlian
doctrine an epistemology at all: it licenses the passage from something
about the conditions of verification to something about the object. And it
is purchased with the apparatus of ideality — the *Idee eines möglichen
Bewußtseins*, an essential possibility rather than an empirical one, which
is why the doctrine survives the fact that no actual consciousness ever
achieves adequate givenness of a transcendent thing. Husserl is explicit
that it never does: "Prinzipiell kann ein Dingreales, ein Sein solchen
Sinnes in einer abgeschlossenen Erscheinung nur 'inadäquat' erscheinen,"
from which it follows essentially that no positing of reason resting on
such an appearance can be "endgültig" or "unüberwindlich."[^44] The Idea
does the work the actual evidence cannot.

The late work does not retreat from this; it re-founds it. In the fourth
Cartesian Meditation Husserl re-reads *Wahrheit an sich* as a matter of
potentiality rather than of a Platonic realm: "Dieser weiteste Sinn des
An-sich verweist also auf Evidenz, aber nicht auf eine Evidenz als
Erlebnistatsache, sondern auf gewisse im transzendentalen Ich und seinem
Leben begründete Potentialitäten" — potentialities grounded in the
transcendental ego and its life, "als Erlebnistatsachen ins Unendliche
wiederholbare Evidenzen," evidences infinitely repeatable as facts of lived
experience.[^45] What secures the in-itself is an *ideale Immanenz* and an
"Ich kann immer wieder."[^46] Truth is independent of any actual act, and
this independence is constructed rather than presupposed — built out of a
structure of repeatable potentialities rather than assumed as a Platonic
datum.

Heidegger has nothing to put in that place, and §44c is visibly the section
where this becomes a problem. He states the dependence at maximum strength
— "Wahrheit »gibt es« nur, sofern und *solange Dasein ist*"; Newton's laws,
the principle of non-contradiction, every truth whatever are true only so
long as Dasein is; before their discovery Newton's laws "waren … nicht
»wahr«"[^47] — and then immediately moves to contain the damage: that they
were neither true nor false before Newton "kann nicht bedeuten, das
Seiende, das sie entdeckend aufzeigen, sei vordem nicht gewesen," and
"[m]it der Entdecktheit des Seienden zeigt sich dieses gerade als das
Seiende, das vordem schon war."[^48]

The archive's notes read this as settling matters: the qualifier "resolves
the open thread," "states the same restriction more sharply," blocks the
idealist reading.[^49] I think that is too quick, and the reason is
instructive. What the qualifier secures is the *beings* — it says that
uncoveredness is Dasein-dependent and that the entities uncovered are not.
That is a genuine result and it does block idealism about the world. But it
is not the result Husserl's converse was needed for. Husserl's converse
underwrites an inference about *aboutness*: given the guaranteed
possibility of adequate givenness, the object truly is — and therefore what
was meant was true of it. Heidegger's qualifier says that the entities
Newton's laws disclose already were. It does not say, and on his premises
cannot say, that the laws were *about* those entities before there was any
uncovering; indeed *Entdecktheit*, the term that would have to carry the
aboutness, is exactly what he has just made Dasein-relative. The asymmetry
he ends with — truth requires Dasein, beings do not — leaves the
intentional relation itself stranded on the Dasein side of the line, with
nothing corresponding to the *Idee eines möglichen Bewußtseins* to give it
standing when no Dasein is about.

Heidegger is not unaware of the pressure. The move he makes is to argue
that the Dasein-relativity does not make truth arbitrary, since "nur *weil*
»Wahrheit« als Entdecken eine *Seinsart des Daseins* ist, kann sie *dessen*
Belieben entzogen werden" — only because truth is a way of Dasein's being
can it be withdrawn from Dasein's caprice — and that even
*Allgemeingültigkeit* is rooted in Dasein's being able to uncover beings in
themselves and set them free.[^50] That answers the charge of subjectivism.
It does not supply a converse. And it cannot, because a *Seinsart* is not
the sort of thing one can quantify over in the way an Idea can: one can say
that to every truly-being object there corresponds *the idea of* a possible
consciousness without committing oneself to any consciousness, but there is
no parallel form for "there corresponds the idea of a possible
being-in-the-world" that does not either collapse into Husserl's
construction or assert a Dasein. [UNSUPPORTED — needs GA 21 or GA 24, the
lecture courses flanking *Sein und Zeit*, where the logic of the
as-structure and the ground of intentionality are treated at length. The
claim that no such quantified form is available to Heidegger is an
argument from the shape of his vocabulary, not from a text in which he
tries and fails to construct one; if he attempts it anywhere, it is
there.]

I do not think this is a defect that Heidegger overlooked, and I do not
think §44c conceals it; the section reads as a man circling a problem
rather than disposing of one. But it is the point at which the
terminological transfer stops paying. Everything else in the Husserlian
apparatus could be rewritten as a mode of being — the act as *Wahrsein*,
the correlate as *Entdecktheit*, the fulfilment as being-uncovering. The
one thing that could not be rewritten was the modal machinery that made
truth outlast the act, and it could not be rewritten because it was the
part of Husserl's doctrine that was never about acts in the first place.
Whether the later Heidegger's shift of the anchor from Dasein to being
itself is an attempt to supply the missing converse is a question this
corpus cannot answer, since it holds nothing after 1927.[^51]

---

What sustains the concealment story, in the end, is a habit of reading
rather than a fact about the texts. Readers weight bodies over apparatus,
and Heidegger's apparatus is where his debts live. A reader who takes §44
as the statement of the doctrine and its footnote as scholarly furniture
will find a doctrine of truth derived from Aristotle and Heraclitus with a
polite nod to a colleague; a reader who takes the footnote seriously will
find a philosopher complaining that nobody has read the Sixth Investigation
properly, at the exact moment he is building on it. Both are reading the
same page.

But there is a deeper reason the story survives, and it is not a
reader's failing. A formally indicative vocabulary is designed to be
unglossable in its own text. It forbids the sentence that would state its
genealogy, because that sentence would turn an indication into a
definition and hand the reader a meaning in advance of the enactment that
alone can confirm it. The method therefore produces, necessarily, a body of
work whose terms cannot say where they came from — and it produces it in a
philosopher who also held that the preservation of results is itself a mode
of covering up.[^52] The very rigour that keeps the coinages honest is what
makes their descent unreadable. That is not a paradox Heidegger stumbled
into. It is the shape of the method, and the marginalia are his
acknowledgment that he knew it needed one.

Which leaves the σοφιστεύω sitting in his table. He put it there himself,
one line above ἀληθεύω, glossed "I act and teach like a sophist; I
conceal." He had already written that all the talk about intentionality can
conceal the lack of direction toward the object — his charge against a word
that had, in his judgement, become a place to stand rather than a way of
facing. The charge was a good one, and it generalizes past its target. A
vocabulary of being-uncovering, being-in-the-world, disclosedness, is
exactly as available for possession without exercise as a vocabulary of
intentional acts; the coinages are no more self-enacting than the word they
replaced, and eighty years of their circulation as counters has proved it.
Heidegger diagnosed the disease that his own remedy would contract. The
diagnosis was intentionality's, and so, one has to say, was the remedy.

## 2. Desiderata

- **An intentionality index across the corpus, on the model of the existing
  truth index.** This is the single largest gap. Every claim in this article
  about what Heidegger does with intentionality rests on five passages in
  GA 61, one gloss in GA 19, and three frequency counts. An index of
  *Intentionalität*, *Verhalten*, *Bezug*, *Richtung*, *Gerichtetsein*
  across the four Heidegger volumes, and of *Intentionalität*, *Noesis*,
  *Noema*, *Auffassung*, *Meinen* across the Husserl volumes, would settle
  whether the *Verhalten*/intentionality identification of GA 61:1160 is
  sustained after 1922 or is a one-off.
- **The German text of GA 61.** The archive holds only the English
  translation. Three steps in the article depend on wording I cannot check:
  "intentionality objectivated" (which German? *vergegenständlicht*?
  *objektiviert*? the pejorative force of the argument depends on it);
  "comportment" (presumably *Verhalten*, which is what makes the link to
  GA 19:1376 work, but this is an inference from the translator's choice);
  and the Latinate coinage series (ruinance, relucence, prestruction,
  larvance), on which the morphological argument in §"The morphology of the
  transfer" deliberately does not rely for that reason. Obtaining the German
  would either secure or break the bridge between GA 61 and GA 19.
- **The remaining seven occurrences of *Entdecktheit* in GA 17.** I read
  three (`:3164`, `:3512`, and the table-of-contents heading at `:353`).
  The dating argument in §"1923/24" would be materially strengthened or
  qualified by the rest, particularly `:1515` and `:2335`, which sit in the
  Husserl and Descartes sections respectively and may show the term being
  used *of* the predecessors rather than in propria persona.
- **The remaining occurrences of *Sachlichkeit*** — 24 in GA 19, 4 in GA 17.
  I read one of each. The claim that GA 19:1376 constitutes a technical
  identification rather than a rhetorical flourish stands or falls on
  whether the term is used technically elsewhere in the course.
- **The single occurrence of *Intentionalität* in *Sein und Zeit*
  (`:5497`, on the intentionality of "consciousness" in ecstatic
  temporality).** I have the location and the immediate phrase, not the
  argument. If that passage says that intentionality is *founded in*
  ecstatic temporality, it is the missing terminus of the story told here
  and would let the article end on a text rather than on a diagnosis.
- **Anything after 1927.** The corpus stops at *Sein und Zeit*. Whether the
  shift of the anchor from Dasein to being itself is an attempt to supply
  the converse that §44c lacks cannot be assessed without, at minimum, the
  1930 *Vom Wesen der Wahrheit*.
- **Husserl's side of the exchange.** Nothing in the corpus records Husserl
  responding to any of this. His marginalia to *Sein und Zeit*, or the
  *Nachlass* material from 1927–29, would settle whether he recognized §44
  as building on the Sixth Investigation or as breaking with it — which is
  directly relevant, since the article's claim is that §44 completes an
  operation Husserl began in LI VI §39.
- **Hua III/2 and the *Erfahrung und Urteil* material on antepredicative
  evidence.** The notes flag both as unmined. If the supplementary texts to
  *Ideen I* revise §142's biconditional, the argument of §"The remainder
  that cannot be renamed" would need rewriting.

## 3. Load-bearing points resting on the notes' commentary with no primary
text behind them

- **The characterization of GA 61 as containing "no worked-out doctrine of
  truth."** I have not read the volume entire and am relying on the notes'
  survey for the negative claim. The article uses this in dating the
  doctrine's emergence.
- **The claim that *Ideen II* takes the account of truth from *Ideen I* as
  settled and does not rework it.** Taken wholly from the notes' corpus
  caveat; I did not open the volume. Nothing in the article depends on it
  except the decision not to consult *Ideen II*.
- **The reading of GA 17 §16 as claiming that the field "consciousness" is
  *disclosed by* the care rather than found prior to it.** I verified the
  section heading and the statement of the task, both of which support it,
  but the argument that discharges the task lies in the following sections,
  which I did not read. The article's genealogy of *Erschlossenheit* uses
  the heading and the task-statement only, which is why it is framed as a
  claim about the word's application rather than about the argument's
  success.
- **The frequency figure of 186 Husserl mentions in GA 17** given in the
  notes. My own count returns 225. The discrepancy is probably a matter of
  what was counted (inflected forms, footnotes, running heads); I use my
  own figure and flag the divergence rather than reconciling it.
- **The identification of *Verhalten* as the German behind GA 61's
  "comportment."** Strictly this is my inference from the translation plus
  the GA 19 usage, not the notes' claim, but it functions in the article
  like a notes-level claim and deserves listing here: if the German is
  *Sichverhalten* or *Haltung*, the bridge to GA 19:1376 weakens.

## 4. Directions opened but not taken

- **The Descartes strand.** GA 17 contains a sustained reading of the
  *Regulae* and *Meditationes* (the notes count 301 occurrences of
  "Descartes"). If *verum → certum* is the inheritance Husserl's
  evidence-ideal descends from, then the question whether Husserl's
  principle of all principles is or is not a Cartesian criterion in
  disguise is the natural next article. The notes explicitly leave it open
  and warn against citing them as settling it; I have respected that and
  used the Cartesian genealogy only as reported doctrine.
- **The five modes of ἀληθεύειν as a pluralism about intentionality.** If
  truth is distributed across τέχνη, ἐπιστήμη, φρόνησις, σοφία and νοῦς,
  and if truth is *Sachlichkeit* understood as comportment, then Heidegger
  is committed to a plurality of irreducible directednesses — which would
  be a substantive alternative to Husserl's single grounding doxic mode
  rather than a mere refusal of it. The material is indexed in the notes
  (GA 19 `:1414`, `:1653`, `:1759`, `:1779`, `:2502`) and I did not open it.
- **Formal indication as a theory of terminology.** The article uses formal
  indication to explain why the equations are paratextual, but treats it as
  a given. A proper treatment would ask whether a formally indicative term
  can have a genealogy at all, and what that implies for the possibility of
  writing the history of Heidegger's own vocabulary.
- **Lask.** Heidegger's footnote names him as the one reader outside
  phenomenology who took the Sixth Investigation up positively. Nothing by
  Lask is in the corpus. If the coinages have a proximate source other than
  Husserl and the Greeks, that footnote is where the corpus points.
- **The *Verfallsgeschichte* applied reflexively.** GA 61:542 says that the
  preservation of philosophical results is itself covering-up and ruinant.
  Applied to Heidegger's own terminology, this predicts precisely the
  reception history in which *Erschlossenheit* becomes a counter. The
  article gestures at this in its last paragraphs; it deserves an argument.

## 5. Places the notes appear wrong or internally in tension

1. **The GA 17 vocabulary claim is false.** The notes state that in GA 17
   "`ἀλήθεια`, `Unverborgenheit` and `Erschlossenheit` are all at zero: the
   positive vocabulary has not arrived yet, it comes the following year in
   GA 19," and repeat this in the corpus map ("Contains **no**
   ἀλήθεια/Unverborgenheit vocabulary"). Those three strings are indeed
   absent. But *Entdecktheit* occurs ten times and *Entdecktsein* alongside
   it, and *Entdecktheit* is one of the two nouns carrying §44 in 1927
   (51 occurrences). Moreover the ἀληθές of Greek ontology *is* discussed at
   `:3164` — so even the Greek vocabulary is not absent, only the noun
   ἀλήθεια. The notes drew a conclusion about the arrival of a doctrine from
   a search over three strings.
2. **The consequence for the notes' sequence.** Because of (1), the notes'
   organizing claim that GA 17 "is the critique, not the doctrine" and that
   one should "never" cite it for Heidegger's own account of truth is
   wrong as stated. GA 17:3164 gives uncoveredness as a determination of
   Dasein, equates it with being-in-a-world, and makes care's flight from it
   the origin of untruth — that is Heidegger's own account, in 1923/24.
3. **"The Heidegger side needed no retraction at any stage; each volume
   tightened the previous one."** True of the theses, false of their
   warrant. Between GA 17 and GA 19 the evidential relation between Dasein
   and the Greeks inverts: in GA 17 ἀληθές is "ein Versuch, der nicht zum
   Ausreifen gekommen ist," measured against *Entdecktheit*; in GA 19
   ἀλήθεια is the phenomenon whose interpretation yields Dasein. The notes'
   framing of a smooth accumulation obscures the one shift in the corpus
   that bears directly on the question of sources.
4. **GA 61:1862 is misfiled.** The notes place it under "Heidegger on
   Husserl explicitly," in a section framed as polemic, and quote only the
   *Destruktion* sentence. The passage's first paragraph is the corpus's
   most explicit *positive* avowal — coinages declared to be expressions of
   intentionality, intentionality declared the basic formal structure of all
   categorial structures of facticity. Filed as polemic, it disappears from
   the notes' synthesis, which is why that synthesis has no category for
   "retained and re-founded."
5. **The divergence schema is biased toward rupture.** The notes' six "cuts"
   are all statements of difference; there is no corresponding inventory of
   retentions. This is a structural feature, not an error of fact, but it
   has an effect: cut 2 ("Mechanism: fulfilment vs. counter-movement")
   presents the exclusion of fulfilling intuition at GA 61:2028 as a
   divergence about intentionality, when the same course says intentionality
   is the basic formal structure of the whole apparatus. What GA 61:2028
   excludes is a *paradigm of givenness*, not a structure of directedness.
6. **The notes under-quote their own best evidence in three places** — the
   SZ §44a footnote (whose reproach about the reception of LI VI is the
   point), LI VI §39 (whose reassignment paragraphs are the point), and
   GA 61:1130–1175 (whose intentionality gloss is the point). In each case
   the notes cite the location correctly and summarize past the sentence
   that matters. This is a hazard of indexing by topic: a truth index
   records what a passage says about truth and drops what it says about
   anything else.
7. **A smaller tension.** The notes assert that GA 61's ἀληθεύω and
   σοφιστεύω are "**both** intransitive: names for a *way of being*, not for
   a relation to an object." Heidegger's table as printed glosses ἰατρεύω as
   "I doctor—someone, going over to (transitive) someone," i.e. it marks
   transitivity where it finds it, and it does not mark ἀληθεύω either way.
   The intransitivity of ἀληθεύω is Heidegger's implicit assumption and the
   notes' explicit claim; it is not on the face of the table.
8. **The reading of SZ `:3454` as "resolving the open thread."** Argued
   against in §"The remainder that cannot be renamed" above. The qualifier
   secures the beings, not the aboutness, and the notes' own cut 6 ("what
   truth depends on") is left in worse shape by it than they allow.
