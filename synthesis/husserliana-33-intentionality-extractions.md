## Key Clusters

### 1. Intentional Consciousness & Apprehension (Lines 81-95)

The editors' introduction establishes that Husserl's doctrine of intentional consciousness forms the framework for understanding retention and phantasy. The fundamental problem is determining how an object maintains identity through consciousness while the representing content transforms.

> **Line 81:** "Husserl found it difficult to precisely determine this perception of the past within his doctrine of intentional consciousness, and the associated difficulties troubled him in the Bernau manuscripts as well."
> *Section III, §The structure of retention*

> **Line 83:** "Problems arose especially from applying the schema of intentional apprehension of a sensation-content (borrowed from the phenomenological determination of outer perception of spatial objects) to time-consciousness and thus to retentional perceptual consciousness."
> *Section III, on time-consciousness*

> **Line 89:** "The first decisive step toward clarifying the modified or unmodified givenness of the sensation-content underlying intentional apprehension is already found in Husserl's earlier time-texts, and these insights are never questioned in the Bernau manuscripts."
> *Section III, on sensation-content*

### 2. Intentional Consciousness as a Species (Lines 91-97)

The commentary traces how Husserl reconceives the primal process and its relation to intentional consciousness. A critical shift occurs: the primal process is no longer assumed to be inherently objectifying or intentional.

> **Line 91:** "This terminological change signals that Husserl no longer took for granted that sensations are given in the primal process as objects and that the primal process itself has the form of an objectifying intentional consciousness."
> *Section III, on terminological changes*

> **Line 93:** "first, the 'absolutely' named consciousness of sensation-data must be a (sub-)species of intentional consciousness—the sensation-data cannot belong to it as 'reell' components"
> *Section III, on absolute consciousness*

> **Line 95:** "the characterization of the primal process as a species of intentional consciousness"
> *Section III, on doubt about constitution*

### 3. Double Intentionality in Absolute Consciousness (Line 105)

A central doctrine emerges: the flow of retentional consciousness harbors **double intentionality**—transverse and longitudinal.

> **Line 105:** "The flow of 'absolute' retentional consciousness thus harbors a double intentionality: one directed at immanent time-objects ('transverse intentionality' / *Querintentionalität*) and one having the form of self-reference or self-consciousness of the flow ('longitudinal intentionality' / *Längsintentionalität*)."
> *Section III, on the solution to infinite regress*

> **Line 105 (continued):** "These two intentional directions belong to one and the same process in the flow of continuous retentional modification and are thus inseparably connected."
> *Section III, on the unity of intentional directions*

### 4. Intentionality in the Primal Stream (Text 1, §1)

The foundational text begins with analyzing the intentionality of ur-presentation, not as a static achievement but as a continuous directedness intrinsic to the flow itself.

> **Line 171:** "The intentionality of ur-presentation. The attentive turning toward a present-new, past, or future"
> *Text Nr. 1, §1 heading*

> **Line 175:** "In the stream of perception, every point has its intentionality; the ur-presentation is not a mere appearing of ur-presences that would only subsequently take on intentionality, but a continual appearing of them in the mode of fulfillment of expectation-intentions."
> *Text 1, §1, on the structure of ur-presentation*

### 5. The Interweaving of Intentionalities (Text 2, §2)

The "complexion" of retention and protention involves an integrated intentional structure where both branches participate in a unified fulfillment-process.

> **Line 181:** "The Interweaving of Retentional and Protentional Intentionality in the Course of the Primal Process"
> *Text Nr. 2, §2 heading*

> **Line 183:** "The 'primal stream' (*Urstrom*)—the stream of the 'last' transcendental life-constituents—includes all events of phenomenological time that 'are' but constitute themselves without any 'participation of attention' or any grasping by the pure ego, as well as the stream of constituting constituents that live in the mode of attention for those events that do constitute themselves in perceptual grasping."
> *Text 2, §2, on the primal stream's intentionality*

### 6. Mediate Intentionality & Horizons (Lines 195-197)

The protentional intentionality is fundamentally mediate—directed at coming events through continuous transformation, never exhausted in a single moment.

> **Line 195:** "But the future-directed expectation is aimed at the coming event or the flowing event-stretch... The expectation targets the coming event, a flowing event-horizon, a variable stretch. Its intentionality is continuously mediately directed at everything ideally distinguishable in what is coming."
> *Text 2, §3, on protentional structure*

> **Line 197:** "Every preceding protention relates to each following one in the protentional continuum as each subsequent retention relates to the preceding one of the same series... On both sides we have mediate intentionality, and to every mediate intentionality belongs the double 'direction' of intentionality: toward the primary object and toward the secondary object."
> *Text 2, §3, on the doubleness of mediate intentionality*

### 7. The Question of Non-Intentional Consciousness (Lines 103-113)

A tension emerges: can the primal process be "absolutely" conscious without itself being intentional? This touches the infinite regress problem at its deepest.

> **Line 103:** "Husserl determines intentional consciousness of a transcendent time-object as an intentional apprehension of a pre-intentional sensation-datum... Is it an intentional, transcendentally constituting consciousness? Does its intentionality still have the form of intentional apprehension of a pre-intentional content?"
> *Section III, on the problem of consciousness of consciousness*

> **Line 113:** "Even if such an unconscious primal process cannot exist, the question remains whether an ungrasped primal process can properly be credited with constitutive achievement. If a subsequent egoic grasping (an act of reflection) were required, both the primal process's independence and its intentional character would become questionable, and its original self-consciousness could scarcely be maintained."
> *Section III, on the Bernau doubts*

### 8. Passive Intentionality (Line 131)

A genetic phenomenology reformulates intentionality: it is not only egoic act-intentionality but arises from pre-egoic tendencies and strivings.

> **Line 131:** "This genetic time-analysis means that Husserl no longer unreflectively treats the intentionality at work in time-consciousness as an egoic act-intentionality with an objectual correlate... Such intentionality plays a role, but Husserl now focuses on its genesis from pre-egoic tendencies, strivings, and inhibitions characterizing the intentionality of a passively running primal process."
> *Section IV, on genetic phenomenology*

### 9. Intentional Structures in Noematic Analysis (Line 125)

The Bernau manuscripts develop a specifically noematic analysis of time, investigating how temporal modes belong to the intentional structure of consciousness.

> **Line 125:** "The Bernau manuscripts also develop for the first time a specifically noematically directed description of time-consciousness: Husserl devoted extensive, independent investigations to noematic time-modalities and their modifications. From this, in connection with the individuation of different kinds of intentional objects, emerged the already-mentioned ontological turn of his phenomenology of time."
> *Section IV, on noematic structures*

## Thematic Summary

The extraction reveals **intentionality** as the leitmotif running through the Husserliana 33 commentary:

1. **Foundational**: Intentionality is intrinsic to consciousness itself, not a property added to passive contents.

2. **Dual-Directional**: The consciousness of time exhibits double intentionality (transverse/object-directed and longitudinal/self-referential).

3. **Mediate**: Intentionality operates through horizons and mediations, never confined to momentary acts.

4. **Passive**: Intentionality is not exhausted by egoic acts but characterizes the pre-egoic primal stream itself.

5. **Problematic**: The nature of absolute or primal consciousness, as intentionality, remains unresolved throughout the Bernau manuscripts—a constant tension between treating the primal process as intentional versus pre-intentional.
