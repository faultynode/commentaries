## The official architecture

Husserl states, more than once and in more than one decade, that intentionality has a single ground: representational, objectivating acts. The Fifth Logical Investigation makes objectivating acts the widest class, to which "the fundamental operation of nominalization" converts any synthetic multi-rayedness into a single-rayed nominal act with a back-pointing matter (`intentionalitaet/hua19-1/040`). The Sixth carries this further: "the acts of meaning-intention, just as well as those of meaning-fulfilment...belong to a single class of acts, to the objectifying", so that no other-kinded act ever functions as a sense-giver (`intentionalitaet/lu6/010`), and a wish's fulfilment is founded in an identification, resting on an objectifying substratum that founds its self-satisfying without exhausting it (`intentionalitaet/lu6/009`). Ideas II generalizes the thesis across feeling and valuing: "every such act, by its essence, is implicitly also objectivating" — not merely built upon objectivating acts in a higher stratum but itself objectivating in what it newly contributes (`intentionalitaet/hua4/010`). Ideas I closes the circle. Every intentional experience carries an "archontic thesis" uniting and governing the others founded on it (`intentionalitaet/hua3-i/004`), and because any thesis can be converted into an actual doxic positing, "all acts whatsoever, including the acts of sentiment and will, are objectivating, originally constituting objects and necessary sources of different regions of being" (`intentionalitaet/hua3-i/005`). Nothing in the corpus, as extracted, shows this claim revised between the first edition of 1913 and the third of 1928 — per gap-010's resolution note, the commentary carrying `hua3-i/005` records the three editions of Ideas I as differing hardly at all on this point. The architecture Ideas I states is the one Husserl kept.

## Two reversals Husserl generated himself, in 1911

Two years before the *Ideas*, in courses that feed directly into it, Husserl's own analysis produces the kind of counter-instance his official architecture cannot absorb — twice, in two unrelated domains, and neither instance is revisited on the spine.

The first is drive. Hua XLIII/2, Part III, lays out a receptivity/spontaneity schema in which "the deepest-lying intentionality lies in the inner consciousness that constitutes the unity in the immanent time-flux" (`intentionalitaet/hua43-2/009`), while the sensation and the primary feeling founded on it have in themselves no intentionality of their own and first become intentional through a "feeling-apprehension" laid over them (`intentionalitaet/hua43-2/010`). This is the official architecture in miniature: a non-intentional layer, then an intentional one built upon it. Extending the picture to the drive-sphere, the text records its own objection: "here at once the objection arises that this is already intentionality", since the drive toward the continuation of an agreeable tone "already has a directedness-toward" whose "striving intention fulfills itself, finds satisfaction or dissatisfaction" (`intentionalitaet/hua43-2/013`, status *criticized*). The layering the architecture needs — a non-intentional primary content beneath an intentional apprehension — will not go through for drive. The unit records the objection as raised. Nothing later in this record, or anywhere else on the ledger's spine, answers it; gap-016 asks for the extraction that would show whether Husserl ever did.

The second is the will. The 1914 course that makes up Part A of Hua XXVIII holds, in its opening pages, the general thesis exactly: to every valuing act there underlie "intellective, objectivating acts" (`intentionalitaet/hua28/004`), and only objectivating acts are "directed upon the objective...whereas valuing acts are directed upon values" (`intentionalitaet/hua28/005`) — value-directedness founded on, yet distinct from, object-directedness. Twelve sections later the same course reverses the direction of founding for one case. On the future-directed will: "instead of the willing being founded upon the belief of the future being, it is rather the belief of the future that springs forth from the willing" (`intentionalitaet/hua28/012`, status *revised*), because "the will as will-certainty sets the future in a manner that first confers on it, for consciousness, the certainty of being" (`intentionalitaet/hua28/011`). Belief — the paradigm objectivating act, the act everything else is supposed to rest on — here rests on the will. The record's pass note is careful to keep this apart from the ordering question of gap-010, which asks how the three statements of the objectivating thesis at Hua XXVIII §9, Ideas II §7 and Ideas I §117 stand to one another; the question here is narrower and sharper: whether founding, for Husserl, runs in only one direction. Gap-018 asks whether the will-founded belief of 1914 was kept, generalized, or withdrawn anywhere later. As extracted, it is answered nowhere.

Neither instance is marginal. Both sit inside courses whose material Husserl was still working over on the way to the *Ideas*: the ledger's own strata re-tagging (recorded in the resolution of gap-010) found that the Part B material of Hua XXVIII on the will-question dates to the 1911 course rather than to a loose 1908–1914 range, which places the will-reversal's groundwork in the same year as the drive-objection. In one short stretch Husserl produced two independent cases in which his own analysis would not sit still under the architecture he was simultaneously building toward. As far as the ledger records, he set both aside rather than revising the architecture; nothing after 1914 on the Husserl side of the spine returns to either by name.

A third version of the same instability surfaces later on Husserl's own analysis of time, and here a reception commentator draws the conclusion Husserl would not. The Hua XXXIII record (1917–18) has the editors conceding that "Husserl no longer took for granted that...the primal process itself has the form of an objectifying intentional consciousness" (`intentionalitaet/hua33/004`), that the primal process "may be pre-intentional" (`intentionalitaet/hua33/007`), and that "the Bernau manuscripts reached no definitive determination" of the matter (`intentionalitaet/hua33/006`) — every one of that record's fourteen units comes from the editors' introduction, not from the text-groups themselves, which is what gap-011 asks to have read. Bernet, working the same retentional structure, states flatly what Husserl left open: because "within absolute consciousness there is no longitudinal without transverse intentionality", the two being inseparable, "absolute consciousness is not absolute" — a consequence he says "Husserl refused to envisage seriously" (`intentionalitaet/bernet-vds/053`). And Bernet locates the significance of this exactly where the present argument needs it: it is "here that his phenomenology of time innovates most radically and runs up against that intentionality of a singular kind which Heidegger will call transcendence" (`intentionalitaet/bernet-vds/050`). On Bernet's reading — and it is Bernet's reading; no primary-side unit puts it this way — Husserl's own time-analysis had already produced, from the inside, something structurally like the move Heidegger would later make against him.

## Heidegger's two stances, not one

The habit of treating Heidegger's critique of intentionality as a single position obscures a shift the ledger's own dating makes visible. In 1923–24, lecturing on the phenomenological research he had inherited, Heidegger names the founding-on-representation thesis directly and rejects it, as the first of Husserl's "disfigurements" (*Verunstaltung*) of the phenomenological findings: intentionality gets "grasped — less expressly than inexpressly — as a specific theoretical comporting", rendered *Meinen*, so that "for every complex intentional nexus theoretical meaning forms the foundation" (`intentionalitaet/ga17/004`, status *criticized*). The claim "that every willing, loving, hating is founded on a representing that first gives the will-able, the hate- and love-able" is, on the course's own words, "a methodological mistake — analogizing the study of emotional experiences to knowing" (`intentionalitaet/ga17/005`). This is the sharpest available statement of the objection Husserl's own 1911 drive-passage had half-raised against itself, now made from outside as an outright rejection of the architecture rather than as an unresolved question left standing inside it. And GA 17 goes further than rejection: it withdraws whole domains from intentionality's competence. "Joy, terror, grief, anxiety" are "phenomena missed when construed as intentionality"; "anxiety cannot be grasped as a being-related-to-something" at all (`intentionalitaet/ga17/007`, status *criticized*); and the being called consciousness "is not 'something relating to something,' a subject before an object" but a being that "in its being-placed-toward the world, co-cares for its own Dasein" (`intentionalitaet/ga17/008`).

Two years on, in 1925, and again in 1927 in the *Grundprobleme*, Heidegger does not repeat the rejection. He affirms the descriptive apparatus at length. GA 20 §5 keeps intentio and intentum, "a peculiar belonging-together of the *how* of being-intended... and the *intentum*" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/007`); keeps fulfilment, since "every intention bears in itself a tendency to fulfillment" with its own determinate mode (`intentionalitaet/ga20/008`); keeps categorial intuition, holding that the discovery of intentionality and the correct grasp of the categorial "are one and the same" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/012`). What it attacks is a pair of misreadings — the perceived as inner image (`intentionalitaet/ga20/006`), and the four being-determinations of pure consciousness treated as if "drawn from the being itself" when they are in fact "precisely suited to bar the way to a question after the being of this being" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/016`). The verdict is explicit: "Not intentionality as such is metaphysically dogmatic, but what is built under the structure, or left unquestioned beneath it" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/010`). GA 24 §9(b)–(c) sharpens this affirming-and-subordinating move into a formula. Against the first misinterpretation, which takes the intentional relation for "an on-hand relation between two on-hand things" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/006`), and the second, a "false subjectivization" arising at the intentum (`intentionalitaet/hga24/010`), Heidegger holds that "it is precisely intentionality and nothing else in which transcendence consists" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/013`); that "the subject is first to be determined in its essence" from intentionality, not the reverse (`intentionalitaet/hga24/014`); and that "we henceforth no longer speak of a subject... but understand the being to which intentional comportments belong as Dasein" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/015`). Then the formula: "Intentionality is the *ratio cognoscendi* of transcendence; transcendence is the *ratio essendi* of intentionality in its various modes" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/016`). Bernet gives the same formula from the reception side almost word for word (`intentionalitaet/bernet-vds/009`) and calls the resulting structure "not vicious but ordered: intentionality gives the access, transcendence gives the ground" (`intentionalitaet/bernet-vds/004`).

So the ledger holds two Heideggerian stances, not one, and nothing in it reconciles them. GA 17 does not subordinate the founding-on-representation thesis; it calls it a mistake and expels mood and anxiety from intentionality's reach altogether. GA 20 and GA 24 subordinate intentionality to a more basic transcendence without rejecting it, and without returning to the domains GA 17 had expelled: no unit in the GA 24 record touches mood or anxiety, and the GA 20 pass note confirms that its Main Part — where the time-phenomenon and, per gap-020, the attunement analysis would sit — was not read. Whether the 1923–24 rejection was superseded by the 1925–27 subordination, softened into it, or simply left standing beside it, is not a question the corpus answers as extracted; no unit on either side names the other. That is a new question, not one of the fifteen already open, and it is recorded below as gap-025.

*Sein und Zeit* inherits the 1927 stance, not the 1923 one — its language is subordination, not rejection — and pushes it one notch further. Knowing is "grounded in an already-being-alongside-the-world" (`intentionalitaet/sz/006`); Dasein, in directing itself toward and grasping, "does not first go outside of an inner sphere in which it is at first encapsulated" (`intentionalitaet/sz/007`), and the commentary's own gloss on this states that "Intentionality...is here out-radicalized" because "there never was an inside to leave" (`intentionalitaet/sz/008`) — a formulation the ledger flags as the commentator's phrase rather than the treatise's word; per the resolution of gap-013, a second commentary on the same section offers no equivalent gloss at all. A note at §69(b) promises the payoff: "that and how the intentionality of 'consciousness' grounds in Dasein's ecstatic temporality, the following division will show" (`intentionalitaet/sz/023`). That division was never published. The nearest place in the finished text where the promise could have been kept — §69(c), on the temporal problem of the transcendence of the world — is a recorded absence; the word does not occur there (`heidegger-sein-und-zeit-commentary#h6-c-the-temporal-problem-of-the-transcendence-of-the-world`). GA 24 grounds intentionality in transcendence, but no unit in its record identifies that transcendence with ecstatic temporality (gap-019 asks for exactly this, and it is open). So the most quotable claim in the reception about what supersedes Husserl in Heidegger — that intentionality is finally grounded in temporality — is, on the evidence this ledger holds, a promissory note the primary corpus never redeems.

## Henry's rival ground, and what it does not engage

A third foundation is proposed decades later, and it is not compatible with either of the first two. *L'essence de la manifestation* (1963) lays down an asymmetric law, "affectivity is never sensible; sensibility is constantly affective" (`intentionalitaet/henry-em/007`), on which affectivity, not representation, is what makes any phenomenon a phenomenon at all: "affectivity is the universal foundation of all phenomena and determines them all originally as affective", and it is "not the grasping of the horizon but the reality of the act that grasps" (`intentionalitaet/henry-em/013`). Henry names his target broadly — "Brentano, Husserl, Scheler, Sartre" — for holding "that affectivity is intentional, consciousness of something", and dismantles the thesis by asking "how can affectivity be understanding...if nothing is more repugnant to its essence than transcendence" (`intentionalitaet/henry-em/012`). This is not subordination of the Heideggerian kind, where transcendence still grounds something called intentionality; it is a denial that affectivity is intentional in any sense. And the target includes Heidegger by name: "the explicit break with *Sein und Zeit*" is that Heidegger's "equiprimordiality of *Befindlichkeit* and *Verstehen*... is exactly the thesis Henry denies, because their bond is one of foundation" — affectivity "has already done its work when the world rises" (`intentionalitaet/henry-em/009`). Henry positions his correction as displacing Heidegger's, not extending it.

Two omissions bound how far this correction reaches on the corpus as extracted. First, Henry's record names Husserl's official architecture and the general intentionalist consensus, but no unit in it cites Hua XLIII/2 §4 — the 1911 passage where Husserl's own analysis had already, in his own words, raised the drive-objection that *L'essence de la manifestation* generalizes. Whether Henry read that text, or whether the 1911 objection reached him only through the general reputation of Husserl's theory of feeling, is not something this record can answer. Second, Henry's engagement with Heidegger is confined, on this record, to the equiprimordiality thesis; nothing in the extracted material shows him engaging GA 24's transcendence-grounding of intentionality at all, although both texts were available to him and both make the kind of foundational claim his argument is built to unseat. A corrective built to answer the question of what grounds intentionality answers only the version of that question *Sein und Zeit* poses, not the version GA 24 poses. Whether Henry's affectivity-thesis is compatible with, subordinate to, or simply does not address Heidegger's transcendence-thesis is accordingly [UNSUPPORTED — needs the extraction of any place Henry names GA 24 or the ratio cognoscendi / ratio essendi formula]; both omissions are recorded together as gap-026.

There is a further complication inside Henry's own corpus that the ledger holds without reconciling. Two years after *L'essence de la manifestation*, in *Philosophie et phénoménologie du corps* (1965), Henry does not treat intentionality as such as the enemy. The subjective body is there "a structure made of virtually compossible intentionalities" (`intentionalitaet/henry-ppc/007`), and "all intentionalities in general... know themselves originally in the immanence of their own being" without ever becoming an object to themselves (`intentionalitaet/henry-ppc/003`) — intentionality is not expelled from immanence here but relocated inside it. This is a different move from 1963's flat opposition of affectivity to intentionality: in the earlier book intentionality is what affectivity is not; in the later, certain intentionalities are themselves modes of the very immanence 1963 had reserved for affectivity alone. No unit in either record states the relation between the two positions, and nothing marks 1965 as a revision of 1963 rather than a change of vocabulary for a different problem — the constitution of the body rather than the phenomenology of feeling. The ledger simply holds both.

## What the pattern shows, and what it does not

Read serially, the corpus does not tell a story of steady correction, each thinker fixing a flaw the last one left standing. It tells a story of the same problem recurring every time someone tries to give intentionality a single ultimate ground — and of two of the tradition's most influential correctives, Heidegger's and Henry's, each seizing on a different one of Husserl's own already-produced anomalies and building a whole architecture on it, without engaging either the anomaly the other chose or each other's proposed replacement. Heidegger's transcendence-grounding radicalizes something closer to the time-analysis instability Bernet reads out of the Bernau material; Henry's affectivity-grounding radicalizes something closer to the drive-objection of Hua XLIII/2. Both anomalies are already there, unresolved, in texts from 1911 to 1918 — before either Heidegger or Henry had written a word on the subject. On the strongest reading this corpus supports, neither corrective discovers a mistake external to Husserl's project; each takes up a thread Husserl's own analysis had already pulled loose and does not let go of it, in a direction Husserl himself did not follow.

The obvious rejoinder is that this gets the direction of authority backwards — that Ideas I's architecture, reissued through 1928, is Husserl's considered position, and the 1911–1914 material is early or peripheral work he implicitly set aside in reaching it. Nothing the ledger records rules this out, but nothing supports it either. The 1911 courses making up Hua XLIII/2 and Hua XXVIII Part B are not juvenilia distant from the *Ideas* project; the ledger's dating places them in the same year, within the same broad course of research, and both feed directly into problems Ideas I and Ideas II take up under the same names — objectivating acts, founding, value-directedness. Nothing in the corpus, as extracted, records Husserl explicitly retracting either the drive-objection or the will-reversal; they are simply never mentioned again on the dated spine. Per the discipline this ledger is built on, that silence proves nothing either way. It may mean the objections were judged, silently, to be resolved; it may mean, as with the "Not covered by this ledger" list this ledger prints for every commentary, that no later text ever returned to them. Deciding between quiet supersession and mere non-return needs a text this corpus does not hold, which is why gap-016 and gap-018 remain the right form for the question rather than a sentence here.

A smaller version of the same asymmetry appears once more, off to the side of the main argument, in Kern's 1964 reconstruction of Husserl's relation to Kant. Kern reports Husserl charging Kant with constructing "hypothetical explanatory principles rather than demonstrating them in intuitive evidence" (`intentionalitaet/kern-hk/013`), against the standard of the "principle of all principles" (`intentionalitaet/kern-hk/012`) — and then reports his own acknowledgment that "Husserl's actually practiced consciousness-research corresponds to a large extent to Natorp's position", with "the constructive character of Husserl's own noetics" conceded outright and Natorp's influence extending to "Husserl's inclusion of the noema within phenomenology" and "his doctrine of the ego-pole" (`intentionalitaet/kern-hk/017`). Gap-023 already asks whether Kern, or the primary material on the noetic form-doctrine, ever closes this admission; it is not this article's question to reopen. It belongs beside the larger pattern as a case of the same shape at smaller scale: a critique of a predecessor's method turning out, on the critic's own later admission, to describe the critic as well.

None of this licenses the conclusion that intentionality has no coherent sense across the corpus, or that Husserl, Heidegger and Henry are talking past one another. What the ledger supports is narrower and, for that reason, checkable: the question of what grounds intentionality, or what it grounds, is not one this corpus shows being settled once, by anyone, and the two best-known attempts to settle it from outside Husserl's own texts each build on evidence Husserl had already generated and set aside, rather than on a defect either successor found for the first time. The sharpest single piece of evidence for this is not a doctrine but an absence: the one moment in the entire corpus where a text promises, in so many words, to show how intentionality is ultimately grounded — Heidegger's own note at *Sein und Zeit* §69(b) — points forward to a division that does not exist, and the nearest place in the finished treatise where the promise could have been kept says nothing at all.

**4. Directions opened but not taken.** Derrida's reading of *L'origine de la géométrie* — "intentionality is traditionality" (`derrida-iog/015`) — proposes a fourth, historicity-based regrounding that this article does not weigh against the three considered; it belongs to a different problem (the constitution of ideal objects across generations) and deserves its own treatment. Franck's reading of the Fifth Cartesian Meditation, which poses "the question of the being of intentionality" from the side of the alter ego and the flesh (`franck-bodies/015`), is a fifth candidate ground — embodiment and pairing — left untouched here; gap-024 already asks for the extraction that would let it be checked against Husserl's own text. Bernet's own positive proposal, an intentionality without object at work both in the relation to a pre-given world and in the subject's impressional relation to itself (`bernet-vds/035`), is a candidate synthesis of exactly the tension this article leaves open, and would be the natural next thing to test against the spine.

**5. Places the notes appear wrong or internally in tension.** The central one is the article's own thesis restated as a tension: the corpus holds two incompatible foundational moves from 1925–1963, Heidegger's transcendence and Henry's affectivity, that never engage each other's textual basis, and it holds, inside Henry's own corpus, two treatments of intentionality four years apart (1963's flat opposition to affectivity; 1965's inclusion of certain intentionalities within immanence) that no unit reconciles. A second: Ideas I's objectivating-acts architecture and Hua XLIII/2's drive-objection belong to overlapping years of Husserl's own research and are never set against each other by any commentary in this corpus, so that whether Husserl's official position is a considered rejection of the 1911 counter-instance or simply a text that never returned to it cannot be settled from what is here. A third, minor: the ledger heads the Hua XXVIII record 1908–1909 to 1914 while its own re-tagging places the units at 1911 and 1914 exactly; the header range is a remnant of the pre-tagging state and slightly misdescribes what the record now carries.
