# Heidegger on intentionality, 1921–1927: one critique, three remedies, and a ground laid in two places

Stage 5 expansion of the section "Heidegger's two stances, not one" in `synthesis/outputs/intentionalitaet-article-draft.md` (Stage 4, written 2026-09-16 from `synthesis/ledgers/intentionalitaet-ledger.md` alone). Stage 4 may write only from the ledger. This pass also reads the commentaries directly, at the sections the ledger's pass notes list as uncovered or keyword-scanned only. It keeps every quotation the Stage 4 section used, adds the context each one comes from, and follows the threads that connect them. Written 2026-09-28.

**Translations.** The commentaries render Heidegger's lectures and treatise in English, so every quotation below is already the commentary's English. The commentaries do not give Heidegger's German sentences, and this pass does not supply them from outside the corpus. That would break rule 3 of `synthesis/CLAUDE.md`, and the only thing it would add is an unverifiable retranslation. What needs translating is the German, Latin and Greek the commentators keep inside their English. Every such term or phrase is translated in square brackets directly after it, on each occurrence where the meaning matters to the argument. Where a commentary quotes a whole German sentence (as SZ does at §69(c)), the German is given as the commentary gives it, with an English translation after it. Bracketed glosses are this article's own and carry no citation. Where a commentary supplies its own gloss, that gloss is quoted instead.

**Three tiers.** As in the Stage 4 draft, three tiers are kept apart. The first is what the commentary reports Heidegger as saying. The second is what the commentator adds in their own voice. The SZ commentary marks the second tier plainly: each paragraph of report is followed by a paragraph of the commentator's gloss, usually opening "The paragraph…", "The unit…" or "The argument…". The third is what this article argues, which carries no citation. Where a quotation belongs to the second tier, the text says so.

## Why the section needed expanding

The Stage 4 section made three claims:

1. Heidegger's critique of intentionality falls into two stances. The 1923–24 stance rejects Husserl's founding of intentionality on representation. The 1925–27 stance keeps intentionality and subordinates it to transcendence.
2. The two stances "never engage" each other, and the later one never returns to the moods GA 17 had expelled from intentionality.
3. The grounding of intentionality in temporality is a promissory note the primary corpus never redeems.

All three were accurate for the ledger. The ledger's own pass notes, however, list as uncovered the GA 20 Main Part, the *Sein und Zeit* analyses of attunement and anxiety, and the §69(c) derivation of the world's transcendence. Reading those sections changes all three claims.

- **(1) is too coarse.** One critique of Husserl runs unbroken from 1921 to 1927: intentionality has been read through theoretical knowing, and the being of the intentional has been left unasked. What changes is the remedy. In 1921–22 Heidegger recasts intentionality as a formal structure of factical life. In 1923–24 he cuts it back and expels whole domains from it. From 1925 he widens it, then subordinates it to transcendence and to attunement.
- **(2) needs correcting.** No text names another, but the domains GA 17 expelled come back in 1925 and 1927. They return as the condition of intentionality, not as instances of it.
- **(3) needs narrowing.** The treatise never says in one place that intentionality is grounded in ecstatic temporality. It does derive the transcendence of the world from temporality (§69(c)), and GA 24 grounds intentionality in transcendence. The chain exists, split across two texts. What stays unredeemed is the single statement, and with it the word.

The sections below develop these corrections as four threads. Each thread follows one question through the dated sequence, and each section gives the context of every quoted claim.

## Thread 1 — Theoretical priority: what founds what

### The charge, 1923–24

GA 17 closes its long Descartes interpretation (§§ 19–47) by turning the diagnosis on Husserl. The course has argued that Descartes' knowing is driven by a "care of certainty". This is a care that seeks a secure foundation and settles there, at the *fundamentum absolutum simplex* [absolute, simple foundation]. The commentary's summary of §§ 46–47 states the concept of being that results as "being = possible domain-being for working-up by a science" (`GA17#h4-ss-46-47-descartes-and-husserl-fundamental-differences-connection-and-unitary-gr`). Heidegger's claim is that Husserl inherits this concept, and §§ 48–49 then name three *Verunstaltungen* [disfigurements] of the phenomenological findings that follow from it.

The first disfigurement is the one Stage 4 quoted. Intentionality "gets rendered 'meaning' (Meinen)" [*Meinen*: meaning, intending, opining], and "for every complex intentional nexus theoretical meaning forms the foundation" (`intentionalitaet/ga17/004`, status *criticized*). The next clause makes the target exact: the claim "that every willing, loving, hating is founded on a representing that first gives the will-able, the hate- and love-able. This is a methodological mistake — analogizing the study of emotional experiences to knowing" (`intentionalitaet/ga17/005`). In context this is the thesis the Stage 4 draft called Husserl's "official architecture": objectivating acts as the widest class and the founding stratum of every other act. Heidegger names it and does not concede it.

The same sentence in the commentary goes on to the ontological consequence, which Stage 4 did not quote. Beings are "taken in the specific theoretical apprehension, exemplified in the real being qua Ding" [*Ding*: thing], "so that nature becomes the foundation onto which culture and history are then built as values laid over natural being" (`GA17#h4-ss-48-49-husserl-s-disfiguring-verunstaltung-of-the-phenomenological-findings-th`). The founding of acts on representation and the founding of regions on nature are one mistake at two levels. The first is about consciousness and the second about beings. That doubling matters for Thread 2.

§ 50 draws the consequence for the phenomena themselves. Intentionality, "consciousness's ground-character — is cut back to its limits, the limits of its interpretive function" (`intentionalitaet/ga17/006`). Joy, terror, grief and anxiety are "phenomena missed when construed as intentionality", and "Anxiety cannot be grasped as a being-related-to-something; it is a phenomenon of Dasein itself" (`intentionalitaet/ga17/007`). Here, and only here in the corpus, the remedy for the theoretical bias is **restriction**: whole domains are placed outside intentionality's competence.

### The same charge before it had a target, 1921–22

GA 61 shows that the charge is older than GA 17 and did not begin as a charge against Husserl. The course distinguishes two senses of *Verhalten* [comportment]: "(1) to comport oneself, to behave; (2) to comport oneself toward, to stand in relation to". The second is *Bezug* [relation], and "detached from the first, it becomes objectified intentionality" (`intentionalitaet/ga61/001`). The first is *Vollzug* [enactment, performance]. The objection is structural, not doctrinal. Intentionality goes wrong when relation is lifted out of the living enactment that carries it. That is the same pattern GA 17 will call reading intentionality off "a specific theoretical comporting".

In the same course, a bracketed reflection on *Ruinanz* [ruinance, factical life's falling movement] keeps intentionality at the centre while asking where it comes from. *Praestruktion* [pre-structuring] and *Reluzenz* [relucence, life's reflecting-back on itself from its world] "are the expression of intentionality — the formal primal structure of facticity (of the sense of being of life)" (`intentionalitaet/ga61/002`). Then: "Is intentionality fallen from heaven? If it is something ultimate, in what ultimacy is it to be accepted? Not secured in a specific theoretical discoverability and experienceability" (`intentionalitaet/ga61/003`). The phrase "specific theoretical" occurs in GA 61 two years before it becomes the heading of GA 17's first disfigurement. The later charge was prepared before Heidegger aimed it at Husserl's theory of acts.

In 1921–22, then, the remedy is **recasting**. Intentionality survives, formally indicated, as "their formal fundamental structure" for "all categorial structures of facticity" (`intentionalitaet/ga61/004`). The course sets as a "destructive" historical task the question "how far intentionality has been seen, in what direction and with what means it has been reinterpreted, what has hindered its appropriation. How and how far it became graspable through Husserl via consciousness" (`intentionalitaet/ga61/005`).

### Widening, 1925

GA 20 does not repeat the word "mistake", and on that point Stage 4's contrast holds. But it goes on making the same objection by a different means. It widens intentionality until the theoretical case becomes one region inside it. Three places in the commentary show this.

- **§ 5**, on Husserl's own terminology. Heidegger "notes Husserl's terms noesis and noema, from noein (to perceive, simply grasp)" [Greek *noēsis*, *noēma*: the act of thinking and what is thought; *noein*: to perceive, apprehend], "flagging that this terminology already carries an interpretation of the directing-toward drawn from theoretical knowing and read across onto the practical sphere" (`GA20#h4-s-5-intentionality`). This is GA 17's first disfigurement, found in miniature in the terms themselves. Here it is judged "harmless for present purposes" rather than disfiguring.
- **§ 8**, on logic's place. "The so-called logical comportments of thinking, of objectifying theoretical knowing, make up only a narrow sphere within intentionality; the task-circle of logic in no way exhausts the region" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/025`).
- **§ 10**, on grasping. This is a guard Heidegger places within his reconstruction of how Husserl wins pure consciousness: "Grasping is one act-mode among others, not the predominant one; in loving I live 'in' the beloved without the beloved being an object of grasping. Directing-oneself-toward is thus wider than grasping" (`GA20#h4-s-10-the-working-out-of-the-thematic-field`; the last sentence also at `intentionalitaet/ga20/031`).

Put next to GA 17, the loving case in § 10 answers the charge. GA 17 said Husserl founds loving on "a representing that first gives … the love-able". GA 20 says loving directs itself at the beloved without making it an object of grasping at all. The answer is not that loving is not intentional, which is GA 17's restriction. It is that intentionality is not grasping, which is widening. GA 24 keeps the widened list flat: comportments comport themselves *toward*, and "mere representing relates to the represented, thinking thinks something thought, judgment determines something judged, loving relates to a loved" (`GA24#h3-b-the-being-constitution-of-perception-intentionality-and-transcendence`). In this list loving stands beside representing, not on top of it.

GA 24 adds a historical point that bears on the same question. "Scholasticism speaks of the intentio of the will, of the voluntas" [*intentio*: a stretching-toward, aim; *voluntas*: the will], "that is, it speaks of intentionality only regarding the will" (same locator). The word began, on this account, as a term for willing, not for representing. The course does not draw the implication for Husserl's order of founding. It is worth recording beside the 1914 will-passage in Hua XXVIII, where belief is founded on will (`intentionalitaet/hua28/012`), which the Stage 4 draft discusses.

### Inversion, 1927

*Sein und Zeit* §29, which no intentionalitaet record covers, completes the thread. It is also the point where the charge becomes a counter-thesis. The commentary reports that mood is "the original mode of being of Dasein, in which it is disclosed to itself before all cognition and willing and beyond their range of disclosure". It also reports that "all immanent reflection can come upon 'lived experiences' only because the Da is already disclosed in disposedness" (`SZ#h6-s-29-da-sein-as-disposedness`) [*das Da*: the "there", Dasein's openness; *Befindlichkeit*, "disposedness": how one finds oneself, attunement]. And: "mood has in each case already disclosed being-in-the-world as a whole and first makes possible a directing-oneself toward..." (same locator). The commentator's gloss spells out the consequence: "the intentional act itself presupposes the tuned openness" (same locator, commentator tier). The founding order of Husserl's architecture is not denied here. It is turned upside down. Where Husserl's architecture put representation at the base and feeling above it, §29 puts attunement at the base and every directedness, representation included, above it.

The same section names the tradition it overturns, in terms that match the Stage 4 draft's "official architecture" exactly. Since Aristotle, "the affects and feelings fall thematically under the psychic phenomena, functioning mostly as their third class beside representation and will: they sink to accompaniment-phenomena (Begleitphänomene)" (same locator) [*Begleitphänomene*: accompanying phenomena, epiphenomena]. The section also credits phenomenology with a partial correction and marks where it stops: "Scheler above all, taking up impulses from Augustine and Pascal, directed the problematic to the founding-connections between 'representing' and 'interest-taking' acts". The commentary gives the Augustinian source as "non intratur in veritatem, nisi per charitatem" [one does not enter into truth except through love] (same locator). "Yet here too the existential-ontological foundations of the act-phenomenon in general remain in the dark" (same locator).

### What the thread shows

Across 1921–1927 the objection does not change: intentionality has been read off theoretical knowing and made to found everything else. Four remedies follow one another:

- **1921–22, recasting.** Intentionality becomes the formal structure of factical life (GA 61).
- **1923–24, restriction.** Intentionality is cut back, and mood falls outside it (GA 17).
- **1925, widening.** Grasping becomes one mode among many (GA 20).
- **1927, inversion.** Attunement founds directedness (SZ §29).

Stage 4 saw two stances because the ledger held units for the second and part of the third, and none for the first or the fourth. Scheler, whom GA 20 §5 criticizes for taking intentionality as the structure of the person and SZ §29 credits with reversing the order of founding, appears on both sides of this history. That is a sign the thread is continuous.

## Thread 2 — The being of the intentional: the Cartesian inheritance

### 1923–24: the care of certainty

The second and third disfigurements in GA 17 are not about acts. They are about the field of consciousness itself. Evidence is read as "the covering (Deckung) of the meant with what is grasped at itself" [*Deckung*: coincidence, congruence], which is "a grasping- and determining-evidence of theoretical knowing, transferred by analogy". And "the eidetic reduction of pure consciousness proceeds under the leadership of ontological determinations foreign to consciousness", so that pure consciousness becomes "a possible domain of a science" (`GA17#h4-ss-48-49-husserl-s-disfiguring-verunstaltung-of-the-phenomenological-findings-th`). From this comes the question the course leaves as its task: "what is the specific being here made a domain?" (same locator).

§ 50 does not treat this blindness as an error of argument. It treats it as a movement of Dasein itself. The care of knowing is "the flight of Dasein before itself (Flucht des Daseins vor ihm selbst)" [the flight of Dasein from itself]. Heidegger names the ground-phenomenon "Verdrehung, distortion (long misnamed 'reflection')", and in its place sets the positive claim Stage 4 quoted: "The being one calls 'consciousness' is not 'something relating to something,' a subject before an object; it is a being that, in its being-placed-toward the world, co-cares for its own Dasein" (`intentionalitaet/ga17/008`; context at `GA17#h4-s-50-repetition-of-the-traversed-characters-of-the-care-of-knowing-and-the-exhib`). The commentary adds that "precisely where reflection is absent the phenomenon shows in its ownmost sense" (same locator). This connects Thread 2 to Thread 4. What reflection distorts is what attunement discloses.

### 1925: the four being-determinations

GA 20 §§ 10–13 is the fullest form of this thread, and its order follows GA 17's third disfigurement closely. § 10 reconstructs how Husserl wins pure consciousness: *epochē* [Greek: suspension, holding back], *Einklammerung* [bracketing], transcendental and eidetic reduction. It also records Heidegger's observation that Husserl "expressly invokes Descartes" at the analysis's high point (`GA20#h4-s-10-the-working-out-of-the-thematic-field`). § 11 then tests the four determinations Husserl gives the field that results:

- immanent;
- absolutely given;
- absolute in the sense of *nulla re indiget ad existendum* [it needs no thing in order to exist], which the commentary glosses as "the old definition of substance";
- pure.

The verdict is the one Stage 4 quoted. They "are in no way drawn from the being itself; insofar as they are set up as being-determinations of consciousness, they are precisely suited to bar the way to a question after the being of this being" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/016`). The reason given echoes GA 17 §§ 46–47. "The pure consciousness is won not phenomenologically, by return to the things themselves, but by return to a traditional idea of philosophy" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/017`), namely the idea of absolute science "occupying modern philosophy since Descartes" (`GA20#h4-s-11-immanent-critique-the-four-determinations-of-pure-consciousness`).

§ 12 carries the charge from the field to the acts. The reduction "gives out of hand the very ground on which alone one could ask after the being of consciousness" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/018`), because "what all constitution-analysis presupposes is that the sense of being at issue is being as objectivity for a theoretical, scientific knowing" (`GA20#h4-s-12-exhibiting-the-neglect-of-the-question-of-the-being-of-the-intentional`). This is GA 17's "being = possible domain-being for working-up by a science" in almost the same words. § 13 finds the same schema even in the personalist turn of *Ideas II* and in Scheler. Behind every title for the intentional stands the old definition *animal rationale* [the rational animal]. That definition is read as "a present-at-hand world-thing (animal) that has reason with it (rationale)" (`GA20#h4-s-13-exhibiting-the-neglect-of-the-question-of-the-sense-of-being-itself-and-of`). Phenomenology is therefore "unphenomenological — while taking itself to be phenomenological" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/020`).

Then comes the ground. GA 17 had called the blindness a flight, and GA 20 calls it a fall. "The two neglects … are not chance negligences of philosophers; in them the history of our own Dasein reveals itself", and the failure is, in the commentator's summary, "an event of Dasein's own falling" (same locator; the second phrase is commentator tier). *Verfallen* [falling] in 1925 does the work *Flucht* [flight] did in 1923–24.

Here Stage 4's key quotation gets its full context. "Not intentionality as such is metaphysically dogmatic, but what is built under the structure, or left unquestioned beneath it, through the traditional failure to ask what it is the structure of" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/010`). The sentence comes from § 5, where it closes Heidegger's critique of Brentano. §§ 11–13 are the long working-out of what exactly was "built under". So the sentence is not a softening of GA 17. It is a thesis about where the fault lies, and GA 17 §§ 48–49 had already placed it there: in the determinations imported into the field, not in the directedness found within it.

### 1927: two misinterpretations and the replacement of "subject"

GA 24 § 9(b) gives this thread its most compact form. The commentary reconstructs two "misinterpretations" of intentionality in order.

**The first misinterpretation** takes the intentional relation to be "an on-hand relation between two on-hand things" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/006`) [*vorhanden*, rendered "on hand": present-at-hand, merely occurring]. It is refuted by the hallucination. "Only because hallucinating perceiving as perception has in itself the character of being-directed-toward can the hallucinator putatively mean something" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/008`). The argument is already in GA 20 § 5, with an automobile driving over the audience's heads in place of GA 24's elephants in the lecture hall: "The deception itself, as such, is a directing-toward, even when the real object is factually absent" (`intentionalitaet/ga20/002`). Whoever describes a hallucination assumes its directedness, which is why neither course treats it as a sceptical objection.

**The second misinterpretation** arises "not, as in the first, in the character of the intentio, but in the character of the intentum" [*intentio*: the directing; *intentum*: that toward which it directs itself] (`intentionalitaet/hga24/010`). It is a "false subjectivization". Because the experiences belong to the subject, what they are directed at is taken to be subjective too, meaning sensations and images. Against it Heidegger sets "natural perception" and a perceptual illusion: in the dark, "In this perceptual illusion the person himself is given to me, and not, say, a representation of the person" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/012`). From this he draws the thesis: "it is precisely intentionality and nothing else in which transcendence consists" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/013`).

Two consequences close this thread. First, the order of determination reverses. "On the basis of the unprejudicedly seen character of intentionality and its transcendence, the subject is first to be determined in its essence" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/014`). Second, the word is replaced. "We henceforth no longer speak of a subject, of a subjective sphere, but understand the being to which intentional comportments belong as Dasein" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/015`). The same paragraph makes intentionality the mark of existence itself: "Dasein exists and is never on hand like a thing. A distinguishing character between the existing and the on hand lies precisely in intentionality" (`GA24#h3-b-the-being-constitution-of-perception-intentionality-and-transcendence`).

Like GA 17 and GA 20, GA 24 then asks where the misinterpretations come from. Its answer continues theirs. "The most dangerous and persistent prejudices regarding the understanding of intentionality are not the explicit ones in the form of philosophical theories, but the implicit ones" (same locator). These are rooted in Dasein's "tendency to apprehend all beings … initially in the sense of an on-hand thing". "Insofar as in this conception of all the given as on-hand, intentionality as a relation within on-hand things is not to be found, one must apparently assign it to the subject; if it is nothing objective, then it is something subjective" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/021`). There are three names for one mechanism: flight (1923–24), falling (1925), and the natural tendency toward the on-hand (1927). In each version, what hides the being of the intentional is a movement of the being that has intentionality.

### *Sein und Zeit* § 13 and the commentator's "out-radicalized"

In the treatise the thread reaches the knowing relation itself. "Knowing is antecedently grounded in an already-being-alongside-the-world (Schon-sein-bei-der-Welt)" (`intentionalitaet/sz/006`) [*Schon-sein-bei-der-Welt*: already-being-alongside-the-world]. And in directing itself toward and grasping, "Dasein does not first go outside of an inner sphere in which it is at first encapsulated" (`intentionalitaet/sz/007`). The commentary's gloss follows: "Intentionality, one may say, is here out-radicalized: where Husserl taught that consciousness is of its object, Heidegger replies that the 'of' was never a bridge from an inside, because there never was an inside to leave" (`intentionalitaet/sz/008`). As Stage 4 noted, this is commentator tier. The word does not occur in the treatise's § 13 as either commentary renders it. The BT commentary expounds the same section without it (recorded absence at `BT#h3-s-13-the-exemplification-of-being-in-through-a-founded-mode-world-cognition`).

GA 24 supplies the sentence the treatise leaves out. "For Dasein there is no 'outside', wherefore it is also absurd to speak of an 'inside.'" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/020`). This is the lecture-course statement of what the SZ commentator says in their own voice, and it is made about intentionality by name. So the commentator's gloss at § 13 has primary backing from the same year, though not in the treatise itself.

## Thread 3 — Transcendence and time: the ground, laid in two places

### The formula and its inner tension

GA 24 § 9(b) ends its corrections with the formula Stage 4 quoted: "Intentionality is the ratio cognoscendi of transcendence; transcendence is the ratio essendi of intentionality in its various modes" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/016`) [*ratio cognoscendi*: the ground of knowing, that through which a thing is known; *ratio essendi*: the ground of being, that through which a thing is]. The commentary calls this "the classical scholastic formula … brilliantly redeployed: we know transcendence through intentionality, but intentionality itself is on the ground of transcendence" (same locator, commentator tier).

Two sentences in the same paragraph complicate it, and Stage 4 did not set them beside it. One sentence says that "the intentional constitution of Dasein's comportments is precisely the ontological condition of the possibility of any transcendence at all" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/017`). The other says that "intentionality is the essential, even if not the most originary, structure of the subject itself" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/019`). Read together, §9(b) says intentionality is grounded in transcendence (016) and is also the condition of possibility of any transcendence (017). The last clause of 019 shows how Heidegger holds the two together: intentionality is essential but not "most originary". Something more originary lies beneath it, and 016 calls that something transcendence.

On the reception side, Bernet names the distinction that makes these sentences consistent. On his reading, Heidegger "everywhere … envisages the relation as one of founding: intentionality is an ontic comportment, an 'ontic transcendence', finding its ontological foundation in the primal transcendence (Urtranszendenz) of being-in-the-world" (`intentionalitaet/bernet-vds/008`) [*Urtranszendenz*: primal or originary transcendence]. With that distinction, 017 is about ontic transcendence: the stepping-over toward beings that intentional comportment *is*. 016 is about primal transcendence, which grounds that comportment. Bernet's summary is "The circle is not vicious but ordered: intentionality gives the access, transcendence gives the ground" (`intentionalitaet/bernet-vds/004`). This is reception tier. The GA 24 commentary does not itself draw the ontic/primal distinction at § 9(b), and the Bernet pass note assigns the chapters it reads to the 1925 course.

§ 9(c) adds a third moment, and it points toward the treatise. "To the intentionality of perception belong not only intentio and intentum but, furthermore, the understanding of the mode of being of what is intended in the intentum" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/025`, status *revised*). "Beings can only be discovered … if the being of beings is already disclosed, if I understand it" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/026`) [*Entdecktheit*, discoveredness, of beings; *Erschlossenheit*, disclosedness, of being]. "Transcending is a discovering (Entdecken). Dasein exists as discovering" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/024`). Here intentionality is carried by an understanding of being, not only by transcendence.

### *Sein und Zeit* § 69(b): the note and the chain

Stage 4 quoted the note at §69(b): "that and how the intentionality of 'consciousness' grounds in Dasein's ecstatic temporality, the following division will show" (`intentionalitaet/sz/023`). In the commentary it closes a sentence about Husserl. "The thesis that all cognition aims at 'intuition' has the temporal sense that all cognizing is making-present … Husserl uses the expression 'Gegenwärtigen' for the characterization of sense-perception" (`intentionalitaet/sz/022`) [*Gegenwärtigen*: making-present, presenting]. So the note's first concern is not transcendence at all. The intentional analysis of perception had already, and correctly, described perceiving in the vocabulary of time (*Gegenwärtigen*). The promised division would show why: the analysis described one ecstasis of temporality, the present, without seeing that it was one.

The same subsection, in units the ledger does not hold, builds the chain the note promises. First: "Transcendence does not consist in objectification; rather, objectification presupposes transcendence" (`intentionalitaet/sz/021`). Then the closing unit generalizes from science to all comportment. "If, further, thematization modifies and articulates the understanding of being, then the thematizing being — Dasein — must, insofar as it exists, already understand something like being." Finally: "And if, finally, Dasein's being grounds in temporality, then temporality must make possible being-in-the-world, and therewith Dasein's transcendence, which in its turn carries the concernful — whether theoretical or practical — being alongside innerworldly beings" (`SZ#h6-b-the-temporal-sense-of-the-modification-of-circumspect-concern-into-the-theoret`). Read link by link, the chain runs: comportment toward beings rests on transcendence, transcendence rests on the understanding of being and world, and these rest on temporality. This is GA 24 § 9(b)–(c)'s order, with temporality added beneath it.

### § 69(c): where the ground is laid without the word

The Stage 4 draft recorded that "intentionality" does not occur at § 69(c), and that holds (recorded absence at `SZ#h6-c-the-temporal-problem-of-the-transcendence-of-the-world`). But the subsection does carry out the grounding that the absence seemed to leave undone. It derives the "horizonal schemata" of the three ecstases [*Ekstase*: a standing-out; the future, having-been and present as raptures "outside itself"]. It then states the theorem in German, which the commentary quotes: "Sofern Dasein sich zeitigt, ist auch eine Welt" [Insofar as Dasein temporalizes itself, a world is too] (same locator). And it reframes the old problem this way. "The 'transcendence-problem' cannot be brought to the question: how does a subject get out to an object? … To be asked is: what makes it ontologically possible that beings can encounter innerworldly and, as encountering, be objectified? The regress to the ecstatically-horizonally founded transcendence of the world gives the answer" (same locator). The commentary also records that "the world is, as it were, already 'further outside' than any object can ever be" (same locator). This gives the post-intentional form of GA 24's "for Dasein there is no 'outside'" (`intentionalitaet/hga24/020`): the only true "outside" is the world-horizon, and it is outside every object.

Taken together, the two texts give this chain:

1. Intentionality is that "in which transcendence consists" and whose *ratio essendi* [ground of being] is transcendence (GA 24, `hga24/013`, `hga24/016`).
2. Dasein's transcendence is made possible by temporality (SZ §69(b), closing unit).
3. The transcendence of the world is "ecstatically-horizonally founded" (SZ §69(c)).

So the promise of `sz/023` is kept in substance, but by combining two texts of 1927. It is not kept in the treatise on its own terms. The word "intentionality" is absent from the step that grounds it. The note spoke of "the intentionality of 'consciousness'", and nothing at §69(c) names consciousness or its intentionality. And the GA 24 commentary stops at § 9(c). The SZ commentator says that "GA 24's horizontale Schemata [horizonal schemata] and the praesens-analysis [analysis of the present] grow from this unit" (same locator, commentator tier), but the corpus holds no commentary on those later GA 24 sections.

Stage 4 concluded that the grounding is "a promissory note the primary corpus never redeems". That should be narrowed. **The corpus holds every step of the grounding. It never holds the grounding stated as one step, and the step that would name intentionality is the step that never names it.** Gap-019 remains the right form for what is still missing, but its question can now be put more exactly (see the register below).

## Thread 4 — Mood and anxiety: what was expelled, and how it returns

### 1923–24: expulsion

Stage 4's evidence for the first of its two stances was GA 17's expulsion of mood: "Anxiety cannot be grasped as a being-related-to-something; it is a phenomenon of Dasein itself" (`intentionalitaet/ga17/007`). Stage 4 also said the later courses never return to those domains. The ledger supports that claim only because its GA 20 record leaves out the Main Part, and both of its *Sein und Zeit* records leave out the attunement analysis (gap-020).

### 1925: attunement as the apriori of uncoveredness

The GA 20 Main Part returns to exactly these phenomena, eighteen months after GA 17. § 28(a) introduces *Befindlichkeit* [disposedness, attunement; literally how one finds oneself]. In all commerce with the world Dasein is "approached and called upon" (*angegangen*) [approached, concerned, "gone at"] by the world's threateningness or unthreateningness. That is possible only because "Dasein is itself, at itself, care". The course then rules out the reading that GA 24 will call false subjectivization: "This does not mean the caring Dasein 'subjectively' apprehends the world so — a complete inversion of the facts; rather caring in-being uncovers the world in its significance". The section closes: "Disposedness is the apriori for uncoveredness and disclosedness, an equiprimordial character beside understanding" (`GA20#h4-s-28-the-phenomenon-of-uncoveredness-entdecktheit`) [*gleichursprünglich*, equiprimordial: equally original].

§ 30(b) takes up anxiety itself. Fear has "the before-which" of "something innerworldly". In anxiety "its before-which can remain indeterminate". When anxiety has passed, "we say 'it was really nothing,' and this speech hits the fact exactly … the before-which of anxiety is the nothing — nothing worldly, nothing determinate — yet … it is the world in its worldhood itself". Then: "in anxiety the before-which and the about-which coincide". And: "Anxiety is nothing other than the disposedness in uncanniness" (`GA20#h4-s-30-the-structure-of-uncanniness-unheimlichkeit`) [*Unheimlichkeit*: uncanniness, literally not-being-at-home].

Read against GA 17 § 50, the 1925 analysis does two things. It keeps GA 17's negative claim: anxiety has no "something" it relates to, since its before-which is "nothing worldly". But it keeps the grammar of directedness: a *Wovor* [before-which] and a *Worum* [about-which]. These are structurally parallel to GA 24's *Worauf* [whereupon, toward-which] of the *intentio* (`intentionalitaet/hga24/004`). Anxiety is therefore neither an intentional act with an object nor a bare, undirected state. It is a directedness whose "toward" is the world as such, and whose before-which and about-which are one, namely Dasein. GA 17's "phenomenon of Dasein itself" is now analysed, not just asserted.

### 1927: founding reversed, anxiety as the world disclosed

*Sein und Zeit* §§ 29 and 40 repeat the 1925 analysis in the treatise and make the founding explicit (Thread 1, *Inversion*). § 40: "the before-which of anxiety is being-in-the-world as such", and "The before-which of anxiety is no innerworldly being — hence it can essentially have no involvement". "That the threatening is nowhere characterizes anxiety's before-which", and "anxiety's before-which is the world as such" (`SZ#h5-s-40-the-basic-disposedness-of-anxiety-as-a-distinguished-disclosedness-of-dasei`). The commentator's gloss connects this to Thread 2 in a single line: "Anxiety is the phenomenological reduction performed by a mood" (same locator, commentator tier). GA 20 § 12 had charged the reduction with giving "out of hand the very ground" for asking about the being of consciousness (`intentionalitaet/ga20/018`). On this gloss, § 40 finds a reduction that does not do that. Beings fall away ("the innerworldly … collapses into itself", same locator), and what remains is not a pure consciousness but being-in-the-world.

The BT commentary's scan of the whole file found the word "intentional" only once, at § 10 (`intentionalitaet/bt/001`). So for that commentary the word's absence from §§ 29 and 40 is a checked fact. In the SZ commentary, § 29 contains the word once, in the commentator's own gloss quoted above, and the word does not occur in § 40.

### What the thread shows

The mood and anxiety that GA 17 expelled come back in 1925 and 1927, but under a new title. They are not brought back into intentionality. They are placed *beneath* it, as "the apriori for uncoveredness" (1925) that "first makes possible a directing-oneself toward" (1927). This answers the question Stage 4 left as gap-025 (whether the 1923–24 rejection was "superseded, softened, or left standing"), more precisely than any of its three options. The expulsion is kept, since anxiety is still not "a being-related-to-something". What changes is its meaning: being outside intentionality now means being prior to it. The 1923–24 restriction and the 1925–27 subordination turn out to be one move seen from two sides. What GA 17 took out of intentionality's reach becomes, from 1925, the ground under intentionality's feet.

That also sharpens Stage 4's Henry comparison. Henry's break with *Sein und Zeit* targets "the equiprimordiality of Befindlichkeit and Verstehen" [attunement and understanding] (`intentionalitaet/henry-em/009`). GA 20 § 28(a) gives that equiprimordiality in so many words ("an equiprimordial character beside understanding") as early as 1925. Henry's target is thus older than the treatise and lies in the Heidegger material that bears most directly on intentionality. The question gap-026 asks, whether Henry engages GA 24's transcendence-grounding, stays open. But what he rejects in Heidegger is the attunement-thread, which has now been shown to be one of the two supports of the subordination of intentionality.

## The threads together

Taken together, the four threads replace Stage 4's two stances with the following account. Each point is either cited above or marked as this article's own reading.

- **One critique throughout.** From GA 61 to SZ, the objection to Husserl's intentionality has two parts. It was read off theoretical knowing (Thread 1), and the being of the intentional was never asked about but supplied from a Cartesian ideal of science (Thread 2). Each lecture course places the source of that failure in a movement of Dasein itself: flight in 1923–24, falling in 1925, the tendency toward the on-hand in 1927.
- **Several remedies in sequence.** The remedies are recasting (1921–22), restriction (1923–24), widening (1925), subordination to transcendence (1927, GA 24) and inversion by attunement (1925–27, GA 20 Main Part and SZ §29). The restriction and the inversion are the same move seen from two sides (Thread 4).
- **The ground laid in two places.** Intentionality is grounded in transcendence in GA 24, and transcendence in ecstatic temporality in SZ §69(b)–(c). No one sentence joins them, and the sentence that would join them is the one that uses the word "intentionality" (Thread 3).
- **Two supports, not one.** In 1925–27 intentionality stands on two supports: transcendence and understanding of being (GA 24, SZ §69) on one side, and attunement (GA 20 §28, SZ §29) on the other. That is this article's reading. The corpus shows that they are equiprimordial (GA 20 §28(a)), not how they relate as grounds of intentionality. The question Henry forces is exactly whether the second support is prior to the first.

On this reading, Stage 4's closing observation about the note at §69(b) still holds in its central claim, but it should be narrower. The treatise never shows the grounding it promises. But what is missing is a statement, not a derivation, and what that statement would have had to name is the thing the treatise had already stopped naming.
