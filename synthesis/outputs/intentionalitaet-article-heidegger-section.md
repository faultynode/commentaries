# Heidegger's two stances, not one

*A readability test, not a Stage 5 output.* This is the Stage 5 sample of
the same name on the `stage5-quote-dossier` branch, rewritten so that it
can be read without the German and without the rest of the draft. It
makes no claim the sample does not make. Three things were added:

1. an English translation after every German quotation and fragment,
   taken from the published translation the archive (`faultynode/sources`)
   holds, and copied from that file rather than retyped;
2. short context notes, set off as block paragraphs beginning
   **Context.**, saying where in the lecture course or treatise a passage
   sits, what comes just before it, and what its technical terms mean;
3. a key to the texts and translations, below.

The argument, its order and its conclusions are the sample's. Where the
sample's wording has been kept, it has been kept verbatim.

*A note on citation (from the sample).* I cite by the handles the texts
supply — GA volume and section numbers, `§` numbers for *Sein und Zeit* —
and add the archive's own line pointer in the form `GA 24:950`, meaning
line 950 of this corpus's copy of that volume. These pointers are not
pagination and will drift whenever a source file is re-converted; they
are given so that every claim below can be checked against the same text
I checked it against. Where the archive holds a translation as well as
an original I name the translation at the point of use, because in an
argument about what a word was made to do, whose English it is stops
being a pedantry.

## GA 17 (1923–24): rejection

The habit of treating Heidegger's critique of intentionality as a single
position obscures a shift that the dating makes visible. In the winter
of 1923–24, lecturing on the phenomenological research he had inherited,
Heidegger names the founding-on-representation thesis and rejects it,
and he does so under a heading that files it among Husserl's
*Verunstaltungen* — his disfigurements — of findings Husserl had himself
made.

> **Context.** The course spends most of its length on Descartes and on
> what it calls the *Sorge der Gewißheit*, the care about certainty: a
> knowing that wants, before anything else, to be certain. In § 48 it
> turns this diagnosis on Husserl. The section is titled "Husserls
> Verunstaltung der phänomenologischen Befunde durch die von Descartes
> abkünftige Sorge der Gewißheit" (GA 17:3033). Dahlstrom renders it "Husserl's mangling of phenomenological finds
> through the care, derived from Descartes, about certainty"
> (GA 17 tr.:4075). So the word this article translates "disfigurements"
> appears in his English as "manglings". The section announces three of
> them: "1. in regard to intentionality itself, 2. in regard to the
> conception of evidence, and 3. in regard to the determination of
> phenomenological research as eidetic" (GA 17 tr.:4079). Subsection (a)
> is headed "Intentionalität als spezifisches theoretisches Sichverhalten"
> (GA 17:3045), in Dahlstrom's English "Intentionality as specific,
> theoretical behavior" (GA 17 tr.:4087).

The first of the three concerns intentionality directly:

> hinsichtlich der *Intentionalität*, sofern diese weniger ausdrücklich
> als unausdrücklich immer als *spezifisches theoretisches Sichverhalten*
> gefaßt wird. Charakteristisch ist, daß Intentionalität in der
> Übersetzung meist mit Meinen wiedergegeben wird, daß man vom
> willentlichen, liebenden, hassenden Meinen usf. spricht.
> (GA 17 § 48a, GA 17:3047)

Dahlstrom's English has it: "with respect to intentionality insofar as
this is always construed (less explicitly than implicitly) as specific
theoretical behavior. Characteristically, intentionality is translated
for the most part as meaning, intending something [*Meinen*]; one speaks
of willing, loving, hating, and so forth as meaning something" (GA 17
tr.:4091). The charge is about a translation habit, and Heidegger treats
the habit as load-bearing: once directedness is rendered *Meinen*, the
whole field has been tilted toward knowing before any analysis starts.

> **Context.** *Meinen* is the everyday German verb for "to mean" or "to
> have in mind". Husserl uses it, in the *Logical Investigations*, for the
> act of meaning-intending, and so it carries the sense of an act aimed at
> an object to be known. *Sichverhalten* is "comporting oneself"; Dahlstrom
> renders it "behavior". The "translation" in question is the rendering
> of the Latinate *Intentionalität* by the German *Meinen*.

What follows is the thesis itself, and here the primary text is wider
than the commentary reports it:

> daß in der Tat bewußt behauptet wird, daß für jeden intentionalen
> Zusammenhang gewickelter Art das *theoretische Meinen* das *Fundament*
> bildet, daß jedes Urteil, jedes Wollen, jedes Lieben auf ein
> *Vorstellen* fundiert ist, das überhaupt das Wollbare, das Haß- und
> Liebbare vorgibt. […] Es ist ein methodisches Mißverständnis, die
> Untersuchung der emotionalen Erlebnisse einfach mit dem Erkennen zu
> analogisieren. (GA 17 § 48a, GA 17:3053)

In Dahlstrom's English: "it is expressly claimed that for every
intentional context of a complicated sort, theoretically meaning
something forms the foundation, that each judgment, each instance of
wanting, each instance of loving is founded upon a presenting [Vorstellen]
that provides in advance what can be wanted, what is detestable and
loveable. […] It is a methodical misunderstanding to make the
investigation of emotional experiences simply analogous to knowing"
(GA 17 tr.:4091).

> **Context.** The ellipsis omits one sentence, which gives the reason
> for the transformation: "the prevailing study of intentionality is
> itself oriented to the intentional in knowing" (GA 17 tr.:4091).
> *Vorstellen* is "representing" or "presenting": the act that puts an
> object before the mind. The thesis Heidegger attributes to Husserl is
> the one the full draft calls Husserl's "official architecture": every
> non-objectivating act (a wish, a valuing, a loving) rests on an
> objectivating act that first gives it its object.

*Jedes Urteil, jedes Wollen, jedes Lieben* [each judgment, each instance
of wanting, each instance of loving]. The commentary this article's
ledger rests on renders the list as "every willing, loving, hating," and
the difference is not decorative. Judgment is the paradigm objectivating
act. To say that willing and loving are founded on representing is to
describe a hierarchy among act-kinds; to say that judgment is too is to
say that the founding thesis reaches the very acts it was built to
privilege. The draft's argument does not need the wider version, but it
is strengthened by it, and a reader working from the commentary alone
would not know it was there.

GA 17 then goes further than rejection. It withdraws whole domains from
intentionality's competence:

> Zugleich ist dieses Phänomen der strukturmäßige Boden, auf dem solche
> Phänomene wie Freude, Schrecken, Trauer, Angst explizierbar werden –
> Phänomene, die übersehen werden, wenn sie als Intentionalität bestimmt
> sind. Das Phänomen der Angst kann ich nicht fassen als ein
> Bezogensein-auf-etwas, sondern es ist ein Phänomen des Daseins selbst.
> (GA 17 § 50, GA 17:3195)

In Dahlstrom's English: "At the same time this phenomenon is the
structural ground on which such phenomena as joy, terror, sadness,
anxiety can be explicated—phenomena that are overlooked if they are
determined as intentionality. I cannot grasp the phenomenon of anxiety as
a manner of being-related-to-something; it is instead a phenomenon of
existence itself" (GA 17 tr.:4173).

> **Context.** "This phenomenon" is what § 50 calls *die Verdrehung*,
> in Dahlstrom's English "the distorting". The course introduces it as
> "a basic phenomenon of existence" revealed in Dasein's
> "being-on-the-run from [taking flight in the face of: Auf-der-Flucht-sein]
> itself" (GA 17 tr.:4165), and adds that it is "a basic phenomenon that
> has long been determined as reflection" (GA 17 tr.:4173). The sentence
> just before the quoted one gives distorting its work in the argument:
> from its vantage point "the basic character of consciousness, the
> intentionality, is cut down to size and led back to its limits, to the
> limits of its interpretative function" (GA 17 tr.:4173). Note that
> Dahlstrom renders *Dasein* as "existence" here.

Two lines earlier the same passage has already refused the subject-object
form outright: the being one means to capture with *Bewußtsein*
[consciousness] is not characterized by "»etwas verhält sich zu etwas«"
[“something relates to something”] but is one that "im Gestelltsein zur
Welt sein eigenes Dasein mitbesorgt" (GA 17:3193). Dahlstrom's full
sentence: "Rather, this phenomenon says that the entity that one intends
to capture with “consciousness” is the sort of thing that, in being
positioned towards the world, is also concerned with its own existence"
(GA 17 tr.:4171). This is the sharpest statement available of an
objection Husserl's own 1911 drive-analysis had half-raised against
itself — made now from outside, as a rejection of the architecture rather
than a question left standing inside it.

> **Context.** The 1911 drive-analysis is the passage from Hua XLIII/2,
> Part III, discussed earlier in the full draft.
> There Husserl objects to his own layering of a non-intentional feeling
> beneath an intentional apprehension, because a drive already has a
> "directedness-toward".

## GA 20 (1925) and GA 24 (1927): affirmation, then subordination

Two years on, and again in 1927, Heidegger does not repeat the
rejection. He affirms the descriptive apparatus at length.

> **Context.** GA 20 is the Marburg course of summer 1925. Its
> Preparatory Part is a long reading of Husserl. §§ 5–7 present
> phenomenology's three "fundamental discoveries": intentionality,
> categorial intuition and the original sense of the apriori. §§ 10–13
> then turn to an "immanent critique" of how Husserl determined the field
> those discoveries open. Kisiel's headings are "§5. Intentionality",
> "§6. Categorial intuition" and "§11. Immanent critique of
> phenomenological research: critical discussion of the four
> determinations of pure consciousness" (GA 20 tr.:650, 776, 1190).

GA 20 keeps *intentio* and *intentum* — "eine eigentümliche
Zugehörigkeit zwischen der Weise des Intendiertseins, der *Intentio*, und
dem *Intentum*" (GA 20 § 5, GA 20:1086), in Kisiel's English "an inherent
affinity between the way something is intended, the *intentio*, and the
*intentum*" (GA 20 tr.:768), where *intentum* means "nicht das
Wahrgenommene als Seiendes, sondern das Seiende im Wie seines Wahrgenommen
seins" ["not the perceived as an entity, but the entity in the how of its
being-perceived", GA 20 tr.:768].

> **Context.** *Intentio* and *intentum* are Latin: the directing and
> that toward which it is directed. The point of the second quotation is
> that the *intentum* is not simply the thing perceived. It is the thing
> as perceived, in the manner of its being perceived. Kisiel's "affinity"
> renders *Zugehörigkeit*, literally "belonging", and he adds "inherent"
> for *eigentümlich* ("peculiar").

It keeps fulfilment: "Jede Intention hat in sich eine Tendenz zur
Erfüllung, und jede hat je ihre bestimmt eigene Weise der
Erfüllungsmöglichkeit, Wahrnehmung überhaupt nur durch Wahrnehmung;
Erinnerung nie durch Erwartung" (GA 20:1069). Kisiel: "Every intention
has within it a tendency toward fulfillment and its specifically proper
way of possible fulfillment: perception in general only through
perception; remembrance never through expectation" (GA 20 tr.:764).

And it ties categorial intuition to intentionality so tightly that the
two cannot be separated: "Man weiß nicht, was man tut, wenn man sich für
diese richtige Auffassung des Kategorialen entscheidet und zugleich die
Intentionalität als mythischen Begriff glaubt abweisen zu können; beides
ist ein und dasselbe" (GA 20 § 6, GA 20:1296). Kisiel: "We do not know
what we are doing when we opt for the correct conception of the
categorial and at the same time think we can dismiss intentionality as a
mythical concept. The two are one and the same" (GA 20 tr.:864).

> **Context.** Categorial intuition is Husserl's claim, in the Sixth
> Logical Investigation, that forms such as "is", "and" or "all" can be
> given in intuition and are not only thought.

What GA 20 attacks is a pair of misreadings, and it attacks them on
phenomenological rather than doctrinal grounds. The image-theory of
perception is to be rejected because "sie entspricht nicht dem schlichten
phänomenologischen Befund" (GA 20:1039), in Kisiel's fuller sentence "we
must keep in mind the real reason for rejecting this transposition: it
does not correspond to the simple phenomenological findings" (GA 20
tr.:754).

> **Context.** The image-theory holds that in perceiving a house I
> first perceive an inner image and take it as a picture of an outer
> house. Heidegger has just analysed picture-consciousness proper, as
> when he looks at "a picture postcard of the Weidenhauser bridge" and
> sees the bridge through it (GA 20 tr.:754). The "transposition" is
> "this transposition of the consciousness of a picture, which is
> constituted in a totally different way, onto the simple apprehension
> of an object" (GA 20 tr.:754).

GA 20 also attacks Husserl's four being-determinations of pure
consciousness, which, "sofern sie als Seinsbestimmungen des Bewußtseins
herausgestellt sind, sind sie geradezu geeignet, den Weg zu einer Frage
nach dem Sein dieses Seienden […] zu verlegen" (GA 20 § 11, GA 20:2224).
Kisiel gives the whole sentence, which also names the four: "All four
determinations of the being of the phenomenological region: immanent
being, absolute being in the sense of absolute givenness, absolute being
in the sense of the apriori in constitution, and pure being, are in no
way drawn from the entity itself. Rather, to the extent that they are
brought out as determinations of the being of consciousness, they
immediately qualify as obstacles in the path of asking about the being
of this entity and so also about the clearer elaboration of this entity
itself" (GA 20 tr.:1236).

The verdict is explicit and carefully placed: "Nicht die Intentionalität
als solche ist metaphysisch dogmatisch, sondern was der Struktur
unterbaut ist, bzw. unterbaut bleibt aufgrund traditionellen
Nichtbefragens dessen, wovon sie angeblich Struktur ist" (GA 20 § 5, GA
20:1103). Kisiel: "It is not intentionality as such that is
metaphysically dogmatic but what is built under its structure, or is
left at this level because of a traditional tendency not to question
that of which it is presumably the structure" (GA 20 tr.:774). Not the
structure: what was built underneath it, and left unasked about.

> **Context.** The sentence stands near the close of § 5, six sections
> before the critique of the four determinations in § 11. It comes just
> after Heidegger's contrast with Brentano, who "saw the *intentio,
> noesis*, and the diversity of its modes, but not the *noema*, the
> *intentum*" (GA 20 tr.:772). It is followed by the rule for how
> intentionality should first be approached: "not to be concerned with
> interpretations but only to keep strictly to that which shows itself,
> regardless of how meager it may be" (GA 20 tr.:774).

The *Grundprobleme* of 1927 sharpen this affirming-and-subordinating
move into a formula.

> **Context.** The passages below come from GA 24 § 9(b). Hofstadter
> heads the subsection "The ontological constitution of perception.
> Intentionality and transcendence" (GA 24 tr.:861). The course reaches
> it by way of Kant's thesis that being is not a real predicate, and asks
> what perception is. It then clears away two misinterpretations of
> intentionality before stating what intentionality is. Hofstadter
> renders *vorhanden* (present-at-hand, merely occurring) as "extant".

Against the first misinterpretation — "Die Beziehung der Wahrnehmung ist
eine vorhandene Beziehung zwischen zwei Vorhandenen" (GA 24 § 9(b), GA
24:809), in Hofstadter's English "The relation of perception is an
extant relation between two extant entities" (GA 24 tr.:881) — and the
second, whose source lies "nicht wie bei der erstgenannten im Charakter
der *intentio*, sondern als der *intentum*" (GA 24:912) ["This time it
does not lie in the character of the intentio, as with the first
misinterpretation, but in that of the intentum, that toward which the
comportment—in our case perception—directs itself", GA 24 tr.:895],
Heidegger holds that the question of how an immanent experience
acquires transcendent validity need not be asked at all, "sondern es gilt
zu sehen, daß die Intentionalität es gerade ist und nichts anderes,
worin die Transzendenz besteht" (GA 24:935). Hofstadter: "rather, what
has to be seen is that it is precisely intentionality and nothing else in
which *transcendence* consists" (GA 24 tr.:901).

> **Context.** Put plainly, the first misinterpretation takes the
> perceiving and the perceived for two things that happen to be on hand,
> joined by a relation that is also on hand. It is answered in the text by
> the case of hallucination, which is directed at something although
> nothing is there. The second misinterpretation begins from the right
> point, that intentionality belongs to the subject's experiences. It then
> concludes wrongly that what those experiences are directed at must also
> be subjective: sensations, images, representations.

The order of determination is then reversed — conversely, Heidegger
says, it is "des unvoreingenommen gesehenen Charakters der
Intentionalität und ihrer Transzendenz allererst das Subjekt in seinem
Wesen bestimmt" (GA 24:940; the governing *aufgrund* [on the basis of]
is broken across a page-break in the archive's copy, ending line 937).
Hofstadter: "rather, just the reverse, the subject is first of all
determined in its essential nature only on the basis of an unbiased view
of the character of intentionality and its transcendence" (GA 24
tr.:901). And so "sprechen wir künftig nicht mehr von einem Subjekt, von
einer subjektiven Sphäre, sondern wir verstehen das Seiende, dem die
intentionalen Verhaltungen angehören, als Dasein" (GA 24:940), in
Hofstadter's English "we shall in the future no longer speak of a
subject, of a subjective sphere, but shall understand the being to whom
intentional comportments belong as *Dasein*" (GA 24 tr.:901).

Then the formula, which Hofstadter's revised English renders without
loss: "Intentionality is the *ratio cognoscendi* of transcendence.
Transcendence is the *ratio essendi* of intentionality in its diverse
modes" (GA 24:905; German at GA 24:950).

> **Context.** The German at GA 24:950 reads "Die Intentionalität ist die
> ratio cognoscendi der Transzendenz. Diese ist die ratio essendi der
> Intentionalität in ihren verschiedenen Weisen." *Ratio cognoscendi* and
> *ratio essendi* are scholastic Latin: the ground of knowing (that
> through which something is known) and the ground of being (that through
> which something is). Heidegger left the Latin untranslated, and so did
> Hofstadter; that is the sense in which the rendering loses nothing.
> Read with the terms spelled out, the formula says: through
> intentionality we come to know transcendence, and transcendence is what
> makes intentionality possible.

## Two stances, unreconciled

So the corpus holds two Heideggerian stances, and nothing in it
reconciles them. GA 17 does not subordinate the founding-on-representation
thesis; it calls it a *methodisches Mißverständnis* [methodical
misunderstanding] and expels mood and anxiety from intentionality's
reach. GA 20 and GA 24 subordinate intentionality to a more basic
transcendence without rejecting it — and without returning to the
domains GA 17 had expelled. Whether the 1923–24 rejection was superseded
by the 1925–27 subordination, softened into it, or simply left standing
beside it is not a question these texts answer; no passage on either
side names the other.

## *Sein und Zeit* (1927): subordination, and a promise

*Sein und Zeit* inherits the 1927 stance rather than the 1923 one.

> **Context.** The passages come from § 13, "Die Exemplifizierung des
> In-Seins an einem fundierten Modus. Das Welterkennen" (SZ:1027). Welch
> renders the title "Exemplification of being-in by way of a founded mode:
> knowing the world" (SZ tr.:1041). The section treats knowing as one way
> of being-in-the-world, and a founded one, not as the relation on which
> the world is first reached.

Its language is subordination throughout: "das Erkennen selbst
vorgängig gründet in einem Schon-sein-bei-der-Welt, welches das Sein von
Dasein wesenhaft konstituiert" (§ 13, SZ:1040), which Welch renders
"cognition is itself already functionally grounded in an
already-being-near-the-world, and the being of being-there is
essentially constituted *as* this being-near" (SZ tr.:1049). And in
directing itself toward and grasping, Dasein "geht […] nicht etwa erst
aus seiner Innensphäre hinaus, in die es zunächst verkapselt ist"
(SZ:1042). Welch: "In directing itself toward something and getting hold
of it, being-there does not first move out of its inner sphere, one
initially encapsulating it; rather, in keeping with its primary manner
of being, it is always already “outside,” near something arising for
encounter within some world already uncovered" (SZ tr.:1051).

It is worth marking what the archive's English does here: it renders
*Schon-sein-bei-der-Welt* as "already-being-near-the-world" (SZ:1049),
where the commentary says "already-being-alongside-the-world." Welch
also renders *Dasein* as "being-there" throughout.

The formulation the draft found most quotable on this point — that
intentionality is here *out-radicalized*, because the "of" was never a
bridge from an inside, since there never was an inside to leave — is not
in the treatise at all. It is the commentator's gloss; a second
commentary on the same section offers no equivalent. It is a good
reading, and it stays a reading.

And then the promise. A footnote to § 69(b) — a note, not body text,
sitting beside a reference to the sixth *Logical Investigation* —
commits the book to the payoff:

> Daß und wie die Intentionalität des »Bewußtseins« in der ekstatischen
> Zeitlichkeit des Daseins *gründet*, wird der folgende Abschnitt
> zeigen. (SZ:5496)

Welch: "How the intentionality of “consciousness” is *grounded* in the
ecstatic temporality of being-there will be shown in the following
Division" (SZ tr.:5334). Welch's English drops the German's "Daß und"
("That and how").

> **Context.** § 69 is titled "The temporality of being-in-world, and
> the problem of the transcendence of the world" (SZ tr.:5086). Its
> subsection (b), in Welch's English "The temporal meaning of the
> modification of circumspect taking-care into the theoretical discovery
> of beings on hand in the world" (SZ tr.:5120), asks how theoretical
> knowing arises out of everyday dealings with things. The footnote
> begins with the thesis that "all cognition is a finding-present". It
> notes that Husserl used *Gegenwärtigen* (Welch: "finding-present";
> more usually "making-present") for sense perception, and that "the
> *intentional* analysis of perception and intuition in general had to
> suggest this “temporal” characterization of the phenomenon" (SZ
> tr.:5334). The promise comes next. "Ecstatic temporality" is the
> treatise's name for time as the unity of future, having-been and
> present, each a "standing-out" (Greek *ekstasis*) of Dasein beyond
> itself.

That division was never published. Welch's note at the same place says
so too: "This is the “Division Three” that was never published" (SZ
tr.:5334). The nearest place in the finished treatise where the promise
could have been kept, § 69(c), on the temporal problem of the
transcendence of the world, does not contain the word. GA 24 grounds
intentionality in transcendence; nothing in it identifies that
transcendence with ecstatic temporality. So the most quotable claim in
the reception about what supersedes Husserl in Heidegger — that
intentionality is finally grounded in temporality — is, on the evidence
this corpus holds, a promissory note issued in a footnote and never
redeemed.
