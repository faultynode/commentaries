# Intentionality: a development narrative

Stage 4 output type 2, over `synthesis/ledgers/intentionalitaet-ledger.md`. Written from that ledger and from the inheritance records in `synthesis/inheritance/`, and from nothing else: not the commentaries, not the primary texts, and not `articles/heidegger-husserl-intentionality-article.md`, which this pass did not open. That last exclusion is the point of the run. The article covers this ground and was written by hand before the pipeline existed; what follows is what the ledger alone supports, so that the two can be set side by side.

## How to read the citations

Every textual assertion below carries a ledger unit id of the form `intentionalitaet/<record>/<NNN>`. It resolves in the ledger to a locator (`slug#section-id`), a quote the validator has checked against the commentary, and the primary-text citation the commentary itself supplies. Where a sentence names a work and a section (Ideas I § 84, GA 20 § 5), that citation is the commentary's, carried through the unit; this pass adds none. Inheritance engagements are cited as `inh/<record>/<NNN>` and resolve in `synthesis/inheritance/`. Recorded absences are cited by their locator.

Three tiers stay apart. Husserl and Heidegger reach this document only through a commentary, and every quotation in the ledger is a quotation of that commentary, in English. When a sentence below says that Husserl holds something, read: the commentary records Husserl as holding it. When a sentence quotes, it quotes the commentator. What this pass argues on its own account carries no locator; the absence of one is the mark, and it borrows the authority of neither tier above it.

## Scope

The ledger holds 578 units and three recorded absences from 29 commentaries. Twenty-one records lie on the dated primary spine, six in the reception layer, two in the undated bucket. Every one of the 29 passes is partial, and each closes with a note naming what it left out. Those notes bound everything said here.

Eighteen further commentaries carry the theme's terms and have never been scanned. The ones that bear on what follows: Marion's *Réduction et donation* (43 hits), GA 19 (16), Hua XXIII on phantasy and image-consciousness (15), Derrida's *La voix et le phénomène* (14), Hua XIII (14), GA 17 (10), GA 61 (8), Hua XXVI (7), and the second commentary on *Sein und Zeit* (1 hit). Nothing below is a claim about those files. The Heidegger side of this ledger therefore begins in 1925: the three earlier courses in the corpus, GA 61, GA 17 and GA 19, are unread for this theme, and no sentence here reaches behind GA 20.

Two facts about the register shape the narrative more than any doctrine does. First, no unit in any of the 29 records carries a `stratum` tag. The chronology behind the ledger distinguishes six layers in Ideas II (1912 to 1928), seven dated texts in Hua XXXIX (1920 to 1934), three deliveries of the passive-synthesis lectures, two editions of the Fifth Investigation, and the 1929 text of *Formal and Transcendental Logic* from Husserl's 1937 marginalia. The units sit under those date ranges without being placed within them. Where a unit's primary citation names a text the chronology dates (Hua XXXIX, Text Nr. 15), this pass says so and marks the date as taken from `chronology.json`, not from the unit. Second, the Sixth Investigation enters the spine at 1921, because its commentary reads the second-edition text and the chronology carries no first-edition stratum for it, whereas the Fifth Investigation spans 1901 to 1913. The ledger therefore orders the Sixth Investigation after the 1919 *Natur und Geist* lectures. That is a fact about the register, not about Husserl, and the narrative treats it as one (gap-009).

## Brentano's word, and what was kept of it

The ledger's earliest units take the word over as a class-mark. The Fifth Investigation, § 9, finds in Brentano's psychic phenomena "a sharply delimited class of experiences" whose value is independent of his classificatory ends (`intentionalitaet/hua19-1/001`, LU V § 9), and § 10 picks intentionality out of Brentano's determinations as the one that directly exhibits the essence of acts and "presses itself upon us unmistakably in arbitrary examples": in perception something is perceived, in love something loved (`hua19-1/002`, LU V § 10). What is kept is the class. What is dropped, already in these sections, is Brentano's title for its members. "Phenomenon" is rejected because it imputes that every intentional experience is itself an object of other intentional experiences (`hua19-1/006`, § 11), and "act" is retained only with the thought of activity "unconditionally excluded" (`hua19-1/014`, § 13).

That appraisal does not move on the Husserl side of the spine. Ideas III § 10 credits Brentano with epoch-making merits for the very enabling of phenomenology and denies that he saw the essence of intentional analysis (`hua5/009`; `inh/hua5/005`, `inh/hua5/006`). Ideas I § 85 calls his psychic/physical distinction path-breaking and says it did not reach the phenomenological ground (`hua3-p34/006`; `inh/hua3-i34/004`). The 1925 lectures on phenomenological psychology crown him with the discovery and say he stayed a naturalist who had not yet seen intentional implication (`hua9/001`, `hua9/005`; `inh/hua9/002`, `inh/hua9/005`, `inh/hua9/006`). In 1923–24, when Husserl introduces "the mere intentional object, the immanent one", he adds "as Brentano too, in connection with Scholasticism, introduces it" (`hua7-i/003`, Hua VII, Fifteenth Lecture). Discovery credited, analysis withheld: five records, twenty-four years, one verdict.

Heidegger's 1925 verdict on Brentano differs in content, and the ledger places it in the same year as Husserl's. GA 20 § 5 says Brentano saw the *intentio* and the variety of its modes but not the *intentum*, wavering over the "intentional object" between the being itself and the how of its being-grasped, so that intentionality as a structural whole was never lifted out and was identified with the psychic (`ga20/009`; `inh/ga20/001`). Husserl's complaint is method: no intentional analysis. Heidegger's is a missing half of the structure. The GA 20 commentary also records that it was through Brentano that Husserl learned to see intentionality (`inh/ga20/001`), and GA 24 records the word as Scholastic, known there only of the will (`hga24/001`; `inh/hga24/001`). None of this reaches behind 1901 on Husserl's side. Husserl's own dating of the breakthrough to about 1898 is a retrospection the Krisis commentary reports (`krisis/009`, Crisis § 46), and no unit sits at that date (gap-014).

## The rejected immanent object and the noema

Within the Fifth Investigation the ledger records the doctrine in two moves. § 11 wards off the idiom of "immanent objectivity": it invites two misreadings, a real process between consciousness and thing, and a relation between two things both really in consciousness (`hua19-1/007`). Against both, only one thing is present, the intentional experience whose descriptive character is the intention; if it is present, the relation to an object is *eo ipso* accomplished, "for the one and the other say exactly the same" (`hua19-1/008`). The immanent object is neither in the experience nor *extra mentem*; it is "not at all" (`hua19-1/009`). Existence changes nothing phenomenologically: Jupiter is presented no differently from Bismarck (`hua19-1/010`). § 20 states the corollary at its sharpest. "For the really phenomenological consideration the objectivity itself is nothing; it is, generally speaking, transcendent to the act", and every difference in the manner of objective relation is a descriptive difference of the experience (`hua19-1/029`). The Appendix concedes the analytic identity of the intentional and the real object (`hua19-1/035`), defines "merely intentional" as the case where the intention exists and the object does not (`hua19-1/036`), and reserves a distinction between the object *simpliciter* and the object as intended, "to which its own analyses and descriptions belong" (`hua19-1/037`).

Between § 20's "the objectivity itself is nothing" and the Appendix's reservation the ledger holds one dated pivot. A second-edition footnote to § 16 records that the description of the intentional objectivity as such "represents another direction of purely intuitive, adequate description, which must also be called phenomenological" (`hua19-1/022`). The unit's own claim dates the footnote to the second edition, so the ledger places this admission in 1913, the year Ideas I announces intentionality as the principal theme (`hua3-p34/001`, Ideas I § 84) and coins a term for what the footnote points at. Ideas I § 88 names the "perceived as such" the perception-noema, correlative to the noesis (`hua3-p34/010`); § 89 says the tree can burn and the perception-sense cannot (`hua3-p34/011`); § 90 rejects the immanent object by the same regress the Investigations used (`hua3-p34/012`); §§ 97–98 give the noema a mode of being that belongs to the experience without being *reell* in it, an *esse* that is *percipi* but not Berkeley's (`hua3-p34/017`, `hua3-p34/018`). § 94 then turns on the earlier book. The "intentional essence" and "epistemic essence" of the Investigations were read one-sidedly as noetic; the pure-logical concept of the proposition is noematic (`hua3-p34/016`; the unit's primary citation is LU V § 21). That is the ledger's one explicitly recorded self-correction of the Fifth Investigation by Ideas I, and its status column reads "revised".

The footnote and the correction do not make the earlier doctrine a draft of the later one. What § 11 rejected under the name "immanent object" is still rejected in 1913 under the same name (`hua3-p34/012`). What changes is what is put in its place. The Investigations put nothing: the intention is present, and the object is "nothing" for phenomenology. Ideas I puts an irreal correlate with its own descriptive science. Ideas III, which the ledger dates a year earlier (1912), already says that after bracketing transcendent being what remains is "consciousness itself" and, "in place of transcendent being, the 'being-meant' of what is transcendent", the noemata (`hua5/010`, Ideas III § 13), and adds that the noema of a thing-intuition is not and does not contain the essence of the thing (`hua5/012`, § 16). On the ledger's spine, then, Ideas III's noema precedes Ideas I's introduction of the term by a year. That ordering is the register's, and the units carry no stratum that would confirm or upset it.

The word "immanent" then comes back. In 1923–24 Husserl describes the object of the intentional relation as "the mere intentional object, the immanent one" (`hua7-i/003`), and in the next unit denies that this bearing-in-itself can be a real (*reell*) immanence, "as though the immanent intentional object dwelt in its consciousness as a real piece" (`hua7-i/006`). The Fifth Investigation had said the immanent object "is thus in truth not immanent or mental at all" (`hua19-1/009`). Kern's 1964 reconstruction supplies the reception-side gloss: Husserl rejects the "real" immanence-standpoint as opposed to "intentional" immanence (`kern-hk/018`). Whether Husserl marks this reuse of the word anywhere between 1913 and 1923 the ledger cannot say. The nearest dated candidate, the commentary on the 1910 *Basic Problems*, has fourteen term hits and no record (gap-017).

Reception splits over what the noema was. Derrida (1961) reads its non-reellity as what "permits the repetition of sense as the 'same'" and makes idealization possible (`derrida-iog/001`), and calls the ideality of noematic sense "nothing but the objectivity of the object" (`derrida-iog/014`). Bernet (1994) finds three concepts of the noema crossing in Ideas I, the punctual appearance, the ideal sense, and the constituted object (`bernet-vds/018`); an amphibolic X that means both the identity-pole of the sense and the actual state of affairs (`bernet-vds/022`); and a famous passage that omits to say the constituted tree "can perfectly well burn" (`bernet-vds/017`). The Krisis commentary states the primary-side position in its late form: consciousness does not contain objects but intends them, and the object "in" consciousness is the object as meant, "not a copy or image of a reality lying elsewhere" (`krisis/022`, Crisis § 70).

## The layer beneath the acts

The Fifth Investigation excludes sensation from intentionality twice. Not all experiences are intentional, as the sensations show (`hua19-1/005`), and the truly immanent contents build up the act and support the intention but are not intended (`hua19-1/011`). § 14 gives the mechanism: apperception is the surplus over the raw existence of the sensation, the act-character that "animates (beseelt) the sensation" (`hua19-1/017`). *Thing and Space* (1907) draws the real/intentional content line for perception (`husserl-thing-and-space/002`, § 6). Ideas I § 85 repeats the exclusion in the hyle/morphe vocabulary: the sensuous data "have, in themselves, nothing of intentionality" and enter an intentional experience only through an animating layer (`hua3-p34/004`, `hua3-p34/005`).

The 1911 studies complicate the picture without abandoning it. Hua XLIII/2 places "the deepest-lying intentionality" in the inner consciousness that constitutes unity in the immanent time-flux, and denies intentionality to the sensible sensation as constituted unity (`hua43-2/009`, Part III § 4). Receptivity divides into a layer of mere affections that "contain nothing of function", in which the primary contents constitute themselves as immanent temporalities, the constituting taking place "through intentionality yet not through acts proper", and the functions of sensible apprehension (`hua43-2/005`, § 1). By 1911, then, the bottom is intentional, but not by acts. The same record adds an objection it does not resolve. Extended to the drive-sphere the articulation fails, because a drive toward the continuation of an agreeable tone already has a directedness that fulfils itself, so there seems to be no layer of non-intentional drive-content (`hua43-2/013`; status "criticized").

Ideas I carries two recorded absences here, the only checked negatives on the whole primary spine. §§ 82–83 work out the threefold time-horizon of the stream and the givenness-modes of viewed and unviewed experiences and do not thematize intentionality; the absence notes say the theme is deferred to § 84 (`husserliana-3-ideas-i-parts-3-4-commentary#h3-s-82-continuation-the-threefold-experience-horizon-at-the-same-time-as-the-horiz`, `husserliana-3-ideas-i-parts-3-4-commentary#h3-s-83-grasping-the-unitary-experience-stream-as-an-idea`). Read beside Hua XLIII/2 this is a datable separation. In 1911 the deepest intentionality is time-constituting; in the 1913 book the time-structure of the stream is analysed before, and apart from, the chapter on intentionality.

The Bernau record is the one place where the ledger puts the question to the bottom layer itself, and every one of its fourteen units comes from the editors' introduction, not from the texts; the pass note says so. As the commentary reports the editors, the schema of intentional apprehension of a sensation-content, borrowed from outer perception, posed problems when applied to time-consciousness (`hua33/002`); a terminological change signals that Husserl "no longer took for granted" that the primal process has the form of an objectifying intentional consciousness (`hua33/004`, status "revised"); the primal process may be pre-intentional, gaining constitutive significance only through subsequent egoic reflection (`hua33/007`); and if that were so, its intentional character and independence would become questionable (`hua33/010`). The editors are recorded as saying the manuscripts reached no definitive determination and the doubts pressed again in the late C-texts (`hua33/006`). Against this the same record introduces the doctrine that absolute retentional consciousness harbours a double intentionality, transverse toward immanent time-objects and longitudinal as self-reference of the flow (`hua33/011`, `hua33/012`). Bernet takes the question to its end: absolute consciousness is "no longer, properly speaking, an intentional consciousness" but a passive consciousness resulting from affection (`bernet-vds/049`), and since there is no longitudinal without transverse intentionality, "absolute consciousness is not absolute", a consequence Husserl refused to face (`bernet-vds/053`). Between 1911's "deepest-lying intentionality" and the editors' "pre-intentional primal process" the ledger records a reversal of direction. Whether Husserl's own texts decide it is exactly what the six unextracted text groups would show (gap-011).

Around 1920 the sensation-datum changes status once more. Hua XXXIX, Text Nr. 2 (ca. 1920 by the chronology) says that reflection on a sensation-datum shows it constituted as an enduring unity in a synthesis and "itself an intentional objectuality, an immanent one", functioning for a transcendent apperception (`hua39/008`), and generalizes: everything that can be attended to is a unity of constituting manifolds, so objectualities function step-wise as "relative hyle" for other objectualities (`hua39/009`). Supplement I calls the intentionality that makes appearances-of out of already-constituted sensation-data a "functioning intentionality" in which the data are transitional unities the gaze passes through (`hua39/015`, `hua39/013`). What the Investigations called non-intentional content and Ideas I called hyle with nothing of intentionality is here itself constituted and itself an intentional unity, of the lowest grade. Kern's summary from the reception side agrees: "absolute" sensibility is for Husserl only primal hyle, "ultimately a mere limiting concept" (`kern-hk/009`), and receptivity "is itself the lowest level of spontaneity" (`kern-hk/008`). *Formal and Transcendental Logic* (1929) closes the sequence on the same side: the "unconscious" into which retentional modification sinks is "nothing less than a phenomenological nothing but itself a limit-mode of consciousness" (`ftl/024`).

## Feeling, will, and the objectivating thesis

The Fifth Investigation admits feelings to the intentional class by an essence-argument: a liking without a likeable is not thinkable, and not because the words are correlative but because the essence of liking demands it (`hua19-1/019`, LU V § 15). The same section splits the word "feeling" between two genera, sensations of pain and pleasure and acts of liking and disliking, with "no generic community but only equivocation" (`hua19-1/021`). § 37 then restricts the division of basic act-classes to act-qualities and delimits the objectivating act, through which the thesis that every act is grounded in presentations "receives a new interpretation" (`hua19-1/038`).

The ethics lectures (1908–09 to 1914) push the apparatus of judgment onto the will and the emotions. Judging is meaning, and the primal mode of belief is certainty (`hua28/001`); willing and wishing are meaning too, only "a meaning that something shall be" (`hua28/003`). Every valuing act has objectivating acts beneath it, and the founding belongs to its essence (`hua28/004`), yet "in the proper sense only objectivating acts are directed upon the objective, the being or non-being, whereas valuing acts are directed upon values" (`hua28/005`). The record's one "revised" unit reverses a founding relation on the act-side: instead of the willing being founded on belief in the future being, "it is rather the belief of the future that springs forth from the willing" (`hua28/012`, Hua XXVIII Part III § 15). The will is a creative intention that fulfils itself in the executing action (`hua28/013`), and analogues of doubt, question and decision appear in the will-sphere "before all assertions" (`hua28/019`).

Hua XLIII/2 (1911) gives the parallel its lowest anchor. A single immanent material, primary content and primary feeling fused in inner consciousness, bears "a double intentionality through double Repräsentanten", the value-intentionality founded in but not reducible to the theoretical (`hua43-2/012`); the primary feeling itself has no intentionality and acquires it by a feeling-apprehension (`hua43-2/010`). Ideas II names the feeling-analogue of perception *Wertnehmung*, the feeling in which the ego is present at the object itself (`hua4/003`, Ideas II § 4), gives it empty and fulfilled forms and anticipatory horizons (`hua4/004`, `hua4/005`), and says every non-objectivating act "is itself objectivating in what it newly contributes" (`hua4/010`, § 7). Ideas I § 116 has the founded noeses constitute "a totally new dimension of sense", values rather than new pieces of things (`hua3-i/003`), and § 117 concludes that "all acts whatsoever, including the acts of sentiment and will, are objectivating" (`hua3-i/005`).

The ledger cannot order these statements. Hua XXVIII spans 1908–09 to 1914, Ideas I 1913 to 1928, Ideas II 1912 to 1928, and no unit carries a stratum. Whether § 117's thesis that all acts are objectivating revises § 9's "only objectivating acts are directed upon the objective" or restates it under the widened sense of "objectivating" that LU V § 37 had already introduced is undecidable from the register as it stands (gap-010). What the ledger does show is the shape of the thesis's afterlife. Bernet reads Ideas I as making feelings doxic acts that exhibit "a perfect correlation between the lived experience and its intentional object" and locates the motive in the phenomenological founding of ethics (`bernet-vds/029`, `bernet-vds/030`). Henry (1963) attacks the thesis at the root: modern philosophy from Brentano to Sartre "gives out as a discovery" that affectivity is intentional, and Henry asks how affectivity can be understanding "if nothing is more repugnant to its essence than transcendence" (`henry-em/012`). Affectivity is not the grasping of the horizon but "the reality of the act that grasps" (`henry-em/013`), and Henry's positive doctrine runs the founding relation the other way: every positing act is, in its self-affection, an affective phenomenon (`henry-em/010`). The drive objection Husserl's own 1911 record raised and left standing (`hua43-2/013`) is not answered anywhere the ledger reaches (gap-016).

## The ego enters

LU V § 12 is recorded as criticizing the ego as relation-point. When we live in the act nothing of the ego is to be noticed, and though the relation to the ego cannot be circumvented in description, the experience itself "does not consist in a complexion that would contain the ego-presentation as a part-experience" (`hua19-1/012`). The pass note lists Chapter One, on the ego and inner perception, as not covered, so the ledger has neither the first-edition doctrine nor any second-edition retraction of it beyond this one unit.

The next dated appearance is Ideas II § 22. In every act-carrying-out lies "a ray of directedness" that takes its departure in the ego, which remains undivided and numerically identical (`hua4/012`); § 25 finds in every act a polarity of ego-pole and object as counter-pole (`hua4/015`). Ideas I § 115 adds the modal difference: act-stirrings are experienced "with all their intentionalities, but the ego does not live in them as an 'accomplishing subject'" (`hua3-i/002`), and § 84 broadens intentionality beyond the actual cogito to the inactual background (`hua3-p34/002`, status "revised"). *Erste Philosophie* (1923–24) states the ego as "an unlosable and numerically identical subject-pole", present in every experience "but indeed not as a part" (`hua7-i/001`), and makes the I the index of a universal synthesis parallel to the object-synthesis (`hua7-i/008`). Hua XXXIX has the belief in being radiate "from the ego-pole" (`hua39/001`); the *Cartesian Meditations* say the ego "only is what it is in relation to intentional objectualities" (`hua1/019`).

Read in ledger order, the ego is absent from the intentional experience as a part in § 12 and present in it as a pole from Ideas II onward, and the two are compatible on their face: neither makes the ego a part. What the ledger cannot show is the step between § 12, where nothing of the ego is to be noticed, and § 22's ray of directedness from the ego, because the sections where the commentary would record it are unextracted. Bernet reads the step as the genesis of egological subjectivism. Ideas I makes the pure I, "far from being the result of an experience", into "its ultimate source" (`bernet-vds/027`), and thereby something that can appear only belatedly, as an object of reflection (`bernet-vds/028`). Bernet's own conclusion is that the inquiry "does not corroborate an intentionality without subject" but does bring to light in Husserl "an intentionality without object" (`bernet-vds/035`). That is a reception verdict. The primary-side units that would test it sit in the uncovered ranges.

## Intention, fulfilment, horizon

The ledger dates three vocabularies for one structure. The first is intention and fulfilment. *Thing and Space* (1907) has perceptual intention directed toward increasingly complete givenness, with intentions continuously set out and fulfilled (`husserl-thing-and-space/004`, § 32), and § 11 reconceives the intentional relation as consciousness's fittingness to found identity-consciousness with other perceptions, "an immanent determination of the consciousness itself" (`husserl-thing-and-space/003`). The Sixth Investigation, on the spine at 1921, makes the contrast between empty meaning-directedness and intuitive fulfilment its master-theme (`lu6/001`), defines objectifying acts by the identification-character of their fulfilment-syntheses (`lu6/002`), and describes every perception as "a web of partial intentions, fused into the unity of a total intention" (`lu6/005`, § 10). The same section insists that "intention is not expectation": it is not essential to an intention to be directed on a future occurrence (`lu6/006`). Fullness becomes a third moment beside quality and matter (`lu6/012`), and the ultimate fulfilment is the *adaequatio* in which "the objective is really 'present' or 'given' exactly as that as which it is intended" (`lu6/019`). § 8's addendum records a doubt "the later inquiry will find serious": the lived coincidence of intention and intuition grounds no act of relating identification (`lu6/004`, status "revised"). The ledger does not say where the later inquiry takes it up.

The second vocabulary is appearance and horizon, and it enters with the 1919 lectures. Outer perception intends the reality of the perceived as I-foreign (`nug/007`), is always given "only presumptively" because its sense points beyond itself to what is not given (`nug/008`), and has its real conscious only as "a meaning-beyond that intends beyond itself and constantly aims at fulfilment" (`nug/009`). Perception is two-layered, presenting and appresenting, the invisible side "conscious only as an indeterminate horizon" (`nug/015`), and appresentation is "not representation by a sign" but a structural moment of the perception (`nug/016`). Existence itself becomes "an index for a certain structure of experiential course", an open infinite horizon of concordant experience (`nug/021`). From here the word runs forward. Hua XXXIX's horizons are "motivated act-possibilities" (`hua39/006`); its horizon-meaning is "indeterminate determinability, and always determinability through objects" (`hua39/019`); the ineffective horizon of the "unconscious" is nonetheless an intentional horizon (`hua39/018`). The *Cartesian Meditations* give every experience "an intentional horizon of reference to potentialities of consciousness" (`hua1/012`) and say the *cogitatum qua cogitatum* "is never something finished and given" (`hua1/013`); the Krisis has a whole horizon of co-functioning appearances implied in each perception (`krisis/008`). Bernet names it horizonal intentionality and notes its origin in a psychology of attention (`bernet-vds/025`). Text Nr. 15 of Hua XXXIX (1934 by the chronology) then folds the second vocabulary back into the first. The original "induction" or anticipation "proves to be a modification-mode of originally instituting activity — a mode of 'intentionality,' the anticipating meaning beyond a kernel of givenness" (`hua39/023`). Set beside the Sixth Investigation's "intention is not expectation" this is no contradiction. The earlier unit denies that intention is essentially future-directed; the later makes anticipation one of its modes. It is a shift in what the word is asked to carry, and the ledger dates both ends of it.

The third vocabulary is evidence. *Formal and Transcendental Logic* makes evidence "the general distinguished form of 'intentionality'" in which the object is conscious in the mode of self-grasping (`ftl/010`, § 59), and gives intentionality a "fundamental lawfulness": every consciousness of something belongs a priori to an open manifold of consciousness-modes of the same, a manifold that includes evidence, so that consciousness has a teleological structure toward reason (`ftl/012`, § 60). Intention as "striving toward self-givenness" is already in the passive-synthesis lectures (`husserliana-11/003`), which also separate epistemic from practical striving (`husserliana-11/004`). The Krisis returns the structure to the perceptual case: while the thing is given, "I mean more than it offers" (`krisis/005`), and the synthesis of perspectives is a progressive enrichment of meaning, not an external fusion (`krisis/006`). The undated *Erfahrung und Urteil* carries the same doctrine (the *plus ultra* of interest, `eu/016`; aiming and attainment, `eu/017`; anticipation as a mode of intentionality, `eu/003`), at one point in words that nearly coincide with Hua XXXIX Text Nr. 15. The ledger holds it out of the sequence, and this pass leaves it there (gap-015).

## Functioning intentionality and the analysis that uncovers it

One term the ledger dates to about 1920 and then follows for fifteen years: the intentionality at work is not the intentionality that is thematic. Hua XXXIX calls the intentionality that makes appearances out of constituted sensation-data "functioning intentionality" (`hua39/015`). *Formal and Transcendental Logic* says that while constituting intentionality "runs as life accomplishing objectification, it is 'unconscious' — meaning it makes thematic but is not itself thematic" (`ftl/006`), and diagnoses the positive sciences' failure to understand their achievements "as achievements of an intentionality that remains unthematic for them" (`ftl/003`). The *Cartesian Meditations* have the phenomenologist penetrate "into the anonymous cogitative life" (`hua1/016`); the Krisis names "a realm of a thoroughly self-enclosed subjectivity ... functioning in all experiencing, all thinking, all living ... and yet never caught sight of" (`krisis/004`). Bernet turns this against the doctrine of constitution: the discoveries of "noematic phenomena, passive and anonymous constitution, and horizonal intentionality have hardly shaken" Husserl's idea of constitution as a subjective accomplishment issuing in an objective result (`bernet-vds/036`), and he defines operative intentionality as "an intentionality devoid of all representation of an object" (`bernet-vds/045`).

On the side of method the corresponding change is in the sense of "analysis". The 1925 lectures credit the Investigations with discovering intentional analysis as an interwovenness that has "no analogue in the physical at all" (`hua9/007`). The Meditations say intentional analysis is "something totally different from analysis in the ordinary sense", its performance "the disclosure of the potentialities implied in the actualities of consciousness" (`hua1/014`), guided by the recognition that every cogito means more than is explicitly meant (`hua1/015`). *Formal and Transcendental Logic* makes the same point genetically. Every sense-formation can be questioned as to its sense-history, and this "determines the proper sense of intentional analysis as uncovering of intentional implications" (`ftl/019`); equivocations in the basic logical concepts are "inner shiftings of intentionality", not verbal accidents (`ftl/018`). Hua XXXIX adds that all intentional analysis "is itself interpretation", though evident and right-bestowing interpretation (`hua39/004`). The correlation itself, which Ideas I § 86 calls "the master-principle of constitutive phenomenology" (`hua3-p34/007`) and Ideas III § 7 states as a correspondence of object-categories to fundamental kinds of giving intuition (`hua5/003`), reappears in 1929 as the correlation of "category of objectuality and category of evidence" (`ftl/013`) and in 1935 as the universal correlation-a priori whose first breakthrough Husserl dates to about 1898 (`krisis/007`, `krisis/009`). The ledger's own spine begins three years after that date.

Two things in this stretch are recorded as revisions rather than continuities. The Meditations' § 39 makes association "a title of intentionality" and calls the naturalistic concept a distortion of it (`hua1/022`); Ideas II § 56(b) had already made association a form of motivation distinct from the motivation of position-takings (`hua4/022`), and the undated *Erfahrung und Urteil* transforms the traditional sense into "a form of lawfulness of immanent genesis" (`eu/009`). Whether the Meditations revise Ideas II or repeat it depends on which Ideas II stratum § 56(b) belongs to, and the unit carries none. The other revision is Hua IX's: consciousness "revealed its true essential character only in such directed work", a teleologically structured performance-life rather than a taxonomy (`hua9/010`). In both cases the status is the ledger's judgement, not this pass's.

## Heidegger, 1925 to 1927: the word kept, the question moved

The GA 20 record opens with a thesis the ledger marks "introduced": intentionality "is a structure of lived experiences as such, not a relation subsequently attached to experiences understood as psychic states" (`ga20/001`, GA 20 § 5). What follows in § 5 defends the structure against its critics with Husserlian materials. The hallucination is itself a directing-toward, so the perception is intentional whether veridical or not (`ga20/002`); the perceived of natural perception is the environmental thing, seen itself and not through images (`ga20/003`, `ga20/006`); the perceived in the strict sense is its perceivedness, the being "in the how of its being-perceived" (`ga20/004`); bodily presence is a distinguished mode of self-givenness (`ga20/005`); every intention has its own determinate mode of fulfilment (`ga20/008`). The basic constitution is "a peculiar belonging-together of the how of being-intended, the intentio, and the intentum" (`ga20/007`). § 6 goes further than anything on the Husserl side of the ledger in one respect: the correction of the prejudice that the non-sensory is the subjective and the discovery of intentionality "are one and the same" (`ga20/012`); constituting is "not producing or making but letting-be-seen" (`ga20/014`); and "scientific ontology is nothing other than phenomenology" (`ga20/015`).

The criticism in the same course is aimed past the structure at its bearer. "Not intentionality as such is metaphysically dogmatic, but what is built under the structure, or left unquestioned beneath it, through the traditional failure to ask what it is the structure of", and taking it as a structure of reason (Husserl) or of the person (Scheler) does not remove that failure (`ga20/010`). §§ 11–13 press the immanent critique. The four being-determinations of pure consciousness "are in no way drawn from the being itself" and bar the question of its being (`ga20/016`); pure consciousness is won from a traditional idea of absolute science, not from the things themselves (`ga20/017`); the reduction "gives out of hand the very ground on which alone one could ask after the being of consciousness" (`ga20/018`); there are two neglects, the being of the acts and the sense of being as such (`ga20/019`); behind the titles for the intentional stands *animal rationale*, and the neglect is an event of Dasein's own falling (`ga20/020`). The ledger places all this in 1925, the year Husserl's own lectures call intentionality "the central psychological theme" (`hua9/006`) and describe phenomenological psychology as correlation-research (`hua9/008`). Two records, one date; the divergence between them is not a development. What the primary-side ledger does not hold is any GA 20 unit founding intentionality in transcendence. The course's §§ 4 and 7–10 are unextracted.

GA 24 (1927) has three units. Phenomenology designates the structure of comportments "following a Scholastic term, as intentionality" (`hga24/001`); Husserl clarified its essence for the first time in the Investigations and continued in the Ideas (`hga24/002`; `inh/hga24/003`); intentionality belongs to the essence of comportments, so that "intentional comportment" is a pleonasm like "a spatial triangle" (`hga24/003`). The inheritance record adds the reservation that the phenomenon "is far from being philosophically adequately grasped" (`inh/hga24/004`). The pass note names the intentio/intentum analysis, the misinterpretations, and the transcendence relation as not covered. So the founding of intentionality in transcendence reaches the ledger only through Bernet: Heidegger's "slightly scholastic formula" that intentionality is the *ratio cognoscendi* of transcendence and transcendence the *ratio essendi* of intentionality (`bernet-vds/009`); intentionality as "ontic transcendence" grounded in the primal transcendence of being-in-the-world (`bernet-vds/008`); an ordered rather than vicious circle in which "intentionality gives the access, transcendence gives the ground" (`bernet-vds/004`). Bernet's pass note assigns these chapters to the 1925 course. The ledger's own GA 20 record, covering §§ 5–6 and 11–13, holds no unit for the founding, so the sections Bernet reads lie either in the course's uncovered range or in a text the units do not name (gap-012).

*Sein und Zeit*, on the spine at 1927 alongside GA 24 and without an order between them, is the record in which the word nearly disappears. Of 23 units, the recorded quotes carry it in three. In two, both at § 69(b), the word belongs to the note the commentary is reporting: Husserl's "Gegenwärtigen" for sense-perception was suggested by "the intentional analysis of perception" (`sz/022`), and "how the intentionality of 'consciousness' grounds in Dasein's ecstatic temporality, the following division will show" (`sz/023`). In the third the word is the commentator's own: "Intentionality, one may say, is here out-radicalized: where Husserl taught that consciousness is of its object, Heidegger replies that the 'of' was never a bridge from an inside, because there never was an inside to leave" (`sz/008`; `inh/sz/003`). That sentence is the ledger's tier-two gloss on § 13, and the pass note says the record covers "the points where Sein und Zeit engages and displaces intentionality". The displacement itself is recorded in Heidegger's terms. Being-in-the-world is minted so that "there is no worldless subject plus world to be bridged" (`sz/001`); "ich bin" says I dwell alongside the world (`sz/002`); two worldless things can never touch (`sz/003`); subject and object "do not coincide with Dasein and world" (`sz/004`); knowing is grounded in an already-being-alongside-the-world (`sz/006`); Dasein "is always already 'outside'" (`sz/007`); the demand for a proof of the external world is "the Cartesian confession" (`sz/012`). No absence is recorded for the treatise. The ledger does not say the word is missing from §§ 12–13 or §§ 43–44, only that the extracted quotes do not carry it (gap-013).

What the treatise puts in the word's place, on the ledger's evidence, is a founding order rather than a structure. Cognition is "a founded mode of access to the real", founded in being-in-the-world, "which has the more original constitution of care" (`sz/011`, § 43(a)); truth is primarily Dasein's discovering, and discoveredness derivative (`sz/013`); the apophantic "as" is founded in the hermeneutic "as" (`sz/017`); the theoretical attitude is a switch-over of circumspect concern (`sz/018`); thematization "frees" beings into objecthood rather than positing them (`sz/020`); and "transcendence does not consist in objectification; rather, objectification presupposes transcendence" (`sz/021`). GA 20's "constituting is letting-be-seen" (`ga20/014`) and the treatise's "Thematization objectifies — not that it first 'posits' the beings: it frees them" (`sz/020`) are the two points at which the Heidegger records touch the constitution vocabulary. The ledger orders them 1925 then 1927 and records no unit in which the first is withdrawn. The § 69(b) note is a promise; the ledger marks it as attached to a division never published (`sz/023`) and holds nothing that redeems it.

## Reception: the being of intentionality and the intentionality of the flesh

The reception layer is ordered by the commentator's date, not the doctrine's, and the ledger says so. Derrida (1961) ends with an identification the record quotes in three words: "Intentionality is traditionality" (`derrida-iog/015`). The route there runs through the non-reell noema as root of repeatability (`derrida-iog/001`), writing as "a sort of autonomous transcendental field from which every actual subject can absent itself" (`derrida-iog/006`), the "virtual intentionality" without which the written mark is "a chaotic literality" (`derrida-iog/007`), and the genitive of the "relation to the object" as neither objective nor subjective (`derrida-iog/012`). Henry (1963, 1965) reads the tradition from Kant through Husserl to Heidegger as "ontological monism", the thesis that nothing is given except through the horizon of being (`henry-ppc/002`, `henry-ppc/009`), distinguishes the narrow Husserlian sense of representation from the broad ontological one (`henry-em/001`), and builds a body that is "a structure made of virtually compossible intentionalities ... accomplished in a sphere of absolute immanence" (`henry-ppc/007`), intentionalities that "know themselves originally in the immanence of their own being" without a second act (`henry-ppc/003`). Henry's "reflection" is a self-knowledge included in every intentionality that is not knowledge of what it aims at (`henry-ppc/001`): an intentionality with a non-intentional inside, the exact inverse of the *Sein und Zeit* commentary's "there never was an inside to leave" (`sz/008`). The ledger records both and records no engagement of either with the other.

Kern (1964) is the one reception record that reads Husserl's intentionality as a critical instrument against Kant. Psychologism is "the confusion of the objective and the experiences directed toward it" (`kern-hk/001`); Kant "does not distinguish pure intuiting ... from the idea of space itself" (`kern-hk/003`); the transcendental deduction is confused for want of a noesis/noema distinction (`kern-hk/004`); "a transcendental logic is possible only within a transcendental noetics" (`kern-hk/005`). Kern also records, against the grain of Husserl's method, that Husserl's practised noetics is constructive and close to Natorp's (`kern-hk/017`), while Natorp is charged with collapsing act into content (`kern-hk/016`). Franck (1981) restates the Meditations' doctrine and locates in the Fifth Meditation the question "of the being of intentionality" (`franck-bodies/015`), the same question Bernet attributes to Heidegger (`bernet-vds/003`).

Bernet (1994), with fifty-nine units, is the record that reads the whole spine. His governing thesis is that Heidegger, Gurwitsch, Sartre, Merleau-Ponty and Henry together put in question "the representationalist objectivism and the egological subjectivism progressively set in place by Husserl" (`bernet-vds/026`). Against Henry he credits Husserl with "an impressional intentionality" (`bernet-vds/031`) and a double intentionality in every perception, impressional among the sensations and objectivating toward things (`bernet-vds/033`). His one recorded absence is telling for this theme. A section on the constitution of the historical world carries its analysis "entirely in the borrowed idiom of living-into and caring-into the world (Hineinleben, Hineinsorgen)" and not in noesis/noema vocabulary (`bernet-la-vie-du-sujet-commentary#h4-ii`). Where a Husserl reader of 1994 drops the intentional vocabulary, it is for Heidegger's.

## Held out: the undated records

*Erfahrung und Urteil* and Picht's *De anima* commentary sit in the undated bucket, and this pass does not place them. The *Erfahrung und Urteil* units run parallel to the dated spine at many points: mere judging as an intentional modification of evident judging (`eu/001`), fore-knowledge that is "never completely empty" (`eu/002`), inner and outer horizon (`eu/004`), sense-transcendence (`eu/005`), affection as stimulus toward turning (`eu/011`), receptivity as "the lowest level of activity" (`eu/014`), kinaesthesis (`eu/019`), and the disconnectedness of phantasy-objectualities from the one world (`eu/022`). Every one of these has a dated counterpart in Hua XXXIX, the Meditations, *Formal and Transcendental Logic* or the Krisis, and none of the counterparts may be used to date it; the chronology's stated reason is that the volume is not in the sources repository. Picht's units are recorded under this theme as a foil: noesis as the *energeia* that "carries its work within itself" (`picht-de-anima/001`), Ideas as "the real structures of what is" and not representations (`picht-de-anima/007`), the Parmenidean "without the beholding, being is not" (`picht-de-anima/010`), and being as manifest-being that implies a horizon whose unity is *alētheia* (`picht-de-anima/011`). Whether Picht writes with or against the phenomenological concept the ledger does not record, and this pass does not guess.

## What this ledger does not support

Ten gaps go to `synthesis/gaps.json` from this pass, gap-008 through gap-017. Four are chronological: the Ideas III Afterword dated under the volume's single 1912 stratum (gap-008); the Sixth Investigation on the spine at 1921 with no first-edition stratum, and the Fifth Investigation's 41 units under two strata with no tag (gap-009); the objectivating thesis spread across three overlapping date ranges (gap-010); and the *Erfahrung und Urteil* parallel to Hua XXXIX that cannot yet be used (gap-015). Four are extraction gaps: the Bernau texts themselves on the pre-intentional primal process (gap-011); GA 24's transcendence material and GA 20 §§ 4 and 7–10, which alone would let the founding of intentionality in transcendence be cited from the primary side (gap-012); absence records for *Sein und Zeit* and a pass over its second commentary (gap-013); and the 1911 drive objection and whether it was ever answered (gap-016). Two are corpus gaps: the 1898 date of the correlation a priori, evidenced nowhere in the corpus (gap-014), and the return of "immanent" between 1913 and 1923 (gap-017).
