## Significant

1. **Husserl, *Natur und Geist. Vorlesungen Sommersemester 1927*, Husserliana
   XXXII (ed. M. Weiler, 2001).** No English translation known (*memory*).
   - Part needed: the systematic lectures on nature and spirit (the whole
     course is one argument; the editor's introduction for dating).
   - Bears on: the last layer. Husserl's insertions in Landgrebe's typescript
     run to 1928 (Hua IV `:7261`). The 1927 course shows what order of
     constitution he then taught: whether nature is still the founding layer,
     or the personal world is the starting point and nature an abstraction
     from it. It could confirm or refute any thesis that the late layer of
     *Ideen II* reverses the order of the early one.
   - Without it: the late direction is argued from the 1924–28 marginalia
     and from Hua XIII Beilage LVII ("wohl 1927") and Hua XIV only, and is
     declared a limit.
2. **Husserl, *Natur und Geist. Vorlesungen Sommersemester 1919*,
   Husserliana Materialien IV (ed. M. Weiler, 2002).** No English
   translation known (*memory*).
   - Part needed: Part II, "Reflections toward a transcendental theory of
     nature and spirit" (so titled in this repository's commentary,
     `commentaries/husserl/husserl-natur-und-geist-commentary.md`, which
     is not evidence).
   - Bears on: the gap between Stein's second arrangement (c. 1918) and
     Landgrebe's typescript (1924/25), for which Hua IV says the work
     "rested" (`:407`). Heidegger says Husserl lectured on "Natur und Geist"
     repeatedly from 1916 (GA 20 `:2493` ff.). It would date when the
     priority of spirit, stated in § 64, became a doctrine Husserl taught,
     and whether § 64 belongs to the 1913 H-sheets or to a later layer.
   - Without it: § 64 is dated by the concordance alone, possibly only to
     "Stein's second arrangement, c. 1918", and the dating is stated as such.
3. **Ricœur, "Analyses et problèmes dans *Ideen II* de Husserl", *Revue de
   métaphysique et de morale* 56 (1951) and 57 (1952)** (both parts; the
   index holds the second). English: in *Husserl: An Analysis of His
   Phenomenology* (1967) (*memory*).
   - Part needed: whole (two parts).
   - Bears on: the nearest precedent. If Ricœur already argues that the
     book's realism of body and person strains against its idealism of
     constitution (*memory*), any thesis about a double order in *Ideen II*
     must be placed against him: what it adds is the dating by layers.
   - Without it: the study states that the nearest known precedent was not
     read, and makes no claim of novelty against it.
4. **Merleau-Ponty, "Le philosophe et son ombre" (1959), in *Signes* (1960);
   English "The Philosopher and His Shadow", in *Signs* (1964).**
   - Part needed: the essay (about 30 pages).
   - Bears on: the most influential reading of *Ideen II* (*memory*), which
     finds beneath the constituting analyses a layer of the "pre-theoretical"
     that constitution presupposes. It is either the strongest rival to a
     thesis about the order of constitution or its precedent.
   - Without it: the opposing reading is Heidegger's alone (held), and
     Merleau-Ponty's is named as a known position not examined.
5. **Sokolowski, *The Formation of Husserl's Concept of Constitution*
   (Phaenomenologica 18, 1964).**
   - Part needed: the chapters on the period 1907–1913 and on *Ideen II*
     (*memory* that it treats it; verify), and the conclusion.
   - Bears on: whether the concept of constitution itself changes across
     the layers (from "sense-giving" to something nearer "making"), which is
     the received account of the concept's development (*memory*).
   - Without it: the development of the concept is stated from the held
     primary texts only (Hua XVI, *Ideen I*, *Ideen II*, CM), with that
     limit declared.

## Useful

6. Nenon & Embree (eds.), *Issues in Husserl's Ideas II* (Contributions to
   Phenomenology 24, 1996). Whole volume's table of contents; any chapter on
   composition or on the order of constitution. Would show whether the
   layering has been studied.
7. Husserl, *Ideas II*, English (Rojcewicz & Schuwer, 1989): a clean copy,
   or clean pages for the passages quoted. The held conversion is badly
   damaged (holdings). Without it: my translations, with the English page

8. Husserl, *Transzendentaler Idealismus. Texte aus dem Nachlass
   (1908–1921)*, Husserliana XXXVI (2003). Contemporary with the layers of
   *Ideen II*; texts on the world's dependence on consciousness. A
   commentary exists here; the text is not held.
9. Edith Stein, *Zum Problem der Einfühlung* (1917); English *On the Problem
   of Empathy*. The arranger's own account of the constitution of the
   psycho-physical individual and the person, written in the years of her
   first arrangement.
10. Eugen Fink, "Das Problem der Phänomenologie Edmund Husserls" (1939),
    pp. 237–38. Cited by Biemel for Husserl's concept of constitution
    (Hua IV `:452`).
11. Dilthey, *Ideen über eine beschreibende und zergliedernde Psychologie*
    (1894), ch. 7. Named by § 48 and by GA 20 as the source of the
    personalistic attitude.
12. Lohmar (2018), "On Some Motives for Husserl's Genetic Turn …", *Studia
    Phaenomenologica*. Bears on dating the genetic layer in section 3.
