## a. Question and received view

**Question.** In *Ideen II*, what is constituted on what? When Husserl builds
nature, the animate body, the soul and the person "in stages", is that order
the order of constitution, or an order inside one attitude that another order
overrules?

**Received view.** *Ideen II* constitutes reality in strata founded from
below (material thing, sensing body, soul, spirit), so that the person stands
on nature. Holders: Heidegger, GA 20 § 13 d (1925), from the manuscript: the
"Fundamentalschicht" stays the actuality of nature, the person becomes a
"mehrschichtiges Weltding"; Husserl's own programme, *Ideen I* § 152 (1913):
material reality "as the lowest level" beneath all others; Mattens (2006):
stratification as "the paragon of constitutive analysis". Moran & Cohen's
"Constitution" gives the general neo-Kantian frame (objects formed by acts).

## b. Anomalies the received view leaves unexplained

1. § 64 (pp. 297–302): "If we strike out all spirits from the world, there is no more nature"; nature is "a field of thoroughgoing relativities" borne by spirit as the absolute.
2. Hua V, Beilage I (1912): nature, body and soul constitute themselves "in mutual relatedness, at one with one another"; the editor reads this as excluding any theory of layers (Hua IV `:400`).
3. § 49 (p. 184, 1913 H-sheets): the naturalistic attitude is not coordinate with the personalistic but subordinates itself to it and gains independence "through an abstraction or rather a kind of self-forgetfulness of the personal ego".
4. § 53 (pp. 208–10, 1913): the "vicious circle": nature presupposed at the start, nature constituted in the association of persons at the end.
5. Beilage I (1913) calls the ascent "the constitutive order" but distinguishes its strata "idealiter", from an ego "thought of as isolated", with further steps as "ideal possibilities, not ideal necessities".
6. § 61 (p. 277) and Beilage XII (Jan. 1917): the lowest stratum is now inside the person ("underground", sensibility), and it "makes" the constitution of nature; personal intentionality is activity or the "sediment" of activity, and spirit has an immanent teleology in "the transformation of the higher into the lower".
7. The 1924–28 additions to § 64 add "intersubjective" three times and the sentence that the ego is "not an empty pole but the bearer of its habituality".
8. Husserl's marginalia in Landgrebe's typescript: ch. 1 "must be completely reworked"; p. 74 "from here on everything seems to need improvement"; Beilage X "Very important!"

## c. Candidate theses

- **T1 (composition).** The priority of spirit is older than the stratified exposition; the book's order is Stein's arrangement, which put the 1913 section 3 after the 1915 sections 1–2. *Explains* 3, 4, 8 and Heidegger's order. *Fails*: Beilage I (1913) already sets out the stratified ascent as "the constitutive order", so both are of 1913 (anomaly 5). Rejected as stated; kept as a fact about the book's order.
- **T2 (two concepts).** "Constitution" names two operations: realization of things through presentation by adumbration (*Darstellung*) and constitution of persons through manifestation (*Bekundung*) and motivation (Beilage X; § 56). *Explains* 1, 3, 4. *Leaves* 2, 6, 7 unexplained, and does not date anything. Ricœur may already argue near this (acquisition 3, not read).
- **T3 (transposition), chosen.** Constitution in *Ideen II* keeps a stratified form from 1912 to 1928, but its strata change their bearer and their direction. In 1912–13 they are strata of constituted realities, built upward in an order that the texts themselves call ideal and abstractive; from January 1917 they are strata of the constituting person, where the lower stratum, sensibility, is in part the sediment of the higher, activity. § 64's relativity of nature to spirit, with the intersubjective and historical additions of 1924–28, follows from the second arrangement, not from the first. *Explains* 1, 2 (mutual constitution concretely; strata only by abstraction), 3–6, 7 (the absolute is carried into intersubjectivity and history), and 8 in part (dissatisfaction with an exposition that still runs upward). *Does not explain*: the numbering Heidegger cites; whether § 64's text (lost manuscript) is of 1913, 1917 or 1918. **Against precedents:** Mattens holds stratification as the method of constitution; T3 accepts this for 1912–13 and dates its displacement. Heidegger saw the priority of the personal and denied it effect; T3 grants his third point (the *animal rationale*) for 1917 and contests the first. Ricœur and Merleau-Ponty may anticipate parts of T3; not read, so no claim of novelty against them.

**Revision during drafting (2026-11-13).** Part 5 found that the relativity of

of a circle; only the 1917 transposition makes the two one order, and the

**Revision after the review (2026-11-13).** The fresh-context reviewer showed
that the underground is older than 1917 (§ 34, 1912; Beilage XIV, before
1917, where it is the soul of naturalistic psychology), that the
de-naturalizing of it in § 61 is Husserl's correction of 1924–28 (280,28),
that Beilage I founds the soul on the body (310), and that § 52 vs § 61 is not
a layer changing sides. T3 now reads: the founding series of 1913 runs thing →
body → soul and stops before the person; January 1917 stratifies the person's
life genetically, still calling its lower layer "nature"; 1924–28 rejects the
naturalistic reading and makes the absolute intersubjective and historical.
See the study's Register 6 and 13.

## d. Consequence

If T3 holds, § 64 cannot be read as the conclusion of the ascent of sections 1–2, nor as an idealist reversal of it. It states what the strata mean once they belong to the person. A specialist would have to stop saying that *Ideen II* builds the person on nature (or, inversely, that § 64 simply cancels the layers): the layers stay, but by 1917 the bottom layer is no longer nature; it is the passive life in which nature is constituted. Heidegger's "multi-layered thing of the world" describes the exposition of sections 1–2, not the doctrine of § 61 and § 64.

## e. Prediction (fixed before reading)

Text: Hua XIII, Beilage XLVII, "Die Konstitution der intersubjektiv an sich seienden Natur auf Grund der Konstitution des Psychophysischen auf der solipsistischen und höheren Apperzeptionsstufe. Die verkehrte Introjektion der Erscheinungen in den Leib" (1918), `husserl-hua-13-…:8256` ff. Only its heading and date have been read.

T3 predicts: (i) the "solipsistic and higher level of apperception" will be a level of apperception in the constituting subject, not a level of being; (ii) the "wrong introjection" will be the error of taking the constituting appearances as states or contents of the body, i.e. reading the constituting subject naturalistically (what § 49 calls self-forgetfulness); (iii) the text will not make physical nature the founding stratum of the person. If (ii) or (iii) fails, the dating in T3 (the shift complete by 1917–18) must be weakened.

## f. Strongest opposing reading

Heidegger, GA 20 § 13 c–d (`:2493`–`:2620`, pp. 165–74). Three points: (1) the inquiry stays inside the question of the constitution of reality and objectivity, and its order is the order of the things: nature as fundamental layer, the psychic on it, the spiritual on that; (2) access to the person is immanent reflection (*inspectio sui*), so the being of acts stays undetermined; (3) the guiding thread is *animal rationale*, the human being as a living thing with reason, a "multi-layered thing of the world". He concedes the priority of the personalistic attitude and quotes § 64 (his n. 42–47), and holds that "ontologically everything stays as before". Passages it rests on: § 18 c, § 49 (founding "physio-psychic" order), Beilage I's "constitutive order", *Ideen I* § 152, § 61 (*intellectus agens* over "pure animality").

## g. Parts

Opening (no heading; about 1,600 words). Received view with holders; anomalies 1, 3, 6 named; thesis in one sentence; texts and omissions (the five significant acquisitions declared unread, with what each would have settled); method by stage.

1. **The book and its strata of composition** (about 3,200). Question: what is the text one cites as *Ideen II*? Passages: editor's introduction `:382`–`:431`; apparatus `:7209`–`:7261`; concordance `:7557`–`:7625`; GA 20 numbering. Result: every claim must be dated by sheet; the order of the book is Stein's (c. 1918) and Landgrebe's (1924/25); the priority of spirit (1913) and the stratified exposition (1912–13 and 1915) are not successive but coexist from the start; the underground doctrine is January 1917; § 64 is undatable beyond c. 1918, its last lines 1912.
2. **The strata of constituted reality, 1912–1915** (about 4,000). Question: in what sense is the ascent "the constitutive order"? Passages: *Ideen I* §§ 151–52; Hua V Beilage I (1912); Hua IV Beilage I (1913) with the blue spectacles; § 18 c; § 8. Descriptive stretch on the spectacles. Senses of "constitution" numbered (M1, guide B1). Result: the strata are realizing apprehensions of an abstractively isolated ego; the order is ideal; concretely the regions constitute themselves together (1912).
3. **The reversal of the attitudes, 1913** (about 3,600). Question: what does the personal attitude do to the order? Passages: § 49 (pp. 183–85), § 53 (pp. 208–11), Beilage X (pp. 321–25; "Very important!"). Result: founding ("physio-psychic") is the naturalistic attitude's order; nature as constituted in personal association; two modes of givenness (presentation, manifestation). Tension left: the order is both "constitutive" and "abstraction".
4. **The underground of spirit, 1917** (about 4,000). Question: where does the lower stratum go once nature is relative to persons? Passages: § 61 (pp. 275–80), Beilage XII §§ 1–2 (pp. 332–34), § 56 (motivation). Descriptive stretch on habit. Hardest case: is § 61 not founding from below again, in the person? Two answers (M9): dependence on nature and on a "dark underground" vs. sediment of activity. Result: transposition and inversion of direction.
5. **Nature relative, spirit absolute: § 64 and its last layer** (about 3,200). Passages: § 64 (pp. 297–302) with the apparatus `:7424`–`:7440`; marginalia `:7270`, `:7331`; Beilage X note "Sehr wichtig". Prediction reported here (Hua XIII Beilage XLVII). Result: § 64 argues from individuation; the late additions carry the absolute into intersubjectivity and history.
6. **Heidegger's reading at full strength** (about 3,600). Passages: GA 20 § 13 c–d; SZ § 10 n. Reconstruction, then ordered replies (M8) to points 1–3; objection turned on point 3 (credit). Then the close (A12–A13; no heading): the title's question answered; limits (lost manuscript of § 64; the 1919 and 1927 *Natur und Geist* courses not held; Ricœur, Merleau-Ponty, Sokolowski unread).

## h. Tensions to show and leave standing (guide M9)

1. Beilage I: the ascent is "the constitutive order" and also an ideal construction from an isolated ego. Husserl does not say which has the last word.
2. § 61: the person depends on nature *and* nature is constituted in the person's underground. Both stand in the same paragraph.
3. § 64: the absolute is the individual spirit (main text) *and* "an intersubjectivity" (1924–28 additions).
4. Whether the 1912 "mutual relatedness" survives the 1917 transposition or is lost in it.
