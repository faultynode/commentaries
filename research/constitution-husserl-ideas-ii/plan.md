## a. Question and received view

**Question.** What does it mean, in *Ideen II*, for something to be
constituted? Is there one concept under the three titles "constitution of
material nature", "of animal nature", "of the spiritual world", and if so, what
does it say about the order of the three?

**Received view.** Constitution in *Ideen II* is the building of reality in
strata, each founded on the one below: material thing, animate body, soul,
spirit. Holders: Husserl's own programme, *Ideen I* § 152 (material reality "as
the lowest level"); Heidegger, GA 20 § 13 (the "fundamental layer" remains
natural reality; the person a "many-layered thing of the world"); Mattens
(2006), stratification as "the paragon of constitutive analysis"; Moran and
Cohen's dictionary entry for the general sense (objects given structure by
acts).

## b. Anomalies the received view leaves unexplained

1. § 18 a (pp. 57–58): "In all constitution and at all levels we necessarily have 'circumstances' related to one another and what 'belongs' to the circumstances; everywhere we find the 'if–then' or 'because–therefore'." A statement about all constitution that mentions no layer.
2. § 15 c–d (pp. 41–45): what makes a thing real is not a further content laid on the schema but its "relation to circumstances"; a thing taken alone cannot be told from a phantom.
3. §§ 30–33: the soul is real "in a similar sense" to the thing, as a "unity of manifestation", yet it "does not schematize itself", has no causality in the strict sense, and has a history; § 33 keeps only a "formal idea": "unity of lasting properties in relation to pertinent circumstances".
4. § 56 h (pp. 245–47): the "founded" unity of body and spirit is made possible by causality between them, i.e. each takes "the function of circumstances" for the other; and the unity of expression "precedes" every such founded unity.
5. § 57 (p. 249): the personal ego, too, is experienced as how I "comport myself" under "subjective circumstances"; the same form a third time, with motivation in the place of causality.
6. § 58 (p. 252): "The ego is originally not from experience, in the sense of associative apperception in which unities of manifolds constitute themselves, but from life."
7. § 56 d (p. 226): "all nature with space, time, causality … is completely dissolved into a web of immanent motivations".
8. § 64: nature is "a field of thoroughgoing relativities"; "no thing has its individuality in itself"; spirits "are not unities of appearances".

## c. Candidate theses

- **T-A (two concepts).** "Constitution" covers two operations, presentation by adumbration for things and manifestation for persons (Beilage X). *Explains* 8. *Fails* on 2–3: Husserl uses "manifestation" (*Bekundung*, *Beurkundung*) first of the thing's real properties (pp. 44, 121–23), and distinguishes it there from presentation by adumbration (p. 131). The opposition of Beilage X is between manifestation through appearances and "absolute" manifestation. Rejected as stated; absorbed.
- **T-B (attitudes).** What is constituted is relative to an attitude; the strata are correlates of the naturalistic attitude, which is subordinate (§ 49). *Explains* 8 in part. *Leaves* 1–6 without a concept; and it was the frame of the first study.
- **T-C (the form of circumstances), chosen.** In *Ideen II* to be constituted as a reality is to be the unity of a rule that binds a manifold of states to circumstances; Husserl works this form out on the thing and carries it to the body, the soul and the person, and at each step one of its terms changes sense: the manifold (schemata; localized sensation fields; lived experiences; position-takings), the circumstances (other things; the body; the soul's own past; the motivating surrounding world), the rule (causality; conditionality; motivation). "Founding" and "strata" are derived from the form in two different ways that the received view runs together: a stratum of constitution is a unity that serves as manifold for a higher unity; a founded reality is one whose circumstances lie in another reality. Motivation stands at both ends of the series, beneath causality (kinaesthetic "if–then") and above it (personal "because"), and § 64's relativity of nature and absoluteness of spirit state where the form applies without remainder and where it stops. *Explains* 1–8. *Does not explain*: whether the person is, in the end, a constituted reality or not (tension h.1); time-constitution, left aside by Husserl here and by the study. **Against precedents:** Mattens is right about stratification inside thing-constitution, which T-C explains as iteration; Heidegger is right that the thing is the model; T-C contests that the model is a basis. Ricœur, Merleau-Ponty and Sokolowski may anticipate parts; unread.

## d. Consequence

If T-C holds, "what is constituted on what?" is the wrong first question for
*Ideen II*; the first question is "in relation to which circumstances?". § 64
then reads as a statement about the concept of reality fixed in §§ 15–17 and
31–33 (each real is what it is only relative to others), and not as an idealist
reversal added at the end. A specialist would have to stop saying that the
priority of material nature in the exposition is a priority of foundation: it
is the priority of the case on which the form of reality is first read off.

## e. Prediction (fixed before reading)

Text: Hua IV, Beilage XII, part II, §§ 1–3, "Die Realität der Seele und des
Menschen", "Psychophysische Kausalität und dinglicher Kausalzusammenhang",
"Möglichkeit der Einordnung der Seele in die Natur" (`:5592`–`:5684`, pp.
340–46). Only the headings have been read.

T-C predicts: (i) the reality of the soul will be determined there by
dependence on circumstances, and not as a layer laid on the body; (ii) Husserl
will separate the dependence of the soul on the body from the causal nexus of
things, in name or in description; (iii) the placing of the soul in nature will
be said to hold only so far as that dependence reaches. If the text founds the
soul on the body without reference to circumstances, or treats psychophysical
and physical causality as one, T-C is weakened for the soul.

## f. Strongest opposing reading

Heidegger, GA 20 § 13 (pp. 165–74), with what speaks for him in Husserl:
*Ideen I* § 152 (lowest level); § 8 (all objects refer back to sense-objects
as "primal objects"); § 18 c ("the real world constitutes itself in levels");
§ 49 e and § 53 (the order of founding, "physio-psychic"); § 35 (the human
being "as material body on which new strata of being are built"); § 62; and
Husserl's own words "founded" and "lower level" in § 56 h. The reading: the
form of circumstances is itself the thing's form, so that carrying it upward
proves Heidegger's point, the person understood from the thing.

## g. Parts

Opening (no heading; about 1,200 words): received view and holders; anomalies
1 and 8; thesis; texts and omissions; method; one sentence on the history of
the text.

1. **The senses of the word** (about 3,000). Programme of *Ideen I* §§ 149–52; §§ 4–10 of *Ideen II*: constitution before theory; spontaneous and sensuous synthesis; primal objects; the numbered senses (presentation, realization, institution, comprehension, intersubjective constitution, self-constitution). Result: the sense that carries the concept of reality is realization.
2. **The thing and its circumstances** (about 4,000). §§ 15–17; the spring and the pyramid (descriptive stretch); schema and manifestation; levels as iteration; § 18: the body among the circumstances; conditionality; the kinaesthetic "if–then" as motivation. Result: the three-term form; two senses of "level".
3. **Body and soul: the form carried over** (about 4,000). §§ 30–33, 36, 40–42, 46: sensation fields as states; the soul as unity of manifestation that does not schematize; history; three kinds of circumstance; "quasi-causality"; founding defined by circumstances; realization through empathy. Result: what changes in each term; the soul real only "in connection".
4. **The person and what motivates** (about 4,200). §§ 49–58; Beilage X: the surrounding world as circumstance; motivation; the servant (descriptive stretch); expression before founding (§ 56 h); § 57 the form a third time; § 58 the ego "from life". Hardest case: § 61 and § 62. Tension h.1 shown.
5. **Relative and absolute** (about 3,000). § 56 d; § 47; § 53; § 64 read through the form: relativity as dependence on circumstances; individuation; what "absolute manifestation" means. The prediction reported.
6. **The stratified reading at full strength** (about 3,500). GA 20 § 13 reconstructed; ordered replies; what Heidegger saw (objection turned); the close (A12–13).

Total own prose aimed at: about 22,000 words.

## h. Tensions to show and leave standing (guide M9)

1. The person is a reality constituted under subjective circumstances (§ 57) and is not a unity of manifolds at all (§ 58; § 64). Both stand.
2. Motivation is a rule of its own kind, opposed to causality (§ 56 f), and causality itself is "dissolved" into motivations (§ 56 d).
3. The soul is "nothing further" than the unity of its properties (§ 30) and has "its unity in itself" (§ 33).
4. Whether the lowest data (sensation) are constituted or simply pregiven (§§ 8, 10, 54).

## Revision after the review (2026-11-14)

The fresh-context reviewer showed (a) that the sentence of p. 57 is said of spatial things; (b) that Husserl himself calls the ascent "the constitutive order" (305) and says the apprehensions "were built on one another" (174); (c) that § 64 does not follow from § 33: the soul has inner circumstances and is still nature, the person's circumstances are "extended outer" ones (§ 34 n.), and § 64's first ground is the individuation of consciousness (300–01). T-C stands in its first clauses (the form and its three terms; three senses of founding; motivation at both ends). Its last clause now reads: § 64 has two roots, the absoluteness of consciousness and the relativity that defines reality, and states where the form applies without remainder. Consequence (d) is kept; "the last consequence of § 15" is withdrawn. Prediction (e): borne out, with the body's privilege unforeseen (study 3.7; Register 12).
