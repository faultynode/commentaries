## 2. Desiderata

- Ricœur, "Analyses et problèmes dans *Ideen II* de Husserl"; Merleau-Ponty, "Le philosophe et son ombre"; Sokolowski, *The Formation of Husserl's Concept of Constitution*: nearest precedents; each would show what part of the reading of reality as a form of circumstances is already in print.
- Nenon and Embree (eds.), *Issues in Husserl's Ideas II*: whether the three senses of "founding" have been distinguished.
- A clean copy of the English *Ideas II*, for exact English pages (item 8).
- Husserl, *Natur und Geist* (1919, Hua Mat IV; 1927, Hua XXXII): whether the later lectures state the relation between the two roots of § 64 found in part 5.
- Hua III/1 in a reviewed conversion, and the *Ideas I* passages on the individuation of consciousness, to test the claim that § 64's first ground is "brought from the first book" (that claim rests on Heidegger's report, GA 20, 171, and on § 64's wording; the passage of *Ideen I* was not re-read for this study).

## 4. Directions opened but not taken

- The intersubjective constitution of objective nature (§ 18 d–h, § 51), treated only through §§ 47 and 64.
- The constitution of time, which § 10 names as the lowest constitution.
- § 60 on the "I can" and § 59 on abilities: the practical side of the personal ego, which bears on whether a "faculty" is a property under circumstances (Husserl's notes to § 57 doubt it).
- § 63 on parallelism, where the limit of conditionality is argued in detail.
- Supplement XIV, from which one sentence is used ("Es ist das Wesen von Realität überhaupt, Einheit zu sein von Abhängigkeiten"); the supplement as a whole was not read.
- The relation of "absolute manifestation" (supplement X) to the later doctrine of habituality.
- The date of each formulation: the first study's theme, deliberately left aside here.

## 5. Places where the notes appear wrong or in tension

- `constitution-ideen-ii-layers.md` treats *Bekundung* as the mode of givenness of spirit against *Darstellung* for nature (from supplement X). The main text uses *Bekundung* and *Beurkundung* first for the real properties of the thing (41–44, 121–23) and distinguishes them from presentation by adumbration (131). The opposition of supplement X is between manifestation through appearances and "absolute" manifestation. Corrected in the note (item 14).
- The same note gives pages from the markers alone; several are one page high (item 7).

## 6. What the primary texts changed

- Plan, candidate T-A (two concepts, presentation for things and manifestation for persons): refuted by § 15 d and § 30, where manifestation is the thing's and the soul's.
- Plan, thesis T-C, clause "§ 64 … states where the form applies without remainder and where it stops": kept. The stronger wording of the first draft, that § 64 "follows from" the outer and inner circumstances of § 33 and is "the last consequence of § 15", was withdrawn after the review: § 33 gives inner circumstances to the soul, which § 64 counts as general so far as it is nature; the person's circumstances are "extended outer" ones (§ 34 n.); and § 64's first ground is the individuation of consciousness (300–01). Part 5.4 now speaks of two roots.
- The opening called the sentence of p. 57 one "about constitution in general". Its context is the constitution of spatial things; the opening now says so and adds the general sentence of supplement XIV (391).
- The draft said that motivation occurs first in § 18. It occurs in § 9 (19) and § 15 (40).
- The draft said that the order thing, body, soul is "the order of the exposition". Husserl calls it "the constitutive order" (305) and says the apprehensions "were built on one another" (174). Part 6 now grants this and limits it to the naturalistic system of experiences.
- Supplement XII (the prediction text) added the privilege of the body ("Alles Reale ist körperlich") and the soul's "Eigenwesen", neither foreseen.
- § 58 keeps the comparison with the thing in the sentence after "aus Leben"; added to 4.7.

## 12. The prediction

Text: Hua IV, supplement XII, part II, §§ 1–3 (340–46), read after the plan was fixed. Predicted: (i) the reality of the soul determined by dependence on circumstances, not as a layer on the body; (ii) psychophysical dependence separated from the causality of things; (iii) the soul in nature only so far as that dependence reaches. Result: (ii) and (iii) borne out in so many words (343–46); (i) borne out in substance ("Seine Realität weist sich aus in der Abhängigkeit von Umständen"; body and soul each "nicht Realität für sich", 341), with the qualification that Husserl calls this dependence "founding" ("die sie fundierende Leibesrealität", 343). Not predicted: the privilege of the body as the corporeal ("Alles Reale ist körperlich", 342), which favours the layered reading; and the soul's "Eigenwesen, das allen Kausalitäten … vorhergeht" (346). Effect: the definition of founding by circumstances stands; the reciprocity claimed for it in 3.6 was limited.

## 13. The review

A subagent in a fresh context, given the study and the primary files, (1) argued the layered reading, (2) checked ten quotations, (3) named the weakest claim.

(1) Its case, from passages the draft had not cited: § 12 (lower and upper stratum), § 14 (animal realities "fundierte Realitäten" with material ones as "Unterstufe", the psychic as "neue Systeme von Eigenschaften"), § 49 (the kinds of apprehension "aufeinander gebaut"), § 49 a (the soul "eine bloße Schicht"), supplement I, p. 305 ("Der konstitutiven Ordnung folgend"), § 56 h ("höhere Einheit zweier realer Einheiten", "Gebilde zweier Stufen"), § 55 (position-takings built on lower strata), § 30 (faculties "aufgebaut"); that the sentence of p. 57 concerns spatial things; that § 33 ends with the concrete unity of body and soul; that § 58 keeps the thing-model. One uncited passage in the study's favour: supplement XIV, "Es ist das Wesen von Realität überhaupt, Einheit zu sein von Abhängigkeiten".

(2) Ten quotations (six block quotations from Hua IV, two from GA 20, one from *Ideen I*, one from supplement XII): all found at the cited places and faithfully rendered, with one discrepancy: "körperlich" (342) had been rendered "bodily"; now "corporeal". Six further quotations verified in passing.

(3) Weakest claim: that § 64 follows from § 33's outer and inner circumstances and that spirit is "the one unity that is among its own circumstances".

Revised: the opening (scope of the sentence of p. 57; supplement XIV added); 2.6 (earlier occurrences of "motivation"); 3.1 (§ 14 acknowledged); 3.6 (§ 33's result); 3.7 ("corporeal"); 4.7 (the thing-comparison in § 58); 5.4 (rewritten: two roots, three reasons against a derivation); 5.6; 6.1 (Heidegger's wording restored); 6.2 (the further passages for the layered reading); 6.3 (the constitutive order granted and limited); 6.4 ("keeps the levels and rejects the sum"); 6.5 (the result weakened; "not a building" withdrawn). Not adopted: that § 30's "aufgebaute Vermögen" and § 55's "Unterschichten" count against the study; they are instances of the first and third senses of "level" distinguished in 4.5.
