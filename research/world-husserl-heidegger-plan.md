# Working plan: the concept of world in Husserl and Heidegger (study)

Prepared 2026-11-12 under `prompts/study-prompt.md`. Not for publication.

## 1. Question and received view

Question: what kind of being does the world have, given that it is not a thing in the world, and in what sense does it belong to the subject? In Heidegger's words (SZ 64): "Does 'world' not thus become something 'subjective'?"

Received view: Husserl's world is the totality of objects, a correlate of consciousness that can be thought away (*Ideas I* § 49); Heidegger's world is a whole of significance that belongs to Dasein's being. The first is theoretical and the second practical.

## 2. Thesis

Both authors hold that the world is not the sum of things, is not itself a being, and is "subjective". They differ in the modality of the bond between world and subject. For Husserl the world is something that holds (a thesis, a belief, a ground of validity). For Heidegger it is something for the sake of which (a whole of possibilities projected in freedom). The difference shows in what each makes of the thought that the world might not be.

The notes (`transcendence-husserl-heidegger.md`, Synthesis C.3, D.2) place the cut at "world as *Umwillen* against world as horizon of objects" and date Husserl's strong statement to 1936. The study contests the second half: the decisive Husserlian determination is not horizon but validity, and it is there in 1913.

## 3. Strongest opposing reading

Heidegger's own (GA 20 §§ 12, 22; SZ § 21), repeated in the notes: Husserl's world is present-at-hand nature with value predicates added. Rests on *Ideas I* § 27 ("vorhanden", "Sachenwelt"), § 49 ("Dingwelt"), § 55 ("All der Realitäten"), *Ideas II* § 50 ("einem Kerne nach ... 'vorhanden'").

## 4. Parts

1. The world as thesis: Husserl 1913. *Ideas I* §§ 27–32, 47–49, 55.
2. The world as ground: Husserl 1912–1936. *Ideas II* §§ 11, 49–50; Hua VIII; Hua IX; CM §§ 7, 28, 60; *Crisis* §§ 28, 37–38, 43, 53, Beilage.
3. The world as significance and as for-the-sake-of: Heidegger 1925–1929. GA 20 §§ 12, 22, 24; SZ §§ 14, 18, 40, 43, 69c; GA 24 § 15; letter of 1927; GA 26 §§ 11–12; VWG II; GA 27 § 36.
4. The opposing reading: the world as present-at-hand nature.
5. Validity and possibility: parallels, differences, root, objections, close.

## 6. Tensions to show and leave standing

- Husserl: is the non-being of the world thinkable? Yes in 1913 and 1923/24; "apodictic in its way" and "not arbitrarily destructible" in the *Crisis* appendix; "a priori world-experiencing" in CM § 60.
- Husserl: are value and use characters as immediate as thing characters (*Ideas I* § 27; *Ideas II* § 11) or built on a present kernel (*Ideas II* § 50)?
- Heidegger: is world the context of equipment (SZ §§ 15–18) or is that analysis "of subordinate significance" (VWG n. 55)?
- Heidegger: does world "exist" (GA 24) or is it "nothing" (GA 26)?
