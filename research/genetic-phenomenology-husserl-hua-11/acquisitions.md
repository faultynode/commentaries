## Decisive

**1. Husserl, *Analysen zur passiven Synthesis* (Hua XI, ed. M. Fleischer,
Nijhoff 1966): the "Einleitung des Herausgebers" (pp. XIII–XXIV) and the
"Textkritischer Anhang" (pp. 441–532).**
- Held otherwise: the volume's text is held; these two parts are absent from
  the file (the table of contents lists them at `:53` and `:306`–`:317`).
- Part or whole: these parts suffice. Within the Anhang, the "Vorbemerkung"
  (p. 441), the notes to the main text (pp. 443–479) and the "Nachweis der
  Originalseiten" (pp. 530–531) matter most.
- Bears on: every claim about *when* Husserl applies the genetic method. The
  main text is a composite of the course of WS 1920/21 and its versions of
  SS 1923 and WS 1925/26. From the file one can date § 16 onward (after
  Christmas 1925) and the "first version" of §§ 12–40 (1920/21). One cannot
  date §§ 1–15 or §§ 40–48, including the two programmatic statements about
  genesis (the end of § 4 and § 48).
- What it could settle: whether the lectures applied a genetic method before
  the treatise of 1921 named it, or whether the passages that speak of
  genesis were written into the course afterwards. If § 48 is of 1920/21, the
  practice precedes the programme. If it is of 1925/26, the lectures were
  rewritten in the programme's light. The study's question ("as it is
  applied") turns on this.
- Without it: the study treats the main text as an undated composite of
  1920–26, confines every claim about development to the layers the file
  dates, and states that limit in the opening.

## Significant

**2. Husserl, *Analyses Concerning Passive and Active Synthesis: Lectures on
Transcendental Logic*, tr. Anthony J. Steinbock (Kluwer, 2001; Collected
Works IX). Edition data from memory.**
- Held otherwise: no English of Hua XI in any form.
- Part or whole: whole, as a markdown file with its page numbers; the
  translator's introduction is wanted as much as the text.
- Bears on: the citation rule (both editions for every quotation), and the
  literature. Steinbock's introduction is, from memory, the fullest account
  in English of the static/genetic distinction as these lectures use it, and
  he rearranges the course (with the Hua XVII opening and the Hua XXXI part).
- What it could settle: whether the thesis is already his; and the English
  page for each quotation.
- Without it: every quotation from Hua XI is "(my translation)", the English
  edition is logged as not in the corpus, and Steinbock is named at the level
  of title and of Bégout's quotation only.

**3. Husserl, *Aktive Synthesen: Aus der Vorlesung "Transzendentale Logik"
1920/21* (Hua XXXI, ed. R. Breeur, Kluwer 2000).** Title and year from the
series list in the Hua XXXIII file (`:5422`).
- Held otherwise: no.
- Part or whole: the main text and the editor's introduction.
- Bears on: how the 1920/21 course went on from passivity to judgment, i.e.
  whether the "genesis" of the lectures was meant to run through to active
  synthesis (the treatise's third kind of genesis) or stops at the passive
  sphere.
- What it could settle: whether the lectures execute the treatise's
  programme (passive genesis, ego-participation, active genesis) or only its
  first item. It also dates the first version independently.
- Without it: the study is confined to the passive part and says so; claims
  about the course as a whole rest on Husserl's one-line description to
  Ingarden.

**4. Elmar Holenstein, *Phänomenologie der Assoziation. Zu Struktur und
Funktion eines Grundprinzips der passiven Genesis bei E. Husserl*
(Phaenomenologica 44, Nijhoff 1972).** Data from Hua Dok IV, no. 1495.
- Held otherwise: no.
- Part or whole: the chapters on the method and on primal association and
  affection would suffice; the whole is better.
- Bears on: Parts on §§ 26–38. The standard monograph on the very sections
  the study reads most closely.
- What it could settle: whether the distinction the reading found, between
  stratifying the living present and explaining awakening from the null
  sphere, and the reading of § 34's rule, are already in the literature.
- Without it: the study's placing against precedent rests on Bégout alone,
  and the opening says that Holenstein was not consulted.

**5. Donn Welton, *The Other Husserl: The Horizons of Transcendental
Phenomenology* (Indiana UP, 2000).** Data from the index record of Crowell's
review.
- Held otherwise: only through Drummond's critical report (NYB III).
- Part or whole: the chapters on static and genetic method (from memory,
  Part II) suffice.
- Bears on: the received view. Welton is, with Steinbock, the account of
  genetic method a specialist will expect the study to answer.
- What it could settle: whether Welton reads the lectures' practice or the
  programme, and whether he already separates the two.
- Without it: Welton is cited only as Drummond reports him.

**6. Husserl, *Erfahrung und Urteil. Untersuchungen zur Genealogie der
Logik*, ed. L. Landgrebe (4th ed., Meiner PhB 280, 1972); English
*Experience and Judgment*, tr. Churchill and Ameriks (1973).** Data from Hua
Dok IV, nos. 616 and 738.
- Held otherwise: no.
- Part or whole: the Introduction (§§ 1–14) and §§ 15–21 and 42 suffice.
- Bears on: the method. The book was worked up from this course (Kern, Hua
  XIV `:514`), and its Introduction states a method of regress and
  dismantling that the literature reads back into the lectures.
- What it could settle: whether the vocabulary of "Abbau" and of the regress
  to pre-predicative evidence is the course's own (the study has it so far
  only in Beilage XIX, February 1926) or belongs to the later redaction.
- Without it: the study does not use *Erfahrung und Urteil* to say what the
  lectures do, and says why.

## Useful

**7. Iso Kern, *Husserl und Kant* (Phaenomenologica 16, 1964)** (Hua Dok IV
no. 1588): the sections on static and genetic constitution. From memory the
source of the pair validity-founding / genesis-founding in the literature.
Would confirm or correct where Bégout's "Geltungsfundierung" reading comes
from. Without it: the pair is attributed to Bégout's use only.

**8. Anthony J. Steinbock, "Husserl's static and genetic phenomenology:
Translator's introduction to two essays", *Continental Philosophy Review* 31
(1998)** (index record; volume number from memory), with his translations of
the Hua XI treatise and Hua XIV Beilage I. Largely covered by no. 2.

**9. Mary Jeanne Larrabee, "Husserl's static and genetic phenomenology", *Man
and World* 9 (1976)** (index record; volume number from memory). An early
statement of the received view in English.

**10. Antonio Aguirre, *Genetische Phänomenologie und Reduktion* (1970); Guido
Antonio de Almeida, *Sinn und Inhalt in der genetischen Phänomenologie E.
Husserls* (1972)** (Hua Dok IV nos. 1015, 1023). Two monographs on genetic
phenomenology from the years after Hua XI appeared. Would show how the
edition was first received.

**11. Husserl's letter to Natorp of 29 June 1918** (in the *Briefwechsel*,
vol. V; reference from memory) on having overcome "static Platonism". Bernet,
Kern and Marbach paraphrase it, but their note 127 is empty in the file.
Without it the letter is cited as they report it.

**12. Paul Natorp, *Allgemeine Psychologie nach kritischer Methode* (1912)**
(from memory; a lead from the abstract of Visser and van Mazijk 2026 and
from Luft's held article). Would test whether Husserl's "erklärende"
phenomenology answers Natorp's reconstructive, "genetic" psychology. The
study infers no influence without it.

**13. Jacques Derrida, *Le problème de la genèse dans la philosophie de
Husserl* (PUF 1990; written 1953–54; from memory).** The fullest critical
reading of genesis in Husserl; the held 1959 essay gives its conclusion.

**14. English editions for the citation rule:** *Cartesian Meditations*, tr.
Cairns (1960); *Formal and Transcendental Logic*, tr. Cairns (1969). From
memory. Without them these works are quoted in my translation and logged.

**15. A reviewed conversion of Hua XVII.** The held file is machine-converted
