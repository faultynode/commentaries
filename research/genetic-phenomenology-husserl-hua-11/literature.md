# Literature: genetic phenomenology in Hua XI

Written 2026-11-15. Sources of the candidates: `study_prep.py lit` (two
queries, in `literature-candidates.md` and `literature-candidates-2.md`; 120
records, harvested and unreviewed), the held secondary works in
`../sources/sources/`, and the *Edmund Husserl Bibliography* (Hua Dok IV) for
titles and years. Each entry says at what level the work is known. A work
known from an abstract or a title supplies no position beyond that abstract
or title.

## 1. Who holds the received view

The received view, as the study will state it: genetic phenomenology is the
inquiry into the *history* of constitution. Static analysis describes
finished constitutive systems with the object as its guide; genetic analysis
asks how those systems, and the habitualities of the ego, came to be, by
laws of motivation and association. The view is drawn from the 1921 treatise
(Hua XI 336–345) and from *Cartesianische Meditationen* §§ 37–39, and the
lectures of Hua XI are taken as its first execution.

- **Bernet, Kern and Marbach**, *Edmund Husserl: Darstellung seines Denkens*
  (1989), ch. 7; English 1993. **Read in full as far as the English file
  goes** (it breaks off in the chapter; the German file holds the rest and is
  located, not yet read). Genetic phenomenology "inquires into the very
  origin" of finished systems; it concerns "the history of the 'I'" and "the
  history of its objects"; it "has to follow" static phenomenology "as a
  second step"; and "Husserl did not elaborate clearly enough the methodology
  of genetic, constitutional analysis". They note the ambiguity over whether
  time-constitution counts as genesis. Their evidence from Hua XI is the
  treatise and two pages of the lectures (118, 128).
- **Ni Liangkang**, "Horizontal-Intention: Time, Genesis, History" (in *On
  Time*, ed. Lohmar and Yamaguchi). **Abstract and hit lines read.** Static
  phenomenology has "transverse" intentionality as its theme, genetic
  phenomenology the "horizontal" one; the historical problem is "an extension
  of the genetic problem". Year of the volume not yet taken from the file.
- **Moran and Cohen**, *The Husserl Dictionary* (2012), entries "Active and
  passive genesis", "Genetic phenomenology". **Entries located, first lines
  read.** Genesis as "the laws-like processes whereby some experienced thing
  comes to be constituted with the particular sense it has".
- **Osswald**, "El concepto de pasividad en Edmund Husserl" (2014).
  **Abstract only.** "The main subject is not anymore the description of the
  essential notes of a phenomenon but rather the search for its origins."
- **Steinbock**, translator's introduction to *Analyses Concerning Passive
  and Active Synthesis* (2001) and "Husserl's static and genetic
  phenomenology: Translator's introduction to two essays" (1998). **Title
  only** (the 2001 volume from memory). Held at second hand: Bégout quotes
  him for the claim that Husserl was engaged in genetic analyses before
  phenomenology became conscious of a genetic dimension.
- **Welton**, *The Other Husserl* (2000) and "Husserl's Genetic Phenomenology
  of Perception" (1982). **Title only**, and through **Drummond**'s report
  (read in full, §§ I–III): the distinction as the systematic expansion of
  Husserl's method beyond its Cartesian formulation.
- **Larrabee**, "Husserl's static and genetic phenomenology" (1976). **Title
  only.**

On the date of the turn: **Bernet and Lohmar**, introduction to Hua XXXIII
(2001), **one paragraph read**: the breakthrough was "bisher meist
angenommen" to lie in the course of WS 1920/21 and lies already in the Bernau
manuscripts of 1917/18.

## 2. Who has argued for something near the candidate theses

The candidates are fixed only in plan.md, after the gate. Three directions
came out of the reading, and each has a neighbour.

**(a) In the lectures the genetic method works by abstraction and
reconstruction from the finished present, not by narrating a history.**
- **Bégout**, *La Généalogie de la logique* (2000). **Two sections read in
  full** (Introduction ch. II on static and genetic; Part I ch. II on
  beginning and abstraction); the sections on association, affection and
  unity, error, and the Conclusion located and not yet read. This is the
  nearest precedent. Bégout holds that the *Analysen* need "l'abstraction
  idéalisatrice", that the first-in-itself is "construit à partir des
  éléments dérivés" by a necessary conjecture, that "Überschiebung" is the
  method, and that static and genetic are joined in a regressive and
  progressive "généalogie". He reads static analysis as
  "Geltungsfundierung" from manuscripts of the 1930s, and treats the lectures
  as "Leçons de 1921". What a study could add or correct, on the reading so
  far: the layer of each passage (1920/21, 1921, 1925/26, February 1926);
  Husserl's own rule in § 34 against "unverständliche Substruktionen"; the
  difference between the stratification of the present (§§ 27–35) and the
  theory of awakening (§§ 36 ff.); and Hua XIV Beilage I's middle term
  between static analysis and the genesis of a monad. Whether these amount to
  a thesis Bégout does not state can be said only when his remaining sections
  are read.
- **Bower**, "Husserl's motivation and method for phenomenological
  reconstruction" (2014). **Abstract only.** Reconstruction of "consciousness's
  immemorial past" within genetic phenomenology.
- **Luft**, on Husserl and Natorp, "Intuition versus Reconstruction, Or
  Intuition and Reconstruction?" (NYB IV, 2004). **Held; headings only.**
- **Visser and van Mazijk**, "Natorp's Neo-Kantian Critique of Husserl and
  the Development of Genetic Phenomenology" (2026). **Abstract only.** Natorp's
  "genetic" method as regressive reconstruction, and Husserl's answer.

**(b) The lectures bring genesis in under a question of validity (the
in-itself of one's own past), so that genesis and validity are not two
departments.**
- **Bégout** again: static as validation, genetic as motivation; passive
  motivation founds "le valable ... non le valide". His section on error and
  the mixing of memories is not yet read.
- **Lerner**, "The Cartesian Meditations' Foundational Discourse" (NYB X,
  2010), § 2 "Validity Foundation and Genetic Foundation in the Ego's
  Self-Constitution". **Held; headings only.**
- **Yang**, "Husserl's Theory of Experience in Genetic Phenomenology:
  Passivity, Rationality, and Normativity" (2025). **Abstract only.** Passive
  synthesis has "a rational and normative character in a primitive sense".
- **Płotka**, "Husserl on How to Bridge the Gap Between Static and Genetic
  Analysis" (2022). **Abstract only.** The two methods are complementary;
  rests on the B III 10 manuscripts (the convolute of the 1921 treatise).
- **Kern**, *Husserl und Kant* (1964). **Title only** (Hua Dok IV no. 1588);
  from memory the source of the pair *Geltungsfundierung* / *Genesisfundierung*
  in the literature. A lead, not a fact.

**(c) The practice outruns, or differs from, the programme.**
- **Drummond**, "On Welton on Husserl" (2003). **Read in full, §§ I–III.**
  The dichotomy is between programmatic rhetoric and actual descriptions; the
  account of method catches up with the analyses. He does not examine the
  genetic method of the lectures.
- **Pugliese**, "Motivational Analysis in Husserl's Genetic Phenomenology"
  (2018). **Abstract only.** Motivation's "ambiguous status oscillating
  between a historical-empirical and a transcendental account".
- **Derrida**, "'Genèse et structure' et la phénoménologie" (1959; in
  *L'écriture et la différence*, 1967; English held) and *Le problème de la
  genèse dans la philosophie de Husserl* (written 1953–54, published 1990;
  from memory; not held). **Held essay not yet read.**

**On Hua XI itself and on association.**
- **Holenstein**, *Phänomenologie der Assoziation* (1972) and "Passive
  Genesis. Eine begriffsanalytische Studie" (1971). **Title only** (Hua Dok IV
  nos. 1495, 1494). The standard monograph on the matter of §§ 26–41.
- **Aguirre**, *Genetische Phänomenologie und Reduktion* (1970); **de
  Almeida**, *Sinn und Inhalt in der genetischen Phänomenologie E. Husserls*
  (1972); **Yamaguchi**, *Passive Synthesis und Intersubjektivität bei
  Husserl* (1982). **Titles only** (Hua Dok IV nos. 1015, 1023, 2335).
- **Kretschel**, on temporal synthesis and association (2017) and on
  remembering (2025); **Eldridge**, "The act of forgetting" (2020);
  **Geniusas**, "The Origins of Sedimentation" (2025); **Di Pinto**, on the
  "dynamics of horizons" in the *Analysen* (1990); **Fleischer**'s notice of
  her own edition (1968); **Shim**, "Presence and Origin: On the Possibility
  of the Static-Genetic Distinction" (2005); **Lohmar**, "On Some Motives for
  Husserl's Genetic Turn" (2018). **Abstracts or titles only**; see the
  candidates files.

## 3. What this allows the study to say

- The received view has named holders read at first hand (Bernet, Kern and
  Marbach; Ni in part; Moran and Cohen in part).
- The nearest precedent, Bégout, is held and partly read; it must be read to
  the end before a thesis is chosen, since a thesis that only repeats his
  "abstraction" would add nothing.
- Welton, Steinbock, Holenstein and Kern are the works a specialist will
  expect to see engaged, and none is held. They can be named as known by
  title, or through Drummond and Bégout, and no further.
