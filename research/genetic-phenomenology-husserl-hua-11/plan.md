## a. The question and the received view

**Question.** What does the genetic method do in the lecture text of Hua XI:
what kind of claim is a "genetic" claim there, and on what evidence does the
lecturer make it, in each of the layers the edition allows one to date
(1920/21, 1923, 1925/26)?

**Received view.** Genetic phenomenology is the inquiry into the *history* of
constitution. Static analysis describes finished constitutive systems with
the object as its clue; genetic analysis asks how those systems, and the
ego's habitualities, came to be. The view is drawn from the treatise of 1921
(Hua XI 336–345) and from *Cartesianische Meditationen* §§ 37–39, and the
lectures of Hua XI are taken to execute it. Holders, at the level read:
Bernet, Kern and Marbach, ch. 7 (read in full in English as far as the file
goes): genetic phenomenology "inquires into the very origin" of finished
systems, "the history of the 'I'" and "the history of its objects".
Steinbock, Translator's Introduction (§§ 1–2 read in full): the lectures are
"exemplary of his genetic method", "genetic method is at the heart of these
lectures", and the matter of genetic phenomenology "concerns monadic
individuation and its genesis". Welton, ch. 9 § F (read in full): the course
is Husserl's "first attempt to apply his newly found genetic analysis";
genetic analysis treats transformation as "a process of development in which
the earlier gives rise to the later". Bégout (five sections read): the
"Leçons de 1921" work by a regressive dismantling and a progressive
reconstruction.

## b. Anomalies (sentences the received view leaves unexplained)

1. **Dates.** The sentences of the main text that name the method ("als
   genetischer Analyse", 116; "statisch und genetisch", 220; consciousness as
   "nie abbrechende Geschichte", 219) stand in leaves of 1920/21 (Anhang, p.
   446), before the treatise "Genesis. 1921" and Hua XIV Beilage I (June
   1921). The treatise looks back on the course: "In den Vorlesungen sagte
   ich ... 'statische'" (340).
2. **The opening does not say it.** The course's statement of its task
   (Hua XVII 374) is "von unten aufsteigend zu zeigen"; Husserl's description
   to Ingarden has no "genetic"; Fleischer's introduction never uses the word
   for the method and unifies the lectures under the problem of the in-itself.
3. **A reversal between the versions.** 1920/21: "Die Phänomenologie der
   Assoziation haben wir von der Wiedererinnerung her angefaßt, und meines
   Erachtens muß sie von daher angefaßt werden" (289–290). 1925/26: the
   reproductive association is only "das für uns Erste"; recollection is put
   aside to be taken up "erst nachher in ihrer Genese" (128), and the
   exposition starts from the living present.
4. **"Wir tun so, als ob".** The "lowest genetic level" is reached by an
   abstraction stated as an as-if (150), not by going back in time.
5. **The rule of § 34.** Units that form without affection would be
   "unverständliche Substruktionen"; "alle Motive einer Theorie entspringen
   aus den Evidenzen der ersten Stufe", the phenomena of the "fertig
   strukturierten lebendigen Gegenwart" (163–165).
6. **Seeing limited.** "Wir sagen nicht, daß wir das 'an etwas erinnern'
   überall sehen, sondern nur, daß wir es in gewissen Fällen sehen" (121);
   "Ferner gibt sich das Phänomen selbst eine Genesis" (121).
7. **§ 37.** "eine theoretische Vorstellung zu entwerfen begonnen" (172–173);
   "Früher meinte ich ... diese Hypothese entbehren" (178); "Vom Schlaf weiß
   man nur durch Aufwachen" (178).
8. **The essential earlier.** In §§ 16–18 (1925/26) the genetic "Früher" is
   "wesensmäßig", and the genetic statement is restated at once as a
   structural one (72–76).
9. **Beilage XIX (12–14 Feb. 1926).** "Voran gegeben und evident ist immer
   die 'fertige' Struktur ... Wie kann man daraus eine ideale Genesis aus dem
   Chaos zeichnen?"; "Abbau", "Konstruktionen"; "Ein Fehler in den
   Vorlesungen" (412–415).
10. **Hua XIV Beilage I.** Three inquiries, not two; "Der Konstitution
    nachgehen ist nicht der Genesis nachgehen"; correlation "ist keine
    Bedingtheit der Genesis, es wird hier nicht das Bedingte aus dem
    Bedingenden"; yet the order of levels is called "genetische
    Betrachtungen" (38, 41).
11. **§ 48's own retrospect.** The work before Christmas gave "ein sozusagen
    statisches Verständnis"; the problem of genesis was taken up only for the
    self-objectivation of consciousness as to its past; the same "müßte nun
    auch für die Konstitution der Natur geleistet werden" (219–220). The end
    of § 4 announces the genetic task for the outer world "nur mit einem
    Wort" (24).
12. **"Keine Theorie".** Of the splitting of a blended memory: "keine
    Theorie, sondern eine in ihrer Intentionalität verstehbare Sachlage"
    (270); of the primal forms of association: "So bin ich genötigt, hierin
    Urformen der Assoziation zu sehen" (286).

## c. Candidate theses

**T1 (chronology).** The lectures do not apply the method of 1921; the
method of 1921 is a reflection on the course of 1920/21, and the association
theory usually quoted as its execution is a text of 1925/26. Explains 1, 2;
partly 3. Does not explain 4–9, 12. Precedents: Steinbock and Drummond
(practice before formulation), Bernet and Lohmar (Bernau). Adds only the
dating by leaf. True, and used, but too small to govern a study.

**T2 (the lectures are constitutive, not genetic).** By Hua XIV's criterion
the stratification of the living present follows constitution, not genesis,
so the core of 1925/26 is static in the treatise's sense. Explains 4, 5, 9,
10 (first half). Refuted by 10 (second half: the order of levels is itself
called "genetische Betrachtungen"), by 8 and by the necessary succession of
impression and retention, which Hua XIV gives as the example of genesis.
Precedent: Welton's constitutive/genetic pair. Rejected.

**T3 (seen and constructed genesis) — chosen.** In the lectures a genesis is
never narrated as a history. It is exhibited in two ways that the layers of
the text keep apart. In 1920/21 the analysis starts from an occurrence in
which a genesis is *seen* (one memory awakening another, a blended memory
coming apart) and goes back from it, by steps marked as compelled, to what
must already hold in the impression. In 1925/26 the order of exposition is
turned round: the living present is built up from below as a "systematic
genesis", which is possible only under an as-if and yields a genesis that is
*constructed*, never seen; § 34 gives the rule under which such a
construction may be asserted (its motives must come from what is evident in
the finished present), and the notes of February 1926 state the problem the
rule leaves. "Genetic" in Hua XI therefore names first a kind of evidence
(what Husserl in 1921 calls explanatory, that is, making-understandable,
knowledge), and "history" in § 48 names the idea that this evidence serves,
not a procedure the lectures carry out. Explains 3–9, 11, 12; with T1 as a
premiss, 1–2; and gives 10 its sense (the two halves of anomaly 10 are the
two ways). Does not explain: why the living present can be called "the most
universal genetic phenomenon" (138) while it is analysed as finished; what
the genesis of expectation is (Fleischer: the future "keeps the character of
the problem").

**Standing to the precedents.** Holenstein (1972) has § 34's rule and says
the abstraction is "keine konstruierende oder postulierende Spekulation"; he
says the distribution of the association section over 1923 and 1925/26
"cannot be made out". Bégout (2000) has the necessity of abstraction and the
regressive/progressive zigzag, but treats the text as "Leçons de 1921",
describes its method by the "Abbau" of manuscripts of 1930 and takes
"substructions" for a name of what the method produces. Steinbock (2001) has
both directions (regress, then ascent "from below") and identifies genetic
phenomenology with monadic individuation. The study adds: (i) the
distinction between a genesis seen and a genesis constructed, in Husserl's
own words (121, 163–165, 270, 286, 412); (ii) its assignment to dated layers
from Fleischer's table, which corrects Holenstein's "cannot be made out" and
Bégout's dating; (iii) the reading of the 1925/26 revision as a change in
the order of exposition, with its cost; (iv) the consequence for "history".
It grounds (i)–(iii) in texts the precedents did not use together: the
Textkritischer Anhang, the first version, Beilage XIX, Hua XIV Beilage I.

## d. Consequence

§ 48's "Bewußtsein ... ist eine nie abbrechende Geschichte" reads as the
statement of an idea under which the analyses stand (the passage is of
1920/21, in Husserl's later square brackets), not as a description of what
they have done. A specialist would have to stop saying that the lectures
"trace the history" of the monad or of its objects, that they "apply" the
static/genetic distinction of 1921, and that their method is the
"dismantling" of *Erfahrung und Urteil* (whose Introduction Landgrebe drafted
after 1935 from texts of 1929–34; in Hua XI "Abbau" occurs once, in February
1926). And the usual pair "static = finished structure, genetic = its
history" has to be replaced, for these lectures, by the pair "what is to be
seen in the finished present / what must be understood as its condition".

## e. Prediction

Text: Hua XI §§ 40–41 (pp. 184–191), "Die Motivationskausalität der
Erwartungen" and the strengthening and inhibition of expectation; hit lines
were opened during the survey, the sections were not read. The thesis
predicts that Husserl presents the genesis of an expectation as belonging to
the first kind: the motivation of what is expected by what has come is said
to be *evident* or *seen* in the phenomenon itself (a "because ... therefore"
given with the expectation), not introduced as a theoretical conception or
under an as-if; and that where he goes beyond this to conditions in the
impression he marks the step. If instead the sections derive expectation
from hypothetical pre-affective formations without marking it, or narrate an
acquired history of the ego, the thesis is weakened. To be read after this
plan is fixed; result reported in the study and in Register 12.

## f. The strongest opposing reading

The contrast between history and evidence is a false one. Husserl's
"history" was never a narrative: the treatise says that the "Geschichte des
Bewußtseins" gives "nur ... der Modus der Genesis" in which an apperception
of a type must have arisen (339); § 18 calls the genetic earlier essential;
§ 48 defines history as "stufenweises ... Konstituieren"; Hua XIV calls the
order of constitutive levels "genetische Betrachtungen" and speaks of
"genetisch höheren Stufen"; CM § 38 says the history "announces itself" in
the finished synthesis. On this reading the stratified living present *is*
the essential history, the "as-if" is only the ordinary abstraction of any
eidetic analysis of a dependent moment, § 37's "urstiftender Prozeß" and
"Sedimentordnung" are historical concepts at work in the lectures, and the
received view (history in the eidetic sense) stands. Passages: 73–76, 138,
177–178, 218–220, 339–340; Hua XIV 38; Hua I 112–113. It gets its own
stretch (Part 5) and is granted its true part: essential history is what the
lectures aim at; what does not follow is that the two ways of exhibiting it
are one, since Husserl himself separates them (121; 163–165; Hua XIV 41) and
asks in February 1926 how the one is to be had from the other.

## g. Parts

Working title: **Genesis without History? The Genetic Method at Work in
Husserl's Lectures on Passive Synthesis (Hua XI)**. Length: about 27,000
words (the user allowed extension beyond the 25,000 first assumed).
Method of analysis by stage is stated in each part (textual-historical;
descriptive re-performance on a carried example; confrontation).

- **Opening** (≈1,300). Received view and its holders; the turn (the text is
  a composite and its genetic sentences are of different years; its own rule
  of method ties genesis to evidence); thesis; texts and omissions; the
  method of the study; limits.
- **Part 1. The word and the layers** (≈4,800; textual-historical). 1.1 Four
  senses of "genetic" in the volume (causal-psychological, excluded;
  necessary succession, "wie Bewußtsein aus Bewußtsein wird"; order of
  levels; history of apperceptions and of the monad). 1.2 The course and its
  three versions; Fleischer's table (a table: pages, leaves, year, what
  stands there); what was "leider umgearbeitet". 1.3 Where the word stands in
  each layer (the opening; § 4; 116; § 48; §§ 16–18; § 26 ff.). 1.4 The texts
  of 1921 as a reflection on the course. Result: the question "what is
  applied" must be put to each layer. Ends on: what a genetic analysis is in
  the leaves of 1920/21.
- **Part 2. Genesis seen: the version of 1920/21** (≈5,200; exposition, then
  descriptive re-performance). 2.1 The question that brings association in:
  the in-itself of one's own past (§§ 23–25; first version nos. 16–17). 2.2
  The carried example: Sils Maria and the Fex valley; variation (a memory
  that does not split; a split that goes on). 2.3 "No theory": what is seen
  in the splitting; awakening as a relation given in the phenomenon. 2.4 The
  steps back: "must be approached from recollection"; primal forms "I am
  compelled to see"; the law of retention and "how is it to be understood".
  2.5 §§ 42–48 and the retrospect: static before Christmas, genetic for the
  past only; history as the idea. Ends on: what the first version declares
  urgent and leaves undone.
- **Part 3. Genesis built: the version of 1925/26** (≈6,200). 3.1 The new
  opening of § 26: Kant, a universal theory of genesis, first for us and
  first in itself, explanatory knowledge. 3.2 §§ 16–18: laws of passive
  genesis and the essential earlier (the seen genesis inside the present:
  impression and retention). 3.3 §§ 27–31: the living present built from
  below; "static" and "kinetic"; overlaying as a method. 3.4 §§ 32–33: the
  as-if. 3.5 § 34: two readings tried, one excluded; the rule. 3.6 §§ 35–38:
  gradation, the zero, a "theoretical conception", a hypothesis dropped. 3.7
  February 1926: "how can one draw an ideal genesis from it?"; the mistake in
  the lectures. Ends on: the rule needs a statement of what genetic claims
  are, which the lecturer had written in 1921.
- **Part 4. The programme of 1921 and the readers** (≈5,200; confrontation
  around one question: what makes a genetic claim evident?). 4.1 The
  treatise: laws of genesis, "history" as mode, explanatory against
  descriptive, the open question whether the genetic can be excluded. 4.2
  Hua XIV Beilage I: three inquiries; conditioned from conditioning. 4.3
  Where the lectures stand. 4.4 Holenstein; 4.5 Bégout; 4.6 Steinbock and
  Welton (each: what they have right, the step that does not hold). 4.7 The
  prediction (§§ 40–41). Ends on: the reading that says all this is history
  after all.
- **Part 5. History after all? The opposing reading and what remains**
  (≈4,300). 5.1 The opposing reading at full strength. 5.2 What is right in
  it. 5.3 What does not follow; the hardest case (time-constitution; the
  living present as "the most universal genetic phenomenon"; "urstiftender
  Prozeß"). 5.4 The later texts (FTL Beilage II; CM § 38; EU Introduction)
  with their dates. 5.5 What the lectures leave undone: the future, nature,
  the active part (Hua XXXI). Close per A12–A13: the title's question
  answered.

## h. Tensions to be shown and left standing (M9)

1. Whether the living present is itself a genesis or the finished ground of
   every genetic claim: "das universalste genetische Phänomen" (138) against
   "fertig strukturierte lebendige Gegenwart" (165) and Beilage XIX.
2. Whether there are pre-affective unities: § 34 tries both answers, ends
   with the rule, and Husserl's later note restricts the result to the living
   present; the Beilagen operate with the excluded assumption.
3. Whether static analysis can exclude the genetic at all (treatise 344;
   Hua XIV 41 with its note).
4. Whether the order of levels is "genetic": Hua XIV 38 says yes, Hua XIV 41
   gives a criterion (the conditioned comes to be from the conditioning) that
   the order of levels does not meet.
5. Whether the revision improved the course: "zum Teil verbessert, zum Teil
   verdorben"; "nicht immer aus guten Gründen ausgeschieden".
