### Misreading guards (Marion, *Réduction et donation*, on *Ideen I*)

1. **Marion's Ideen I is Biemel's 1950 text, not the 1913 text.** Biemel incorporated Husserl's later marginalia. A Marion quotation may carry words Husserl added after 1913. Check every quotation against Hua III/1 and the III/2 apparatus before attributing it to Ideen I (1913). Found so far: "νοεῖν" in §19 (R I n. 83) is a blue-pencil marginal note (Hua III/2, "Zu S. 43", D 1), not 1913 text.
2. **Who gives.** In Ideen I "gebend" qualifies acts and consciousness: originär gebende Anschauung, gebendes Bewußtsein, Gegebenheitsart / Gegebenheitsweise (mode of givenness of an object to consciousness). Gegebenheiten (plural) are givens. Marion's *donation* is the phenomenon's own giving ("ce qui se donne"). Mark each time whether a "donation" in R renders Husserl's act-side giving, the object's being-given, or Marion's self-giving phenomenon. The slide is Marion's move (see R I n. 83, "données (ou : donations ? *Gegebenheiten*)").
3. **The principle of all principles (§24) belongs to Part I, before the reduction.** §§18-26 defend eidetic intuition against empiricism; the principle legitimates every originarily giving intuition, essence-seeing included, and carries a limits clause ("aber auch nur in den Schranken, in denen es sich da gibt"). The reduction is Part II (§§27-62). Check the function of the principle before accepting Marion's uses of it as a principle of givenness or setting it against the reduction (chapter II).
4. **Ideen I's being-talk is regional and eidetic, not the question of being.** "Sein als Bewußtsein / Sein als Realität" (§42), "absolutes Sein" (§§44-49), "Seinsregion" (§33), "Seinssinn" (§55): these are Husserl's terms for a difference of regions and modes of givenness. Do not read SZ or GA 20 back into them, and do not take Heidegger's critique of them (as Marion reports it) for Ideen I itself.
5. **Ideen I's object is formal and wide.** "Gegenstand" is a title for essential connections of consciousness (§145, Hua III/1 336); formal ontology is the eidetics of "Etwas überhaupt" (§10) and is widened to values and practical objects (§147-148). Marion's charge that Husserl consecrates "objectivity" must be tested against this width; Marion himself makes it in chapter V. Keep *objectivité* (objectivity) and *objectité* (objectness, Gegenständlichkeit) apart.
6. **Ideen I's ego is the pure I.** §57: "eine eigenartige — nicht konstituierte — Transzendenz in der Immanenz" (Hua III/1 124); §80 relates experiences to it. "Je transcendantal" is the vocabulary of the Cartesian Meditations. The 1930 Nachwort, Ideen III (Hua V), the Krisis and CM are not Ideen I, even where Marion cites them together.
7. **Ideen I keeps the Logical Investigations' doctrine and corrects its self-description.** The Introduction (Hua III/1 4-5) names the LU a "first breakthrough" of pure phenomenology and denies that it is descriptive psychology; §3 n. (Hua III/1 14) replaces "Ideation" by a freer concept of essence-seeing; §3 n. (Hua III/1 16) defends "meine Lehre von der kategorialen Anschauung" against Külpe. Do not treat 1913 as a break with 1900-1901 where Ideen I itself claims continuity, nor as continuity where it corrects.
8. **Marion's verdicts on "presence" are Heidegger's and Derrida's categories.** "Metaphysics of presence", "accomplishment of metaphysics" are not Husserl's terms. Ideen I's own reserve against total presence is the inadequacy of thing-givenness and adequate givenness as an "Idee im Kantischen Sinne" (§§138, 143, Hua III/1 321, 331). Test "sans reste" against these.
