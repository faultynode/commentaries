### Misreading guards (Marion, *Réduction et donation*, on *Ideen I*)

1. **Marion's Ideen I is Biemel's 1950 text, not the 1913 text.** Biemel incorporated Husserl's later marginalia. A Marion quotation may carry words Husserl added after 1913. Check every quotation against Hua III/1 and the III/2 apparatus before attributing it to Ideen I (1913). Found so far: "νοεῖν" in §19 (R I n. 83) is a blue-pencil marginal note (Hua III/2, "Zu S. 43", D 1), not 1913 text; "als strömende Gegenwart" in §46 (R II n. 32; Hua III/2 "Zu S. 97", D 21) and "trotz dieser leibhaften Gegebenheit" in §46 (R II n. 44; "Zu S. 98", D 18) are copy-D additions, and in both cases Marion's argument rests on the added words. Commentary R repeats both as Husserl's.
2. **Who gives.** In Ideen I "gebend" qualifies acts and consciousness: originär gebende Anschauung, gebendes Bewußtsein, Gegebenheitsart / Gegebenheitsweise (mode of givenness of an object to consciousness). Gegebenheiten (plural) are givens. Marion's *donation* is the phenomenon's own giving ("ce qui se donne"). Mark each time whether a "donation" in R renders Husserl's act-side giving, the object's being-given, or Marion's self-giving phenomenon. The slide is Marion's move (see R I n. 83, "données (ou : donations ? *Gegebenheiten*)").
3. **The principle of all principles (§24) belongs to Part I, before the reduction.** §§18-26 defend eidetic intuition against empiricism; the principle legitimates every originarily giving intuition, essence-seeing included, and carries a limits clause ("aber auch nur in den Schranken, in denen es sich da gibt"). The reduction is Part II (§§27-62). Check the function of the principle before accepting Marion's uses of it as a principle of givenness or setting it against the reduction (chapter II).
4. **Ideen I's being-talk is regional and eidetic, not the question of being.** "Sein als Bewußtsein / Sein als Realität" (§42), "absolutes Sein" (§§44-49), "Seinsregion" (§33), "Seinssinn" (§55): these are Husserl's terms for a difference of regions and modes of givenness. Do not read SZ or GA 20 back into them, and do not take Heidegger's critique of them (as Marion reports it) for Ideen I itself.
5. **Ideen I's object is formal and wide.** "Gegenstand" is a title for essential connections of consciousness (§145, Hua III/1 336); formal ontology is the eidetics of "Etwas überhaupt" (§10) and is widened to values and practical objects (§147-148). Marion's charge that Husserl consecrates "objectivity" must be tested against this width; Marion himself makes it in chapter V. Keep *objectivité* (objectivity) and *objectité* (objectness, Gegenständlichkeit) apart.
6. **Ideen I's ego is the pure I.** §57: "eine eigenartige — nicht konstituierte — Transzendenz in der Immanenz" (Hua III/1 124); §80 relates experiences to it. "Je transcendantal" is the vocabulary of the Cartesian Meditations. The 1930 Nachwort, Ideen III (Hua V), the Krisis and CM are not Ideen I, even where Marion cites them together.
7. **Ideen I keeps the Logical Investigations' doctrine and corrects its self-description.** The Introduction (Hua III/1 4-5) names the LU a "first breakthrough" of pure phenomenology and denies that it is descriptive psychology; §3 n. (Hua III/1 14) replaces "Ideation" by a freer concept of essence-seeing; §3 n. (Hua III/1 16) defends "meine Lehre von der kategorialen Anschauung" against Külpe. Do not treat 1913 as a break with 1900-1901 where Ideen I itself claims continuity, nor as continuity where it corrects.
8. **Marion's verdicts on "presence" are Heidegger's and Derrida's categories.** "Metaphysics of presence", "accomplishment of metaphysics" are not Husserl's terms. Ideen I's own reserve against total presence is the inadequacy of thing-givenness and adequate givenness as an "Idee im Kantischen Sinne" (§§138, 143, Hua III/1 321, 331). Test "sans reste" against these.

9. **Check the direction of Marion's French against the German.** (Added 2026-10-20, chapter II.) R II n. 4 renders §153 "nichts von einer Begründung der ersteren [the constitutive phenomenologies] durch die letzteren [the ontologies]" as "n'impliquent aucunement que les premières fondent les secondes": the direction of grounding is reversed, and R II.1 ¶2 argues from the reversed version (Boyce Gibson's 1931 translation confirms the German). Marion also adds brackets that carry his thesis ("[comme étant] là" in §24, R II n. 28) and renders "in Anspruch nehmen" (§59, a rule about premises) as "revendiquer", the word of chapter VI's call.
10. **Marion cuts at Husserl's qualifying clause.** (Added 2026-10-20.) §49 "heißt zwar beides »seiend«, »Gegenstand«" is quoted without "aber ... nur nach den leeren logischen Kategorien gleich benannt" (Hua III/1 105); §111's "gegenwärtig seiendes" experience (cited as §110) without the memory and phantasy modifications it introduces (Hua III/1 251). Read every Ideen I sentence Marion quotes to the end of its period.

11. **Husserl's Cartesianism is in part corrected Cartesianism.** (Added 2026-10-20, chapter III.) Ideen I replaces doubt by the epoché (§31, doubt modifies the thesis into an antithesis; §32 limits the epoché "mit gutem Grunde"), switches off God (§58), and leaves "nulla »re« indiget" (§49) unattributed, glossed by the next sentence ("die Welt der transzendenten »res«"). Marion, after GA 20, runs Husserl into Descartes ("Descartes, donc aussi Kant et Husserl"). Test each "donc" against these sections.

12. **§43's "unüberbrückbarer Wesensunterschied" is not the consciousness/reality difference.** (Added 2026-10-20, chapter IV.) It lies between perception and image or sign representation (Hua III/1 89-90). Marion uses it for the cardinal difference of modes of being at R II n. 24 and III.2 ¶2, and counts it among the proofs at IV.4 ¶2 although his sentence there states its terms correctly. The chapter II and III units were amended on 2026-10-20 to say so.
13. **Marion classifies Husserl's difference two ways.** (Added 2026-10-20.) "Quasi ontologique", a difference of manners of being (R IV.4), and "strictement ontique", "rien d'ontologique" (R IV.5 ¶2, figure α). §42 speaks of "Seinsweisen" and §49 of a likeness only under empty logical categories. Hold Marion to the IV.4 reading when later chapters use the IV.5 one.

14. **Marion's "Beilagen IX-X" are autumn-1929 texts.** (Added 2026-10-20, chapter V.) Biemel's Hua III p. 394 ("Region des »absoluten Seins«", "unweltlich, unreal", "absolut eigenständige Region") = Hua III/2 Beilagen 37-38, "Herbst 1929", in copy D (Hua III/2 589-594). They postdate SZ. The 1913 text has "des in einem bestimmten Sinn »absoluten« Seins" (§76, Hua III/1 159) and "ein Eigensein" (§33).
15. **Ideen I restores the name "ontology" for the eidetic sciences that phenomenology switches off.** (Added 2026-10-20.) It does not identify phenomenology with ontology; "die wahre und echte universale Ontologie" is CM §64 (1929). The 1913 relation is dominion and clarification (§§62, 76, 149-153). §60 brackets "transzendent-eidetische" domains, which Marion renders "transcendantaux" (R V n. 49).
