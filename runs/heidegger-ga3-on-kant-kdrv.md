### Misreading guards (Heidegger, *Kant und das Problem der Metaphysik*, on Kant, *Kritik der reinen Vernunft*)

1. **Four voices in GA 3.** Kant quoted; Kant paraphrased under Heidegger's interpretation; Heidegger's fundamental ontology;
   and the later Heidegger of the marginalia (Handexemplar, 1930s on) and of the 1973 preface, who corrects the book from outside.
   Assign each claim before judging its relation to Kant. Marginalia and later prefaces are retrospective.
2. **"A, B" together hides revisions.** Heidegger cites both editions at once from Schmidt's parallel text, even where a passage
   exists in one edition only. A-only: the A-Deduction (A 95-130), the A Paralogisms. B-only: B 1-6, B 7, B 67-69, B 72, the
   B-Deduction §§15-27 (B 129-169), the Refutation of Idealism (B 274-279). His thesis of Kant's shrinking back (§31) rests on
   reading A against B; check which edition every quoted Kant sentence belongs to.
3. **Heidegger's words are not Kant's.** Finitude, ontological/ontic knowledge, understanding of being, transcendence,
   letting-stand-against, look, taking-in-stride, horizon (in Heidegger's sense), retrieval are Heidegger's; most re-inscribe a
   Kantian term. The commonest slide: Kant's *transzendental* (our a priori mode of cognition, B 25) becomes Heidegger's
   *Transzendenz* (finite reason's stepping-over to beings); Kant reserves overstepping (*überschreiten*) for the *transzendent*
   and says "transcendental and transcendent are not the same" (A 296/B 352). Never write as though Heidegger's terms were Kant's.
4. **Metaphysics, ontology, critique.** Kant calls the Critique a propaedeutic whose speculative use is "only negative"
   (A 11/B 25), a "treatise on the method" (B xxii), and gives up "the proud name of an ontology" for "a mere analytic of pure
   understanding" (A 247/B 303). He also says the Critique is about metaphysics (B xxii, B 22-24) and that transcendental philosophy
   is *Ontologia* (A 845/B 873). Heidegger's "laying of the ground of metaphysics" as positive ontology selects the second set;
   track what the first set does to his reading.
5. **The primacy of intuition is Heidegger's emphasis.** "All thought as a means is directed [to intuition]" (A 19/B 33) is Kant;
   that knowing is *primarily* intuiting (§4) is Heidegger's reading; Kant also says cognition arises only from the unification of
   both, neither preferred (A 51/B 75-76). Track whose premise it is.
6. **The root is unknown in Kant.** "Two stems ... which may perhaps arise from a common but to us unknown root" (A 15/B 29; cf.
   A 835/B 863). Heidegger names it the transcendental imagination. Test each step against where Kant places the imagination: a
   "blind though indispensable function of the soul" (A 78/B 103; Kant's own copy: "of the understanding"), one of three subjective
   sources with sense and apperception (A 94, A 115), mediating between "two extremes" (A 124), "an effect of the understanding on
   sensibility" (B 152).
7. **Time and self-affection.** Kant's self-affection (B 67-68, B 152-157, B-only) is the affection of inner sense by the
   understanding's synthesis, introduced to explain why we know ourselves only as we appear. Heidegger's time as pure self-affection
   (§34) radicalizes it; his mapping of the threefold synthesis (A 98-110, A-only) onto present, past and future (§33) is his.
8. **Commentary P stops at B §24.** For later loci work from Guyer-Wood and the German alone.
9. **Chronology and unverifiable Kant.** Heidegger had all of the Critique and the Nachlass published by 1928 (Akademie vol. 18,
   Erdmann's Reflexionen); GA 3 also cites the Prolegomena, Fortschritte, Heinze's and Pölitz's metaphysics lectures, the Logic,
   the Anthropology, the Critique of Judgment, the reply to Eberhard. None is in the corpus: report, do not verify. Retrospective
   Heidegger: Die Frage nach dem Ding (WS 1935/36, pub. 1962), Kants These über das Sein (1961), the 1973 preface's "Überdeutung",
   the marginalia. GA 25 (WS 1927/28, pub. 1977; English in corpus) is the lecture course GA 3 grew from.
10. **Kant's own diagnoses are often silent sources.** Several moves Heidegger makes "against the tradition" have a Kantian
    counterpart he does not cite: Kant's critique of Baumgarten's definition (A 843/B 871), his derivation of the three special
    metaphysics from reason (A 334-335/B 391-392), his critique of the mathematical method in philosophy (A 712-738/B 740-766),
    his coinage "ontotheology" (A 632/B 660). Name the silence where it bears on the reading.

11. **Kant's word, Heidegger's sense.** (Added 2026-10-23, §§9-15.) Single Kantian words carry Heidegger's theses: *ursprünglich*
    "original" (Kant: underived, A 32/B 48, B 40; and the intuitus originarius we lack, B 72) becomes "letting spring forth";
    *affizieren* at A 77/B 102 (space and time "must always also affect the concept of these objects") becomes time affecting pure
    thinking, the first step to §34; Kant's "function" (A 68/B 93) is treated as the *Logic*'s "reflection"; "the same function" /
    "the same understanding" (A 79/B 104-105) becomes a structural belonging-together; "pure synthesis brings itself to the concept"
    (GA 3, 63) where Kant has the understanding bring it (A 78/B 103); "reflecting concepts" (Reflexion 5051) against the Critique's
    "concepts of reflection" (A 269/B 325). Check each Kant word Heidegger glosses against its sentence.
12. **Kant's corrections and A-only frames.** (Added 2026-10-23.) Kant's own copy replaces "function of the soul" at A 78 with "function
    of the understanding" (Guyer-Wood note, Erdmann Nachträge XLI); Heidegger withholds it until §31. A 94 (A-only) makes sense,
    imagination and apperception three sources that "cannot themselves be derived from any other faculty of the mind"; Heidegger cites
    A 94 only for "synopsis". B 159 says the metaphysical deduction established the "origin" of the categories; B 145-146 concedes no
    further ground for the functions of judgment. Kant has a discipline for the unity of category and intuition: the "transcendental
    doctrine of the power of judgment" (A 136-137/B 175-176).

13. **The A-Deduction's own conclusion.** (Added 2026-10-23, §§16-18.) A 126-127 ends the A-Deduction with the understanding as
    "the legislation for nature" and "source of the laws of nature"; Heidegger's "the understanding gives up its preeminence" (§17, 84)
    contests it without reporting it. Kant says B changed "nothing" in propositions or proofs, only the presentation (B xxxvii-xxxviii),
    against Heidegger's decision to read A alone (69). *Vor der Apperzeption* (A 118): "prior to" (Guyer-Wood), "for" (18th-c. *vor* =
    *für*; 1787 corpus reads *für* at A 287/B 343), Heidegger's *coram* (from A-only A 249). A 124's "und": corpus German keeps it,
    Guyer-Wood follow the editors' sense; Heidegger's reading is defensible and carries §28. Kant's object = "something in general = X"
    (A 104, A 109); Heidegger's "a nothing" (72).

14. **Cut quotations in the Schematism.** (Added 2026-10-23, §§19-23.) GA 3 101 quotes "Dieser Schematismus ... ist eine verborgene
    Kunst" and omits "unseres Verstandes, in Ansehung der Erscheinungen und ihrer bloßen Form" (A 141/B 180), which SZ 23 quoted whole.
    GA 3 103 quotes "Das reine Bild ... aller Gegenstände der Sinne aber überhaupt [ist] die Zeit" and omits "aller Größen (quantorum)
    vor dem äußeren Sinne ist der Raum" (A 142/B 182; Kant's *vor* = "for", bearing on A 118). GA 3 102 cuts A 142/B 181 before Kant's
    positive definition of the pure schema (pure synthesis, determination of inner sense, unity of apperception). GA 3 91 cuts A 120's
    object, "das Mannigfaltige der Anschauung" (Taft's note). Kant: schematism = "the procedure of the understanding" (A 140/B 179);
    schema restricts the use of concepts (A 140/B 179; A 146/B 185). Heidegger never uses Kant's "monogram" (A 141-142/B 181).
    NB: §29 (GA 3 151) does quote "Verfahren des Verstandes mit diesen Schematen" (A 140/B 179) and "Schematismus unseres
    Verstandes" (A 141/B 180) and argues the understanding only "proceeds with" schemata the imagination produces; units amended.
    Also: §27 (136) quotes A 94's three underivable sources in full; §28 (143) cites A 291/B 347 *ens imaginarium*.

15. **Highest principle and ontology misreported.** (Added 2026-10-23, §§24-25.) GA 3 118 gives as the highest principle's
    "conclusive formulation" Kant's second sentence (A 158/B 197) without Kant's formal statement ("Every object stands under the
    necessary conditions of the synthetic unity of the manifold of intuition in a possible experience") and without its last clause
    ("and on this account have objective validity in a synthetic judgment a priori"). GA 3 124 says Kant replaces "the proud name of an
    ontology" with "Transcendental Philosophy"; A 247/B 303 says "a mere analytic of pure understanding" (transcendental philosophy =
    Ontologia is A 845/B 873). GA 3 122 quotes A 250 (A-only, cut in B) and stops before "cannot even be separated from the sensible
    data ... no object of cognition in itself" (A 250-251). "Herbeigerufen" (GA 3 118) is Kant's word for the reproductive imagination
    (A 156/B 195).

16. **Shrinking-back evidence.** (Added 2026-10-23, §§26-31.) B 103 (1787 text) still reads "Funktion der Seele"; the "Funktion des
    Verstandes" correction is a note in Kant's copy of A (Erdmann Nachträge XLI), date unknown. A xvii: subjective deduction subordinated
    because "something like a hypothesis"; the objective deduction "would come into its full strength", A 92-93 "sufficient by itself".
    A 97-98 (A-only) "three subjective sources ... which make possible even the understanding" supports Heidegger, not quoted. B 160-161 n.
    derives the unity of pure intuition "as the understanding determines the sensibility" (cited only in GA 3 146 n. 203, not reported).
    A 124: apperception "must be added to the pure imagination in order to make its function intellectual". A 649/B 677: imagination
    "perhaps even understanding and reason" as regulative idea of a "fundamental power". CPrR Typic: moral law has "no faculty but the
    understanding ... (not the imagination)". CPrR: respect presupposes sensibility "and therefore also the finiteness" (supports GA 3).
    B 151-152 (B-only) gives the imagination's definition, productive imagination, transcendental synthesis of the imagination; GA 3 131
    concedes the definition is B. GA 3 uses B 33 "uns Menschen wenigstens" two ways (§5 clarification; §31 symptom).

17. **Self-affection and the predicates of the I.** (Added 2026-10-23, §§32-35.) Every Kant statement of self-affection is B-only
    (B 67-69, B 152-158). B 153-154 names the understanding as the affecting power ("under the designation of a transcendental synthesis
    of the imagination") and rejects the psychologists' identification of inner sense with apperception; §31 (GA 3, 164) cites B 154 for
    B's retreat ("d. h. die Zeit"), §34 quotes only B 67-68. B 68 *ihrer Vorstellung* (1787 corpus): antecedent "Form der Anschauung",
    Heidegger's reading sound; B 67 names what is posited (representations of outer sense). GA 3 192 cuts A 123 before "just as all
    sensible intuition ... belongs to a pure inner intuition, namely that of time". "Die Zeit ... bleibt und wechselt nicht" is B 224-225
    only (GA 3 cites "A 182, B 224 f."). Kant's *stehend und bleibend* also at A 350 (I not "a standing and abiding intuition") and A 381
    (time "has in it nothing abiding"); A 382 I = "mere form of consciousness"; B 278 I has no persistent predicate to serve as "correlate
    for time-determination"; B 157 "only that I am"; A 107 "unchanging consciousness". GA 3 193 n. "A 548" = A 348 (OCR). A 126
    *durchspähen* is said of the understanding. A 118 allows only productive synthesis a priori; A 101-102 asks for a pure transcendental
    synthesis of reproduction (Heidegger's Riehl note defensible, A 118 unreported). A 100: sensibility "provides", synthesis "generates"
    (Heidegger merges them); space named with time at A 100, A 102. B 291 "time constantly flows" is from the argument that substance
    needs space; A 142/B 182 already puts space in the Schematism; B 293-294 states the General Remark's purpose (limits of
    self-cognition from inner sense). Kant's time "has only one dimension" (A 31/B 47). Mediation: Husserl's time lectures (ed.
    Heidegger, 1928) for just-now/right-away and *Vergegenwärtigung*. GA 41 175-176 explains the omitted "zugleich" in Kant's terms.
