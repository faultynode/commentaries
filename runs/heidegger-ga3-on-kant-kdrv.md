### Misreading guards (Heidegger, *Kant und das Problem der Metaphysik*, on Kant, *Kritik der reinen Vernunft*)

1. **Four voices in GA 3.** Kant quoted; Kant paraphrased under Heidegger's interpretation; Heidegger's fundamental ontology;
   and the later Heidegger of the marginalia (Handexemplar, 1930s on) and of the 1973 preface, who corrects the book from outside.
   Assign each claim before judging its relation to Kant. Marginalia and later prefaces are retrospective.
2. **"A, B" together hides revisions.** Heidegger cites both editions at once from Schmidt's parallel text, even where a passage
   exists in one edition only. A-only: the A-Deduction (A 95-130), the A Paralogisms. B-only: B 1-6, B 7, B 67-69, B 72, the
   B-Deduction §§15-27 (B 129-169), the Refutation of Idealism (B 274-279). His thesis of Kant's shrinking back (§31) rests on
   reading A against B; check which edition every quoted Kant sentence belongs to.
3. **Heidegger's words are not Kant's.** Finitude, ontological/ontic knowledge, understanding of being, transcendence,
   letting-stand-against, look, taking-in-stride, horizon (in Heidegger's sense), retrieval are Heidegger's; most re-inscribe a
   Kantian term. The commonest slide: Kant's *transzendental* (our a priori mode of cognition, B 25) becomes Heidegger's
   *Transzendenz* (finite reason's stepping-over to beings); Kant reserves overstepping (*überschreiten*) for the *transzendent*
   and says "transcendental and transcendent are not the same" (A 296/B 352). Never write as though Heidegger's terms were Kant's.
4. **Metaphysics, ontology, critique.** Kant calls the Critique a propaedeutic whose speculative use is "only negative"
   (A 11/B 25), a "treatise on the method" (B xxii), and gives up "the proud name of an ontology" for "a mere analytic of pure
   understanding" (A 247/B 303). He also says the Critique is about metaphysics (B xxii, B 22-24) and that transcendental philosophy
   is *Ontologia* (A 845/B 873). Heidegger's "laying of the ground of metaphysics" as positive ontology selects the second set;
   track what the first set does to his reading.
5. **The primacy of intuition is Heidegger's emphasis.** "All thought as a means is directed [to intuition]" (A 19/B 33) is Kant;
   that knowing is *primarily* intuiting (§4) is Heidegger's reading; Kant also says cognition arises only from the unification of
   both, neither preferred (A 51/B 75-76). Track whose premise it is.
6. **The root is unknown in Kant.** "Two stems ... which may perhaps arise from a common but to us unknown root" (A 15/B 29; cf.
   A 835/B 863). Heidegger names it the transcendental imagination. Test each step against where Kant places the imagination: a
   "blind though indispensable function of the soul" (A 78/B 103; Kant's own copy: "of the understanding"), one of three subjective
   sources with sense and apperception (A 94, A 115), mediating between "two extremes" (A 124), "an effect of the understanding on
   sensibility" (B 152).
7. **Time and self-affection.** Kant's self-affection (B 67-68, B 152-157, B-only) is the affection of inner sense by the
   understanding's synthesis, introduced to explain why we know ourselves only as we appear. Heidegger's time as pure self-affection
   (§34) radicalizes it; his mapping of the threefold synthesis (A 98-110, A-only) onto present, past and future (§33) is his.
8. **Commentary P stops at B §24.** For later loci work from Guyer-Wood and the German alone.
9. **Chronology and unverifiable Kant.** Heidegger had all of the Critique and the Nachlass published by 1928 (Akademie vol. 18,
   Erdmann's Reflexionen); GA 3 also cites the Prolegomena, Fortschritte, Heinze's and Pölitz's metaphysics lectures, the Logic,
   the Anthropology, the Critique of Judgment, the reply to Eberhard. None is in the corpus: report, do not verify. Retrospective
   Heidegger: Die Frage nach dem Ding (WS 1935/36, pub. 1962), Kants These über das Sein (1961), the 1973 preface's "Überdeutung",
   the marginalia. GA 25 (WS 1927/28, pub. 1977; English in corpus) is the lecture course GA 3 grew from.
10. **Kant's own diagnoses are often silent sources.** Several moves Heidegger makes "against the tradition" have a Kantian
    counterpart he does not cite: Kant's critique of Baumgarten's definition (A 843/B 871), his derivation of the three special
    metaphysics from reason (A 334-335/B 391-392), his critique of the mathematical method in philosophy (A 712-738/B 740-766),
    his coinage "ontotheology" (A 632/B 660). Name the silence where it bears on the reading.
