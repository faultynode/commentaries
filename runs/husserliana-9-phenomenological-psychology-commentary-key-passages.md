## Backfill conventions

- **Unit.** No segmentation list survives from the original run. A unit is the block's ordinal under its lowest heading: "§3d.5" is the fifth non-empty paragraph under the heading "d) Brentano as Pioneer…" of §3; "§24.3" is the third paragraph under §24. Bullet items and `---` rules do not count. In the Supplementary Texts the commentary's own heading labels are used: "EB1" is the First Draft, "EB2" the Second Drafting Attempt, "EB4" the commentary's "Fourth, Final Version" and "Amst." its "Amsterdam Lectures". So "EB1 II §2.2" is the second block under §2 of Part II of the First Draft, and "Beil. XI.1" the first block under Beilage XI. EB4 and Amst. follow the commentary's headings even where they misassign the text (see Corrections).
- **Found by.** Each reason opens with `commentary:`, `source:` or `both:`.
- **Citation.** The commentary cites only § numbers and headings (no Hua pages). The citation gives the §, then the Hua IX page taken from the source file's page markers, then `src L<n>`, the line of the passage's key sentence, read in this run.
- **Status.** Every row is `nominated`. Translations of German in this file are my working renderings.

## Source caveats for Stage 2 (and for any close reading)

- **Page numbers are ±1.** Pages come from HTML-comment page markers (`<!-- 4 PHÄNOMENOLOGISCHE PSYCHOLOGIE -->`, `<!-- EINLEITUNG 7 -->`, sometimes trailing a line, as `<!-- 16 -->`). A marker opens the page it names. There are 508 of them, but not one per page, so confirm a page against print before citing it in a reading.
- **Italics survive** as `*…*` (some 736 lines), mostly on proper names and on terms Husserl emphasizes. The commentary adds its own italics freely; e.g. the emphasis on "connection" and "hypothetically added in thought" in §2.6 is not in the source (src L381). Argue emphasis only from the source.
- **Spaced type is nearly gone.** Only 12 spaced words survive in the whole file (e.g. "W a s" [what], src L2364; "W i e" [how], L2838; "d a r", L2915), so most of the print's spaced emphasis was either turned into italics or lost. The file cannot settle a claim that a word is *not* emphasized.
- **False page markers.** In four stretches the print's margin line numbers came through as markers (`<!-- 5 -->` … `<!-- 35 -->`: src L2198–2206, L2585–2603, L2903–2915, L3098). They are not pages. The pages given here skip them.
- **Stage 2 searched** italic markup; spaced type; „…“ and ,,…'' quotation marks around single terms (279 lines; the file has no »…«); hyphenated coinages; Greek and Latin set off in the text (Latin: *res extensae*, *ego cogito*, *philosophia perennis* and the like; Greek: four lines only, in clean Unicode, ἓν ἐπὶ πολλῶν [one over many] in §9, src L1828–1829, and ἐποχή [epoché] in the EB Fourth Version, src L4841, neither nominated); the phrasing *heißt* [is called, means] (41 lines), *besagt* [says, means] (46), *das Wort* [the word], *der Ausdruck* [the expression], *wörtlich* [literally], *Terminus* [term]; and the names of other authors (Dilthey, Brentano, Kant, Locke, Hume, Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz, Heidegger). Hits were read by range only where they fell in a Stage 1 candidate or clustered (§§2–4, §§9, 11, 16, 21, 23, 28, 31, 34, 37, 42; the EB drafts; Beilagen VII, XXI, XXX, XXXI). **Not searched:** the editor's introduction (src L111–145), the text-critical apparatus (*Textkritischer Anhang*, src L11189 to the end), and most Beilagen beyond their openings. Variants and Husserl's later pencil additions (*Bleistift-E.*, e.g. src L11355) are recorded in that apparatus, so criterion (e) is under-searched.
- **Duplicate footnote labels.** In the Supplementary Texts, several footnote labels are defined twice (e.g. two `[^28]` at src L4290–4291 and two `[^29]` at L4395–4396, Heidegger's notes to the EB drafts). A note number does not resolve on its own; give the line.
- **Footnote seam in §37.** The body text runs on inside a footnote definition: `[^2]: < Beginn einer neuen Vorlesung. > nehmung richten, …` (src L3226) continues "…äußeren Wahr-" from L3222. No words seem lost, but quote §37's first pages from the print.
- **Mixed line layout.** Some stretches are reflowed paragraphs, others are hard-wrapped print lines with end-of-line hyphenation (about 1,460 lines end in a hyphen). A phrase can break across lines, so grep can miss it.
- **Editorial brackets.** The editor's angle-bracket supplements (`<mit dem>`, `<§ 37. …>`) are kept. Some § headings are bracketed or only in the body text, and the §21 heading survives only inside an HTML comment (`<!-- <§ 21. Stufenbau des Seelischen. > -->`, src L2535).

## Corrections to the commentary

- §21.1–5: the blocks set out a three-layer psyche: hyletic data and passive syntheses, then objectivating acts, then valuing and willing, which "presuppose objectivating consciousness". The source's §21 (Hua IX 130–133) draws a different line. It contrasts the psychic of the lower, passive levels, including the "somatological psychic" localized in the lived body, with the specifically spiritual, ego-centred acts. It then distinguishes two concepts of animation (*Beseelung*): the animation that makes the body a lived body (*Leib*), and the higher animation of the lived body by the personal spirit (*Geist*). Through these, spirit gains locality and reality in the spatial world and enters psychophysical causality. Hyletic data and a founding of valuing on objectivating acts are not mentioned there. Source: husserl-hua-9-phaanomenologische-psychologie-vorlesungen-sommersemester-1925.md:2543, :2563–2575.
- EB4 (headings "§1. The Twofold Sense…" through "§4. The Significance of the Concept of Purity", commentary lines 1377–1425) and "The Amsterdam Lectures: Closing Section" (lines 1435–1445): the commentary files §§1–4 under the Encyclopaedia Britannica's Fourth, Final Version. The source has these four titles as §§1–4 of the **Amsterdam Lectures** (Hua IX 302–310; contents src L94–98; body from src L5167). The Fourth Version (src L4740–5160, Hua IX 277–301) is numbered 1–16 under different titles. Conversely, the block the commentary heads "Amsterdam Lectures — Closing Section: Transcendental Phenomenology as Ontology, and Philosophy as Universal Science in Absolute Grounding" is Part III, §11, of the **Fourth Version** (Hua IX 296; src L5046–5052, "III. … 11. Die transzendentale Phänomenologie als Ontologie"). The two headings are swapped. As a result, the Fourth Version's §§1–10 and the Amsterdam Lectures' §§5–16 have no block. Source: husserl-hua-9-phaanomenologische-psychologie-vorlesungen-sommersemester-1925.md:83–110, :4740, :5046, :5167.
- Beil. VII.1: the block says the encapsulated-soul conception "has dominated psychology since Locke". The Beilage does not name Locke. The psychologist it names is Brentano, inclined "like Brentano himself" to take the human being as a double-layered being. Source: husserl-hua-9-phaanomenologische-psychologie-vorlesungen-sommersemester-1925.md:6683.
- Beil. XXVIII.3: the block quotes "Das Apriori Wir und unsere Umwelt geht allem Apriori vorher und schließt alles in sich" (the a priori of We and our environment precedes all a priori and contains everything in itself). The source file does not contain this sentence. No match for "geht allem", "schließt alles in sich" or "Apriori Wir" anywhere in the file, and none for "precedes all a priori" in the Scanlon file. The quotation is unverified and may be invented. Source: husserl-hua-9-phaanomenologische-psychologie-vorlesungen-sommersemester-1925.md:10320–10698 (Beilage XXVIII).
- Beil. XXX (heading and block): the block heads it "(to the III. Part of the EB Article): Phenomenology and Anthropology" and says it takes up phenomenology and anthropology "under the impact of Heidegger and Scheler". The source's Beilage XXX is "to p. 296: From the closing part of the third version of the Encyclopaedia Britannica article". It treats transcendental phenomenology as universal ontology (against "the Baumgarten-Wolffian ontology") and does not mention anthropology, Heidegger or Scheler. Source: husserl-hua-9-phaanomenologische-psychologie-vorlesungen-sommersemester-1925.md:10759–10770.

The source has "Phenomenological Psychology and Transcendental Phenomenology" (src :10888), which opens with the transcendental-phenomenological reduction, and "Individual Psychology and Intersubjective Psychology" (src :11024).
