## Backfill conventions

- **Unit.** No segmentation list survives from the original run. A unit is the block's ordinal under its lowest heading: "§3d.5" is the fifth non-empty paragraph under the heading "d) Brentano as Pioneer…" of §3; "§24.3" is the third paragraph under §24. Bullet items and `---` rules do not count. In the Supplementary Texts the commentary's own heading labels are used: "EB1" is the First Draft, "EB2" the Second Drafting Attempt, "EB4" the commentary's "Fourth, Final Version" and "Amst." its "Amsterdam Lectures". So "EB1 II §2.2" is the second block under §2 of Part II of the First Draft, and "Beil. XI.1" the first block under Beilage XI. EB4 and Amst. follow the commentary's headings even where they misassign the text (see Corrections). Since the fix of 2026-11-04 the headings match the source: "Amst. §1.1" is the first block under §1 of the Amsterdam Lectures, and "EB4 III §11.1" the first block under the Fourth Version's "III. … — 11. Transcendental Phenomenology as Ontology".
- **Found by.** Each reason opens with `commentary:`, `source:` or `both:`.
- **Citation.** The commentary cites only § numbers and headings (no Hua pages). The citation gives the §, then the Hua IX page taken from the source file's page markers, then `src L<n>`, the line of the passage's key sentence, read in this run.
- **Status.** Every row is `nominated`. Translations of German in this file are my working renderings.

## Source caveats for Stage 2 (and for any close reading)

- **Page numbers are ±1.** Pages come from HTML-comment page markers (`<!-- 4 PHÄNOMENOLOGISCHE PSYCHOLOGIE -->`, `<!-- EINLEITUNG 7 -->`, sometimes trailing a line, as `<!-- 16 -->`). A marker opens the page it names. There are 508 of them, but not one per page, so confirm a page against print before citing it in a reading.
- **Italics survive** as `*…*` (some 736 lines), mostly on proper names and on terms Husserl emphasizes. The commentary adds its own italics freely; e.g. the emphasis on "connection" and "hypothetically added in thought" in §2.6 is not in the source (src L381). Argue emphasis only from the source.
- **Spaced type is nearly gone.** Only 12 spaced words survive in the whole file (e.g. "W a s" [what], src L2364; "W i e" [how], L2838; "d a r", L2915), so most of the print's spaced emphasis was either turned into italics or lost. The file cannot settle a claim that a word is *not* emphasized.
- **False page markers.** In four stretches the print's margin line numbers came through as markers (`<!-- 5 -->` … `<!-- 35 -->`: src L2198–2206, L2585–2603, L2903–2915, L3098). They are not pages. The pages given here skip them.
- **Stage 2 searched** italic markup; spaced type; „…“ and ,,…'' quotation marks around single terms (279 lines; the file has no »…«); hyphenated coinages; Greek and Latin set off in the text (Latin: *res extensae*, *ego cogito*, *philosophia perennis* and the like; Greek: four lines only, in clean Unicode, ἓν ἐπὶ πολλῶν [one over many] in §9, src L1828–1829, and ἐποχή [epoché] in the EB Fourth Version, src L4841, neither nominated); the phrasing *heißt* [is called, means] (41 lines), *besagt* [says, means] (46), *das Wort* [the word], *der Ausdruck* [the expression], *wörtlich* [literally], *Terminus* [term]; and the names of other authors (Dilthey, Brentano, Kant, Locke, Hume, Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz, Heidegger). Hits were read by range only where they fell in a Stage 1 candidate or clustered (§§2–4, §§9, 11, 16, 21, 23, 28, 31, 34, 37, 42; the EB drafts; Beilagen VII, XXI, XXX, XXXI). **Not searched:** the editor's introduction (src L111–145), the text-critical apparatus (*Textkritischer Anhang*, src L11189 to the end), and most Beilagen beyond their openings. Variants and Husserl's later pencil additions (*Bleistift-E.*, e.g. src L11355) are recorded in that apparatus, so criterion (e) is under-searched.
- **Duplicate footnote labels.** In the Supplementary Texts, several footnote labels are defined twice (e.g. two `[^28]` at src L4290–4291 and two `[^29]` at L4395–4396, Heidegger's notes to the EB drafts). A note number does not resolve on its own; give the line.

## Considered and dropped

- §1.17: Dilthey's *Verstehen* (understanding) and the "causality of motivation". It is (a, c), but row 1 carries the Dilthey wording.
- §3e.3: the a priori psychology as "the most important" novelty of the *Logical Investigations*. It is (b), but row 4 states the thesis in its finished form.
- §4.8: "as a philosopher no one can be born, for essential reasons" (*Als Philosoph kann aus Wesensgründen niemand geboren sein*, src L964). The wording is striking, but nothing later depends on it.

## Corrections to the commentary

- §21.1–5: the blocks set out a three-layer psyche: hyletic data and passive syntheses, then objectivating acts, then valuing and willing, which "presuppose objectivating consciousness". The source's §21 (Hua IX 130–133) draws a different line. It contrasts the psychic of the lower, passive levels, including the "somatological psychic" localized in the lived body, with the specifically spiritual, ego-centred acts. It then distinguishes two concepts of animation (*Beseelung*): the animation that makes the body a lived body (*Leib*), and the higher animation of the lived body by the personal spirit (*Geist*). Through these, spirit gains locality and reality in the spatial world and enters psychophysical causality. Hyletic data and a founding of valuing on objectivating acts are not mentioned there. - §30.1–2 and §31.1–4 (close-reading run, 2026-11-27): the §30 blocks make "spatial primal presence" the theme of §30, set against "the merely co-meant, the anticipated, the past-related" and the horizons. The source's §30 (Hua IX 164–166) mentions primal presence only in its heading and in the parenthesis "(das urpräsente Wahrnehmungsfeld)" [the primally present perceptual field]. Its subject is the sensation fields. The momentary perceptual space presents itself in the visual sensation field, which is two-dimensional, while the space of perceived objects is three-dimensional. "It would be wrong to say" that objective space presents itself in a two-dimensional space, because "the possibility of motion" belongs to a space and "in the sensation field the concept of a motion makes no sense" (*im Empfindungsfeld der Begriff einer Bewegung keinen Sinn gibt*). The rest of §30 (the tactile field, which never merges with the visual one; data that change their shading function; the concept of the sensation datum; the "wondrous" wider class of functions such as reminding) is in the commentary, but filed as §31.1–4: the source's §31 begins only at "Gewisse bloß subjektive Daten" [certain merely subjective data], which is §31.5. The two-dimensional field and the argument from motion are in no block. The apparatus shows that the two mentions of primal presence are late: the § title is a red-pencil marginal note, and "(das urpräsente Wahrnehmungsfeld) oder die räumliche Urpräsenz" (164,7–8) an addition (src :11652). - §37.2 (close-reading run, 2026-11-27): the block says that "the *phenomenological-psychological* reduction is to be distinguished from the *transcendental* reduction", that the transcendental reduction "brackets the *being* of the world", and that the psychological one "brackets the world-positing" while remaining in the natural attitude, a "difference of attitude" that "separates two parallel sciences". §37 draws no such contrast.
