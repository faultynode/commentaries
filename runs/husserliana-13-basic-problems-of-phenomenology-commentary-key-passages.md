## Backfill conventions

- **Unit.** No segmentation list survives from the original run. A unit is the block's ordinal under its lowest heading: "§10.7" is the seventh non-empty paragraph under the § 10 heading. Bullet items and `---` rules do not count; the commentary has no bold lead-ins. The commentary covers §§1–42 only, none of the Beilagen (appendices) to Nr. 6, so neither does this list.
- **Found by.** Each reason opens with `commentary:`, `source:` or `both:`.
- **Citation.** § and Hua XIII page, then `src L<n>`, the source line of the passage's key sentence, read in the current file. The commentary itself cites no pages, so every page comes from the source file's page markers (see caveats).

## Source caveats for Stage 2 (and for any close reading)

- **Pages are ±1.** Pages come from HTML-comment page markers (`<!-- 116 TEXT NR. 6 (1910/11) -->`, `… 113 -->`), sometimes trailing mid-line. Some are missing (no marker for p. 148 between src L3326 and L3336). Footnote definitions are gathered after the block, so give a footnote the page of its reference marker, not of its definition line.
- **Footnote numbers are not unique in the file.** They restart and collide across texts (`[^38]` occurs four times in the volume; `[^1]` is reused inside Nr. 6 at L2745, L3257, L3432). Resolve a footnote only inside the Nr. 6 range.
- **Emphasis is lost.** Only 56 italic spans in Nr. 6, almost all Latin (*cogitatio*, *res extensa*, *nota bene*). Two non-Latin exceptions: *formale Ontologie* in the §9 heading and *transzendenten Einheiten* in the §35 heading. Husserl's spaced-type emphasis did not survive and the search found no spaced type, so the commentary's many italics are its own, and the absence of emphasis in the file proves nothing.

## Considered and dropped

These qualify but were dropped as weaker than the rows above, per the "strongest few" rule.

- §1.4: "Phenomenology is by no means psychology… it lies in a new dimension" (src L2754). It is (b), but row 1 carries the course's own thesis, and the psychology line is worked out in §§15 and 29.
- §5.1: the I as zero-point of the coordinate system (*Nullpunkt*, src L2836). It is (a), but the term is not developed further in Nr. 6.
- §8.4: "there are no two separate worlds, called things and souls" (*keine zwei getrennten Welten*, src L2982) and all-nature (*Allnatur*). It is (b), but it is not a step on which later sections depend.
- §10.4: "all natural science… is a priori bound to the real ontology" (src L3151). It is (b), but row 6 carries the same apriori claim in the form that the Avenarius critique uses.
- §12.1: the editor's note that the leaves from here on were moved into the 1912 "Introduction to Phenomenology" behind *Ideas* I, with the date "26 November 1910" (src L3257). It is (e), but it is a fact about the manuscript and carries no argument of its own.
- §13.1: "Thing, *res extensa*, is factually *res cogitans*…" (src L3269). It is (b, c), but row 9 takes the term together with the thesis it rests on.
- §14.5: "not a Nothing remains, but… the feeling in itself" (src L3305). It is (b), but it is the example that §14.7 and row 11 generalize.
- §15.3: the warning against confusing phenomenological perception with Lockean reflection, or inner perception (src L3349). It is (c), but §29.2 (row 20) carries the same line in the form of a term.
- §19.1: the pure I is left undecided, with Husserl's later note that it "is indeed the case" (src L3462; note 53 at L3496, inserted 1924 or later). It is (e), but the pure I is not taken up again in Nr. 6.
- §20.2: "nature and empirical knowledge are correlatives". It is (b), but it is an objection's premise (src L3488) that §42 answers.
- §24.3: the note on the first appearance of an apodictic critique of phenomenological experience (src L3632). It is (e), but the file truncates the note and attaches it to the chapter title (caveats), so it cannot be read in full here.
- §26.2: the two contradictory phenomenological recollections of the same perceivings. It is (b), but it is the example that §27.1 generalizes.
- §27.2: the bullfinches and the gnat, the expectation that fails. It is (b), but it is illustrative and dependent on §26.
- §31.3: the identity of the datum across acts is itself "a phenomenologically given, a fact of phenomenological experience" (src L3851). It is (b), but §37 (rows 26–27) makes the claim into its principle.
- §34.3: "so far not even been seen by anyone" (src L3967). It is a priority claim, folded into row 23.
- §35.7: "every experienced thing as such is an index for a certain regulation of consciousness" (src L4029). It is (b), but it restates row 24.
- §36.1: the question whether the reduction restricts to the single consciousness. It is (b), but it only poses the question that rows 27–30 answer.
- §38.3: empathy is not even fantasy-imaging; the note that "an empty appresentation" (*eine leere Appräsentation*) necessarily stands in front (src L4226). It is (a), the term's first appearance in Nr. 6, but it is only a note, and row 28 carries the section.
- §39.3: the presentified now and the Roons restaurant (src L4172; the editor's gloss L4229); probably replaced in 1921 by an appendix. It is (b, e), but it defends row 29 and does not advance the argument.
- §42.2: whether phenomenology could win its cognition "without preceding natural cognition", and the existence-connections (*Daseinszusammenhänge*) that apriori consciousness-cognition might yield only when applied to natural cognition (src L4276). It is (b), the course's last question, but it sharpens row 32 and adds no new step.

- §13.2: the block says the cut is made "without thereby performing an *abstraction* in the sense of a *distinctio* of essentially non-self-sufficient and inseparable moments of a concretum". The source names the foil: "ohne damit eine Abstraktion in dem Sinne der *distinctio realis* Humes … zu vollziehen" [without thereby performing an abstraction in the sense of Hume's *distinctio realis*]. The block drops both "realis" and the attribution to Hume, and so the contrast that gives *distinctio phaenomenologica* its name. Husserl's "realis" is odd for Hume, whose distinction of inseparable moments is a distinction of reason; flag it, do not repair it.
