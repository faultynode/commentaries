## Backfill conventions

- **Unit.** No segmentation list survives from the original run. A unit is the block's ordinal under its lowest heading: "§15b.2" is the second non-empty paragraph under the heading "b) Movability and Changeability…" of §15. Bullet items and `---` rules do not count; bold lead-ins that are not headings do. In the Editor's Introduction, "EI.n" counts blocks under "## Editor's Introduction" and "LC.n" blocks under "### Later Changes to This Plan".
- **Found by.** Each reason opens with `commentary:`, `source:` or `both:`.
- **Citation.** § (and subsection letter), Hua IV page as the commentary uses it (the First and Second Sections cite no pages, so their pages come from the source file's running heads), then `src L<n>`, the source line holding the key sentence, read for this list.

## Source caveats for Stage 2 (and for any close reading)

- **The German file includes the editor's introduction** (Marly Biemel, "Einleitung des Herausgebers" [Editor's Introduction], src L350–455, pp. XIII–XX, with roman page markers), so Stage 2 covered it.
- **Pages are ±1.** Pages come from running-head comments (`<!-- PHÄNOMENOLOGISCHE UNTERSUCHUNGEN ZUR KONSTITUTION 15 -->`) at page tops. No running head survives for pp. 45, 111, 145, 179, 192, 205, 211, 212, 216, 218, 220, 225, 226, 230, 232, 234, 240, 245, 273. Only p. 111 was checked (see next item); the others may be dropped markers, not dropped text.
- **A seam at p. 111 (src L2196).** §28 breaks off mid-word ("bloße intentionale Ein-" [mere intentional uni-]) and runs straight on into a later sentence ("…fassen kann, vielmehr: ich bin auch darin…" […can grasp, rather: I am also in it…]). The end of §28, the §29 heading and the opening of §29 are missing. The commentary's §28.1 (end) and §29.1 cannot be checked against this file. Whole-file greps for fragments of the lost text found it nowhere else: the §29 title fragments "bleibenden Meinungen" [lasting opinions] and "Niederschläge im reinen Ich" [deposits in the pure ego] occur only in the contents (L165); "monadisch getrennt" [monadically separated] (§28.1) occurs nowhere; "intentionale Einheiten" [intentional unities] occurs only at L5231, in a Beilage (Supplement), in another context; and "Habitus"/"habituell" [habitus, habitual] occur only from L3244 on, never in §§28–29.
- **Italics survive** (about 620 lines with `*…*`; about 160 with bold), so emphasis can be argued from, with care: some spans are broken by stray asterisks (e.g. src L3327).
- **Spaced type survives in part**, inside italics and often garbled: `*I c h*` (L2138), `*e n t - *` (L2720), `*f ü r*` (L3327). Searchable, but its absence proves nothing.
- **Line format is mixed.** The First Section is mostly hard-wrapped with end-of-line hyphens (about 630 lines); later many paragraphs sit on one line (L1663 carries much of §18f; L7397 carries a long stretch of the text-critical apparatus). Quote by sentence, not by line.
- **Headings are inconsistent.** §28 is plain text (L2193), §29 has no heading (see above), §30 is bold (L2356). Grep the contents (L57–300) for § titles, not the body.
- **§34 heading damage (src L2709, L2715).** The body heading reads "naturalistischer und humanistischer[^1] Einstellung" [naturalistic and humanistic attitude], with a conversion-era note about a misprint. The contents (L175) has "personalistischer" [personalistic], as the commentary does. Do not quote the body heading.
- **OCR slips seen in passing:** "Siningegenstände" for *Sinnengegenstände* [sense-objects] (§8 heading, L733); "Im aktuellen Gebiet" for *taktuellen* [tactual] (L2819); "Kollegten" (L352); "Steinchenschen" (L431). Not repaired.
- **The text-critical apparatus is present** (src L7209–7600, keyed "page,line"), so additions Husserl made in the Landgrebe typescript (1924–28) can be identified. Rows 4 and the Third Section rows use it.
- **Stage 2 searched** italic and bold markup, spaced type, single terms in „…“ and ,,…'' quotation marks, hyphenated coinages and connectives (*Wenn-So*, *weil-so*, *physio-psychisch*), definitional phrasing (*heißt* [means], *besagt* [says], *nichts weniger als* [anything but], *nennen wir* [we call]), and the names of other authors (Descartes, Kant, Leibniz, Dilthey, Ebbinghaus, Lipps, Spinoza, Hobbes, Avenarius). It could not search p. 111.
- **Lexicon.** `synthesis/lexicon.json` has 311 Husserl entries; 179 occur in the commentary. Most first hits are ordinary uses in the editor's introduction or §§1–4. ## Considered and dropped

These qualify but were dropped as weaker than the rows above, per the "strongest few" rule.

- §3.2: the theoretical attitude as "living in" the act. Row 5 carries the attitude change with a wording stake.
- §9.3: aesthetic synthesis, with Husserl's note that Kant had only this synthesis in view (src L767). It is (a, c), but the note is a promissory footnote.
- §10.1, §10.5: spatial phantom and sensation datum; αἰσθητὰ πρῶτα (first perceptibles). Row 7 takes the phantom where it is defined.
- §12.4: Descartes on *extensio* (src L903). It is (c), but agreement, not correction.
- §15e.2: "real properties are eo ipso causal". It is subordinate to row 8.
- §18g.3–4: God's view and "distorting glasses"; the thing as a rule of possible appearances. Strong, but row 11 carries the chapter's step.
- §22.1, §23: the pure ego as ego-pole, and Descartes's "magnificent Meditations" (src L2102). It is (a, c), but the term is settled in *Ideen I*, and row 12 carries the chapter's new step.
- §26.1: Leibniz on the monad's involution, and Kant's "I think must be able to accompany" (src L2157, L2164). It is (c), but the borrowings are glossed, not contested.
- §32.3: "the soul does not schematize itself". It is (b), but it applies row 7's distinction, and row 14 carries the chapter's turn.
- §34.3: two apprehensions, psychological and human-scientific. It is (b), but §49 restates it in full, and the body heading in the file is damaged (see caveats).
- §46.1: "introject" in quotation marks (src L3081). The word is already in §42 (src L3020) and §45 (src L3075); it is taken up at §49a (row 20).
- §50.9: the stimulus (*Reiz*) takes on "a fundamentally new sense" on the ground of the person–surrounding-world relation (Hua IV 190, src L3284). It is (a, b), but row 26 carries the contrast of motivation and causation.
- §56d.1: "all nature … completely dissolved into a web of immanent motivations". It is (b), but the block marks it as an aside, and row 26 states the thesis.
- §56h.5–9: the book "ensouled" by a meaning; the wording as the "body" of the sense. It is (d), but row 27 takes the same analysis where its term is fixed.
- §58.3: the ego "can be more and other than the ego as apperceptive unity". It is (b), but it prepares row 28 and has no wording claim.
- §59.1: the ego as a system of "I can". It is (a), but row 28 reads the "I can" where it is tested.
