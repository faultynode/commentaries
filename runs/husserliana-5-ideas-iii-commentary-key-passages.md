## Corrections to the commentary

- §9.2–9.3: the blocks give, as the continuation of §9, the text "the true method is …" and "phenomenology is therefore nothing less than mere descriptive psychology …". The source has these at the end of §11 (Hua V 69–70), where the commentary gives them again as §11.12. Source §9 instead goes on to criticize psychological descriptions: the "act, content, object" catchwords, the failure to distinguish presentation from the modes of presentification, the "origin" questions. It then says that phenomenological concepts are valued not for "verbal univocity" but for fitting essences seen in intuition, with words only indicating a direction or serving as pictorial aids (n. 4 cites Pfänder). The commentary has none of this. Source: husserl-hua-5-ideen-iii-die-phanomenologie-und-die-fundamente-der-wissenschaften.md:947, 949, 953 (§9); :1133, 1137 (§11).
- §15.2: "the science of 'origins,' of the 'mother' of all cognition". The source has the plural in quotation marks, "der „Mütter“ aller Erkenntnis" (of the "mothers" of all knowledge), with "„Ursprünge“" (origins) also in quotation marks. The singular loses the figure that row 15 nominates. Source: husserl-hua-5-ideen-iii-die-phanomenologie-und-die-fundamente-der-wissenschaften.md:1226.
- §19.4: "bringing a noematic essence to relief in the intuitively given". The file reads "ein noetisches Wesen zur Abhebung zu bringen" (to bring a *noetic* essence to relief). The surrounding argument (coincidence of word-meaning with the intuitively given; §20's "noematic of intuition") makes "noematic" the likelier sense, so this may be a conversion error in the file rather than a commentary error. It cannot be settled without the print, so check before either is quoted. Source: husserl-hua-5-ideen-iii-die-phanomenologie-und-die-fundamente-der-wissenschaften.md:1414.
