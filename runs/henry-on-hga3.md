### Misreading guards (Henry, *L'essence de la manifestation* Section II, on GA 3)

1. **Three voices in P.** GA 3 is Heidegger interpreting Kant. A sentence Henry quotes with "dit Heidegger" may be Kant cited by Heidegger, Heidegger's paraphrase of Kant, Heidegger's own thesis, or an objection Heidegger raises in order to answer it. Fix which before judging Henry's use. Henry treats GA 3 as the paradigm statement of the "philosophy of transcendence", Heidegger's own ontology, and sets aside the Kantian frame and the interpretive violence Heidegger admits.
2. **Henry's categories are not Heidegger's.** "Ontological monism", "the essence" objectivating itself, "the milieu of exteriority", "phenomenological distance" and "receptivity" as the essence's reception of itself belong to Henry's frame. Never write that GA 3 is monist or posits exteriority as though these were GA 3's terms.
3. **Objectivation is not letting-stand-over-against.** The 1953 French renders *Gegenstehenlassen* as "objectivation" and *Vorstellen* as "représentation". Both shade a letting into a producing of an object. Henry's argument builds on that shading ("l'essence s'objecte l'horizon"), so the rendering counts as his move wherever he leans on it. Check each use against *Gegenstehenlassen*, *Sich-zuwenden-zu* and *Vorstellen*.
4. **Self-affection is not Henry's auto-affection.** GA 3 §34 (188-194) makes time as pure self-affection form the "from-itself-toward ... and back-to-itself" that first constitutes selfhood. It is not the affecting of a self already there, and it is not the non-ecstatic self-feeling Henry calls auto-affection. Henry's verdict that Heidegger's self-affection is really affection by a horizon is Henry's thesis; test it against §34, not against Henry's restatement.
5. **Taking-in is not empirical reception.** GA 3 defines finitude as dependence on taking-in (*Hinnahme*), but makes pure intuition a taking-in that gives itself what it takes, and warns explicitly against the narrow concept of taking-in as receiving something present (§32, 173-174). When Henry presses "receptivity" as the unsolved problem, check whether he reimports the narrow concept Heidegger set aside.
6. **The primacy of intuition is Heidegger's Kant.** GA 3 §4 (21, 23) says knowing is primarily intuiting, as an interpretation of Kant. Henry turns it into Heidegger's own unquestioned premise and the tradition's (R §23, note 62). Track whose premise it is.
7. **Chronology.** Henry read GA 3 in the 1953 French translation of the second edition (1951). He could not know the GA 3 marginalia (1991), the 1973 preface, the Kant lecture course GA 25 (1977), or GA 24 (1975) and GA 26 (1978). Mark any use of these as retrospective.
8. **Kantbuch and *Holzwege* are different Heideggers.** R §§17-21 read the 1942-43 essay on Hegel's concept of experience (*Holzwege*, 1950); R §§22-36 read the 1929 Kantbuch. Henry treats both as one philosophy of transcendence. Note where the turn between them matters, for instance on forgetting: in GA 3 §42 (233) forgetting belongs necessarily to the understanding of being and is answered by retrieval (*Wiedererinnerung*), not yet the epochal withdrawal of being.

9. **Original time is not intuition minus temporality.** GA 3 §32 (175) makes time "in one the forming intuiting of what it intuits" and calls imagination original time because it lets the now-sequence spring forth (175-176). Henry's split of the intuited succession from the intuiting, and his claim that what matters in time is its intuitive and not its temporal character (R §24 ¶11), is his thesis against GA 3, not a reading of it.

### Glossary (fixed for this run)

- Henry *étant* and Heidegger *Seiendes*: "beings", "a being". (Commentary R's "the existent" is not adopted.)
- Henry *l'essence*: "the essence" (of manifestation), always Henry's term.
- Henry *objectivation*: "objectivation" when reporting Henry; Heidegger *Gegenstehenlassen*: "letting-stand-over-against" (Commentary P's rendering).
- Henry *réceptivité*: "receptivity". Heidegger *Hinnehmen*/*Hinnahme*: "taking-in". Kant *Rezeptivität*: "receptivity" (marked as Kant's where it matters).
- Heidegger *Selbstaffektion*: "self-affection". Henry *auto-affection*: "auto-affection". Henry's *affection par soi*: "affection by itself" (time affected by nothing but itself, through the horizon it forms); *affection de soi*: "affection of itself" (time as what is affected). They spell out Heidegger's two phrasings, *Selbstaffektion* and *reine Affektion ihrer selbst* (GA 3 §34, 189). The French "auto-sollicitation" renders *Sich-selbst-angehen*: "going-at-oneself". The 1953 French renders *reine Selbstaffektion* as "auto-affection pure" and *Affektion ihrer selbst* as "affection pure de soi" (Henry n. 103, R §31): Henry's central term reaches him through the translation of Heidegger's.
- Heidegger *Vernehmbarkeit*: "perceptibility" (Henry *perceptibilité*). *Angebot*: "offer" (Henry *offre*). *Anblick*: "look" (Henry *vue*). *Bild*: "image". *Schema-Bild*: "schema-image". *Versinnlichung*: "making-sensible" (Henry *transposition sensible*). *Dawider*: "the against".
- *Vorstellen* / *représentation*: "representing", "representation".
- Henry *phénoménalité*, *manifestation*, *révélation*: "phenomenality", "manifestation", "revelation". Henry *monisme ontologique*: "ontological monism". *Conscience naturelle*: "natural consciousness".
- Retained untranslated: Dasein (being-there).
