## Pair-specific notes
(misreading guards, glossary decisions, edition mappings, settled once and kept)

### Misreading guards (Henry, *L'essence de la manifestation* Section II, on GA 3)

1. **Three voices in P.** GA 3 is Heidegger interpreting Kant. A sentence Henry quotes with "dit Heidegger" may be Kant cited by Heidegger, Heidegger's paraphrase of Kant, Heidegger's own thesis, or an objection Heidegger raises in order to answer it. Fix which before judging Henry's use. Henry treats GA 3 as the paradigm statement of the "philosophy of transcendence", Heidegger's own ontology, and sets aside the Kantian frame and the interpretive violence Heidegger admits.
2. **Henry's categories are not Heidegger's.** "Ontological monism", "the essence" objectivating itself, "the milieu of exteriority", "phenomenological distance" and "receptivity" as the essence's reception of itself belong to Henry's frame. Never write that GA 3 is monist or posits exteriority as though these were GA 3's terms.
3. **Objectivation is not letting-stand-over-against.** The 1953 French renders *Gegenstehenlassen* as "objectivation" and *Vorstellen* as "représentation". Both shade a letting into a producing of an object. Henry's argument builds on that shading ("l'essence s'objecte l'horizon"), so the rendering counts as his move wherever he leans on it. Check each use against *Gegenstehenlassen*, *Sich-zuwenden-zu* and *Vorstellen*. (Amended 2026-10-04 after reading the 1953 text: the translators write "ob-jectivation", hyphenated in 33 of 38 uses, to keep the "against" visible; Henry's own prose drops the hyphen. They render *Hinnahme* as "réceptivité", *Selbstheit* as "ipséité", setting-before as "se pro-poser".)
4. **Self-affection is not Henry's auto-affection.** GA 3 §34 (188-194) makes time as pure self-affection form the "from-itself-toward ... and back-to-itself" that first constitutes selfhood. It is not the affecting of a self already there, and it is not the non-ecstatic self-feeling Henry calls auto-affection. Henry's verdict that Heidegger's self-affection is really affection by a horizon is Henry's thesis; test it against §34, not against Henry's restatement.
5. **Taking-in is not empirical reception.** GA 3 defines finitude as dependence on taking-in (*Hinnahme*), but makes pure intuition a taking-in that gives itself what it takes, and warns explicitly against the narrow concept of taking-in as receiving something present (§32, 173-174). When Henry presses "receptivity" as the unsolved problem, check whether he reimports the narrow concept Heidegger set aside.
6. **The primacy of intuition is Heidegger's Kant.** GA 3 §4 (21, 23) says knowing is primarily intuiting, as an interpretation of Kant. Henry turns it into Heidegger's own unquestioned premise and the tradition's (R §23, note 62). Track whose premise it is.
7. **Chronology.** Henry read GA 3 in the 1953 French translation of the second edition (1951). He could not know the GA 3 marginalia (1991), the 1973 preface, the Kant lecture course GA 25 (1977), or GA 24 (1975) and GA 26 (1978). Mark any use of these as retrospective.
8. **Kantbuch and *Holzwege* are different Heideggers.** R §§17-21 read the 1942-43 essay on Hegel's concept of experience (*Holzwege*, 1950); R §§22-36 read the 1929 Kantbuch. Henry treats both as one philosophy of transcendence. Note where the turn between them matters, for instance on forgetting: in GA 3 §42 (233) forgetting belongs necessarily to the understanding of being and is answered by retrieval (*Wiedererinnerung*), not yet the epochal withdrawal of being.

9. **Original time is not intuition minus temporality.** GA 3 §32 (175) makes time "in one the forming intuiting of what it intuits" and calls imagination original time because it lets the now-sequence spring forth (175-176). Henry's split of the intuited succession from the intuiting, and his claim that what matters in time is its intuitive and not its temporal character (R §24 ¶11), is his thesis against GA 3, not a reading of it.

10. **Henry's French is the translators' before it is his.** Since 2026-10-04 every K quotation has been checked against the 1953 text. Translators' renderings Henry's argument rests on: "se montre" and "comprendre" for *faßbar* and *begreifen* (K 279), with "comprendre" also for *verstehen* (K 283); "caractère intuitif" for *Anblickcharakter* (K 157) and "intuitivité" for *Erblickbarkeit* (K 160); "doit être entendue à la fois comme" for *muß sich gabeln* (K 180); "réceptivité" for *Hinnahme*. Henry's own cuts, with the words present in his French: "(apparemment non fini)" (K 98), "représentée dans sa fonction de règle" (K 157), "intime et" (K 176), the warning against the narrow concept of taking-in (K 229), "originel" (K 248), "mais n'est pas encore connaissance" (K 84), "si donc" (K 149). Translators' insertions that favour Henry: "[l'objet]" (K 244), "[se trouve chez un sujet]" (K 245); "reflue" for *zurückblickt* (K 244) and "rétrospection" for *Zurück-auf-sich* (K 246).
11. **The translators' introduction is an intermediary.** De Waelhens and Biemel (K 10-41) name the doctrine "auto-affection pure" and gloss time as what "se forme soi-même en sortant de soi pour se diriger vers soi", "le devenir de soi dans l'extériorisation" (K 40-41); they read the Kantbuch's nothing as the "expérience du néant" of *Was ist Metaphysik?* (K 26); they question the *intuitus originarius* as foil (K 19 n. 1); they quote the K 199 sentence as the key to pure intuition (K 33). Henry does not cite the introduction. Treat these as the edition's framing that Henry's reading follows, not as proven influence.
12. **Henry's Hegel is doubly mediated, and it is the template for his Kantbuch.** (Added 2026-10-05.) R §§17-21 read Hegel through Heidegger's *Holzwege* essay and directly in Hyppolite; "Parousia" is Heidegger's gloss on Hegel's "près de nous" (PhE I 66), and the companion commentary R misassigns H 126 and H 190 to Hegel. Hyppolite's notes are part of Henry's Hegel: the unhappy consciousness "of Judaism" (R §19) is the translator's attribution (PhE I 176 n. 19, 177 n. 20), not Hegel's text. Henry's ellipses in PhE fall where Hegel's next clause tells against his use: I 198 (idealism of Kant and Fichte, Hyppolite n. 11), I 318 (the actual as being-for-another), II 303 ("la science" as subject), II 305 ("pas encore achevé"); at I 77 the omitted "l'être-en-soi ou l'être-pour-nous" would have helped him. The Appendix (R §§71-77) defines monism on Hegel (R §73 ¶2), joins Kant to Hegel (R §73 ¶7), reads Hegel's time as "l'essence même de l'objectivation" (R §75), and argues against Hegel's return-in-alienation (R §76 ¶¶8-9) what Section II asserts against GA 3 §34's "back to itself". GA 3 itself sets Kant's finitude against Hegel (§45, 244). Do not read the Kantbuch's self-affection as Hegel's alienation because Henry does; test the identification each time. GA 3 §29, 150 (K 207, "« soi » ... « extériorisé »") is the one Kantbuch sentence in the Hegelian form; Henry does not quote it.
13. **The Hegel essay is Heidegger's frame, not Hegel's text, and Henry translates it himself.** (Added 2026-10-05.) In "Hegels Begriff der Erfahrung" the Parousia is defined by representation (H 125, 130, 171) and named as Hegel's subjectity; Henry keeps the word and denies it representation. Henry renders *darstellen*/*Darstellung* as "(se) représenter"/"représentation" (H 174, 175, 176, 178, 181), so that the essay's thesis that presentation belongs to appearing (H 168) reads as an act of conscious life belonging to the essence (R §21 ¶5). His cuts: "schon" (H 126), "doch noch" before "nicht" and the next sentence "vorontologisch" (H 163), "schon zu sein" (H 167), the need for the reversal after "where it already is" (H 189-190; quoted in Section I, R §13 ¶6). His H 134 rendering turns "Schauplatz" into "le champ où l'Erscheinen parvient à l'intuition de soi" and "gebildet" into "fait". Many of his "corrections" restate the essay's next sentence (H 136, 163). The essay maps natural/real knowledge onto ontic/ontological (H 161-163); Henry substitutes existential/ontological. The essay's "für uns = an sich" (H 191), "Davor ... Vordergrund des Lichtes" (H 164; Henry's "avant-plan de lumière"), "Sicherscheinen des Erscheinens" (H 170, 173; Henry's "s'apparaître de l'apparaître"), and its reading of *Entäusserung* as *Er-innerung* with experience going out and "auf sich zurückkommt" (H 176; R §77 ¶9) are the sources to check whenever Henry speaks of Parousia, self-appearing, foreground of light, or alienation.

### Glossary (fixed for this run)

- Translation rule (user instruction, 2026-10-05): every German or French quotation carries an English rendering, in the citation parenthesis ("German" (English, H 126 / GA 5 137)) or as an apposition ("le fait du philosophe", the philosopher's doing). - Henry *étant* and Heidegger *Seiendes*: "beings", "a being". (Commentary R's "the existent" is not adopted.)
- Henry *l'essence*: "the essence" (of manifestation), always Henry's term.

Henry's *affection par soi*: "affection by itself" (time affected by nothing but itself, through the horizon it forms); *affection de soi*: "affection of itself" (time as what is affected). Henry's split exploits the French "de soi" (by itself / of itself). The French "auto-sollicitation" renders *Sich-selbst-angehen*: "going-at-oneself". (Corrected 2026-10-04: the 1953 translation renders *reine Selbstaffektion* as "affection pure de soi" some ten times and as "auto-affection pure" once, K 244, both in the passage of Henry's n. 103; *reine Affektion ihrer selbst* is "pure affection de lui-même", K 244. - Henry *phénoménalité*, *manifestation*, *révélation*: "phenomenality", "manifestation", "revelation". Henry *monisme ontologique*: "ontological monism". *Conscience naturelle*: "natural consciousness".
- Retained untranslated: Dasein (being-there).
