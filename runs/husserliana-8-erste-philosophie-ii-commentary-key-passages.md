## Backfill conventions

- **Unit.** No segmentation list survives from the original run. The commentary has no § numbers; its lowest heading is the lecture heading, so a unit is the block's ordinal under it: "Lecture 31.17" is the seventeenth non-empty paragraph under "#### Lecture 31". Lecture 53 has two sub-headings, a) and b), so its units are "Lecture 53a.n" and "Lecture 53b.n". Bullet items and `---` rules would not count; the commentary has none.
- **Found by.** Each reason opens with `commentary:`, `source:` or `both:`.
- **Citation.** Lecture and Hua VIII page, then `src L<n>`: the line of the source file where the passage's key sentence sits, read and checked. The commentary cites no pages, so every page comes from the file's page markers (±1; see caveats).
- **Translations.** All English renderings of the German are my own working renderings, not a published translation. English leads and the original follows in parentheses; originals are kept only where the nomination turns on them.

## Source caveats for Stage 2 (and for any close reading)

- **Layout.** English Kluwer/Nijhoff front matter at L36–60 is genuine (corpus map: `:41`–`:60`). The lectures run L263–c. L3010; Beilagen from L4097; Beilage I is Landgrebe's summary of contents (*Inhaltsübersicht*, L4099 ff.), which gives Hua VIII page numbers for each step; the text-critical apparatus is at L9124 ff. Lecture headings are mostly not Markdown headings; find them with `grep -n "Vorlesung:"`.
- **Page 157 is missing (a seam that lost text).** The file runs from the end of p. 156 (L2574, Lecture 50) to a marker `<!-- p. 158 -->` (L2578), a format used nowhere else in the lectures. The heading of Lecture 51 and its first page are absent. Checked against the whole file with several fragments ("in korrelativer Wendung" [in correlative turn], "So heißt mein Leben überschauen" [so to survey my life means], phrases from the house-reflection analogy): they occur only in the apparatus (L9237: "157 51. Vorlesung nur in B" [157: 51st lecture only in B], "157,11f. in korrelativer Wendung") and in Landgrebe's summary (L4197: "So heißt mein Leben überschauen, die Welt überschauen" [so to survey my life means to survey the world]). Consequence: commentary blocks Lecture 51.1–51.2 cannot be checked against the file; 51.3 starts at L2578. `conversion-history.md` says the excisions lost nothing of the original, since the excised leaves were blank. For this page that is not so, and it should be flagged there. The other pages without markers (44, 82–83, 132, 167, all section or lecture openings) have their text present.
- **Page markers** are HTML comments, nearly one per page for pp. 4–190. Pages here are taken from the last marker before the line, so they may be one off where a marker sits inside a long paragraph line.
- **Emphasis is present but uneven.** Italics survive throughout (about 650 italic spans in the lectures), but their density varies (Lectures 32 and 36 have three each), so the absence of italics proves nothing. The print's spaced type comes through three ways: as bold (L750, L1071, L1422), as spaced letters (L1424, L2172, L2194, L2528, L2702, L2722) or as both. Do not argue from which of the three a word has.
- **Mixed line format.** Most of Lectures 28–41 and 45 are hard-wrapped print lines with end-of-line hyphens (search with and without the hyphen); Lectures 31, 42 and 48–50 are one line per paragraph. Print-margin line numbers leak into the text (e.g. "Nicht anders, 35 wenn", L2456).
- **Footnotes are displaced.** Notes can be printed far from their markers, and note numbers restart on each page: the note for L1485 (Lecture 38) is at L1845; for L2333 (Lecture 46) at L2500; for L2804 (Lecture 53b) at L3008. Resolve notes by content, not number.
- **Greek.** Clean Unicode in the lectures (L415, L451, L469, L495, L524, L1960, L1986), except some instances of *ἐποχή* (epoché), which are LaTeX garble (L2038–2039, L2333).
- **Stage 2 searched:** italic and bold markup and spaced type; quotation marks („…“, ,,…'') within candidate passages; hyphenated coinages (*quasi*-, *Ich*-, *Welt*-); Greek; Latin set off in the text; definitional phrasing (*das Wort* [the word], *heißt* [means], *besagt* [says, means], *nennen wir* [we call], *Bezeichnung* [designation], *passend* [apt], *sozusagen* [so to speak], *terminologische Bemerkung* [terminological remark]); and the names of other authors (Descartes, Kant, Hume, Herbart, Plato, Leibniz, Locke, Bacon, Riemann, Lobachevsky). Not searched exhaustively: every single term in quotation marks (too frequent to triage outside candidates) and Latin beyond names and formulas. Criterion (a) used the 311 Husserl entries of `synthesis/lexicon.json`; most first hits fall in Lectures 28–31 as uses, not discussions, so (a) rows rest on blocks that define or contrast the term.

## Considered and dropped

These qualify but were dropped as weaker than the rows above, under the "strongest few" rule.

- Lecture 29.11 (src L511): "the recollection of the word 'vocation' (*Berufung*) gives us the answer". It is (d), but the block's weight is on personal affinity, and the appeal to the word is rhetorical, not an etymology the argument uses.
- Lecture 30.6 (src L575): "put out of validity in advance, in a certain way" (*vorweg in gewisser Weise außer Geltung gesetzt*), the first formulation of the epoché over the sciences. It is (b), but an anticipation; the epoché is defined later.
- Lecture 30.10 (src L613): Descartes' overthrow "once in a lifetime". It is (c), but Lecture 31.18 reconstructs the Cartesian rule more fully.
- Lecture 31.1 (src L690): the anonymity of functioning subjectivity. It is (a), but the term is used, not discussed.
- Lecture 34.6: Husserl's own guard against misreading (real possibility, empirical doubt, pure conceivability). It is (b), but it restates row 9's distinction.
- Lecture 34.7–34.13: the "objection from madness". It is (c), but its lesson is drawn in rows 12 and 13.
- Lecture 36.5 (src L1352): "is therefore judged" (*ist also gerichtet*), the juridical verdict on "the world exists". It is (d), but a single image.
