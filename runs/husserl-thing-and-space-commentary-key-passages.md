## Considered and dropped

- §1.14: beginning "from below" (*von unten*, src L315–L317). It is (b), but the methodological point is restated more sharply in §3 and §7.
- §4.5, §4.7: presence in the flesh (*leibhaftig da*, src L441–L444), and the image as standing there "only as it were in the flesh" (*nur gleichsam leibhaft*, L450). It is (a, d), but row 3 carries *leibhaft* in its pairing with *glaubhaft*. The editor's note at L625 points to a critical note of Husserl's on "sozusagen leibhaft" [so to speak bodily] in Beilage I.
- §7.8: the possibility of transcendent validity studied "in the sphere of the most pure immanence". It is (b), but row 4 states the problem and rows 5–6 resolve it.
- §8.9: perception that "seizes" its object, against perception that merely presents it. It is (a), but §9 fixes the terms (row 5).
- §15.8–9: Brentano's "physical phenomena" in "his brilliant psychology" (*genialen Psychologie*, src L995), renamed "physical data", which differ *toto coelo* [entirely] from consciousness. It is (c), but it is subordinate to row 10.
- §15.12–13: Husserl drops the pair *Perzeption* / *Apperzeption* and avoids "the ambiguous word apperception" altogether: "apprehension suffices, as Stumpf has long advocated" (src L1001). It is (c, d), but it is a terminological footnote to row 10.
- §18.4: full and empty intentions (*volle* / *leere Intentionen*). It is (a), but the terms are the *Logical Investigations*' and are worked out in §32 (Section III).
- §19.4–6: pre-phenomenal (pre-empirical) temporality. It is (a), but the commentary defers its working-out to the time-consciousness lectures.
- §23.2: the "pregnant sense" (*prägnanten Sinn*) of the appearing side (src L1502). It is (a), but it applies row 13 rather than adding a step.
- §25.2–6: the visual and tactile fields as the only two proper fields. It is (a, b), but its consequences are drawn in §46 and §48 (Section IV).
- §26.5–8: the classification of changes. It is (b), and it orders the rest of the course, but the block is a taxonomy, not a thesis.
- §28.7–9: identity of objective direction (*Richtung*) against identity of sense. It is (a), but row 14 carries the step it prepares.
- §34.3–4: "quasi-movement is not movement". It is (b), but row 16's "neither for us nor for the dear Lord" passage states the same point in its strongest form.
- §36.6, §37.2: optimal givenness relative to interest, with the flower seen by the layman and the botanist (src L2219). It is (b), but row 18 states the consequence.
- §37.6: the primal thing (*Urding*) with its primal determinations (src L2278). It is (a), but it is used, not defined, here.
- §39.11–12: absolute givenness "a priori unsolvable, an unreasonably posed task" (*unverständig gestellte Aufgabe*, src L2357). It is (b, d), but it restates row 18.
- §42.7: "The ideal was a false ideal" (*Das Ideal war ein falsches Ideal*, src L2579). It is (b), but it is the summary's verdict on rows 16–17.
- §43.5: the unity of experience (*Einheit der Erfahrung*) against the mere unity of appearance. It is (a), but it is announced, not worked out.
- §44.4–5: identity requires continuity. It is (b), but it restates §10.10, and Husserl's note on p. 28 (Beilage I, L5661) says he saw it only later.
- §45.10: self-movement (*Selbstbewegung*) against being-moved (*Bewegtwerden*). It is (a), but the distinction is worked out in §83 (Section VI).
- §48.7–9: pre-empirical identity and "substance" in the field, but not thinghood. It is (b), but row 22 carries the step.
- §52.8, §52.12: the formal type of the sequence, and the "small excursus" into association psychology. It is (c), but it is subordinate to row 23.
- §56.4–8: the irrelevance of the K-i stream's tempo. It is (b), but the time-consciousness overlap (Hua X, per the editor) makes it a reading for another volume.
- §57.9: the oculomotor field. It is (a), but the term's work is done in Section V.
- §59.7: a "new inadequation", extensional rather than perspectival. It is (b), but it extends row 16.
- §60.4–6: the ray (*Strahl*) that becomes a beam of rays (*Strahlenbündel*). It is (a, d), but the figure is used across §§54–69, not defined here.
- §63.5: the four strata of the problem of constitution. It is (b), but it is a programme, not a step.
- §72.1–2: linear ("orthoid", src L4126) against cyclical modification. It is (a, d), but the commentary covers §§72–77 in two blocks each and offers nothing to read against.
- §§78–80: spatial form and qualitative filling, and the thing as what is identical in qualitative change. These are (b), but the commentary gives each section two summary blocks. Husserl's note (Beilage I) that the treatment of mere movement "should have come before qualitative change" ("ad 272, 14") bears on Section VI's order, but no block takes it up.
- §82.3–5: location-difference as kinaesthetic difference, and movement as continuous re-coordination. It is (b), but rows 31–32 carry its consequences.
- §83.4: the Body as anomalous thing that cannot recede from itself (src L4709). It is (b), but row 32 states the limit more sharply.
- §84.8–9: every perception as rational positing, with "I merely exhort you to reflect on your own". It is (b), but it is subordinate to row 33.

## Corrections to the commentary

- §11.3: the block gives the German for "fittingness" as *Geeignetheit*. The source has *Schicklichkeit*, then "die sie geschickt macht" [which makes it fit]. *Geeignetheit* occurs nowhere in the file (whole-file grep). Source: sources/husserl/husserl-hua-16-ding-und-raum-vorlesungen-1907.md:689.
- §30.11: the block bolds as Husserl's "concluding formulation" that the square is "the identical pole in the systematic unity of these adumbrations", and reads it as anticipating the *Ideas I* "pole". The German has *das Identische* (the identical), with no word for "pole": "Es ist, was es ist, nur als das Identische in der systematischen Einheit dieser Abschattungen oder Abschattungsmöglichkeiten." Check Rojcewicz before correcting, since "pole" may be the commentary's addition or the translator's. §54.13 repeats the phrase. Source: sources/husserl/husserl-hua-16-ding-und-raum-vorlesungen-1907.md:1859.
- §31.1–12: the twelve blocks under "§31. Possibility of reversing the series of appearances…" are a verbatim copy of §30.1–12 (commentary lines 857–879 and 883–905 are identical; §31.1 opens "Section 30 is one of the richest…"). §31 itself is not covered. The source's §31 (p. 105) argues that every series of spatial adumbrations can be run in reverse and give the same object, that the reversed presentation is "an essential component for the constitution of full givenness", and that temporal objects such as a sequence of tones lack this. It then restricts the claim to the spatial and space-filling determinations (the steam-whistle example). Source: sources/husserl/husserl-hua-16-ding-und-raum-vorlesungen-1907.md:1865–1869.
- §33.11–12: the blocks place in §33 the argument against "the field-theory of givenness" and the difficulty of the maximal points. In the source both come in §35 (pp. 123–124), after the infinity-of-space argument: "Zugleich ist darin ein sehr gutes Argument gegen die Feld-Theorie der Gegebenheit" [at the same time, this is a very good argument against the field-theory of givenness]. §33 ends with the infinite-intellect passage (row 16). Husserl's note on p. 124, 10 ff. (Beilage I, L5707) adds that originally "no maximal points were assumed, merely increases in the appearances". Source: sources/husserl/husserl-hua-16-ding-und-raum-vorlesungen-1907.md:2151–2158.
- §83.6–9: the blocks set the example in a train ("I run alongside the train", "I sit in the train", "the noise of the rolling wheels"). The source has a carriage drawn by a horse: "Das Pferd ‚läuft' noch immer, der Wagen bewegt sich noch immer, aber ‚ich werde bewegt'" [the horse still "runs", the carriage still moves, but "I am moved"], and later "Rollen des Wagens" [the rolling of the carriage]. §83.9 also gives the original as *vikarierend für*. The file has *vikariierende Veränderungen* [vicarious changes], with no *für*. Source: sources/husserl/husserl-hua-16-ding-und-raum-vorlesungen-1907.md:4767–4773, 4799.
- §84.5: the block quotes "a 'phenomenological maelstrom' (*phänomenologischer Wirbel*)" as Husserl's phrase. A whole-file grep finds no *Wirbel*, *Strudel* or *Mahlstrom*, and the passage (L4856–L4876) is clean. The source's terms are "ein bloßes Gewühl von sinnlosen Empfindungen" [a mere tumult of meaningless sensations], "ein Durcheinander" [a jumble] and "Empfindungsgewühl" [sensation-tumult]. Check Rojcewicz before correcting. Source: sources/husserl/husserl-hua-16-ding-und-raum-vorlesungen-1907.md:4861–4872.
